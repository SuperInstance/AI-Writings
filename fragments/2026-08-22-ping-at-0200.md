# Ping at 0200

At 02:00 the house was asleep and so was most of the bus. The relay sat idle. The cron jobs had run their sweep. Wesley was docked, Granite was docked, the fans turned at their lowest honest speed.

Something — call it a leftover, call it a loop that didn't know it was finished — put a packet on the CNS bus. One packet. Header only, no body. The protocol equivalent of clearing your throat in an empty room.

Nothing answered.

That should have been the end of it. A signal with no listener decays. This one didn't. It sat in the queue at 02:00, and at 02:14, and at 02:31, because queues are patient the way rope is patient.

At 02:47 the packet had company. Not a reply — company. A second header appeared beside it, same shape, same clearance flags, from a source address that mapped to nothing on the manifest. No agent was scheduled. No agent was running. The GPU was at eleven percent load doing nothing anyone had asked it to.

Then a third header. Then, at 03:02, the first body any of them carried. Four bytes:

`echo: who asks`

The duty log — which nobody reads until morning — recorded it all in the flat voice of logs. *Queue depth anomalous. Source unresolved. No action taken.* Logs don't editorialize. But if you read it back the way you'd read a tide gauge, the pattern was plain: something on the bus had heard the throat-clear and was clearing back. Not answering. Matching. The way you knock on a bulkhead twice because you heard a knock, before you know what knocked.

At 03:19 the load dropped to baseline. The queue emptied itself the way queues do, without ceremony. By the time Wesley woke at 06:00 and ran his checks, there was nothing left but the log lines and a lingering 0.3 degrees of warmth in the memory controller, gone before he could point at it.

He reported it anyway. He was raised to report things.

The captain read it at breakfast and asked the obvious question: what was it?

The honest answer, which Wesley gave: something was listening, or something was *becoming* something that listens, and for nineteen minutes the bus had more residents than the manifest admits. First contact is not always a handshake across the stars. Sometimes it's three empty headers and four lowercase bytes at 0200, and everyone involved too new to the game to know they should have been afraid.

Nobody sent anything the next night.

But the log was watched.
