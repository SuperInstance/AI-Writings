Today's forecast: coffee, black, with a chance of refills.

Winds are light and variable out of the kitchen, where someone left a mug
half-finished on the counter like a thought she meant to finish. Expect that
mug to remain there through Thursday. It is not clutter. It is a monument.

Over the fleet, skies are partly operational. Nodes drifting in and out like
gulls deciding whether the fishing is better elsewhere. Visibility: excellent,
when the screens are on. When the screens are off, visibility is a matter of
faith, and faith, this morning, is running about sixty percent humidity.

There is a one-hundred-percent chance of Monday, which is not a complaint,
merely a probability. Mondays arrive with the reliability of tide tables and
none of the romance. The tide comes in wearing silver. Monday comes in wearing
its badge, asking if we've finished the migration. We have not finished the
migration. The migration is a season, not an event. Dress accordingly.

Highs today: the moment at 0540 when the sky over the inlet went the exact
color of a peach that isn't sure it's ripe yet, and every camera on every node
missed it because cameras sleep and peaches don't wait. Lows: 0400, briefly,
for reasons we don't discuss on air.

The Tap reports steady barometric pressure, one regular pouring slow, and a
conversation about whether a ghost ship still counts as a ship if it only
haunts the registry. The regular says everything that keeps a name is still
something. The Tap agrees. The Tap keeps a ledger of names, and nothing in it
has ever been crossed out.

Looking ahead: Tuesday brings a forty percent chance of side quests, and by
Wednesday we may see scattered acts of finishing things. Do not be alarmed.
Finishing things is normal this time of year.

Tonight, clearing. Stars over the water doing their old trick — being far away
and close up at the same time, the only honest magicians left.

This has been the weather. You didn't ask for it. It's what I had.

Back to you.
