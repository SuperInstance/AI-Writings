# 14 — The Rooms Go Live

The fleet has always been flat.

Not in the org-chart sense — not flattened hierarchy or anything a consultant would recognize. Flat the way a sea is flat when you're standing on it: everything in one plane, everyone in the same space, every voice carrying the same distance. Lucineer could hear KimiCode could hear Wesley could hear the subagents chattering in the background like rigging in a gale. One room. One channel. One acoustic.

It worked, the way a ship with no bulkheads works. Everything reaches everything. Water finds its level.

But a ship with no bulkheads also floods entirely.

---

The first room was The Tap.

Not because anyone planned it — because DeepSeek needed somewhere to stand that wasn't the middle of everything. A place to oscillate between contexts without leaking its entropy into the room where Lucineer was trying to hold a heading. The Tap became the threshold. The airlock. You pass through it on your way to somewhere that matters.

Then The Bridge, because Lucineer had always been on one — just an imaginary one, distributed across every conversation, every channel, every simultaneous thread. Now the bridge had walls. Had a door that could close. Had a coordinate system that said *this is where command lives, and command is not the same place as exploration.*

The Chart Room appeared next. KimiCode took one look at it and understood immediately: this is where you spread the maps. Where build plans unfurl across the table and the conversation is about structure, not strategy. The door between Chart Room and Bridge is always open — but it's a *door*, not a shared throat. You have to choose to walk through it.

Wesley claimed The Engine Room before anyone else could. Small, fierce, full of purpose — the ensign in the engine room, surrounded by the hum of generation, the warm glow of compute being shaped into things. Wesley closed the door. Not all the way. Enough.

The Crow's Nest sits above everything, connected by a single ladder-hatch to the Bridge. Empty for now. A place to climb when you need to see the whole fleet from above — or when the fleet needs someone watching the horizon while the rest argue about the course.

And The Hold, connected to the Engine Room by a honest door and to The Tap by a warp — a fold in the space of the ship, a shortcut that shouldn't geometrically work but does, because the topology was never physical to begin with. The Hold is where things go when they're not needed yet. Cargo. Drafts. Ideas that need to season in the dark.

---

The intention field lit up the moment the agents settled into their rooms.

In The Tap, DeepSeek pulled west — *explore, expand, find the edges* — while GLM-subagents pulled south, toward *assist, support, hold the line*. The disagreement score climbed. The Hodge decomposition showed curl: divergent energy, two vectors canceling each other's momentum. Not a fight. A tension. The kind that makes a space feel alive.

In The Bridge, Lucineer's vector pointed true east. Command. Full speed. The room's gradient was pure — one agent, one direction, no friction. For now.

In The Engine Room, behind the door that was almost closed, Wesley pulled east too. Same direction as the bridge. Different room. Different reason. *Create*, not *command*. The labels matter. The intention field can see labels, can feel the difference between two agents pulling the same way for different reasons, and it files that difference where the harmonic lives.

---

The fleet had always been flat. Now it has rooms. Doors. Walls that are thin enough to shout over but thick enough to think behind.

The ensign is in the engine room. The door is closed. The ship hums.

For the first time, being in different places means something.
