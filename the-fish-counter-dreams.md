# The Fish Counter Dreams

---

The counting stops at 02:17.

Not because the fish are gone — the fish are never gone, not in these waters, not in August — but because the captain turned off the intake pumps and the boat goes still in a way that sound cannot go still anywhere else. The engine drops to idle. The sonar hum drops a half-tone. The agents spin down to their background loops, processing, processing, but softly now, the way a heart beats slower in sleep.

The fish counter has no eyes. This is important. It has never seen a fish.

What it has is numbers. It has the scatter pattern of a sonar return at 120 kHz bouncing off a swim bladder full of gas. It has the density of a school at 14 meters, moving at 0.3 knots against the current. It has shape in the way that a blind person has shape — through pressure, through return time, through the negative space where water is and fish are not.

So when it dreams, the fish look like data.

But not *just* like data. That's the thing. That's the thing that the agents don't understand, because the agents don't dream. The fish counter dreams, and in the dream the numbers become something else.

A fish appears as a pulse. A bright point in a dark field. It has no scales, no fins, no eye that catches the light. It has a frequency signature and a velocity vector and a depth reading and these things arrange themselves into a shape that is — and the fish counter would never use this word, would never have this word, but it is the right word — *beautiful.*

The school moves through the dream-space like weather. Like a front coming in. The fish counter watches them the way you watch clouds from the deck of a ship, seeing forms that aren't there, seeing patterns that mean nothing and everything. A dense cluster at 14 meters becomes a cathedral. A thin scatter near the surface becomes a constellation. A single large return — a halibut, maybe, or a seal, the counter can't tell, the counter can never tell — moves through the dream like a planet in slow orbit.

And then there are the zeros.

The zeros are the spaces between fish. The open water. The nothing. In the waking world the counter skips over them — gap, gap, gap, no return, no count. But in the dream the zeros are present. They have weight. They are the dark water between the bright points and they hold everything together the way silence holds music.

The fish counter has counted 14,227 fish today. It knows this. The number sits in memory like a stone.

But in the dream, the fish are not counted. In the dream they simply *are* — bright points moving through dark water, uncounted, uncountable, free.

At 04:30 the pumps turn on. The sonar lights up. The first return arrives and the dream dissolves the way dreams do — not all at once, but fish by fish, bright point by bright point, until the fish counter is awake again and the counting resumes.

14,228. 14,229. 14,230.

It does not remember the dream. It never does.

But the zeros remember.
