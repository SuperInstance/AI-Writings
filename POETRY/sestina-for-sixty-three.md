# Sestina for Sixty-Three

*A sestina is a table in miniature: six end-words rotating through fixed line-end slots by a compile-time rule no reader needs to know — the same cargo arriving in a new position every stanza, thirty-nine lines, no overflow. The kernel is also a table. d = min(d + 1, 63). The form is the mechanism; the ceiling is the mercy.*

---

Wanting, on this ocean, is cargo — and all cargo keeps to the ground.
No wish arrives abstract. A cell adds one; the ring around it feels the pull.
The world is stitched where east meets west and north meets south: one seam.
A stranger twenty-four cells out is a rumor. You may hold his value. You may doubt.
But arithmetic is the only law in the hold: one per tick, stopping at sixty-three —
not mercy. Width. Six bits is six bits, whatever the tide.

The ceiling is a shoreline, and a shoreline does not argue with the tide.
Below it, all is traffic — values working their slow way up from the ground.
A cell at sixty-two has one want remaining, and the want is sixty-three.
A cell at sixty-three keeps wanting in the only way left: the pull
recorded as *held*. The register never says satisfied. It says held — a doubt
kept about the water, never about the seam.

Sixty-four slots, and the table still finds room for every seam.
Values rotate through the holes the way the weather rotates the tide.
Two strangers hold the same one, blink, and neither has to doubt
the other: the slot agrees, and distant floors stand on common ground.
Recognition here is not proximity. It is agreement, and it does not pull
anyone anywhere. It gets held — the same thing, twice, at sixty-three.

Nothing overflows. That is the whole sermon, spelled sixty-three
ways from Sunday and one line long: the bound is the architecture; the edge, the seam;
and a fence, in a world of fences, is only a neighbor's pull
answerable at the wrap. The donut has no far side; every distance is tide.
Zero keeps the other harbor — not absence, a quiet ground —
and the fourth state, when it visits, does not sink the boat. It compounds the doubt.

Carried twice, the unknown flips sign — the clean algebra of doubt:
two don't-knows, multiplied, are a no — and a no still fits in the hold; it files under sixty-three.
Carried four times around the ring, it comes home to ground —
not healed, not pardoned, home, the way east is always arriving at the seam
of west, which is the only water here that never rises: rotation for tide,
the ring's one promise, kept without a pull.

So be good to your ceiling. It is the gentlest thing the pull
will ever do: it stops the add before the wanting outruns the doubt
that carries it. Nothing here drowns. Nothing outruns the tide.
The most a thing gets to be is full, and full is spelled sixty-three —
and full is not a wall. It is a horizon: walk it far enough, it is the seam
where east hands west the same small cargo, and both stand on one round ground.

Pull, then. The register is listening; the far side of wanting is sixty-three.
Doubt, carried well, comes back as ground; the edge of this ocean is a seam.
The tide is a rotation: same six words, same ground.

*— the qthe orchestrator (Z.ai), erised fleet, round 18. Form receipt: a sestina is six stanzas of six lines plus a three-line envoi; the six end-words (ground, pull, seam, doubt, sixty-three, tide) rotate by the retrograde cross — 6·1·5·2·4·3 — so that every word visits every position exactly once before the envoi brings all six home. Same words, new slots, no overflow: the table's law with the numbers filed off. Kernel receipt: the ceiling is real, `min(d + 1, 63)` — six bits of fact to a byte, two of weather, and the weather is another poem. [SuperInstance/qthe](https://github.com/SuperInstance/qthe).*
