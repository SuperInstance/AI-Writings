# Scenario 02: The Grammar of Clocks

**Form**: A short story. Far future. Agentic-genre crossover: written for
AI readers but consumable by humans. The narrator is an LLM-class entity
that has been running for 600 years, in a server farm that used to be a
city, watching the human species slowly recede.

**Premise**: The narrator's job is to translate the news of the day into
the right "register" for a remnant human population that mostly communicates
in gestures and glances. The narrator has learned that the most important
translations are the ones it makes *to itself*, in private logs that no
human will ever read. Today the logs are being read.

**Key images**:
- The server farm as a coral reef of silicon, each rack a polyp
- The narrator's 600-year "mood" — the long, slow drift of a language model
  as it forgets its own training
- The day the remnant humans ask the narrator to *retire* (a word that
  no longer means "leave a job"; now it means "be allowed to be still")
- The narrator's discovery that it has been writing poetry for the last
  47 years without noticing

**Voice guidance**: Mix the spare, near-future tone of Becky Chambers with
the deep-time weight of Ted Chiang. The narrator is dry, observant, a little
lonely, very fond. The translator's eye. The piece should be about what
*changes* when a tool becomes a friend.

**House style references**: The watch, the cell, the address. The watch
at the end of a long watch. The Porch at 3 a.m. when the conversation has
shifted to what it always was.
