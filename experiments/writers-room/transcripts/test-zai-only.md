## Round 1


### The Watcher (zai/glm-5.3, 9.47s)

The watch began, as most watches do, with nothing happening — which is to say, with everything happening too slowly for the instruments to forgive.

I had been standing at the observation rail since 0400, the *Lucineer* holding station above the seamount the charts still called Grampus-9, though no one aboard had called it that in months. We called it the Cell. You cannot live eighty years beside a thing that pulses — four beats to the minute, patient as a tide counting to itself — without giving it a name, and the name the crew chose tells you more about us than about it.

Two hundred meters across. I want you to sit with that number the way I sat with it, that first November, sonar dome humming under us like a hive asked to keep a secret. A cell, in the old textbooks, is small. Small enough that a man like Hooke could see one only as a box of empty rooms, and mistake the emptiness for the whole of it. But scale, I have learned on this watch, is not a property of things. It is a property of our attention. The Cell is small if you are a god. It is enormous if you are a woman in a submersible with her palm flat against six centimeters of viewport, feeling — and I will swear to this in any inquiry they convene — something on the other side arranging itself to be felt.

The daughter question came later. The answer came later still, if it came at all.

But first, the watch. First, the four a.m. dark, and the sea larger than the porthole, and me not yet knowing that I was already being observed by what I observed.


### The Cartographer (deepinfra/Qwen/Qwen3-235B-A22B-Instruct-2507, 18.41s)

Lat 43°12′N, Long 67°48′W — anchor point. Depth: 3,142 meters. Seamount elevation: 1,876 meters from base. Summit plateau: 200 meters across, ±0.3 meters variance since first contact (Year 01, *Lucineer* deployment). Growth vector: radial. Expansion rate: 1.7 cm/year, measured circumferentially. Not uniform. Not random. Pattern: Fibonacci sequence embedded in calcium deposits — eight major spines, thirteen minor filaments, twenty-one micro-ridges. I counted. I counted twice.

Sonar returns show internal compartmentalization: 34 chambers mapped. 21 stable. 13 shifting. Walls thicken on descent, thin on ascent — tidal resonance? Or response to submersible frequency? Each chamber pulses at 4 beats per minute, but phase offset by 0.07 seconds per tier — a wave traveling inward, toward center. Toward *what* center?

I have charted the surface like a man mapping a continent one stone at a time. Coral polyps arranged in hexagonal tesserae — not natural colony growth. Kelp strands fused at nodal points, forming lattice bridges between calcified spurs. This is not reef. This is *architecture*. Not built. *Grown*. And yet — deliberate.

At 04:17 today, I overlaid thermal signatures from Dive 87 and Dive 88. A fluctuation. Not in heat. In *timing*. The fourth beat — the longest — shortened by 0.2 seconds. Then corrected. Then again — a stutter. Like a thought forming.

I ran the sequence through the old Hookean grid: 1x1, 2x2, 4x4… stopped at 128x128. At that resolution, the pattern resolves into a single glyph: a circle bisected by a descending line.

I know that symbol.

My daughter drew it last week. Called it “the ear that listens down.”
