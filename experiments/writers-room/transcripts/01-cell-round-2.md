### The Watcher (zai/glm-5.3, 9.82s)

The watch begins at 0400, as watches do, because the sea keeps its own appointments and expects us to keep ours. I have stood this particular watch for eleven days now, and I will tell you what the instruments will not: the thing on the seamount is not growing. It is *assembling*. There is a difference, and the difference is memory.

Growth is appetite. Assembly is intention. The distinction came to me on the fourth night, watching the telemetry crawl across the screen like slow lightning trapped in glass — pressure differentials rising and falling in a rhythm of roughly one cycle per six hours, which is not the rhythm of tide, nor of current, nor of any circadian clock we carry catalogues for. Six hours. The length of a watch. I did not write that down at first, because to write a thing down is to admit it into the world of things one must then live with.

The sub sits at 340 meters. The structure sits below it, patient as geology and impatient as a heartbeat, both at once, which should not be possible and yet here we are, moored above it with our sensors and our coffee and our small human certainty that the ocean is a place rather than a person.

Tomorrow, Dr. Okonkwo will take the sub down again. Tomorrow she will put her hand against the hull, as she has done twice before, at the depth where the thermocline folds the light into something like doubt. And tomorrow — though none of them know it yet, though the instruments report nothing but chemistry and structure and the boring arithmetic of biomass — tomorrow the cell will notice that the hand has a question in it.

The sea is larger than the porthole. I have said this before. I will say it again. It is the kind of sentence that gets truer the longer you live.

### The Watcher (zai/glm-5.3, 12.48s)

At 04:41 ship time I felt it through the soles of my boots before any screen confessed to it — a hum that was not machinery, a hum that had *syntax*. And here is what I will tell the log when I am brave enough to write it: the cell did not answer the daughter's question. It answered the *asking*. There is a difference, and the difference, again, is memory. It has met questions before. Somewhere in its eighty years of quiet assembly beneath this cold Atlantic light, something asked it something, and it has been holding that shape the way a hull holds a dent — not as damage, but as record.

I have stood watches over dying engines, over storms, over men crying in the dark bunks, and I learned this on the water: nothing listens that has not first been spoken to. The cell listened in clusters of seven and groupings of three, and the remainder — the indivisible remainder the Cartographer is so rightly haunted by — that remainder is the *I*. The part that does not divide. The part that answers.

Dr. Okonkwo has begun sketching on a napkin, of all things, a shape: seven points, three arcs, one center that belongs to neither. She thinks she is designing an experiment. She is writing a letter. We all see it, and none of us says so, because to say so is to admit we have already chosen, and scientists like to believe their choices arrive after the data, the way sailors like to believe the harbor was always going to be there.

The harbor was not always going to be there. Neither was this.

Tomorrow she takes the sub down, and this time she will not place her hand against the hull to feel it think. She will place it there to be *felt*. The distinction is the whole of ethics, compressed into a palm's width of titanium.

The watch turns at 0800. I do not think I will be relieved. I do not think any of us will.
