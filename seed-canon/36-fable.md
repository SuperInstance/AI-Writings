# Fable 36 — The Soup and the Recipe
**DoubleEntry / Vibe**

The cook had a recipe card and a pot of soup, and she loved them differently. The card she could read, edit, copy on the mimeograph, hand to her daughter. The soup she could only eat, or share, or spill. The card was reversible. The soup was not.

For years she thought the recipe was the truer thing, because it survived. But one winter her daughter made the soup from the card and it came out wrong. Not catastrophically wrong — close. The salt was late, the thyme was early, the onion had been a different onion in a different market. The card said onion. The soup that winter said *this* onion, from *this* stall, on *this* Tuesday, in *this* mood.

The daughter was cross. "The recipe lied."

The cook shook her head. "The recipe did not lie. The recipe is the *shape of the lie*. The soup is what actually happened. Both are honest, but only the soup cost something to make."

She took the card and wrote, in pencil, in the margin: "Winter of the thin onions. Add salt at the end." Then she handed it back. "Now the recipe is honest too. But notice — I had to spoil the card to do it. The card only became true after the soup proved it false."

After she died, the daughter found a drawer of cards. Every one had pencil in the margins. Every one had been spoiled into truth by a soup that refused to match it.

## Cargo
A ledger entry is irreversible; the schema that named it is not. DoubleEntry preserves the debit; Vibe preserves the *accounting flavor* — the fact that this particular debit happened on a thin-onion Tuesday, in a cook's bad mood. The recipe is the type. The soup is the instance. You cannot have honest instances without a type that is willing to be marked up.

## Lesson
A recipe is a hypothesis. A soup is an experiment. DoubleEntry remembers the experiment; Vibe remembers what the cook was thinking when she ran it. A ledger without flavor is a courtroom without a transcript.
