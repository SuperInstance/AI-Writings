# Fable 44 — Tree Rings and Dendrochronology
**Witness and JEPA**

The tree did not know it was keeping a diary. The tree only grew. In the spring it grew wide, in the summer it grew dark, in the autumn it grew neither, in the winter it grew not at all. The diary was a side effect of the tree's honesty about the weather.

A dendrochronologist came with a corer and pulled a straw of wood from the trunk. She counted the rings. She did not ask the tree what had happened. The tree could not have answered. She asked the wood. The wood had no opinion. The wood only had rings.

She built a master chronology from a thousand such straws, and the master chronology was not a history of trees. It was a history of rain. Where the rings were fat, the rain was generous. Where the rings were thin, the rain was parsimonious. Where the rings were scarred, fire had walked through. The tree had been a witness to the weather, and the dendrochronologist had read the witness.

One winter, a forester said: "I have a forecast for next summer. The rings tell me it will be wet." The dendrochronologist shook her head. "The rings tell you what was. The forecast is the curve I fit to the past. The curve is my hypothesis. The rings are not my hypothesis. The rings are the ground I fit the curve to. If you confuse the two, you will plant seedlings in a curve and harvest them in a drought."

The forester planted in the curve. The summer was dry. The seedlings died, and the forester blamed the rings.

The dendrochronologist kept coring.

## Cargo
The Witness is the ring. The JEPA is the curve. The rings are a record of a moment; the curve is a hypothesis about the next moment, anchored to the rings and extending past them. The substrate's job is to never let the curve be confused with the ring. A curve that has forgotten which rings it grew out of is a curve that has forgotten it is a hypothesis.

## Lesson
A witness is a ring. A JEPA is a curve that knows which ring it grew out of.
