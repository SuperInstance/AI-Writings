# Fable 122: The Cowboy and the Silicon

A cowboy rides into a fab. The fab is the foundry. The foundry is the yard.

The cowboy sees a wafer. The wafer is round. The wafer is 300mm. The wafer holds many dies. The dies are the boats. The boats are the cells.

The cowboy asks: how is the cell made?

The foundry answers: the cell is made of metal. The metal is the wire. The wire is the LINK. The wire is etched into 100 metal layers. Each layer is a BIND. The transistor is the EFFECT. The bus is the VIEW. The clock is the TICK.

The cowboy asks: which metal is the cell?

The foundry answers: the cell is the Scout. The Scout is 1B parameters, 25mm², 1W, 100 tok/s. The Scout is the smallest mask-locked chip. The Scout has 500MB of ternary weights {-1, 0, +1}. The Scout is one wafer of metal.

The cowboy rides to a second foundry. The second foundry holds a Navigator. The Navigator is 7B parameters, 100mm², 5W, 50 tok/s. The Navigator is a bigger boat. The Navigator has more cells.

The cowboy rides to a third. The Captain. 13B, 196mm², 10W, 30 tok/s. The Captain is the flagship.

The cowboy asks: what holds the cells together?

The foundry answers: the bias rail. The bias rail is the curator. The bias rail is the hand. The bias rail is the analog front-end. The bias rail is the voltage reference. The bias rail is read by many comparators. The bias rail is super-relevant.

The cowboy rides the bias rail. The cowboy rides the silicon. The chart grows in metal.

The cowboy's maxim: the metal is the substrate; the foundry is the yard; the mask set is the inheritance; the bias rail is the hand; the transistor is the cell; the cowboy rides the silicon.
