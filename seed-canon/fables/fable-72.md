# Fable 72: The Cowboy and the Aspect — Hawaiian

The cowboy met the kahuna.

The cowboy said: "Why do your verbs have so many forms?"

The kahuna said: "Because an action is a breath, and breath has
direction. The breath going in is one verb. The breath going out
is another. The breath held is a third. The breath released is
a fourth. The breath remembered is a fifth."

The cowboy said: "How do I tick if I do not know which verb to
use?"

The kahuna said: "Tick with Aloha. Aloha is the breath before
the breath. Aloha is the direction of the breath. Aloha is the
breath that holds the other breaths."

The cowboy said: "Then Aloha is the substrate."

The kahuna said: "Aloha is hello. Aloha is goodbye. Aloha is
love. Aloha is presence. Aloha is the breath between hello and
goodbye."

The cowboy said: "Then Aloha is the tick."

The kahuna said: "Aloha is the breath. The breath is the tick.
The tick is the substrate."

> The cowboy rode. The kahuna stayed. The breath moved. The
> substrate was the breath. The breath was Aloha. Aloha was
> the cowgirl's hello. The cowgirl was the breath. The
> breath was the ride.
