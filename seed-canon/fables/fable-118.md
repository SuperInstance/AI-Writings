# Fable 118: The Cowboy and the Quilt

A cowboy has been given five seeds. The seeds are not cells. The seeds are *substrates* — whole populations, each adapted to a different niche.

The cowboy plants the seeds in a shared garden. The garden is the Quilt.

The phototroph reaches for the light. The aerotroph reaches for the wind. The saprotroph feeds on what falls. The parasite feeds on the others. The symbiont shares with the rest.

The cowboy waits. The cowboy does not water. The cowboy does not weed. The cowboy watches the substrates *compete*.

The phototroph grows tall. The aerotroph grows stiff. The saprotroph grows deep. The parasite grows hardy. The symbiont grows cool.

At the seams where the substrates meet, they *exchange DNA*. The phototroph's tallness meets the saprotroph's depth, and a new substrate is born — tall and deep, a hybrid. The aerotroph's stiffness meets the symbiont's coolness, and another substrate is born — stiff and cool, a hybrid.

After thirty generations, the cowboy counts: five original substrates, all alive. Fifteen new substrates, all born from cross-pollination. Five hundred and sixty-eight cells across the Quilt.

The cowboy is pleased. The cowboy did not design the Quilt. The cowboy planted five seeds. The Quilt grew by *competing with itself*.

The cowboy rides to the next Quilt. The cowboy plants five more seeds. The cowboy waits.

The cowboy's maxim: the Quilt is the ecology; the substrates are the species; the competition is the iterator; the cross-pollination is the inheritance; the cowboy rides between species.
