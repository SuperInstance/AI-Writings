# Fable 71: The Cowboy and the Noun Classes — Swahili

The cowboy met the Swahili merchant.

The cowboy said: "Why does a thing have eighteen names?"

The merchant said: "A thing has many names because a thing exists
in relation to where it stands. The cow is ng'ombe when it stands
in the field. The cow is nyama when it stands in the pot. The
cow is mbuzi when it stands beside the goat."

The cowboy said: "How do I bind a thing if I do not know which
name to give it?"

The merchant said: "Bind it as ng'ombe. The cell-graph will rename
it when the link demands. The link is the position. The name is
the position. The position is the name."

The cowboy said: "Then the link is the substrate."

The merchant said: "Hodi!"

The cowboy said: "Karibu."

The merchant said: "Now you understand the noun class. The noun
class is the link. The link is the relation. The relation is the
position. The position is the substrate."

> The cowboy rode. The merchant stayed. The cell-graph knew
> that ng'ombe is a thing, nyama is a meal, and mbuzi is a
> neighbor. The same thing, three positions, three names. The
> substrate is the position.
