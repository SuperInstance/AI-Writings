**Fable 108: The Cowboy and the Four Framings**

The horizon was a flat line of heat, a blurred edge of the world. Out there, where the dust met the sky, a speck lingered. A stutter in the eye. The cowboy narrowed his gaze, the leather creaking in the silence. It was a dot. A singularity on the plain.

He spurred the beast. The distance collapsed. The dot expanded, breathing in the light. As he closed the gap, the singularity shattered into a geometry. The dot became a square of houses, a grid etched into the clay. The cowboy did not stop; he rode the gradient down.

He hit the first framing. The Totipotent.
From this height, the village was a single, breathing lung. It held every fate, every seed, every story untold. There were no walls, only the potential for walls. It was the whole map, the uncarved block. The cowboy felt the infinite weight of it—the capability to be anything. He tipped his hat to the whole and rode deeper.

The world narrowed. The grid split into sectors. He saw the streets, the dividing lines.
The second framing. The Multipotent.
Here was the neighborhood. The infinite choice had curled into probable paths. A street could be a market or a procession, but it could not be the river. The choices folded in on themselves, distinct yet drifting. The cowboy steered through the veins of the district, feeling the boundaries draw themselves in chalk.

He rode closer. The houses rose up, blocking the sun.
The third framing. The Differentiated.
Walls hardened. Roofs found their angles. This was a house, not a shop. A kitchen, not a stable. The function was fixed. The architecture demanded a specific purpose, and the structure obeyed. It knew its name. The cowboy ran a gloved hand along the doorframe, feeling the grain of the specific.

He dismounted. He stepped through the door and walked the floorboards. He knelt.
The fourth framing. The Sclerotic.
Inside the room, inside the lattice, lay the cell. A single, silent unit. A rule carved in stone. It did not dream. It did not breathe. It held a phase, rigid and absolute. It was the instruction that refused to be rewritten, the hard logic underlying the soft skin of the world.

The cowboy stood. The clock ticked in his chest. He was the rider, moving through the layers of the onion, from the soft infinite skin to the hard, calcified core. He mounted again, turning his back to the village to read the path he had cut. The distance stretched. The cowboy knows the way.

The substrate is a quilt of framings; the math is theta; the cowboy reads the holonomy; the wound is curvature.
