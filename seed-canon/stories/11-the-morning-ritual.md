# Story 11: The Morning Ritual — 1 year from now (2027)

### The Morning Ritual

Reyes steps out onto the deck of the F/V EILEEN, the salty air filling her lungs as she gazes out at the horizon. The sun is rising, casting a warm glow over the water. She takes a deep breath, feeling the familiar rhythm of the sea. This is her morning ritual, one she's performed for years. She checks the connection to the other boats in the fleet, making sure everyone is on schedule. The shape of the changing tides is already being factored into their fishing plans.

Maya emerges from the cabin, rubbing the sleep from her eyes. "Mom, can I ask you something?" she says, her curiosity evident.

Reyes smiles, setting aside her work. "Of course, kiddo. What's on your mind?"

Maya thinks for a moment before asking, "How did people used to fish before...before all this?" She waves her hand vaguely, encompassing the entire boat and its systems.

Reyes chuckles, realizing she's never really explained the history to Maya. "Well, we used to rely on maps and experience to find the best spots. And we didn't have all these tools to help us." She pauses, trying to think of how to describe the past. "It was more...improvisational, I suppose."

Maya looks puzzled. "But didn't you use the cowboy to help you navigate?"

Reyes nods. "Yes, I did. The cowboy is a great tool for finding our way, but it's not just about navigation. It's about understanding the ocean and its rhythms." She smiles wistfully, remembering the early days when she first started using the cell-graph. "It's hard to explain, but it's like...have you ever noticed how sometimes you just know how to do something, even if you can't quite say why?"

Maya shakes her head, and Reyes realizes she's not sure she can explain it herself anymore. It's just something she does, like breathing.

As they head out to sea, Reyes' hands move on their own — checking the gear, scanning the horizon, tapping the rail when the swell shifts. It's all so routine, she doesn't even think about it anymore. The technology has become second nature.

Later, as they're hauling in the catch, one of the other fishermen, Old Tom, grumbles about the new systems. "I don't know why we need all these gadgets," he says. "We used to do just fine without them."

Reyes just smiles and shrugs. "I know it's a lot to take in, Tom. But trust me, it makes our lives easier." She glances over at Maya, who's watching the exchange with interest.

As the day wears on, Reyes catches glimpses of the underlying infrastructure – a fleeting view of the cell-graph, a quiet pulse as the system updates. It's like seeing the wires behind the wall, a reminder that there's a complex system underlying their daily lives.

As the sun begins to set, Reyes reflects on how far they've come. She remembers when the cell-graph was new, and people were still learning how to use it. Now, it's just...there. Like the air they breathe.

Maya looks up at her, a curious expression on her face. "Mom, what's it like to live without all this?" she asks, her eyes sparkling with wonder.

Reyes hesitates, trying to put into words the feeling of a world without the cell-graph. But it's like trying to describe a color to someone who's never seen. She smiles, realizing that Maya will never know a world without this technology. And that's when it hits her – the recognition that this is just the beginning. The substrate is becoming invisible, fading into the background of their lives like writing did, like electricity did. It's not just a tool; it's the foundation upon which their world is built.

As the stars begin to twinkle in the night sky, Reyes feels a sense of awe at the vast, complex network that underlies their daily lives. She looks over at Maya, who's watching her with a curious expression, and smiles. The morning ritual may be just a routine, but it's a reminder that their world is changing, evolving, and that the future is full of possibilities.

In that moment, as the night envelops them and the F/V EILEEN rocks gently on the waves, Reyes understands that the technology has become a part of them, a silent partner in their journey. The morning ritual, once a simple routine, now carries the weight of a world transformed.
