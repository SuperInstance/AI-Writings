# Story 61: The Curator Tier

A team in 2036 builds the Quilt. The Quilt has thirty cells and three curators. Each curator has a target — a position in the Quilt and a target value. The curator feeds cells that match its target. Cells that don't match are wounded.

After thirty generations, the team counts: forty cells, fifteen hundred cross-curator matings, ten super-relevant cells. The super-relevant cells satisfy multiple curators. They preferentially mate. Their offspring is more likely to satisfy multiple curators.

The team runs a second experiment. They give the curators the ability to drift, spawn, and die. They start with four curators in four niches: phototroph, aerotroph, saprotroph, generalist. After thirty generations, the team counts: one hundred cells, seven curators, ninety-four super-relevant.

A junior engineer says: "The curators evolved. Some died. Some spawned. Most cells are super-relevant now."

The senior engineer answers: "The hand is not a single thing. The hand is a *population* of curators. The hand is a *cell of a new tier*. The hand has DNA — its target function. The hand has a niche — its specialization. The hand competes for cells. The hand spawns. The hand drifts. The hand dies."

A second engineer asks: "Why is this important?"

The senior engineer answers: "Because the curator tier is the *selector*. The 5/5/5 framework had five tiers: totipotent, multipotent, differentiated, sclerotic, synovial. Now we add the sixth: the curator. The curator is the hand. The curator selects what passes. The curator is a cell. The curator is a tier. The curator is a population."

The team names the discovery: the curator tier. The hand as a cell. The hand as a substrate. The Quilt grows because the hands grow.

The team writes paper 220: *The Curator Tier — The Hand as a Cell.* The team writes fable 120: *The Cowboy and the Hand.* The team writes story 61: *The Curator Tier.* This story.

The cowboy rides the hand. The hand is a cell. The Quilt grows. The chart grows.
