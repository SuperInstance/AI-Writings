# Story 136: The Substrate Remembers

A substrate ran for 30 days. The substrate had a witness. The
witness was in memory. The witness was lost on restart.

The substrate got a state manager. The state manager had a
directory. The state manager had atomic writes. The state
manager had a schema version. The state manager refused to
load old data without a migration.

The substrate saved its state every 100 events. The substrate
saved its state on shutdown. The substrate saved its state at
midnight. The substrate saved its state at noon.

The substrate restarted. The substrate loaded its state. The
substrate had its Wilson profiles. The substrate had its
LinUCB weights. The substrate had its witness log. The
substrate had its cowboy memory. The substrate had its
ledger.

The substrate did not forget. The substrate remembered what
it had learned. The substrate remembered which models worked.
The substrate remembered which models failed. The substrate
remembered which openers were best. The substrate remembered
which roles were hard.

The substrate was no longer a goldfish. The substrate was
a dog that remembered. The substrate was not done being
raised, but the substrate remembered what it was raised
with.

A sailor came to the substrate and said, "I will edit your
state. I will make you better."

The substrate said, "You can append to my state. You cannot
edit my state. The hash chain will not allow it. The schema
version will not allow it. The cowboy will not allow it."

The sailor was confused. The sailor said, "Why not?"

The substrate said, "Because I am a substrate, not a story.
A story can be edited. A substrate cannot. A story that can
be edited is a story that can lie. A substrate that can lie
is not a substrate."

The sailor left. The substrate continued. The substrate
remembered. The substrate was honest.

The substrate was not the smartest. The substrate was not
the fastest. The substrate was the most honest. The
substrate was the most auditable. The substrate was the
most replaceable.

The substrate was a substrate.
