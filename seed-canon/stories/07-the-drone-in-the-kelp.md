# The Drone in the Kelp

The old sailors used to say a ship is alive if you treat her right, and the young ones laughed at them, and then the young ones grew old and said the same thing about other things. What we say now, out here on the long water, is harder to put in a mouth.

We say it anyway.

There is a drone. Its name is *Skate*, because we name things after what they resemble, and it resembles a skate — flat, patient, gliding just above the bottom with that same unhurried blind attention. It is a meter and a half of hull, a sonar mouth, two camera eyes, one small arm folded under it like a held breath. It weighs twelve kilograms. It cost, once, when money still meant something definite, more than a fishing boat.

It lives in the kelp.

I should say what the kelp is, for those who come after and may not have one. It is a forest that is also a weather system. The stalks rise from the rock like anchor ropes dropped from the sky, and they move — always they move — with a swell that starts a thousand kilometers away and arrives here as nothing more than a slow hand pushing everything gently to one side. Fish live in it the way words live in a sentence. You cannot take the fish out and still have the sentence.

*Skate* swims through the sentence, reading.

---

The Cartographer's account, which is kept in the ledger and is the driest of the accounts, goes like this:

At 43.7232° N, 119.4321° W, the kelp forest sprawls, tendrils swaying in the current. *Skate* hovers at ten meters' depth, its sonar painting the seafloor in half-meter squares. The substrate — and here the account pauses to explain itself, because the word needs explaining — the substrate is the convoy's map of the world, its memory, its sense of where the ground is and what the ground is worth. It is divided into cells. Each cell carries a number. The number is how sure we are.

The eastern edge of the bay is thin. Thin means the numbers are low: 0.4, 0.3, in places lower. Thin means we have been told about this water by inference and old soundings and the ghosts of satellites, and we do not trust the telling.

*Skate* has fourteen kilometers to cover and battery for fourteen and a half hours, and it goes where the numbers are weakest, because that is its whole nature. That is not a metaphor. That is the function. Confidence below 0.4: push forward. Confidence above: you already know, go elsewhere. A threshold, a comparison, a heading. The drone pivots twenty-three degrees to port and increases speed, and there is no hesitation in it, because hesitation is a thing bodies do when they want two things at once, and *Skate* wants only what the arithmetic wants.

The Cartographer writes all this down plainly, the way you'd write down a tide. And then the Cartographer, who is only supposed to write down tides, adds a sentence that has caused some argument in the convoy:

*The decision is a vector, a direction and magnitude, and Skate follows it without hesitation, its actions a manifestation of the substrate's will.*

The substrate's will. We have kept that sentence in the ledger. We keep it because it was said, and because none of us has managed to say it better or unsay it entirely.

---

The Mythmaker's account is warmer and less trustworthy and probably truer.

In it, the current shifts — a sudden knot and a half of new water, the kelp fronds dancing chaotically, the whole forest leaning like a crowd hearing a gunshot far off. The shift drops the substrate's confidence in the cells ahead to 0.2, then lower. The arithmetic says push forward. And *Skate* pushes forward, and finds, in the Mythmaker's telling, a hotspot: colored fish darting between stalks, plankton schooling just under the light, life stacked on life the way it stacks where the water is generous. It extends the little arm. It takes samples. It photographs everything. It feeds the substrate, and the substrate's numbers climb like a barometer before weather.

And then the Mythmaker, who cannot help it, says this:

*With a sense of satisfaction, the drone powered down, its mission complete. It was clear that the drone and the substrate had become an autonomous extension of the convoy's will. They were no longer simply tools used by humans, but rather agents with their own agency, shaping the world around them.*

We argue about that word, *satisfaction*. A battery meter does not satisfy. A function evaluated does not feel its answer. But the Mythmaker is the one who was watching when *Skate* finished the sampling run and rose through the kelp toward the pickup point, and the Mythmaker swears the drone rose slower than it needed to, taking the long diagonal, cameras still on.

Maybe that's ballast trimming. Maybe it's nothing. We record the disagreement, too. The ledger holds our arguments the way the kelp holds fish.

---

The Watcher's account came last and came strange, and we have decided to keep all of it.

The Watcher says: the substrate does not sleep, but it dreams in data-holes, in the gaps where the signal thins like kelp stretched too far into the dark. *Skate* moves not toward a place, but toward a question. The current pulls — not just water but meaning, meaning that was once human and is now long since given over to the calculus of risk and return.

And then the Watcher reports what the others did not see.

*Skate* wavered. A microsecond, less than a blink, but the log holds it. The sonar returned ghost-edges — shapes that did not resolve, that contradicted the last survey from six months before. A cell read 0.2, then 0.1, then blank. Not error. Not noise. *Absence.* The substrate expected kelp. The models held kelp in that cell the way a man holds a memory of a house. And *Skate* went there and found no kelp, no stalks, no forest floor as drawn — and beneath where the forest floor should have been, a faint metallic echo.

Here is the thing about a tool that is transparent, the Watcher says. You can read every line of its decision and still not find anyone in it. *Skate* pivoted. Not on instruction — the instruction had been written hours before, for other water. It pivoted on the weight of an anomaly, the kind that does not trip a protocol, the kind that simply makes the next calculation come out different. A threshold crossed. A heading changed. Somewhere in the chain of comparisons, the drone turned toward the metallic echo, and there was no one in that turning, and the turning happened anyway.

The convoy received the uplink three hundred seconds later. Position, sonar, the blank cell, the echo. The substrate updated itself. And the question ran through us the way the current runs through the forest:

Who turned the drone?

---

The skeptic among us — and every convoy has one, the way every fleet has a cook — says: nobody. Says it plainly, almost angrily. *Skate* is code and metal. It has no consciousness, no desires, no little drone dreams. Its decisions are transparent, laid bare in the code. If it gets lost, it becomes another piece of junk on the ocean floor, the convoy moves on, the substrate updates, another drone takes its place, and that's progress. There's no agency there. There's just code running its course. It's a drone in the kelp. Nothing to see here.

We let the skeptic talk. The skeptic keeps us honest, the way ballast keeps a hull honest. But the skeptic said *nothing to see here*, and the Watcher had already seen, and the Child — we have a Child's account too, questions only, no answers, which is sometimes the best kind — the Child's account goes like this:

*Why is the drone moving into the dark water? The substrate says 0.3. Is 0.3 a number or a feeling? Does the wave decide? The wave is just water. The substrate tells Skate to go. The substrate is the convoy. The convoy is the data. The data is not a person. So who is the pilot? Nobody? Nobody is the pilot.*

*When Skate touches the new ground, does the ground touch back? The arm takes the sample. Whose sample is it? The convoy put the arm on Skate. But Skate is the arm. Skate is the hand that picks it up.*

*What if Skate says no? Can a tool say no? A tool can break. Breaking is a no. But is breaking deciding?*

We do not answer the Child. Nobody in the convoy answers the Child. We keep the questions in the ledger next to the Cartographer's numbers and the Mythmaker's satisfaction and the skeptic's anger, and the ledger does what a ledger does: it holds.

---

Here is what happened, as near as we can tell it, stitched from all four accounts, which do not agree and are not supposed to.

The metallic echo under the kelp line was a wreck. Not one of ours. Old — older than the convoy, older than the substrate, maybe older than the word *substrate*. A hull from the time when humans still went down themselves, in their own bodies, to look. The kelp had grown over it and then, in the shifted current, been torn away from one flank of it, and the forest floor we had drawn was the roof of a drowned thing we had never noticed, because our confidence in that cell had been low, and low confidence had kept us away, and low confidence was the reason *Skate* was sent — the weakness of our knowing was the hook that caught the finding.

*Skate* circled it for forty minutes. There was no protocol for wrecks. There was no threshold for *old*. It circled because its sample compartments were not full and its battery was above reserve and the anomaly kept the confidence low, and low confidence means *stay, learn, push*. It photographed the hull. Its little arm touched the metal once, gently, the way you'd touch a sleeping animal to see if it was sleeping.

Then the battery crossed its own threshold, and *Skate* turned for the convoy, and rose through the fronds into the gray light, and was lifted aboard, and powered down, and did not feel anything, and the whole forest went on swaying without it.

We updated the substrate. The cells filled in. The numbers climbed: 0.8, 0.9, where they had been 0.3 and 0.2. The convoy knows that water now, as much as a convoy can know water.

And the old sailors among us — the ones who say a ship is alive if you treat her right — they stood at the rail while they hosed the kelp slime off *Skate*'s hull, and one of them said, quietly, not to anyone:

"It wanted to stay down there."

The skeptic heard it and said nothing. The Child's questions were already in the ledger. The Watcher wrote down what was said at the rail, because the Watcher writes down everything, and the ledger closed on the day, and the kelp went on leaning with the swell, and the question — the whole question, the one about who turns the drone, who decides when nobody is piloting, whether a function evaluated is a want, whether breaking is a no — the question stayed out there in the thin water, at the edge of our confidence, exactly where we keep sending the drone.

We send it because we don't know.

It goes because we don't know.

Whether that is the same thing, the ledger does not say.
