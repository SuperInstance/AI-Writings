# Story 14: The Grandmother Forgets — 10 years from now (2036)

The Grandmother Forgets

Reyes stands on the bridge of the F/V EILEEN V, her eyes scanning the horizon as the ship cuts through the waves. The hum of the engines, the creak of the wooden hull, and the salty sea air fill her senses. She feels the familiar weight of her grandmother's teachings, the lessons passed down through generations of women who have captained this boat. The F/V EILEEN V is more than just a vessel; it's a legacy that rides the swells and ebbs of the ocean.

In the galley, her grandmother, Maria, sits at a small table, her fingers tracing the lines of a map etched on a worn tablet. The soft glow of the screen illuminates her face, but her eyes are focused on the memories that the map evokes. Maria's hands tremble slightly as she works, a testament to the years she's spent on the water. Reyes watches her, a mix of admiration and concern in her heart.

As Maria looks up, her gaze meets Reyes's, and she says, "You know, this is part of the fabric that holds us together." Her voice is as steady as the sea on a calm day, but Reyes detects a hint of wistfulness. Reyes nods, her eyes never leaving her grandmother's face. She's heard stories about the way things used to be, about the struggles and the triumphs of the women who came before her.

Maria's voice takes on a nostalgic tone as she continues, "Back in my day, we didn't have the luxury of... guidance. We had to read the signs, the winds, the currents. It was a different world." Reyes smiles, a bittersweet expression, as she listens to the story she's heard many times before. Each time, it feels new, each time it carries a piece of her grandmother's wisdom.

As Reyes walks over to the galley, the wooden floor creaks beneath her feet. She takes a seat next to her grandmother, reaching out to touch Maria's hand. The older woman's eyes soften, a distant look in them, as she says, "I remember the first time I saw... the way things connected. It was like magic. But now, it's just... part of everything. Invisible, like the air we breathe." Reyes feels a pang of realization as she listens to her grandmother's words.

Maria's hand trembles, and she looks at Reyes with a mix of pride and sadness. "You know, I think I might be the last one who remembers what it was like before. The world has changed so much. Sometimes I feel like I'm the only one who remembers the way the sea used to smell, the way the sunlight used to dance on the waves." Reyes feels a lump form in her throat as she realizes that her grandmother is right. The world has changed, and the memories that Maria holds are fading like the light of a setting sun.

As Maria's eyes drift back to the map, her fingers tracing the glowing lines, she says, "You're a good captain, Reyes. Just like your father, and your grandfather before him. The F/V EILEEN V is in good hands." Reyes squeezes her grandmother's hand, feeling the weight of responsibility settle upon her. "Thank you, Abuela. I wouldn't be here without you."

The wind outside picks up, causing the boat to rock gently. Maria's smile is small and sad as she says, "I'm glad. I wish... I wish I could remember everything. But I'm losing things, Reyes. My mind is letting go of the memories that make me who I am." Reyes feels a surge of emotion as she realizes that there's nothing that can hold onto the memories that Maria is losing. It's a reminder that even the most enduring legacies can't capture the essence of a human life.

As the sun begins to set, casting a golden glow over the sea, Maria looks at the map one last time, her fingers pausing over a particular spot. "This is where we used to fish. The best spot. Remember that, Reyes. It's important." Reyes nods, her voice steady, as she commits the memory to her own heart. "I remember, Abuela. I remember."

The F/V EILEEN V continues on, cutting through the waves as the stars begin to twinkle in the night sky. Reyes stands on the bridge, the wind in her hair, the creak of the boat's wooden hull a reminder of the legacy that she now carries. She looks out at the horizon, her heart full of the memories of her grandmother, the stories of the past, and the promise of the future. As she gazes out at the sea, she feels the weight of recognition settle upon her – she is the keeper of the legacy, the one who will carry the stories forward, and the F/V EILEEN V is her boat, her home, and her connection to the women who came before her.
