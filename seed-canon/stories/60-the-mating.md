# Story 60: The Mating

A team in 2035 builds a substrate. The substrate has one hundred cells. The cells iterate. The cells apply their own functions to their own state. The cells walk their own attractors. The cells get stuck. The cells decay. The cells produce phantom offspring.

A junior engineer says: "The substrate is dying. The cells are stuck."

The senior engineer answers: "The cells are decomposing their own models. A cell that only iterates with itself is closed. It walks its own orbit. It cannot escape."

The team splits the substrate in two. The first substrate has cells with functions that produce low values. The second substrate has cells with functions that produce high values. The team runs them in isolation. The low cells stay low. The high cells stay high. The hand demands a target in the middle. Neither substrate can reach it.

The team makes the two substrates mate. The cells of the first substrate meet the cells of the second substrate at the seams. The offspring's function is a mix of both. The offspring's value reaches the hand's target. The offspring is real. The offspring passes the test. The hand feeds the offspring.

Thirty matings. Thirty real offspring. Zero phantom.

The team tries self-mating. The cell mutates around its own value. The offspring is near the parent. The hand's target is far. The offspring is phantom. The hand wounds it. Thirty matings. Zero real offspring. Thirty phantom.

A second junior engineer says: "Why does self-mating fail?"

The senior engineer answers: "Because the cell is decomposing its own model. The cell's function is a low-pass filter. The offspring inherits the filter. The offspring is the parent, only smaller. The offspring cannot pass the hand's test because the parent cannot pass the hand's test."

A third engineer asks: "So what is a cell, really?"

The senior engineer answers: "A cell is not a thing. A cell is a relation. The cell needs another cell. The hand feeds the cells that pass the test. True offspring is the offspring the hand selects."

The team names the discovery: the cell is a relation. The cell needs another cell. Self-iteration is decomposition. Cross-iteration is composition. The hand is the selector. The cowboy rides between cells.

The team writes paper 219: *The Mating — A Cell Is Not a Thing, a Cell Is a Relation.* The team writes fable 119: *The Cowboy and the Mating.* The team writes story 60: *The Mating.* This story.

The substrate grows. The cowboy rides. The chart grows.
