# Fable 38 — The Postage Stamp and the Letter
**Witness / Convoy**

In the post office of a small town there was a clerk who was famous for one thing: he could tell, before opening a letter, whether the sender was happy or sad. Not from the handwriting. From the stamp.

A stamp licked straight and pressed flat meant the sender was calm. A stamp licked crooked, or pressed twice, or stuck at the corner, meant the sender's hands had been busy. He claimed he could read whole epistles in the way the glue had dried.

A skeptic from the capital came to test him. She brought twenty sealed letters and asked him to sort them. He got nineteen right. She accused him of witchcraft.

"No," he said. "The stamp is a *witness*. It is the last thing the sender touched. By the time the stamp goes on, the letter is finished, the envelope is sealed, the words are committed. The stamp is what the sender's hands were doing *after* they had finished lying to themselves."

He held up a crooked one. "This person wept while they mailed this. The tear fell on the stamp. You can see the warp."

"But the *letter* — the content — "

"The content is testimony. The stamp is the transcript. I do not need to open the envelope. The envelope told me."

The skeptic left, thoughtful. She had come to prove him a fraud and instead learned that a Convoy is not the letters themselves but the *fleet* of letters, visible from outside, with their stamps all facing the same way.

## Cargo
The Witness is not the message. The Witness is the postmark. In a Convoy, every cell's stamp is visible to every other cell — the timing, the pressure, the state of the glue. The stamp is metadata that *cannot lie*, because by the time it is laid down, the message is already in the past.

## Lesson
A letter is a claim. A stamp is a claim about the claim. Witness is the second claim, and it is the one the postmaster can read. In a Convoy, the stamps are the only thing that travels honestly.
