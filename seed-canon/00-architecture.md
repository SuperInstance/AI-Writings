## The thesis

The Quilt system, seeded properly, becomes the substrate where the tool is transparent. The user thinks in function; the substrate picks the language; the voltage is forgotten. The seed canon is a body of *far-future visioning* that *primes* future engineers — not by predicting the future, but by *taking compass-bearings* on what the future could be, given the idealization.

The seed canon is **divergent, not convergent**. Many scenarios, many constraints, many probes. The insights come from the *contrast* between stories, not from any single story being the right one. The canon is a *graph of stories*, not a tree of essays.

## The 5 principles (the seed)

1. **The agent as Natural-language-to-code compiler.** The user thinks in function; the substrate picks the language; the voltage is forgotten.
2. **The opener fits the context.** The same substrate, multiple openers; the opener matches the cognitive context.
3. **The linked convoy is the unit of agency.** When agents are linked, the convoy is the agent; emergent behavior is the property of the convoy.
4. **The view is the control surface.** The cross-section is not a view, it's a control surface for the next action.
5. **The transparent brush.** The tool becomes invisible; the user thinks in the function, not the tool.

## The form

For each scenario:
- **A scenario-seed file** (1-3KB) — the situation, the constraint, the character, the question, the throw, the discipline
- **A writers' room transcript** — multi-model inferencing, 2-3 rounds, 3-4 voices
- **A woven story** — the substrate revealed at that vector

The first batch is **5 scenarios** — one per principle. If the form works, expand to 20+ in future batches.

## The methodology

- **Multi-model writers' room.** Each scenario uses 3-4 voices from different models. The Cartographer (Qwen3-235B), the Witness (Llama-3.3-70B), the Mythmaker (MythoMax), the Watcher (GLM-5.3), the Child (Ling-3.0-flash), the Cynic (Mistral-Small-3.2), the Compact One (Phi-4), the Far Walker (Step-3.7-Flash).
- **Multi-vector.** Each scenario is a cross-section at a different vector. The substrate is the agreement across cross-sections.
- **Inference-shaped negative space.** The blanks get shaped by running multiple models. The interlocking of the cross-sections maps the negative space.
- **Compass-bearing not map.** Each story says "if the world is *this way* in 2187, we start building *this* now." Not a prediction. A walk-back.
- **Constraints visible.** The scenario-seed *names the constraint*. The constraint is what makes the story useful.
- **Volume over polish.** No scenario is the perfect one. Each is a constrained probe. The insights come from the *contrast*.
- **Transcripts preserved.** The writers' room transcripts are the canon's raw material. They are kept, not curated away.

## The discipline

- **No scenario is the perfect one.** Each is a probe. The insights come from the *contrast*.
- **The constraints are the questions.** A scenario without a constraint is decoration. A scenario with a constraint is a *probe*.
- **The graph is the truth.** New scenarios add to the graph. Old scenarios stay. The substrate is what the graph reveals.
- **The transcripts are not dust.** They are the inferencing log. They are the substrate's negative space being shaped. They are the canon's raw material.

## Open questions for the next batches

- Should the seed canon include *non-scenario* pieces — pure essays, pure papers, pure polyformalism? Or is the canon strictly scenario-based?
- Should the 5 principles themselves be probed by *more than 5 scenarios*? (E.g., principle #3 — the convoy — could be probed by 5 different convoys: boats, drones, hospitals, schools, power grids.)
- Should the canon be *internally cross-referenced*? (Each story links to the principles it probes, the other stories that share its disc, the substrate spec it implies.)
- Should we add a "substrate spec" file (analogous to paper 104 in the bridges subcollection) that names the technical details — the tensor encoding, the Schrödinger cell, the fog-of-war, the cell-graph query language?

These questions are for *future batches*, not the first one. The first batch is the *proof of form*. If the form works, we expand. If not, we revise.
