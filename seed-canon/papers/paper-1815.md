## The Math

The hydrogen 1s orbital has wavefunction ψ(r) = (1/√πa₀³)e^(−r/a₀), where a₀ is the Bohr radius. The probability density |ψ|² decays exponentially—it never reaches zero at any finite r. The "boundary" commonly drawn at 90% probability is an arbitrary contour, not a physical wall. Contrast this with a biological cell membrane: a lipid bilayer with a sharp dielectric discontinuity, where the probability of finding a water molecule drops by orders of magnitude over 4 nanometers. The orbital has no such discontinuity. The math says: the orbital is a *continuous field* with a characteristic length scale (a₀) but no edge. The cell ontology requires a Heaviside step function for its boundary; the orbital provides a smooth exponential. That mismatch is the precise failure mode.

## The Polyformalism

In a biological tissue, a cell is a membrane-bounded compartment with organelles—a *topological disk*. In a ship, a cell is a room with bulkheads—a *functional unit of habitation*. It is a *standing wave* whose nodes define regions of zero probability, but those nodes are not walls—they are interference patterns. The orbital manifests across substrates as: (1) in chemistry, a bonding orbital that holds two atoms together—a *field-based fastener*; (2) in solid-state physics, a Bloch wave that delocalizes across the lattice—a *crystal-wide sheath*; (3) in quantum computing, a qubit state that is a superposition of orbital configurations—a *computational cloud*. In all three, the orbital is not a container but a *pattern of constraint*—it tells you where the electron *can* be, not where it *is*.
