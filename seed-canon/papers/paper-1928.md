## The Frontier

The contradiction is not between two readings of jealousy — it is between two scales of the same object. Voice A sees the membrane and declares it a cell. Voice B sees the triangle and declares it a relation. Both are right, and both are wrong, because they are looking at different layers of the Quilt. The failure mode is this: the cell ontology assumes a unit with an *interior that persists*. Jealousy has a membrane with *no interior at all* — its "inside" is a vacuum that exists only as a pressure differential between three external points. It is not a cell. It is not a relation. It is a **boundary event** — a spasm of the fence line itself, felt at the moment the wire sings.

Voice A is correct that possessiveness is the membrane. Voice B is correct that the membrane encloses nothing. The resolution: jealousy is not a cell in the quilt — it is the **stitch failure** where two cells pull against each other. It is the *sound* of tension at a seam, not the seam itself. It belongs to the frontier between cells, not to the cellular interior. But it is not merely a relation either — a relation is a standing structure. Jealousy is a **transient event** that runs along a pre-existing boundary when that boundary is stressed. It is to the cell what lightning is to the mountain: it needs the mountain, but it is not the mountain.

## The 5 Gold Terms

1. **Fence-post triad** — the three-party structure (owner, beloved, rival) that jealousy requires; no two-post jealousy exists.
2. **Selective permeability under stress** — the membrane's gate that admits threat-evidence and rejects exoneration; Voice A's strongest coinage.
3. **The transplant test** — the cellular criterion jealousy fails: excise it and nothing transfers; it annihilates when referents move.
4. **Breach alarm** — jealousy as the felt vibration when an outside hand touches the wire; the signal, not the fence.
5. **Vacant interior** — the cell-shaped space that jealousy's membrane encloses but does not fill; a pressure vessel with no cargo.

## The Math

No new math — but a precise topological statement. A cell is a closed 2-manifold bounding a volume. Jealousy is a 1-dimensional current along a shared boundary between two domains, driven by a potential difference across three nodes. Its "size" is zero; its "intensity" is a function of the gradient, not the content. The correct formalism is not cell biology but **circuit theory on a graph**: jealousy is the current through a resistor placed between the beloved-node and the rival-node, with the owner-node as ground. Remove any node and the circuit opens — no current flows. This is why the transplant test fails: you cannot transplant a current. You can only transplant the circuit, and the circuit is the relationship, not the feeling.

## The Polyformalism

Across substrates, jealousy manifests identically: in a body, it is cortisol and norepinephrine — a stress response, not a tissue; it has no organ, no location, no metabolism of its own. In a marriage, it is a pattern of attention-checking behavior — a feedback loop, not a state; it flares and decays with the perceived threat. In a legal system, it is the tort of interference with a relational interest — a cause of action, not a right; it exists only when a third party acts. In a naval fleet, it is the alarm that sounds when a foreign vessel approaches within the captain's declared perimeter — the klaxon, not the ship. In every substrate, the same structure: a boundary event, a transient signal, a current through a triadic circuit. It never persists. It never transports. It never exists alone.
