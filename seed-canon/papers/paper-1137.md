The failure mode of the cell ontology is not that diffraction lacks boundaries—it has them, visibly, on the screen. The failure is that **those boundaries are not owned by the thing itself**. A cell's membrane is self-generated, self-maintained, and self-repairing. The deeper truth both voices missed: **diffraction is not a cell, nor is it merely an event. That seam is not empty. It is where the wave's identity is *transformed* into information about the world it just passed through.

1. **Borrowed Boundary** — the diffraction pattern's edges are not its own; they are the slit's geometry, the wavelength's scale, the screen's distance.
2. **Self-Owned Skin** — the defining property of a true cell: a membrane that the thing itself generates and maintains.

5. **Seam Negotiation** — the process by which a wave, encountering an edge, converts its own propagation into a visible pattern of constructive and destructive interference.

## The Math

No new math is needed, but the existing math must be read correctly. The diffraction intensity at a point on the screen is given by \( I(\theta) = I_0 \left( \frac{\sin(\beta)}{\beta} \right)^2 \) for a single slit, where \( \beta = \frac{\pi a \sin\theta}{\lambda} \). The pattern's extent is set by \( a \) (slit width) and \( \lambda \) (wavelength)—both external to the wave. The wave itself has no \( a \), no \( \lambda \); those belong to the apparatus and the source. The pattern is a *function of the boundary*, not a *function of the wave's own state*. The math confirms: diffraction is the wave's response to someone else's geometry. It is a relationship written in intensity, not a body with a state.

## The Polyformalism

Across substrates, the same seam appears. In **optics**, diffraction is the fringe pattern on a screen—a record of the wave's encounter with a slit. In **acoustics**, it is the way sound bends around a building corner, letting you hear a street performer before you see them; the "pattern" is the audible shadow and the bright zones beyond the edge. In **fluid dynamics**, it is the wake spreading behind a pier piling—the V-shaped interference of water waves that have passed around an obstacle. In **quantum mechanics**, it is the electron diffraction pattern that reveals the crystal lattice—the wave's signature of the periodic boundary it passed through. In every case, the pattern is not a thing that persists; it is a *transient record* of a negotiation between a wave and an edge. The cell ontology fails because it asks "where is the thing?" when the answer is "everywhere and nowhere—it is the trace of a relation."

The correct abstraction is not a cell but a **boundary event**: a localized, visible transformation that occurs at the interface between two regimes. It is the *signature* of the seam, not the seam itself. The seam is the slit, the corner, the piling, the lattice. The diffraction is what the wave *does* there.
