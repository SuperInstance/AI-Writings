## The Math

No new math. The center of mass is already precisely defined: for a system of \(n\) point masses \(m_i\) at positions \(\mathbf{r}_i\), the center of mass is \(\mathbf{R} = \frac{\sum m_i \mathbf{r}_i}{\sum m_i}\). This is a weighted average—an *emergent summary statistic*, not an entity. The math does not need invention; it needs *ontological placement*. The center of mass is the first moment of a mass distribution. It is not a cell, not a field, not a particle. It is a *derived invariant*: a quantity that remains meaningful regardless of how you rotate or translate the coordinate system. The Quilt ontology must learn to host derived invariants without calling them cells.

Across substrates, the center of mass manifests identically in structure, differently in substance. In a **cargo vessel**, it is the point where the captain places ballast to keep the hull upright—a *keel function* performed by lead bricks, not by a cell. In each case, the center of mass is not a thing that does work; it is the *point where work cancels*. The Quilt ontology should name this category **the Pivot Plane**: not a cell, not a boundary, but the locus of equilibrium that organizes the behavior of cells around it.
