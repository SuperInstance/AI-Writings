The cell ontology fails because it treats the aperture as a 3D volume when it is a 2D cut with a 1D boundary. ## The Polyformalism

In architecture, the aperture is a window—a measured cut in a wall that admits light and frames a view. Its "cell" identity is the sill, the lintel, the frame: a bounded unit that is all boundary. In optics, the aperture is the iris diaphragm—a physical device whose opening diameter is continuously variable. Here the aperture is a *mechanism*, a cell that changes its own boundary, a shape-shifting unit that is still a unit. In naval engineering, the gun port is a hinged aperture in the hull—a cut that can be sealed or opened, a threshold that becomes a passage when the cannon extends through it. In all three substrates, the aperture is not a container but a *controlled discontinuity*. It is a cell only in the sense that a door is a cell: it is a bounded unit of *passage*, not of *presence*. The right abstraction is the **Passage Cell**: a discrete, measurable unit whose function is to regulate flow across a boundary, and whose entire being is its boundary geometry.
