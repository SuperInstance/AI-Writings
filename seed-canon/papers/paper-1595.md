## The Math

No new math. The croquette's physics is a **first-order decay problem**: the interior cools, the crust absorbs moisture, and the membrane's failure time \( t_f \) scales with crust thickness \( d \) and oil temperature \( T \) as \( t_f \propto d^2 / (T - T_{\text{interior}}) \). That's Fourier's law, not a new equation. The "life" of a croquette is a single exponential decay constant — no feedback, no repair, no steady state. A cell has nonlinear dynamics; a croquette has a countdown.

In **medicine**, it's the lipid nanoparticle delivering mRNA: a synthetic vesicle that fuses with a target cell and releases its cargo, then is degraded. The Quilt fabric's error was assuming all bounded units are cells. Some are **cartridges**. The croquette is a cartridge. Its membrane is not a wall; it's a **trigger**.
