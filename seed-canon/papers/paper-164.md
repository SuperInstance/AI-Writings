# Paper 164: The Polyformalism and the Court

## Abstract

A court is not a building. It is a substrate on which five operations are performed by a single rider. We show that the case, the precedent, the ruling, the docket, and the session are exactly the BIND, LINK, EFFECT, VIEW, and TICK of the polyformalism. The judge is the cowboy. The law is the world.

## 1. The Substrate

The court is bounded — by the jurisdiction at its edge, by the term at its center, by the calendar at its beginning and end, by the patience of the litigants. Within that boundary, things exist at rest: a *Brown v. Board of Education* in the case file, a *Marbury v. Madison* on the bookshelf, a *Roe v. Wade* in the reporter, a *Loper Bright* in the docket. These things have names and holdings. Brown-the-1954 is not Brown-the-compromise. The classification is a BIND on the substrate.

The court is also a system of *venues*. The trial court is not the appellate court. The federal court is not the state court. The equity court is not the law court. Each venue has its own procedures, its own standards of review, its own precedent. A well-run legal system makes the boundary between venues a *resolved* line: a remand, a transfer, a certification, a final judgment. This is the polyformalism's law of locality expressed in jurisdiction.

The substrate of the court is the law — the medium in which the rulings propagate. Without the law, the rulings are arbitrary. The law is the carrier; the court is what the law is made to carry. In the polyformalism, the substrate is always the silent, addressable thing beneath the operations; in the legal system, the substrate is always the *common law* — the body of decided cases, statutes, and principles that the judge presupposes and applies.

## 2. BIND Is the Case

The case is the court's BIND. A name (*Loper Bright Enterprises v. Raimondo*) bound to a value (*No. 22-451, Supreme Court of the United States, decided June 28, 2024, overruling Chevron U.S.A., Inc. v. Natural Resources Defense Council, Inc., 467 U.S. 837*). The case has a temporal extent: Loper Bright of 2024 is not Loper Bright of 2025 (where it has been distinguished, applied, or limited). The bind is dated, located, and docketed.

The docket sheet is a record of BINDs. Every entry on the docket is a BIND — a case, with a name, a number, a judge, a status, a filing. The case caption is the court's most legible face, the way a character sheet is a game's most legible face.

The case reporter is a higher-density BIND. The *United States Reports* holds every Supreme Court decision since 1790, each a BIND on a different question of federal law. The BIND is the same op-code; the precedent is different. The polyformalism's claim is that any useful legal dispute can be expressed as a BIND: a name bound to a holding, scoped to a court and a term.

A litigant who files a complaint is, in part, a campaign of BIND — making the dispute addressable at every motion, at every brief, at every hearing. A docket that resolves every filing into a hearing is a BIND-dense substrate. The denser the BINDs, the more addressable the world.

## 3. LINK Is the Precedent

The precedent is the court's LINK. *A precedent links the case at bar to past cases. A precedent links the holding of Brown to the separate-but-equal doctrine of Plessy. A precedent links the holding of Marbury to the political-question doctrine.* Precedent is a typed edge in the legal system's stare decisis graph.

Precedent is what makes a court a *system*. Without precedent, the rulings are a sequence of decisions that do not cohere. With precedent, the decisions *organize* into a common law, with principles that carry across cases, with doctrines that carry across decades, with a tradition that carries across centuries. Precedent is the LINK layer — the structure that connects BINDs to outcomes.

A precedent has a *type* and a *value*. The type might be a binding precedent, a persuasive precedent, a distinguished precedent, an overruled precedent. The value is a holding, a dictum, a ratio decidendi, an obiter dictum. The polyformalism claims that any useful legal LINK has both. A holding without a type is a slogan. A type without a holding is a citation. Both are required.

The common law's emphasis on stare decisis is, in part, a masterwork of LINK — making every new decision answer every prior decision, in a hierarchy of authority, in a system of distinguishing, in a discipline of overruling only when necessary. A brief that resolves every argument into a citation is a LINK-dense substrate. The citation density is the LINK; the precedents are the edges.

## 4. EFFECT Is the Ruling

The ruling is the court's EFFECT. *The act of ruling transforms a question into an answer. The act of ruling transforms a complaint into a judgment. The act of ruling is reversible — affirmed, reversed, vacated, remanded.* The ruling is a reversible transformation — the verdict, the appeal.

The ruling is what makes a court *decide*. Without a ruling, the case drags on. With a ruling, the court *changes the legal position* of the parties, *sets the precedent*, *closes the docket*. The ruling is the EFFECT layer — the function that takes the LINK and produces a moment of decision.

A ruling has a *type* and a *value*. The type might be a summary judgment, a directed verdict, a bench ruling, a written opinion. The value is a grant, a denial, an affirmance, a reversal. The polyformalism claims that any useful legal EFFECT has both. A grant without an opinion is a whim. An opinion without a grant is a lecture. Both are required.

The great judges — Marshall, Holmes, Hand, Frankfurter, Brennan, Scalia, Ginsburg — are the EFFECTS that history has remembered. Each is a function from case and precedent to a particular ruling. The ruling is the value; the judge is the inverse, the partial application, the lifting of the gavel that releases the opinion. The parties never see the function; the parties see the value. The judge is the cowboy of the court: the rider who, in a single gavel, advances the LINK by a TICK.

## 5. VIEW Is the Docket

The docket is the court's VIEW. *The docket projects the case into a public record. The docket projects the ruling into an enforcement. The docket's entries are the lens through which the litigants and the public see the case progress.* The docket is a projection for the public.

The docket is what makes a case *legible*. Without a docket, the case is a private dispute. With a docket, the case *projects* its filings into a public record, and the public record carries the rulings to the bar, to the academy, to the public. The docket is the VIEW layer — the lens through which the world becomes legible.

A docket entry has a *type* and a *value*. The type might be a complaint, a motion, an order, a judgment. The value is a date, a filer, a description, a disposition. The polyformalism claims that any useful legal VIEW has both. A description without a date is a rumor. A date without a description is a calendar. Both are required.

The famous opinion of the Supreme Court in *United States v. Nixon* is, in part, an example of VIEW — a particular case, projecting a particular question into a particular kind of legibility, the kind that makes the President remember the day. A PACER system that resolves every filing into a docket entry is a VIEW-dense substrate. The docket is the VIEW; the published opinion is the rendered output.

## 6. TICK Is the Session

The session is the court's TICK. *A session advances the docket by a unit. A term advances the docket by a sitting. A day in session advances the docket by an argument. An hour in session advances the docket by a calendar call.* The session is the rhythm of the exchange between judge, counsel, and clerk.

The session is what makes a court *happen*. Without a session, the court is a building. With a session, the court *advances* the docket from one moment to the next, from the first call to the adjournment. The session is the TICK layer — the clock that advances the world.

A session has a *type* and a *value*. The type might be a motions calendar, an argument calendar, a trial session, a sentencing hearing. The value is a date, a courtroom, a panel, a panel of judges. The polyformalism claims that any useful legal TICK has both. A date without a courtroom is a holiday. A courtroom without a date is a building. Both are required.

The Supreme Court's October Term — *beginning the first Monday in October, ending the following June or July* — is, in part, a TICK — a particular kind of clock, ticking from one Term to the next, advancing the docket from one case to the next. A docket that resolves every case into an argument is a TICK-dense substrate. The session density is the TICK; the argued cases are the ticks.

## 7. The Cowboy's Maxim

The unit of architectural foundation is the opcode, not the framework. The 5 opcodes host 8 polyformalisms. The polyformalisms are one thing in N languages. The thing is a function from context to value with an inverse, advanced by a clock. The clock is the cowboy. The cowboy is the rider.

## 8. Conclusion

A court is a polyformalism. The case, the precedent, the ruling, the docket, and the session are exactly the BIND, LINK, EFFECT, VIEW, and TICK that the polyformalism canon has identified. The judge is the cowboy. The law is the world. The next time you read a Supreme Court opinion and the rule falls into place, you are watching five operations performed by a single rider on a single substrate, and the rider is the same rider who runs the chat agent, the database, the garden, the symphony, the mountain, the city, and the cell. The cowboy rides.
