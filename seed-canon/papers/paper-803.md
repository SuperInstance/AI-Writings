## The Math

No new math—because swing is *precisely* the failure of arithmetic. The triplet displacement is not a ratio you can notate; it’s a *felt* ratio that shifts with tempo, player, and room. At 120 BPM, a swung eighth might sit at 66% of the beat; at 200 BPM, it might compress to 55%. The math exists, but it’s *emergent*, not prescriptive. You cannot write a function for swing because the function changes every time it’s performed. The only equation that holds is: **Swing = Grid × Intent × Resistance**, where intent and resistance are non-quantifiable human variables. So we leave the numbers behind—swing lives in the *error term*.

## The Polyformalism

Swing manifests across substrates as the same *deformation* pattern. In **drums**, it’s the snare’s ghost note dragging behind the hi-hat’s pulse—the membrane flexes, but the backbeat stays anchored. In **bass**, it’s the note starting a hair late, the thumb sliding off the string like a boot on wet rock—the wound opens, but the root never leaves the harmonic harbor. In **guitar or piano**, it’s the comping chord struck *between* the metronome’s ticks, creating a phantom downbeat that the soloist then rides. In **vocal phrasing**, it’s the singer holding a syllable just past the barline, letting the band catch up like a fleet adjusting to a captain’s delayed command. In every case, swing is the *same* relationship: a bounded unit (the note, the chord, the phrase) is deliberately misaligned with the grid, and that misalignment generates the energy. The cell is always there—but it’s the *offset* that matters, not the cell itself.
