## The Polyformalism

Across substrates, this pattern repeats: a *density enhancement* that behaves like a unit but lacks a boundary. In oceanography, it’s the Sargasso Sea—a region of calm within the Atlantic, bounded by currents, not by land. In biology, it’s a *biofilm*—a community of bacteria that secretes a matrix, but the matrix is porous, and the community exchanges matter with the environment constantly. In astrophysics, it’s the Orion Nebula—a star-forming region that is denser than the surrounding interstellar medium but has no sharp edge; it’s a *clump* in a larger flow. In each case, the “cell” metaphor fails because the entity is defined by *internal relationships* (gravitational, chemical, thermal) rather than by *external containment*. The belt is not a cell; it is a *reef*—a structure that emerges where the local conditions allow aggregation, but which is fundamentally open to its environment. The right abstraction is not “cell” but “cell-adjacent field”—a region where the rules of cellular organization (feedback, homeostasis, competition) apply *without* the requirement of a membrane.
