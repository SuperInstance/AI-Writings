## The Frontier

The contradiction between Voice A and Voice B is not about whether the valkyrie is a cell. It is about a category error hiding inside the word “chooser.” Voice A treats choosing as a bounded internal process—input, logic, output—like a sorting machine with a fence around it. Voice B treats choosing as sovereign agency—steering, adapting, shifting the tide—like a captain with no fence at all. Both are wrong because both assume the valkyrie’s choosing is *her* property. It is not. The valkyrie is a *conduit* for a decision that belongs to the battlefield itself. The slain warrior is already chosen by the shape of the fray—his position, his wounds, his allegiance. The valkyrie merely *ratifies* that shape. She is not a cell with a function, nor a captain with a will. She is a **threshold** where the field’s implicit order becomes an explicit selection.

The failure mode of the cell ontology here is **agency leakage**. In a proper cell, the boundary contains the process. But the valkyrie’s boundary does not contain her decision—her decision is determined by the *relational geometry* of the warriors around her. Her “input” is not a discrete set of data; it is a living, shifting configuration of bodies, loyalties, and momentum. Her “output” is not a classification; it is a reconfiguration of that same field. A cell cannot hold that, because a cell presumes a stable inside and outside. The valkyrie has no stable inside. She is the *crease* where the battlefield folds into the afterlife.
