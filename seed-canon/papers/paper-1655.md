That's not a failure of cell logic — that's cell logic *exported* to the organ level.

## The 5 Gold Terms

1. **The Bloom-Hull** — the epicuticular wax layer that functions as a selective barrier, exactly like a cell membrane, but built from secreted extracellular material rather than a phospholipid bilayer.
2. **The Flotation Test** — the industry's water-sorting line that separates intact berries (sinkers) from breached ones (floaters); a membrane-integrity assay applied to a fruit.
3. **The Abscission Scar** — the clean pinch-off point where the berry detaches from the stem, homologous to vesicle scission at the plasma membrane.
4. **The Turgor Gradient** — the maintained pressure difference between the sugar-concentrated interior and the dry exterior, proof that the boundary is doing thermodynamic work.
5. **The Cargo Nucleus** — the seeds, which are not the berry's "cells" but its genetic payload, analogous to the contents of a transport vesicle.

## The Math

No new math. The relevant formalism already exists: vesicle trafficking. A transport vesicle is defined by (1) a membrane boundary, (2) a maintained luminal composition distinct from cytosol, (3) cargo loaded within, and (4) scission from a donor membrane. The blueberry satisfies all four conditions at a different scale. The only missing equation is a scaling law: the blueberry's boundary is ~10^5 times larger than a typical transport vesicle, and its cargo is multicellular rather than molecular. But the *topology* is identical — a closed compartment, pinched off from a parent surface, carrying payload to a distant target. The math of vesicle formation (curvature generation, scission energetics) applies; only the numbers change.

## The Polyformalism

This vesicle logic recurs across substrates. In **botany**, the blueberry is a fleshy fruit — a seed-dispersal vesicle — and the abscission zone is its scission machinery. In **cell biology**, the same topology appears as exocytosis: a Golgi-derived vesicle fuses with the plasma membrane to release neurotransmitters. In **marine engineering**, the blueberry is a pressure hull — a sealed compartment that maintains internal pressure against the external sea, and the flotation test is a hull-integrity check. In **information theory**, the blueberry is a packet: a bounded payload with a header (the bloom's chemical signature), a body (the pulp), and a destination (a disperser's gut). The pattern is consistent: a boundary, a gradient, cargo, and a clean release. The blueberry is not a cell — but it is *cell-shaped* in the most fundamental sense: it is a compartment that does compartment work.
