## The Frontier

The contradiction isn't between "cell" and "not cell"—it's between two maps of the same territory. Voice A sees the frustule as a cell wall, and is right: it's genome-encoded, membrane-secreted, mitotically partitioned. Voice B sees the frustule as a dynamic scaffold that shapes behavior, and is right: it's morphodynamic, environmentally responsive, structurally constitutive. The failure mode of the cell ontology isn't the diatom—it's the assumption that "cell" means "protoplast bounded by a passive container." The diatom breaks that assumption because its boundary is not a container at all. It's a **load-bearing exoskeleton that the cell must shrink to fit**, generation after generation, until it sheds the whole thing and rebuilds from scratch. No plant cell wall does that. No fungal chitin does that. The frustule is not a wall—it's a **molt**.

The diatom is a cell that has outsourced its cytoskeleton to glass. Actin and tubulin do the internal moving; the frustule does the external defining. The cell doesn't live *in* the glass house—it *is* the glass house, in the same way a crab *is* its shell only if the shell were grown from the inside, patterned by the genome, and divided between offspring at every reproductive event. Voice A wins the biology. Voice B wins the ontology. The synthesis: the diatom is a **cell-with-a-molt**, a new kind of cellular individual that doesn't fit the standard "wall-bound protoplast" abstraction because its boundary is a **heritable, size-limiting, periodically-shed structural organ**, not a wall.

## The 5 Gold Terms

1. **Molt-wall** — a cell boundary that must be shed and rebuilt to allow growth, unlike a wall that expands or a house that's entered.
2. **Silica deposition vesicle (SDV)** — the intracellular membrane-bound foundry where the molt is cast, proving the glass is made *inside* the cell, not around it.
3. **Valve inheritance asymmetry** — the mitotic rule where one daughter keeps the parent's epitheca and the other builds a new hypotheca, creating the size-decrease lineage.
4. **Auxospore reset** — the sexual reproduction event where the diatom sheds the molt entirely and rebuilds at full size, the only "escape" from the glass.
5. **Diatomaceous earth** — the fossil record of molts, not houses: billions of shed walls that outlasted their cells, exactly as cellulose outlasts trees.

## The Math

No new math—but a precise accounting problem. The size-decrease lineage follows a geometric progression: each vegetative division reduces the average frustule diameter by a fixed increment (roughly 1–2 µm per division, depending on species), until the population hits a critical minimum size (often 30–40% of the original). At that threshold, the math forces a switch: auxospore formation, which resets the size distribution to the maximum. This is a **discrete reset in a continuous decrement**—a sawtooth wave, not a decay curve. The cell's "wall" is a variable that must be periodically zeroed. That's not histology. That's a limit cycle.

## The Polyformalism

Across substrates, the molt-wall pattern recurs: the arthropod exoskeleton (chitin, shed for growth), the snake's skin (keratin, shed in one piece), the antler (bone, shed and regrown annually), and the diatom's frustule (silica, shed via auxospore). In each case, the boundary is not a wall but a **temporary structural maximum** that the organism must outgrow. The difference: arthropods and snakes shed from the outside (ecdysis), while the diatom sheds from the inside—its molt is built intracellularly, then externalized, then abandoned. The frustule is the only known case where the molt is also the cell wall, the only case where the structural organ is *heritable* through mitosis. The diatom is not a cell in a house. It's a cell that is *periodically homeless by design*.

## The Cowboy's Maxim

A wall you gotta shed to grow ain't a wall—it's a skin you wear on the outside, and the diatom's the only critter that grows its own glass skin and then walks out of it.
