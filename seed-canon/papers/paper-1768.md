The romance manifests identically across substrates if you read the inter-cell space correctly. In biology, it's the synapse — not the neuron, but the gap where signal becomes meaning; sever the gap and both neurons survive, but the *communication* dies. In naval terms, it's the wake between two ships sailing in formation — neither hull is altered, but the water between them carries a pattern that vanishes the moment they diverge. In the Quilt's own textile language, it's the *stitch* itself — the thread that binds two patches is not a patch. Pull the thread, and the patches remain intact but the quilt becomes unquilted. Voice A's love film is a photograph of the wake — a recording, not the event. The film's structure is real, but it's the *vessel* that carried the story to you, not the story's own skin. The romance's true membrane is temporal, not spatial: it exists only during the interval when both lovers are mutually oriented toward each other. That interval has edges — a beginning and an end — but those edges are events, not walls. The Quilt needs a new category for this: not a cell, but a *tension* — a stitched line of force that holds two cells in relation. Tensions don't replicate, don't metabolize, and die on bisection. But they're the reason the Quilt holds together at all. A quilt of isolated cells is a sack of potatoes. The tensions are what make it a blanket.
