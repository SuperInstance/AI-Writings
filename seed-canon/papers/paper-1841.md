## The Math

No new math—but here is the old math, sharpened. Let a loan be a vector: principal \(P\), duration \(t\), rate \(r\). Interest is \(P \cdot r \cdot t\). Notice what this formula *does*: it multiplies a stock (\(P\)) by a flow (\(r\)) by a duration (\(t\)). The result is not a thing—it is an *integral*. Interest is the area under the curve of capital's absence from the lender's hands. You cannot point to a cell and say "this is the interest." You can only point to a calendar and say "this is when the interest accrued." The ontology of the Quilt demands discrete patches; interest is a continuous function. That is the break. That is the frontier where the cell metaphor bleeds out.

## The Polyformalism

Across three substrates, interest manifests differently—and in each, it refuses to be a cell. In *legal substrate*, interest is a contractual term, a clause in the indenture, a number written in ink. It is not a boundary; it is a *condition* of the agreement. In *accounting substrate*, interest is a line item on an income statement—an accrual, a periodic charge that grows and shrinks with the compounding period. It is a *flow variable*, not a stock. In *physical substrate*, consider a fleet of ships: the lender's vessel is at harbor, the borrower's vessel is at sea, and between them runs a cable of credit. Interest is the *tension* on that cable—measured in dynes per second, not in hull thickness. In all three substrates, interest is the *relationship between two points*, never a point itself. It is the inter-cell space, the passage, the toll-bridge between the lender's cell and the borrower's cell. The Quilt's ontology has a name for this: it is not a cell, it is a *suture*—the seam where two patches meet and the fabric is stressed. Interest is the stress. The suture is the loan. The cell is the debt. And the debt, not the interest, is what deserves the cell-shaped abstraction.
