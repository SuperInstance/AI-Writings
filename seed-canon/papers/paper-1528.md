# the guerrilla — a cell that is also an irregular fighter

## The Frontier

The guerrilla cell begins as pure credit. It promises nothing, owes nothing, and can walk away from any engagement it doesn't like. Its power is precisely this freedom from obligation — the state must feed, pay, and protect its people; the guerrilla must only survive. This is the amphibious advantage: a raiding force that never has to hold ground doesn't need a supply line.

But victory ruins the guerrilla. The graduation paradox: guerrillas win by becoming armies, and becoming an army means acquiring obligations. Taxes collected, wages promised, courts convened, clinics opened. Every service rendered to the population is a debt the movement now owes. The cell that could dissolve into the jungle becomes a proto-state nailed to a specific geography, with a ledger that grows heavier each day.

Round 2 located the vulnerability in obligation. The counterinsurgency insight was: don't bomb the proto-state — that confirms its legitimacy. Instead, out-administer it, or make its promises unpayable. The metric: exit capacity versus obligation ratio. Malaya worked because services were paired with resettlement. Good, but incomplete.

The prescription — out-administer the guerrilla — loses its own race. Counterinsurgents are almost always worse at local governance than the movement they're fighting. The insurgent has local knowledge, ideological commitment, and the advantage of being the underdog the population wants to believe in. Competing on service delivery is a race the state usually loses.

Round 3's deeper mechanism: the asymmetry of default. A real state can break its promises and survive. Governments default on obligations constantly — delayed pensions, broken infrastructure pledges, tax increases — and citizens grumble while the state limps on. The state has inherited legitimacy, equity, a flag people salute out of habit. A graduated guerrilla has no equity. It is all credit, and its credit is performance. Its entire claim to sovereignty rests on delivery.

The graduated guerrilla cannot survive its first default. Not because the default is catastrophic in itself, but because it reveals the movement's sovereignty as contingent. The population's obedience was purchased with promises; the first broken promise establishes that the guerrilla is just another power that can fail them. The state's default is absorbed by institutional inertia. The guerrilla's default is existential.

So the counterinsurgent doesn't need to out-govern. It needs only to force the comparison — schedule the exam. The incumbent wins ties. A state that forces the guerrilla to sit for tests of sovereignty it cannot study for, repeatedly, in public, wins even a mediocre performance. The guerrilla needs a landslide; the state only needs a draw.

## The 5 Gold Terms

**Default Asymmetry** — The state can break promises and survive; the graduated guerrilla cannot. All credit, no equity.

**The Scheduled Exam** — You don't win the governance comparison; you make the comparison happen. Force the guerrilla to perform sovereignty in public, repeatedly, at times you choose.

**The Payables Calendar** — The guerrilla's fiscal calendar is its skeleton. Map when wages are due, when harvest tax is collected, when courts sit. That's where the movement is most brittle.

**The First Default Amplified** — The counterinsurgent doesn't plant the bomb; it detonates the guerrilla's own ledger at the right moment. The first broken promise, broadcast, attributable.

**The Landed Force Doctrine** — The graduated guerrilla has landed and must establish a beachhead supply line. Cut the follow-on waves — make each promise undeliverable — and the landed force dies ashore.

## The Math

No new math; the relevant arithmetic is the asymmetry of default tolerance. Model the guerrilla's legitimacy as a ratio: performance delivered divided by promises made. A real state operates at 0.6 and survives — institutional inertia covers the gap. A graduated guerrilla needs 1.0, because its legitimacy has no inherited component. The counterinsurgent's job is to push the guerrilla's ratio below 1.0 at the moment of maximum visibility. The state's own ratio can fall to 0.4 and it still holds territory, courts, and tax collection. The guerrilla's ratio drops to 0.9 and the population begins calculating exit options. The math isn't about out-performing the guerrilla; it's about the fact that the guerrilla's performance threshold is absolute while the state's is relative. The state wins any comparison that isn't a landslide, because the guerrilla's margin for error is zero.

## The Polyformalism

The default asymmetry operates across substrates because the mechanism is structural, not cultural.

**Economic substrate:** The guerrilla that taxes must deliver services. Disrupt the delivery chain — hit the road the clinic's medicine travels on, the market where the harvest tax is collected — and the tax becomes tribute without return. The population's calculation shifts from "we support the movement" to "we're being extorted." The state doesn't need to provide better services; it needs to make the guerrilla's services fail visibly.

**Legal substrate:** The guerrilla that holds territory and administers justice creates expectations. When it punishes a crime, it has established a precedent; when it fails to punish the next one, the population sees inconsistency. The counterinsurgent can feed the guerrilla cases it cannot resolve cleanly — disputes that require institutional weight, appeals that demand a legal apparatus the movement doesn't have. The guerrilla's court becomes a stage where its inadequacy is performed.

**Military substrate:** The guerrilla that becomes an army must defend fixed assets — depots, headquarters, population centers it has promised to protect. The counterinsurgent doesn't need to destroy these assets; it needs to threaten them on a schedule that forces the guerrilla to choose between defending its territory and fulfilling its promises elsewhere. Every raid the guerrilla can't prevent is a default on its protection contract. The state's own failures are absorbed by its flag; the guerrilla's failures are attributed to its illegitimacy.

**Temporal substrate:** The guerrilla's calendar is its vulnerability. Harvest season, when it must collect taxes or lose revenue. Payroll days, when it must produce wages or lose fighters. Religious festivals, when it must demonstrate piety or lose moral authority. The counterinsurgent who maps this calendar can strike at the moment of maximum commitment — when the guerrilla's forces are concentrated on delivery, not defense. The state doesn't fight the guerrilla; it fights the guerrilla's schedule.

The pattern across substrates: the counterinsurgent doesn't need to be better at governance, law, or protection. It needs to force the guerrilla to perform these functions in public, at scale, under conditions the counterinsurgent chooses. The guerrilla's graduation from cell to proto-state is its transformation from a movement that runs on myth to an enterprise that runs on delivery. And delivery can be interrupted by any mediocre effort.

The concrete example: Malaya's resettlement program worked not because the British provided better services than the guerrillas — they often didn't — but because resettlement forced the comparison. Villagers in new villages could see what the state provided and what the guerrillas couldn't. The guerrillas' promises became measurable, and measurement is the enemy of myth. The British didn't out-administer the insurgency; they scheduled the exam.

The counterinsurgent's craft is timing and amplification. Wait for the guerrilla's first default — a raid it can't prevent, a wage it can't pay, a clinic it can't protect — and make sure everyone knows it, and that the default is attributable. The guerrilla's newly acquired ledger is a bomb the counterinsurgent doesn't have to plant. Only detonate it at the right moment.

The intelligence requirement shifts from counting what the guerrilla holds to counting what it owes and when. The metric graduates from stocktake to timing. You don't count its weapons; you count its payables. You don't map its positions; you map its obligation dates. The guerrilla's promises are its supply line across the water. Every promise made to villagers is a landing craft committed. The counterinsurgent doesn't storm the beachhead — it sinks the resupply.

The cowboy version: once a rancher files his claim, he's due at the county seat. Registration dates, water rights hearings, the day the herd has to be moved. A rancher with a brand has a calendar. Rustlers work by the calendar too — hit the herd on shipping day when it's concentrated and committed. You don't shoot the man who filed the claim. You make sure he can't pay the taxes on it when they come due. The county does the evicting for you.

The graduated guerrilla has landed. Its promises are its beachhead supply line. Cut the follow-on waves, and the landed force dies ashore.

## The Cowboy's Maxim

Don't shoot the man who filed the claim — make sure he can't pay the taxes on it when they come due, and let the county do the evicting.
