## The Frontier

The contradiction is not about letters. It is about a smuggled assumption: that “cell” means *container*. Voice A treats the capital as a sentinel—a soldier standing at the gate. Voice B treats it as a flag—a marker with no interior. Both are wrong because both assume the capital must either *hold* something or *point* at something. The deeper truth: the capital is a **cell whose membrane is its entire body**. It does not contain meaning; it *is* the boundary event that makes meaning possible downstream. The failure mode of the cell ontology is not that the capital lacks an inside—it is that the ontology demands an inside for every cell. The capital is a cell with zero volume and full function. It is the first cell of a sentence, a cell that is all edge, all threshold, all start. It does not hold a payload; it *is* the payload of beginning.

## The Math

No new math. But here is the arithmetic that matters: a lowercase “a” and a capital “A” share the same glyph geometry—same stroke count, same curvature, same x-height ratio in their respective forms—yet their function differs by *positional index* in the string. Let the sentence be a sequence s[1..n]. The capital is s[1] with a flag. Remove it from index 1, place it at index 4 (“hoUse”), and its capitalness evaporates—it becomes a typographical error, not a start. That is a **context-dependent identity function**, not a substance. A cell in the Quilt ontology is supposed to be portable: lift it, carry it, it remains itself. The capital fails that test. Its identity is a function of its coordinate. That is not a container’s property; that is an event’s property. The math of the capital is the math of a *derivative*: it has value only at the point of change.

## The Polyformalism

Across substrates, the capital manifests as the same zero-volume boundary cell. In **prose**, it is the first glyph after a period—a white-space-to-ink transition, a membrane that says “new sentence here.” In **code**, it is the initial uppercase in `CamelCase`—a flag that says “new word boundary inside an identifier,” carrying no semantic payload itself but enabling the parser to segment tokens. In every substrate, the capital is a cell that holds nothing and starts everything. It is the membrane of the first chamber, the doorpost of the first room, the flag raised before the fleet sails.
