1. **Compás genome** — the twelve-beat rhythmic code that transmits heredity across bodies and oceans, stable enough to recognize, mutable enough to spawn fandanguillo and son jarocho.
2. **Borrowed membrane** — the tarima circle and triple-time boundary that the fandango does not own but commandeers from the community each time it fires.
3. **Lytic replication** — reproduction that requires a host (dancers, guitarists, listeners) and destroys its host's ordinary time while it occurs.
4. **Spatiotemporal pulse** — the fandango's true mode of existence: not a persistent object but a recurring wave that exists only during its performance window.
5. **Descent without container** — lineage that persists across centuries while no single instantiation ever holds the whole pattern.

## The Math

No new math. The fandango's structure is rhythmic, not numerical — the compás is a 12-count cycle that can be counted (3/4 or 6/8 hemiola), but the essential mathematics is topological: the fandango is a **knot that ties and unties**. It has no fixed boundary because it is a *crossing pattern* — the dancers' feet, the guitarist's rasgueado, the singer's coplas all interlace and then dissolve. The only stable invariant is the count itself, which behaves like a conserved quantity: twelve beats always present, always recombined. This is not math in the sense of equations; it is math in the sense of a loop that closes only while performed.

## The Polyformalism

The fandango-as-lytic-event manifests identically across three substrates. **Biological**: a virus particle has no metabolism, no membrane of its own, yet reproduces with heredity and variation by hijacking a cell — the fandango does exactly this to a community's evening, converting social energy into patterned movement and leaving behind new carriers (people who now know the steps). **Musical**: the compás is a rhythmic cell that exists only when played — it has no score that persists independent of performance, yet it transmits across generations through imitation, mutating like a melodic strain. **Naval**: a fleet is not a ship; it is a formation that exists only while multiple vessels hold relative position — the fandango is a fleet of bodies that assembles, performs its pattern, and disperses, leaving no physical trace except the tarima's worn surface. In all three, the pattern outlives its instances, but never exists outside them.

**A dance ain't a cell — it's a storm that borrows your barn to happen in, and leaves the fence lines changed when it's gone.**
