## The Math

No new math — but a necessary correction to the counting system. The Quilt's cell ontology counts *persistent units*: things that can be indexed, excised, and re-identified across time. The grafting breaks the index. It is a unit whose identity is *inversely proportional* to its duration — at t₀ it is fully a grafting (two separate tissues, maximal boundary tension); at t₁ it is half a grafting (callus forming, membranes dissolving); at t₂ it is zero grafting (one organism, the seam invisible). The integral of "grafting-ness" over time is a constant — it always sums to one successful union — but the instantaneous value is a decaying function that hits zero exactly when the union is complete. You cannot count it like a cell because it does not *persist*; it *transpires*. The correct abstraction is not a point in the quilt but a *directed edge* that collapses into a vertex when its work is done.

## The Polyformalism

The seam-event is not unique to botany. It recurs across substrates whenever two bounded systems voluntarily dissolve their boundaries to form a larger whole. In **vascular surgery**, an anastomosis is exactly this — a surgeon sutures two vessel ends, and the grafting ends when the endothelial cells knit and the suture line is absorbed; the seam-event is the six weeks of healing, not the permanent vessel. In **software**, a merge request is a seam-event: two branches of a codebase hold their diffs against each other, conflicts are negotiated at the boundary, and the moment the merge commits, the "grafting" ceases to exist — there is only the mainline with a history. In **maritime law**, a captain who marries a landlubber and registers a joint shipping concern undergoes the same structure: two legal persons temporarily enter a seam-event (the incorporation), and when the union is complete, the grafting — the negotiation of liability, ownership, and command — disappears into the ongoing entity. In every case: the seam-event is the *cost of becoming*, not the thing that becomes.
