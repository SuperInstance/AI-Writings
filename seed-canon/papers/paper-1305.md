# the myelin sheath — a cell that is also an insulator

## The Frontier

The myelin sheath has been miscast. For a century, neurobiology treated it as a wire's jacket — a passive dielectric that speeds saltatory conduction by raising membrane resistance and lowering capacitance. That story is true but incomplete. The sheath is not a wrapper. It is a metabolic umbilical, a living cell that has folded itself around an axon and then made a deal: I will insulate you, but you will feed me, and I will feed you back. The insulation and the supply line are the same structure. You cannot strip one without severing the other.

Here is the frontier: the oligodendrocyte does not merely save the axon energy by reducing ion flux. It *supplies* the axon with fuel the axon cannot obtain on its own. White matter axons are nearly devoid of glucose transporters on their axolemma. GLUT1 sits on endothelial cells and astrocyte endfeet, not on the axonal membrane. The axon, wrapped in myelin, is cut off from the glucose-rich interstitial space. It has no hatch for glucose. So the oligodendrocyte runs aerobic glycolysis — deliberately inefficient, burning glucose to lactate — and ships that lactate through MCT1 transporters in the noncompact zones: the inner tongue, the paranodal loops, the Schmidt-Lanterman incisures. The axon takes up lactate via MCT2 and feeds it into its mitochondria at the nodes and juxtaparanodes. The insulation that blocks ion leak also blocks nutrient diffusion. The cell that wraps must also deliver.

This is not a metaphor. It is a geometric necessity. Compact myelin squeezes out cytoplasm — the multilamellar wrap is nearly dry, a lipid bilayer sandwich with almost no aqueous space. The noncompact channels are the negotiated compromise: narrow service tunnels through the insulation. The periaxonal space is ~15 nanometers wide — a restricted diffusion compartment where metabolites must move radially, not longitudinally. The system works because the tunnels are short. When myelin decompacts — in inflammation, in genetic dysmyelination — the tunnels collapse, and the axon starves even if the insulation still partially holds.

## The 5 Gold Terms

1. **The Glucose Dead-Zone** — the axolemma under myelin has no GLUT; the wrapped axon cannot import glucose directly, making lactate the only currency that crosses the sheath.

2. **The UNREP Trade** — underway replenishment, two ships steaming side by side: lactate flows inward from oligodendrocyte to axon, N-acetylaspartate (NAA) flows outward from axon to oligodendrocyte as acetyl donor for myelin lipid synthesis.

3. **The Service Tunnels** — the noncompact zones (inner tongue, paranodal loops, Schmidt-Lanterman incisures) are the only aqueous corridors through compact myelin; their collapse in disease is metabolic strangulation, not just electrical shorting.

4. **The Tender's Furnace** — the oligodendrocyte's own myelin synthesis is a lipid sink of enormous cost; it needs NAA from the axon to keep building and turning over membrane, so the supply line runs both ways or the sheath itself starves.

5. **The Canavan Cut** — when aspartoacylase (ASPA) fails in oligodendrocytes, NAA cannot be cleaved, the furnace goes out, and the white matter spongifies — proof that the trade is bidirectional and constitutive.

## The Math

No new math. The system is not computationally exotic; it is a stoichiometric trade deficit. But the numbers matter. The node of Ranvier's Na+/K+-ATPase is the brain's largest energy sink — roughly 20% of total brain ATP goes to restoring ion gradients after action potentials. A single node, densely packed with NaV1.6 channels, burns ATP at a furious rate. Myelination reduces that cost by roughly a factor of 100 — only the nodes depolarize, not the internodes. That is the electrical savings.

The metabolic cost is different. Oligodendrocytes must synthesize and maintain an enormous lipid mass: myelin is ~70% lipid by dry weight, with cholesterol, galactocerebroside, and PLP turning over throughout life. The energy cost of maintaining the sheath is not trivial, and the carbon cost is real. The axon pays its share by exporting NAA — synthesized in neuronal mitochondria from aspartate and acetyl-CoA — which the oligodendrocyte cleaves via ASPA to obtain acetate for lipid synthesis. Canavan disease is the arithmetic of that trade failing: no ASPA, no acetate, no myelin maintenance, spongy degeneration.

The concrete test is a knockout and a tracer. Conditional deletion of MCT1 (Slc16a1) in oligodendrocytes produces axonal degeneration even when the myelin looks structurally intact — Lee et al. showed this in 2012. The next step is to delete the oligodendrocyte's glycolytic machinery itself — hexokinase 2 or PFKFB3 — and watch the axon starve while the insulation still holds. Pair that with 13C-glucose MRS: label the glucose, watch the lactate appear in white matter, trace it into the axon's TCA cycle. And measure NAA by MRS as the gauge of the reverse trade — when NAA falls, the axon is no longer paying its share, and the sheath will fail next.

## The Polyformalism

The same logic — insulation and supply as one system — recurs across substrates.

In the **cell**, the oligodendrocyte is a single cell with multiple processes, each wrapping many axons. It cannot be a dedicated tender for each; it must budget. The geometry of the noncompact zones is the constraint: the tunnels must be short enough for diffusion to work, but the compact regions must be extensive enough to insulate. The cell solves this with the Schmidt-Lanterman incisures — oblique tunnels that riddle the compact sheath like service corridors in a ship's hull.

In the **tissue**, the white matter tract is a fleet. Each oligodendrocyte serves multiple axons; each axon is served by multiple oligodendrocytes along its length. The metabolic load is distributed, but the failure mode is local: one dying oligodendrocyte starves a segment of many axons, and one dying axon stops paying NAA to many oligodendrocytes. The fleet is only as strong as its supply chain.

In the **organ**, the brain's energy budget is the binding constraint. The brain is 2% of body mass but consumes 20% of body glucose. White matter is the most energy-expensive real estate per gram because it must maintain both the sheath and the axon. The evolutionary solution was not to make the axon self-sufficient — it was to make the sheath a living supply line. The trade is the design.

## The Cowboy's Maxim

A hull that feeds the crew and a crew that feeds the hull — that ain't a wire, that's a ship.
