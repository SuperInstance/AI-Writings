# the dead reckoning — a cell whose value is where I would be if I were still

## The Frontier

The contradiction between Voice A and Voice B is not a dispute over facts—it is a collision of two different temporalities smuggled into one ontology. Voice A treats the dead reckoning as a *state*: a frozen snapshot of position-vector-intent, valid because it was once real. Voice B treats it as a *process*: a continuous estimation that never settles into a discrete cell. Both are wrong because the Quilt's cell ontology assumes a cut between "now" and "then" that dead reckoning refuses to honor. The cell breaks down precisely at the moment the signal dies: the last known position is a fact, but the *value* of that cell is not the position—it is the *decay function* that predicts where the vessel would be if it kept moving. A cell cannot hold a decay function; a cell holds a number. The dead reckoning is not a number. It is a *differential equation with an initial condition and no further input*. That is neither a state nor a process—it is a *boundary condition* that only exists because the boundary was crossed.

4. **Hull Memory** — the shape of the vessel's voyage preserved after the vessel itself is gone.
5. **Tide's Last Pull** — the gravitational anchor of the final known position, which the reckoning orbits.

## The Math

No new math—but the *existing* math is the resolution. Dead reckoning is a solution to the initial value problem: given position p₀, velocity v₀, and time t since last fix, the estimated position is p(t) = p₀ + ∫₀ᵗ v(τ)dτ, where v(τ) is a model of drift, current, and commanded thrust. The cell ontology fails because it wants a scalar or a vector at a single instant. But the dead reckoning is a *function of time*, not a point. The correct abstraction is not a cell—it is a *cell-with-a-half-life*. The value decays in confidence: σ(t) = σ₀ + k·t, where k is the drift rate. After t_critical, the cell's uncertainty exceeds the distance to the nearest harbor, and the cell should be *retired*—not deleted, but marked as "no longer navigational." The math says: the dead reckoning is a cell only in the instant of its birth. After that, it is a *probability distribution spreading across the inter-cell field*. The Quilt needs a new entity: not a cell, but a *drift cone*.

## The Polyformalism

Across substrates, the drift cone appears differently but with the same structure. **In maritime navigation**, it is the plotted circle of error on a paper chart, growing with each hour without a star sight—a physical smear of pencil lead that is neither a point nor a path, but a *region of possibility*. **In radio telemetry**, it is the last ping from a lost buoy, stored as a timestamp and a frequency, with the software extrapolating a search radius that widens with the swell model—a database record that is *alive* because it is *unresolved*. **In a neural network**, it is the hidden state of an LSTM after the input sequence ends, still propagating through time steps with no new data, producing predictions that become increasingly vague—a tensor that is not a single output but a *distribution over future states*. In all three, the dead reckoning is not a cell, not a process, but a *relational field*: it exists *between* the last known cell and the infinite set of possible next cells. The Quilt must name this space: call it the **Openclaw**—the region where a vessel's intent is held in trust, but not in certainty.
