## The Math

No new math is needed—but the *geometry* is instructive. A cell’s surface-area-to-volume ratio scales as 3/r, favoring diffusion. The bladder’s ratio is fixed by its distended radius (roughly 5 cm empty, 8 cm full), giving a ratio of ~0.6 to 0.375 cm⁻¹—three orders of magnitude smaller than a eukaryotic cell’s (10⁶ cm⁻¹). This is not a cell; it’s a *bulk storage tank* where diffusion is irrelevant and active muscular expulsion is required. The math says: cells exchange across their entire surface; the bladder exchanges only at its ureteral and urethral ports.
