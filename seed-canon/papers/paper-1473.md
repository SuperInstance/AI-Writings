## The Frontier

The failure mode of the cell ontology here is **boundary attribution**: the mambo has no stable membrane because its rhythmic structure is not a wall but a *pulse that propagates*. A cell membrane separates inside from outside; a mambo rhythm *entrains* inside and outside. The dancers, the musicians, the floor, the spectators—none are inside or outside; all are pulled into a shared oscillatory field. The cell metaphor breaks precisely at the point where the mambo's "boundary" is not a surface but a *frequency*. 2. **Pulse-permeable threshold** — the mambo's tempo range (roughly 180–220 BPM) that admits steps that match its subdivision while rejecting those that don't, acting as a frequency-based gate.

5. **Transient membrane** — the mambo's rhythmic structure that exists only during performance, dissolving when the music stops—a membrane that is an event, not a structure.

## The Math

The cell ontology requires a spatial boundary; the mambo's boundary is temporal. You cannot integrate a cell membrane over time—it is a spatial surface. So: no new math, because the mambo's ontology is not spatial, and the cell ontology is fundamentally spatial. In all three, the mambo is a **transient membrane**—a boundary that exists only while energy is supplied. When the music stops, the cell vanishes. There is no residual structure, no preserved interior. This is the deeper truth both voices missed: the mambo is a *cell that only exists during its own mitosis*—a self-organizing pulse that assembles a temporary boundary from its own activity, then dissolves. It is not a cell in the fabric; it is a **cell-event** in the fabric's time dimension.
