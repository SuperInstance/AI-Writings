## The 5 Gold Terms

1. **Transit node** — a unit whose function is defined entirely by what passes through it, not by what it contains.
2. **Boundary host** — an object that does not perform a function but provides the geometric condition under which a passing thing performs its own transformation.
3. **Directional lease** — the temporary assumption of a property (direction change) by a node, which is borrowed from the moving line and returned the instant the line departs.
4. **Containment failure** — the specific breakdown where a proposed cell cannot locate its defining function inside its own membrane.
5. **Curvature-as-terrain** — the service a pulley provides: not the turn itself, but the curved ground that makes the turn possible.

## The Polyformalism

Across substrates, the pulley's true nature repeats. In **mechanical systems**, a pulley is a wheel with a groove—a passive constraint that redirects a tension vector. In **electrical networks**, the analog is a **bus bar** or a **junction node**: current flows in, current flows out, and the node itself stores nothing, changes nothing, but provides the topological connection that lets the current alter its path. In **information systems**, the analog is a **router** or a **switch**: packets arrive on one interface, depart on another, and the device's entire function is to provide the geometric (logical) condition for redirection—it holds no packet content, only path information. In **biological systems**, the analog is a **synapse** or a **gap junction**: the signal passes through, but the junction itself does not generate the signal—it hosts the transmission. In every substrate, the pattern is identical: a **node that provides curvature, connection, or redirection** for something else's motion. The pulley is not a cell. It is a **boundary condition made physical**—a piece of the field, not a unit of the fabric.
