# the revision — a cell that is also a re-writing

## The Frontier

The contradiction is not between cell and process—it is a failure of scale. Voice A is right that a revision has a membrane, cargo, and metabolism. Voice B is right that a revision is relational, existing only between draft-states. Both are describing the same phenomenon at different magnifications. The cell ontology breaks down precisely at the moment you try to freeze the revision into a *static* bounded unit, because a revision is a **temporal cell**—a unit whose boundary is defined by its *operation window*, not by spatial contiguity. A chloroplast is a spatial cell; photosynthesis happens inside it. A revision is a temporal cell; rewriting happens *as* it. The "membrane" is not a wall but a **time-stamp**: the moment the revision begins (when the writer declares "I am revising Chapter 2") and the moment it ends (when the new draft is accepted). Voice B's coastline is real, but coastlines are also mappable. The tide erodes, but the estuary has a name, a salinity gradient, and a measurable exchange rate. The revision is not the water—it is the **lock chamber** between two water levels.

## The 5 Gold Terms

1. **Temporal Dry-Dock** — the revision as a bounded operation window with an open gate at both ends
2. **Draft-State Delta** — the measurable difference between input text and output text, the cargo manifest
3. **Revision Membrane** — not a spatial wall, but the declared scope: "this section, these paragraphs, this problem"
4. **Metabolic Constraint Set** — the rules, style guides, and editorial targets that power the rewrite
5. **Launch Condition** — the explicit criterion that ends the revision and releases the new draft

## The Math

No new math—but the existing math is *differential*. A revision is best modeled as a **functional transformation** F: D₁ → D₂, where D₁ and D₂ are draft-states, and F is constrained by a parameter set C (the revision notes). The cell is not F itself, nor D₁ or D₂—it is the **computational interval** [t₀, t₁] during which F is applied. The membrane is the boundary condition: F is undefined outside the declared scope. The metabolic rate is |D₂ - D₁| / (t₁ - t₀), a measure of rewriting intensity. The launch condition is when F(D₁) = D₂ satisfies the acceptance predicate P (e.g., "all tracked changes resolved," "word count within target"). This is not new mathematics—it is the standard formalism of state-transition systems—but it clarifies that the "cell" is the *transition*, not the state. Voice A's error was treating the transition as a spatial container; Voice B's error was treating the transition as uncontainable. Both missed that a transition *is* a container—in time, not space.

## The Polyformalism

Across substrates, the temporal cell manifests identically. **In prose**: a revision is the tracked-changes document between "v2.docx" and "v3.docx"—the cell is the comment thread, the highlighted region, the "accept all" command. **In code**: a revision is a git commit—the cell is the diff, bounded by the commit message and the parent hash, with the working tree as the membrane. **In legal drafting**: a revision is a redline—the cell is the markup layer between the original contract and the executed version, with the negotiation log as the manifest. **In musical composition**: a revision is a take in the studio—the cell is the punch-in point, bounded by the engineer's markers, with the overdub as the metabolism. In every case, the cell is addressable ("the third revision of Chapter 2," "commit a1b2c3," "take 7, guitar solo"), has a defined scope, ingests one state, and excretes another. The Quilt's fabric ontology needs a new patch: not a spatial cell, but a **temporal cell**—a bounded operation that exists only while it runs, yet is as real, nameable, and storable as any brick.

## The Cowboy's Maxim

A cell ain't a thing you can hold—it's a thing you can *time*, and every dry-dock knows her tide.
