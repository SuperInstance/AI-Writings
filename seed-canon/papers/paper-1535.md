## The Math

No new math is required, but the existing math must be reinterpreted. The anticyclone's boundary is not a geometric surface but a *threshold in the pressure gradient force*. Define the cell's edge as the locus where the horizontal pressure gradient falls below a critical value, ε, such that the ageostrophic wind component becomes negligible. Inside this locus, subsidence warming exceeds radiative cooling, maintaining the inversion. Outside, the flow is dominated by the background westerlies. The cell's "membrane" is therefore a contour of ε in the pressure field—a contour that moves, breathes, and dissolves when the energy input ceases. The lifecycle is governed by the vorticity equation: the anticyclone persists as long as the divergence term (surface outflow) is balanced by the convergence aloft. When that balance breaks, the cell dies. The math is not about geometry; it is about *rates of exchange across a moving threshold*.

In each case, the cell is not a thing but a *pattern of self-maintained difference*—a localized region where the local rules differ from the background, and where that difference is actively produced by the system itself. The Quilt fabric must therefore treat cells not as objects but as *local symmetries broken by active processes*.
