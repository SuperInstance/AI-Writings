## The Math

No new math—but a re-reading of existing equations. The mountain's identity condition is a balance equation: uplift rate (U) ≈ erosion rate (E) + isostatic compensation rate (I). When U > E + I, the mountain grows; when E + I > U, it decays. The standing wave condition is ∂h/∂t ≈ 0 (height roughly constant over geological time) while U, E, and I each remain nonzero. That's the same mathematics as a cell's steady state: d[internal]/dt = 0 while influx = efflux ≠ 0. The mountain is a steady-state solution to the Navier-Stokes-like equations of crustal deformation, with the same formal structure as a cell's chemical homeostasis. The difference is only in the diffusion coefficient of the medium—rock diffuses stress at centimeters per millennium, not micrometers per second.

In each substrate, the mountain is not a container but a *relation*—a place where two regimes meet and exchange.
