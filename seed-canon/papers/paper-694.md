Voice A treats the stall as a *cell with a function*: a guard post, a lighthouse, a regulator. Voice B treats it as a *rupture in the fabric*: a jammed loom, a dead zone, a pressure valve. It is a *threshold event*—a moment when the quilt’s normal relational grammar (cell-to-cell adjacency, flow, containment) breaks down, and the only thing that remains is a *command to halt*. It exists only as a *transition*, a phase between moving and stopped, between safe and unsafe. You cannot biopsy a stall. You can only experience it as a change in the field’s tension.

No new math—but a new *use* of existing math: treat the stall as a *phase transition marker*, not a state.
