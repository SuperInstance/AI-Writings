## The Math

No new math — but the existing math is precise. The katabatic wind obeys a Froude-number regime: the dense underflow moves as a gravity current, with a dimensionless ratio of inertial to buoyancy forces. The Antarctic katabatic at Terra Nova Bay runs at sustained speeds of 40–60 knots, draining from the polar plateau at ~2,800 m elevation, over a fetch of ~200 km, and its density excess over ambient air is roughly 1–2% (a temperature deficit of 10–15°C). The boundary layer height is 100–500 m. The wind's "membrane" is a Richardson-number instability: when shear exceeds stratification, the seam breaks into turbulence — the cell lyses. The math says: this is a gravity current, not a cell. Gravity currents have a head, a body, and a tail; they entrain ambient fluid at their upper surface and mix at their front. There is no conserved interior; there is a *flux* with a shape.

## The Polyformalism

In **atmospheric physics**, the katabatic is a boundary-layer flow — a slab of dense air accelerating under gravity, parameterized by slope angle, surface cooling rate, and ambient stratification. In **oceanography**, the same structure appears as a turbidity current or a dense bottom plume — the cell metaphor fails identically; these are called "gravity currents" and are studied as *events* with a Froude number and an entrainment coefficient. In **biology**, the closest analog is not a cell but a *chemotactic stream* — Dictyostelium amoebae aggregate into a migrating slug that is not a multicellular organism but a transient, coherent collective with a front and a back, streaming toward a destination, then fruiting and dying. In **geomorphology**, the katabatic is a *sediment-transport event* — it carves sastrugi, builds dunes, polishes ice. Across all substrates, the pattern is the same: a *self-organizing gradient current* — a transient coherence that borrows its boundary from the environment and spends it downslope.
