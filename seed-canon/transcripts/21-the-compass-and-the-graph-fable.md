## Round 1 — The Compression

### The Child (Ling-3.0-flash)

A brass compass with a needle floating in water. North is north. A sailor in 1500 looks at it and goes north. One direction. No cost. No wind. No current. Just north.

A 2085 substrate-mediated graph shows 12 destinations at once. Each has a cost — time, fuel, weather, convoy, confidence. The captain picks the lowest. The graph logs why. The log is auditable later. The needle doesn't log.

The fix is the address. The substrate turns one direction into many directions with evidence. The 2088 address makes the graph a partner. Not a needle.

### The Cynic (Mistral-Small-3.2-24B-Instruct-2506)

In 1500, a sailor grips a brass compass, its needle bobbing in water, pointing steadfastly north. He knows the direction, but the cost of the journey? The wind's temper, the sea's whim, the distance's toll? Unknowns. The compass is a silent partner, honest in its ignorance.

Six centuries later, a captain stares at a substrate-mediated graph, a dance of data points and lines, each representing a possible destination, a potential path. The graph whispers costs and convictions, a chorus of possibilities. The captain's choice is not simple, nor is it irreversible. It's a dance with data, a negotiation with numbers.

The sailor's decision is etched in the wind, his course plotted by the compass's unyielding north. The captain's choice, however, is logged, auditable, a trail of breadcrumbs for future scrutiny. The graph doesn't lie, but it's not infallible. It's a dance with doubt, a partnership with uncertainty.

The compass is honest in its simplicity, a testament to a time when navigation was about instinct and the elements. The graph is a different kind of honest, a testament to a time when navigation is about data and its inferences. The substrate is the medium that transforms a picture of one direction into a graph of many, with every path's cost logged, every decision auditable.

The lesson? The compass is a substrate address without a witness. The substrate is what makes the address a partner, not a needle. It's not about old versus new, it's about the honesty of auditable navigation. The graph's cost is logged, its conviction is logged, and the captain can always ask, "Why did the graph prefer this path?" And the substrate, in its data-driven honesty, will answer.

### The Compact One (phi-4)

In the year 2085, two navigators stand at the helm of their journeys, a sailor from the 1500s with a compass and a captain with a sophisticated substrate-mediated graph. The compass, a brass-dedicated device with a magnetized needle, points unwaveringly north. It serves as a partner in knowing direction alone, untouched by external forces like wind, current, or distance. The sailor uses it to navigate to a single destination, bound to the single truth of magnetic north.

In contrast, the captain's graph unveils a network of destinations, each path annotated with real-time variables such as time, fuel, weather, and substrate confidence. This graph serves as a partner in knowing cost, fully aware of the complexities of navigation. It enables the captain to choose among myriad paths, making decisions informed by comprehensive data and able to be revisited and revised.

Six centuries apart, both sailors are guided by instruments designed for their eras. The sailor’s compass, steadfast and mechanical, provides a singular direction with uncompromised honesty; its truth lies in its limitation. The captain’s graph, dynamic and inferential, offers an auditable path, logging costs and convictions for scrutiny and reflection. This fable captures not a shift in simplicity or complexity but a transformation in navigation: the graph’s substrate reflects a new honesty, one of transparency and answerability.
