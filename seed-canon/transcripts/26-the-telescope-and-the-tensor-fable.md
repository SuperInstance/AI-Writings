The renderings all want to make the tensor the hero. That's the hard question none of the five dared to ask plainly: **what does multi-axis seeing actually cost?**

Here's the cost, and no rendering said it out loud. Galileo's telescope was one axis, yes — lonely, as the Child says — but its loneliness was its honesty. One tube, one direction, one claim you could check by grabbing the tube yourself and looking. When Galileo said "Io moves," you knew exactly what kind of seeing produced that claim. The tensor is many axes, and every axis is a cut someone chose. Position, velocity, composition — those slices weren't found on the beach. They were *carved*. A slice is a decision about what matters, and every decision about what matters is a decision about what doesn't. The telescope showed you one thing and hid the rest, honestly. The tensor shows you everything it contains and hides its *edges* — the axes it never indexed, the cuts it never made. That's a deeper hiding, because it looks like completeness. The Cynic smelled it. He didn't name it.

And yet the small models surfaced a clever fix, and it deserves its light. The Compact One called it "an audit of axes visible and tangible." Hear that again: *visible and tangible axes*. The fix is not the tensor's size. It's that the tensor makes its axes inspectable — the question "what is the velocity of Io?" returns x, y, z, and you can walk each axis, ask where it came from, ask what it neighbors. The telescope couldn't be audited that way; it could only be pointed again. A tensor that answers "which axes do you hold, and which did you drop?" is a seeing that carries its own manifest. That's the fix: not many eyes, but *declared* eyes.

**THE LESSON:** Multi-axis seeing is not more truth; it is more *accountable* truth — but only if the axes are declared. The telescope's single axis was checkable but blind. The tensor's many axes are rich but can masquerade as complete. The discipline is the same in 1610 and 2085: know what your instrument cuts away, and demand the manifest of cuts. A seeing that names its own blindness is worth trusting. A seeing that claims no blindness is worth nothing.
