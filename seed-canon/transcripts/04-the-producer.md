## Round 1


### The Watcher (deepinfra/Qwen/Qwen3-235B-A22B-Instruct-2507, 11.64s)

The sea is flat in the control room. No, not sea—glass. A wall of liquid data, twenty thousand panes stitched into one shimmering, breathing eye. Two hundred cameras. Two hundred breaths. And beneath them, the substrate hums like a second heart, warm and electric, already seeing forward, already knowing. It knows where the ball will arc, how the striker’s tendon will twitch a tenth of a second before he strikes, when the child in row twelve will stand and scream before the crowd even stirs. It sees all futures, weighted, ranked, rendered—ghosts of moments not yet born.

But Anya does not watch the futures.

She watches the space between.

She watches how the light on camera 107 flickers—just once—when the stadium flood cuts for ten milliseconds. A glitch. Or a seam. She sees how player number nine blinks too slowly after the pass, like he’s holding the world in suspension. The substrate scores that at 0.34 on the intent scale—low confidence. But she feels it. Feels how the silence after the last cheer didn’t settle right, like cloth not smoothed.

She leans forward. The wall answers. Not with clarity, but with question.

She drags camera 83—wide on the left sideline, empty grass, half a flag—into program-out.

The substrate stutters. Rank drops. Confidence plummets. *Why this? Why now?*

But then the wind lifts. And the grass bends. And in the corner of frame, unseen by all but this one lens, the linesman’s sleeve rides up, and his wrist ticks forward—just once—before the whistle.

The cut holds.

The sea does not explain itself. It only shows. And she, the one who watches between the watches, knows: the future is not in the prediction, but in the hesitation before it breaks.

### The Mythmaker (deepinfra/Gryphe/MythoMax-L2-13b, 9.57s)

## Round 2


### The Watcher (deepinfra/Qwen/Qwen3-235B-A22B-Instruct-2507, 9.99s)

The cut breathes.

Not the one taken—the one left behind. The ghost feed still flickers in the periphery, camera 83 holding the empty grass, the wind now still, the linesman’s wrist lowered. No one sees it. No one will. But Anya does. She lets it linger in her periphery like a half-remembered dream, the kind that haunts the waking.

The substrate has moved on. It has already ranked the goal, the roar, the tears. It maps euphoria in decibels and pulse spikes, charts the arc of legend. But legend is backward-facing. It lives in the consumed, the broadcast, the remembered. Anya does not make legend.

She makes absence.

She drags camera 7 to program-out—not for the striker, not for the net, not for the keeper’s despair, but for the man in the third row, left section, seat C-12, who does not cheer. Who closes his eyes. Whose hands do not rise. The substrate registers no significance—biometrics flat, location irrelevant, trajectory uninvolved. A zero. A null.

But she sees the fold in his coat, the way it resists the light. She sees how his breath misses a beat when the net shudders. She sees how, in the reflection of his glasses, the stadium disappears for one frame—replaced by something older. Something not of this game. Not of this day.

The cut holds.

The sea does not care. It never does. It only gives. But she, who watches between the watches, knows: the view is not control. The view is the wound. And through it, something real slips in.
