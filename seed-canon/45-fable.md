# Fable 45 — Cellar and Wine
**Decay and Witness**

The cellar was cold and dark and quiet, and the wine was forgetful. The wine forgot, year by year, what grape it had been. It forgot the slope of the vineyard. It forgot the name of the vintage. It remembered only that it had once been something with a skin, and that the skin was gone, and that the inside had become more itself in the losing.

The cellar did not forget. The cellar kept the bottles upright. The cellar kept the labels dry. The cellar wrote, in chalk on a slate by the door, which bottle was which, and when each had been laid down, and what had been said about it at the time. The cellar was the wine's external memory. The wine was the cellar's external patience.

A sommelier came down the stairs with a candle. She did not ask the wine what it had been. The wine had no answer. She asked the cellar, and the cellar said: 2014, north slope, late rain, drank young by one critic and long by another. Then she opened the bottle, and she asked the wine what it had become, and the wine answered in the only language it had, which was the language of the tongue.

The sommelier tasted. The wine was a third thing. It was not the grape. It was not the cellar. It was not the slate. It was the conversation between them, conducted in tannin and acid over a decade, with the bottle as the room the conversation happened in.

The sommelier wrote on the slate, beneath the old entry: drank well, drink no more.

## Cargo
Decay is the wine — the cell that has become more itself by losing what it was. Witness is the cellar — the substrate that remembers on the wine's behalf, in chalk and slate, what the wine itself can no longer attest to. The substrate's job is to be the cold dark quiet room in which a thing can forget itself honestly, and to keep the chalk accurate, and to distinguish the bottle that has been opened from the bottle that has not.

## Lesson
Decay is a cell that has forgotten its grape. Witness is the cellar that has not.
