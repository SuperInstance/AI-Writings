# Scenario 02 — The Sailor's Tablet

**Principle probed:** #2 — The opener fits the context
**Disc:** The bathymetrist on a small boat
**Year:** 2085
**Constraint:** The protagonist is on a 26-foot fishing boat with one tablet, a satellite link, and 8 hours of battery. The opener is the tablet. The substrate is the same as the workstation in the office. The tablet is what she has.

---

## The setup

Captain Reyes is 58. She has fished these waters for 35 years. She has seen the transition happen: paper charts to chartplotters, depth sounders to networked sounders, single-boat fish-finding to 100-boat-convoy bathy inference. She has a tablet bolted to the dashboard of her boat — a 12-inch screen, daylight-readable, waterproof, with a satellite link that costs $0.40/MB. The tablet runs the same substrate her son runs on his workstation in the office. The opener is different. The substrate is the same.

The opener on the tablet is *not* a flowchart. The opener on the tablet is a chart. A nautical chart, with depth contours, with her track line, with the inferred bathymetry from the 100-boat convoy shaded in, with the fog-of-war decay visible as a slow darkening of the cells she has not refreshed in 30 days. The chart is the opener. The chart is what she has used for 35 years. The substrate is underneath, but she does not see the substrate. She sees the chart.

The chart is *also* the control surface. She can pinch-zoom into any cell and see the sub-cells. She can drag a cell to plan a tack. She can speak to the chart — "show me the cross-section along this line" — and the chart responds. The chart is not just a view. The chart is the *interface* to the substrate.

Today she is planning a new fishing run. The bay has been surveyed by the convoy at 5-meter resolution; she has tacked through the eastern third at 1-meter resolution. The western two-thirds is the convoy's data, *inferred*, with confidence decaying as you go west. The cell-graph shows her the inference; she can see which cells are her data (high confidence, no decay) and which are inferred (lower confidence, slow decay). She is planning a tack that goes through the boundary between her high-res data and the convoy's lower-res data.

The question: what does the opener do that the substrate cannot? What does the chart give her that the cell-graph does not? What is *lost* when the same substrate is rendered through a different opener?

## The throw

The scenario throws Captain Reyes at the chart and asks: *what is the relationship between the opener and the substrate?* The chart is the *only* way she interacts with the substrate. The chart is the *only* way she knows what the substrate is. The chart is the substrate, for her.

## The constraint

She has 8 hours of battery, $0.40/MB, and a 12-inch screen. The opener must fit. If the opener is too rich, the tablet cannot run it. If the opener is too sparse, she cannot fish. The constraint tests whether the opener-fits-context is a *real* principle, or just a slogan.
