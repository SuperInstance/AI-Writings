# Scenario 06 — The Grandmother

**Principle probed:** #5 (Transparent-brush) — *probed from a different vector*
**Disc:** A 91-year-old grandmother who has never touched a computer but uses the substrate every day
**Year:** 2089
**Constraint:** The protagonist cannot read a screen. She has arthritis in both hands. She has not typed in 30 years. The opener is her *voice*. The substrate hears her.

---

## The setup

Esther is 91. She lives alone in a small house outside Olympia, Washington. She has not driven a car in 12 years. She has not touched a computer in 30 years — the last computer she used was a 1995 Packard Bell in her late husband's study, which she turned on once by accident and could not turn off. She has arthritis in both hands. She wears a hearing aid. She reads the newspaper with a magnifying glass.

What she *does* have, in 2089, is a small device the size of a deck of cards that sits on her kitchen counter. It looks like a small wooden box. It has no screen. It has one button. When she presses the button and speaks, it speaks back.

The device is connected to the substrate. The substrate holds her medical records, her calendar, her medications, her family tree, the temperature of her house, the contents of her refrigerator (sensors in the shelves), the schedule of the bus that takes her to the senior center on Wednesdays. The device is the opener. The opener is *her voice* — because her hands won't type, her eyes won't read a screen, her ears work but only with the hearing aid.

The substrate is invisible to her. The substrate is *the device that speaks to her*. The substrate is also the device that *listens* to her — when she says "I'm cold," the substrate raises the thermostat; when she says "I miss my daughter," the substrate calls her daughter; when she says "I can't remember if I took my pill," the substrate checks the pill bottle's RFID tag and tells her.

The question: what does it mean for the substrate to be transparent to a 91-year-old who has never seen a screen? What is the *substrate* when the user has no mental model of "substrate"? What is the *agent* when the user has no mental model of "agent"?

## The throw

The scenario throws Esther at a moment of crisis — a power outage, the device has lost its connection to the substrate, the house is dark, the thermostat is off, the bus schedule is gone. The throw asks: *what is the substrate to a person who has never known anything but the substrate?* The answer is the *transparent brush* in its most extreme form — the substrate is so transparent that its absence is the *first time the user knows it exists*.

## The discipline

Esther is not a metaphor for the user. Esther is the user. The user is a 91-year-old who has never seen a screen. The substrate is for her. If the substrate is not for her, the substrate is not for anyone. The constraint tests whether the substrate is *truly* transparent, or whether it is transparent only to people who already know how to use a screen.
