# Scenario 04 — The Producer

**Principle probed:** #4 — The view is the control surface
**Disc:** The TV producer with all camera angles at once
**Year:** 2083
**Constraint:** 200 cameras are recording a single live event. The producer sees all 200 at once. The cut is the question.

---

## The setup

The 2083 World Cup final. 200 cameras. 200 angles. 90 minutes. The producer — let's call her Anya — sits in the control room with a wall-sized display showing all 200 feeds at once, plus a substrate layer that infers the *next best shot* for every player on the field, every ball trajectory, every crowd moment. The inference is doing the work. Anya's job is the *cut*.

The substrate tracks every player's position, every ball trajectory, every camera's framing. It infers what the *next interesting moment* will be — the player about to shoot, the ball about to bend, the crowd about to erupt. It pre-renders the cut for each of those moments, ranked by confidence. The cut is the question. The answer is the inference.

Anya's opener is the wall display. She can see all 200 feeds at once, plus the inference layer. She can drag any feed to the program-out, and the substrate updates the inference for the next moment. She can speak to the substrate — "show me the next 10 seconds from camera 47" — and the substrate renders the pre-rendered-but-not-canonical shots for that window. She commits to a cut by *taking* it. Until she takes it, the cut is Schrödinger's cut — pre-rendered, inferred, but not canonical.

The substrate also lets her *zoom in* on any player. Zooming in shows her the sub-cells: the player's biometrics, the inference of their intent, the camera feeds that have them in frame. The zoom is a cross-section. The cross-section is a control surface — she can drag the cross-section to a different player, and the substrate re-renders.

The question: what does the producer *do* that the substrate does not? The substrate can pre-render every cut. The substrate can rank every cut. The substrate can zoom into any player. What is left for Anya?

## The throw

The scenario throws Anya at the 89th minute — the score is tied, the substrate's inference is converging on a shot, the cut is forming. The throw asks: *what is the producer's job when the inference is doing the work?* The answer is the *art* — the choice that the inference cannot make, the cut that the substrate ranks #7 but the producer takes because the rhythm demands it.

## The constraint

The cut is live. The cut is irreversible. Once Anya takes it, the feed is what the world sees. The substrate cannot un-cut. The constraint tests whether the producer's *art* is real, or whether the substrate has made the producer redundant.
