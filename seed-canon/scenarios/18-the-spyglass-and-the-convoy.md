# Scenario 18 — The Spyglass and the Convoy

**Fable form:** two objects in one image.
**The image:** A 17th-century sailor's spyglass, and a 2080s substrate-mediated fleet view. The spyglass is a *picture of one ship at a distance*. The fleet view is a *conversation with all ships at once*.
**The lesson:** The spyglass is for *seeing far*. The fleet view is for *being with many*. The fix is the convoy.
**Year:** 2084
**Constraint:** A captain in 1700 looks through a spyglass at a single ship. A captain in 2084 looks at a substrate-mediated display of 100 ships. The fable compares the two acts of looking.

---

## The setup

The spyglass is a 17th-century sailor's spyglass. It is a tube of polished wood and glass. The captain looks through it and sees a single ship on the horizon. The spyglass is a *picture of one ship at a distance* — the spyglass does not know what the other ships are doing, the spyglass does not know the ship's intent, the spyglass is a single point of focus. The spyglass is a *partner in seeing*, not a partner in *being with many*.

The fleet view is a 2084 substrate-mediated display showing 100 ships in a bay. The captain looks at the display and sees all 100 ships at once, with their positions, their headings, their inferred intents, their confidence levels. The fleet view is a *partner in being with many* — the fleet view knows the captain is looking, the fleet view updates as the captain's attention moves, the fleet view is a continuous conversation between the convoy and the captain. The fleet view is a *partner in being with many*.

The two are captains, three centuries apart. The 1700 captain *sees one ship*. The 2084 captain *is with many ships*. The convoy is the *medium* that makes the being-with-many possible. The fix: the substrate converts *pictures of one ship at a distance* into *partners in being with many ships at once*. The lesson: **a spyglass is a substrate convoy without a witness; the substrate is what makes the convoy a partner, not a lens.**

## The throw

Both are at the moment of decision. The 1700 captain decides whether to engage the single ship. The 2084 captain decides whether to engage the convoy's suggestion. The 1700 captain's decision is based on the spyglass's picture. The 2084 captain's decision is based on the convoy's inference. The fable is *not* about which decision is better. The fable is about *what the convoy adds* — the convoy's inference is the spyglass multiplied by 100, and the multiplication is logged.

## The discipline

The spyglass is not deprecated. The spyglass is *honest* in a way the fleet view is not — the spyglass is optical, the spyglass does not lie, the spyglass does not pretend to know the ship's intent. The fleet view is inferential, the fleet view can be wrong, the fleet view can be misled. The fable is *not* "old is bad, new is good." The fable is "the substrate is a new kind of honesty: the honesty of distributed seeing, where every ship's data is logged, and the captain can ask: 'why did the convoy suggest this?' and the substrate can answer with the 100 ships' worth of evidence."
