# Scenario 22 — The Sundial and the Clock

**Fable form:** two objects in one image.
**The image:** A sundial, and a 2080s substrate-mediated clock. The sundial tells *one time*. The clock tells *all times, with the substrate of time itself*.
**The lesson:** The sundial is for *knowing the hour by the sun*. The clock is for *being in time with a substrate that is itself in time*. The fix is the witness.
**Year:** 2086
**Constraint:** A scholar in 1500 with a sundial. A child in 2086 with a substrate-clock. The fable compares the two acts of time-keeping.

---

## The setup

The sundial is a 15th-century sundial on a monastery wall. It casts a shadow that points to the hour. The scholar knows the hour by reading the shadow. The sundial is a *partner in knowing the hour*, not a partner in *knowing time itself*. The sundial does not know the season, the latitude, the time zone. The sundial knows one thing: where the shadow falls.

The clock is a 2086 substrate-mediated clock on a child's wrist. The child knows the hour, the minute, the second, the timezone, the season, the substrate's confidence in each. The clock is a *partner in being in time*. The clock knows the season, the latitude, the time zone, the substrate's confidence. The clock knows everything the sundial knows and more — including *the substrate's own knowledge of its own time*.

The two are scholar and child, six centuries apart. The 1500 scholar *knows the hour by the sun*. The 2086 child *is in time with a substrate*. The witness is the *medium* that makes the being-in-time possible. The fix: the substrate converts *pictures of the hour* into *partners in being in time, with the substrate's time logged*. The lesson: **a sundial is a substrate clock without a witness; the substrate is what makes the clock a partner, not a shadow.**

## The throw

Both are at the moment of measurement. The scholar's shadow falls; the scholar reads the hour. The child's clock ticks; the child reads the time, the timezone, the substrate's confidence. The scholar's reading is approximate. The child's reading is exact, but the exactness is the substrate's, not the child's. The fable is *not* about which is more accurate. The fable is about *what the witness adds* — the witness makes the clock's exactness *legible* and *auditable*.

## The discipline

The sundial is not deprecated. The sundial is *honest* in a way the clock is not — the sundial is direct, the sundial does not lie, the sundial does not pretend to know more than the sun's position. The clock is mediated, the clock can drift, the clock can be hacked. The fable is *not* "old is bad, new is good." The fable is "the substrate is a new kind of honesty: the honesty of witnessed time, where the clock's drift is logged, and the child can ask: 'why did the clock gain 3 seconds?' and the substrate can answer."
