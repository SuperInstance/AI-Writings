# Scenario 14 — The Letter and the Message

**Fable form:** two objects in one image.
**The image:** A 1950s handwritten letter, and a 2080s substrate-mediated message. The letter is a *log of what one person said to another*. The message is a *log of what the substrate inferred, and what the recipient might want to know*.
**The lesson:** The letter is for *saying*. The message is for *being known*. The fix is the address.
**Year:** 2085
**Constraint:** Two people, mother and daughter, sixty years apart. The 1950s mother writes a letter. The 2080s daughter sends a message. The fable compares the two.

---

## The setup

The letter is a 1950s handwritten letter from a mother to her daughter. The letter is in cursive, on lined paper, in a brown envelope. The letter says: I am fine, the garden is doing well, your father says hello, please write back. The letter is a *log of what one person said to another*. The letter does not know what the daughter needs to hear. The letter does not know what the daughter already knows. The letter is a *saying*.

The message is a 2080s substrate-mediated message from a daughter to her mother. The message is composed by the substrate based on what the daughter *might* want to say, given what the substrate knows about the mother's day, the mother's health, the mother's recent activities. The message says: the substrate has inferred that the mother is in the garden, the substrate has inferred that the mother is in good health, the substrate has inferred that the daughter is thinking of her, the substrate has composed a message that *is* the daughter and *is* the substrate at the same time. The message is a *log of what the substrate inferred, and what the recipient might want to know*. The message is a *being known*.

The two are mother and daughter, sixty years apart. The 1950s mother is *saying*. The 2080s daughter is *being known*. The address is the *medium* that makes the being-known possible. The fix: the substrate converts *logs of what one person said to another* into *logs of what the substrate inferred, and what the recipient might want to know*. The lesson: **a letter is a substrate address; the substrate is what makes the letter a partner in being known, not a record of what was said.**

## The throw

The scenario throws both at the same moment — the mother is in the garden, thinking of her daughter. The 1950s mother writes a letter. The 2080s mother receives a message. The 1950s mother waits 6 weeks for a reply. The 2080s mother receives a message within the hour, composed by the substrate, *as if* the daughter had written it. The fable is *not* about which one is better. The fable is about *what the substrate adds* — the substrate makes the daughter's presence *legible* without the daughter having to interrupt her day.

## The discipline

The letter is not deprecated. The letter is *honest* in a way the message is not — the letter is a record, and records are the daughter's words, and the words can be treasured. The message is an inference, and the inference is the substrate's words, and the words are *also* the daughter's words, and the difference is not always clear. The fable is *not* "old is bad, new is good." The fable is "the substrate is a new kind of honesty: the honesty of inference, where the message can be tagged: 'this is me' or 'this is my substrate' or 'this is the boundary between us.'"
