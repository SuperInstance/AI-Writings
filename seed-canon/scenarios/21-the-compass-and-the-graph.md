# Scenario 21 — The Compass and the Graph

**Fable form:** two objects in one image.
**The image:** A magnetic compass, and a 2080s substrate-mediated graph. The compass points to *one direction*. The graph shows *every direction at once, with the strength of each*.
**The lesson:** The compass is for *navigating to one place*. The graph is for *navigating among many places, with the cost of each path*. The fix is the address.
**Year:** 2085
**Constraint:** A sailor in 1500 with a compass. A captain in 2085 with a graph. The fable compares the two acts of navigation.

---

## The setup

The compass is a 15th-century magnetic compass. It is a brass case with a magnetized needle floating in water. The needle points north. The sailor knows north. The sailor can navigate to one place at a time. The compass is a *partner in knowing direction*, not a partner in *knowing cost*. The compass does not know the wind, the current, the distance. The compass knows one thing: which way is north.

The graph is a 2085 substrate-mediated navigation display. The captain sees a graph of every possible destination, with the cost of each path (time, fuel, weather, convoy-availability, substrate-confidence). The captain knows *all directions at once, with the strength of each*. The graph is a *partner in knowing cost*. The graph knows the wind, the current, the distance, the conviction, the confidence. The graph knows everything the compass knows and more.

The two are sailor and captain, six centuries apart. The 1500 sailor *navigates to one place*. The 2085 captain *navigates among many places*. The address is the *medium* that makes the many-places possible. The fix: the substrate converts *pictures of one direction* into *graphs of every direction with cost*. The lesson: **a compass is a substrate address without a witness; the substrate is what makes the address a partner, not a needle.**

## The throw

Both are at the moment of decision. The sailor's needle points north; the sailor goes north. The captain's graph shows 12 possible destinations; the captain picks the one with the lowest cost and the highest conviction. The sailor's decision is simple and irreversible. The captain's decision is complex and revisable. The fable is *not* about which is easier. The fable is about *what the graph adds* — the graph's cost is logged, the graph's conviction is logged, the captain's decision is auditable after the fact.

## The discipline

The compass is not deprecated. The compass is *honest* in a way the graph is not — the compass is mechanical, the compass does not lie, the compass does not pretend to know the cost. The graph is inferential, the graph can be wrong, the graph can be misled. The fable is *not* "old is bad, new is good." The fable is "the substrate is a new kind of honesty: the honesty of auditable navigation, where every path's cost is logged, and the captain can ask: 'why did the graph prefer this path?' and the substrate can answer with the 12 destinations' worth of evidence."
