# Scenario 08 — The Regulator

**Principle probed:** #1 (Agent-as-compiler) — *probed from a different vector*
**Disc:** A regulator in a hearing room, trying to write a law about "what is a compiler"
**Year:** 2082
**Constraint:** The protagonist is a 60-year-old lawyer who has been asked to draft a regulation for the substrate. She does not understand the substrate. She does not understand the agents. She does understand *jurisdiction*, *liability*, *definition*. The throw is whether a regulator can write a law for a system she does not understand.

---

## The setup

Judge Maria Santos is 60. She has been on the Federal Trade Commission for 12 years. She has regulated banks, telecom companies, airlines, and pharmaceutical companies. She has never regulated a *substrate*. She does not know what a substrate is. She knows what a *compiler* is — sort of, in the way most lawyers in 2082 still do, which is to say, in the way most lawyers in 1992 knew what a "computer program" was, which was *barely at all*.

She has been asked to write the *Substrate Accountability Act of 2082*. The Act is supposed to define: who is liable when a substrate-driven agent causes harm; who owns the data the substrate holds; who has the right to inspect the substrate; what *transparency* means for a system whose core property is *transparency to its users*.

The hearing room is full of experts. The agents. The substrate engineers. The civil liberties lawyers. The industry lobbyists. They all use words Judge Santos does not understand. They talk about *convoys*, *fog-of-war*, *Schrödinger cells*, *tensor encoding*, *the watch*. They use metaphors she cannot parse.

The question: can a regulator regulate a system she does not understand? Can a *jurisdiction* be written for a *substrate*? The throw probes the *limits of law* in a world where the substrate is the lower layer of everything.

## The throw

The scenario throws Judge Santos at the moment of decision — she has to write the definition of "agent" in the Act. Every expert has a different definition. The substrate engineers say the agent is the *combination* of the user, the substrate, and the model. The civil liberties lawyers say the agent is the *user*. The industry lobbyists say the agent is the *company that built the model*. Judge Santos has to *choose*. Her choice will define liability for the next 50 years.

## The discipline

The constraint is *epistemic*. Judge Santos does not understand the substrate. She cannot understand the substrate in the time she has. She has to make a decision anyway. The throw tests whether the substrate can be *legible to a non-technical regulator* — or whether the substrate has become so fundamental that no one outside the substrate-builders can regulate it at all.
