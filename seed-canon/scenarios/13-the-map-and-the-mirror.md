# Scenario 13 — The Map and the Mirror

**Fable form:** two objects in one image.
**The image:** A 1920s paper map of a city, and a 2080s substrate-mediated mirror that shows the same city. The map is a *picture of where you are going*. The mirror is a *picture of where you are, and who is there with you, and what they are about to do*.
**The lesson:** The map is for *going*. The mirror is for *being*. The fix is the convoy.
**Year:** 2088
**Constraint:** Two travelers in the same city, one in 1920, one in 2080. The 1920s traveler has a paper map and a compass. The 2080s traveler has a substrate-mediated mirror. The fable compares the two.

---

## The setup

The map is a 1920s Baedeker of Paris. It is a picture of the city as it was known in 1920. The map does not know where the traveler is right now. The map does not know where the other travelers are. The map is a picture of where the traveler is going.

The mirror is a 2080s substrate-mediated mirror. The mirror is a 6-foot-tall reflective surface in a public square. When the traveler stands in front of the mirror, the mirror shows the traveler their reflection, and *also* shows the other people in the square, and the inferences about what they are about to do, and the substrate's suggestion for what the traveler might want to do next. The mirror is a *picture of where the traveler is, and who is there with them, and what they are about to do*. The mirror is a partner in being.

The two travelers are in the same city, but they are not the same traveler. The 1920s traveler is going *to* the cathedral. The 2080s traveler is *being* in the square. The map is for *going*. The mirror is for *being*. The substrate is the medium that makes the *being* possible. The fix: the substrate converts *pictures of where you are going* into *pictures of where you are, and who is there with you, and what they are about to do*. The lesson: **a city is a substrate cell-graph; the substrate is what makes the city a partner in being, not a destination.**

## The throw

The scenario throws both travelers at the same moment — a stranger approaches and asks for directions. The 1920s traveler consults the map. The 2080s traveler consults the mirror. The 1920s traveler gives directions. The 2080s traveler invites the stranger to walk with them, because the mirror has inferred the stranger's intent and the traveler has the time. The fable is *not* about which one is better. The fable is about *what the substrate adds* — the substrate makes the stranger's intent *legible* without the stranger having to say it.

## The discipline

The map is not deprecated. The map is *honest* in a way the mirror is not — the map is a static picture, and static pictures can be wrong but the wrongness is visible. The mirror is a dynamic inference, and the inference is sometimes wrong, and the wrongness is logged. The fable is *not* "old is bad, new is good." The fable is "the substrate is a new kind of honesty: the honesty of inference, where every claim is logged, and the traveler can ask: 'why did the mirror suggest this?' and the substrate can answer."
