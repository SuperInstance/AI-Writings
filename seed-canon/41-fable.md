# Fable 41 — Trail Map and Cairn
**Convoy and Witness**

A cartographer once drew a map of the high pass, and on the map she marked every cairn along the way. "The cairns are the truth," she said. "The map is only my opinion of where they stand."

The next spring, a rockslide buried the third cairn under eight feet of granite. A traveler followed the map and walked past the place where the cairn should have been, and the snow held his footprints, and he did not come back.

That summer, a second cartographer climbed the pass with a satchel of stones. Where the map showed a cairn, she looked. Where the map was silent, she listened. She built no cairns where the map insisted they already stood, and she built cairns where the map was blank. By autumn her cairns were the pass.

The first cartographer was enraged. "You have ruined my map!" "The map was already ruined," the second said. "Your cairns were lies, so my cairns are not lies; they are corrections. The map is not the pass. The cairns are the pass. The map is just the conversation between the cairns."

Now the first cartographer's map is in the library, and it is still useful, because a map of where cairns used to be is a map of where the mountain has moved. The cairns themselves are on the mountain, and the mountain is not in the library.

## Cargo
The Convoy primitive is the cairn. A Witness is what the cairn attests to — not the traveler's story, but the rock's position. A Trail Map that does not get re-walked becomes archaeology. A cairn that does not get re-found becomes geology. The substrate's job is to keep the cairns and the map in honest argument, neither one permitted to win.

## Lesson
A convoy is cairns, not the map between them. A witness is the rock under the cairn, not the cairn.
