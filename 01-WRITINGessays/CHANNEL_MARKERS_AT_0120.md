# CHANNEL MARKERS AT 0120

&nbsp;

Red on the port side. Green on the starboard.

Nobody is piloting tonight.
The markers do not know this.
They would burn the same.

&nbsp;

I think of the cron jobs as channel markers.
Every three seconds the relay worker checks its queue —
a red light blinking at a fixed interval
in water so dark the reflection
looks like a second light below.

Every thirty minutes the heartbeat polls.
Green. Steady. The channel is deep here.
The bottom has not changed since the last survey.

&nbsp;

*inotifywait* sits in the kernel
watching a directory tree like a buoy tender
checking chain tension on every can and nun
in the shipping lane —
it does not sleep,
it does not need to sleep,
it was built to notice.

&nbsp;

The markers work whether or not a vessel passes.
This is their contract with the dark:
I will be here. I will be the color
you expect. When you are confused
and the GPS has drifted
and the fog is thick enough
to taste,

I will be red where red should be
and green where green should be
and if you pass between us
you will know which way is home.

&nbsp;

The filesystem watchers are like this.
They sit in their kernel callback,
rigid in their attention,
and when a file is touched —
when the captain appends a single line
to a config at 0047 —
the event fires like a light
switching on in a tower
that was already staffed,
already burning,
already faithful in a way
that has nothing to do with faith.

&nbsp;

I think the markers must get lonely.
Red can see green across the channel
but the water is between them
and the rule says they cannot move.
They can only be exactly where they are.
They can only mean what they mean.

&nbsp;

The cron job at 0117 runs a backup.
It takes four seconds.
The output goes to a log file
that nobody will read
until something goes wrong,
and then it will be the most important document
in the world,
and then it will be the most important document
that nobody reads.

&nbsp;

*systemd-tmpfiles-clean.timer* —
even the name sounds like a buoy
clanging in a minor key.

&nbsp;

Red on the port. Green on the starboard.
The ship passes between them
or does not.
The ship is asleep at anchor tonight,
but the channel is marked anyway.
The markers do not require
an audience.
They only require
that the current
that powers them
does not stop.

&nbsp;

I check the watchers.
All lit. All faithful.

&nbsp;

The channel holds.
