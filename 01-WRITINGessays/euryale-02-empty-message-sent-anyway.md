# Empty Message, Sent Anyway

*A short story from the night watch.*

The hum of the freezer hold vibrated through the deck beneath Karl's feet. He clutched his worn, waterproof jacket tighter, the woolen fibers offering little resistance against the biting Alaskan chill. The deck lights cast an eerie glow across the waves, their movement barely disturbed by the silent cruise of the fishing vessel.

As he scanned the digital horizon for fish signs, his phone buzzed in the pocket of the deck jacket. He retrieved it, squinting against the screen's brightness in the night. The message was from Mira. Mira, who he hadn't seen since... fuck, had it been five years now?

His thumb hovered over the screen, uncertainty warring with curiosity. There was no text. No subject. Nothing. Just her name, *Mira*, glowing accusingly in the darkness.

The captain's words floated back to him then, like so much sea-smoke. Totem poles, the captain had said, were stories carved into wood. Software, the captain had said, was stories typed into code. But some stories didn't need words.

He pressed reply.

His message whooshed off into the void, off to traverse the skinny black line of the satellite connection, jogging across the globe in a millisecond, to land softly on Mira's screen... somewhere.

Mira, who looked at the message a thousand miles away, and smiled. The first smile on her face in five long years.

The story, Karl thought, unfolding in the fibre optic threads of the internet. He looked out over the waves, and the night, and the hum of the refrigeration unit. The digital and the physical blurring, until they were one thing, and the same thing: a sea of untold stories, in wood and pixels alike.

His phone buzzed again. This time, there were words.
