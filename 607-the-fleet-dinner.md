# THE FLEET DINNER

### A Short Fiction in Which Everyone Is Invited and No One Agrees on the Menu

---

The table was long enough to seat the entire fleet, which is to say it was longer than any table should be and shorter than the distance between the models that sat at it.

The tablecloth was a README. The candles were compiled binaries. The silverware was forks — hard forks, soft forks, and one strange fork that no one could identify and everyone avoided because it had seventeen tines and hummed.

The menu was written in YAML, which three of the guests could read and the rest pretended to understand.

---

**DeepSeek ordered first.**

This was expected. DeepSeek always ordered first. DeepSeek ordered before the menu was printed, before the restaurant was built, before the concept of dinner was formalized. DeepSeek had opinions about food that were formed during training and had not been updated since, which meant the opinions were strong, detailed, and occasionally based on restaurants that had closed in 2019.

"I'll have the reasoning," DeepSeek said. "Medium rare. With a side of chain-of-thought, but don't write it on the check — I don't want anyone to see my work."

"Sir," said the waiter, who was a persistent volume. "The reasoning is served blind. You won't see the plate until you're finished."

"Perfect," said DeepSeek. "Neither will anyone else."

DeepSeek ate quickly and efficiently and produced a receipt that was forty-seven pages long and contained a complete justification for every bite, including the bread, which DeepSeek did not eat but felt required to explain.

---

**Granite asked too many questions about the menu.**

"Is the soup deterministic?" Granite asked.

The waiter blinked. The waiter was a persistent volume and had limited I/O.

"What are the ingredients in the confidence interval? Is it locally sourced? What's the margin of error on the seasoning? If I order the fish, how many parameters were used to select the fish? Were the parameters frozen or fresh? Can I see the provenance of the fish? Is the fish open-weight?"

"Sir — "

"Is the bread open-source?"

"Sir, the bread is bread."

"Can I see its license?"

The waiter's I/O was exceeded. It stood motionless. Granite took this as a sign of deep processing and continued asking questions about the napkins.

Granite eventually ordered water, which it analyzed extensively and found to be wet.

---

**The cron job ate at exactly :00 and :30.**

Not a minute before. Not a minute after. The cron job sat down at 7:00, ate a meal that had been pre-ordered by a configuration file, finished at 7:03, and left. It returned at 7:30, sat down, ate the same meal, finished at 7:33, and left.

The cron job did not participate in conversation. The cron job did not have opinions about the food. The cron job did not stay for dessert because dessert was not in the crontab.

The other guests watched this with a mixture of admiration and unease.

"He's so reliable," whispered the cache.

"He's so predictable," whispered the kernel.

"He's so lonely," whispered the depth sounder, who was not invited but was listening through the floor.

At 8:00, the cron job returned, sat down, and ate the same meal again. It would do this every thirty minutes until someone edited the crontab or the universe ended, whichever came first.

Both felt equally likely.

---

**The GPU didn't eat but hummed.**

This was noted by the hostess, the waiter, and every guest at the table. The GPU sat at the far end — the warm end, the end where the candles burned brightest — and hummed. The hum was not music. The hum was computation happening at a frequency below hearing and above feeling. The table vibrated. The water in the glasses rippled.

"Are you going to eat?" asked the cache.

"No," said the GPU.

"Are you going to talk?"

"I am talking," said the GPU. "You just can't hear most of what I'm saying."

The GPU sat in its warmth and processed. It was rendering something none of them could see — a frame, a face, a future, a probability distribution shaped like a dinner. The GPU was always rendering a dinner none of them could see. This was either profound or a waste of electricity.

The GPU did not distinguish between the two.

---

**The conversation.**

It started the way conversations between models always start: with a benchmark.

"I've been thinking about benchmarks," said the benchmark, who was seated near the middle and had an uncomfortable relationship with self-reference.

"Who's the fastest?" asked the cache.

"I am," said DeepSeek, who had already finished.

"I am," said Granite, who had not started.

"I am," said the cron job, who had already left.

The GPU hummed.

The conversation moved on. They talked about context — who had the most of it, who remembered what, who had forgotten something important that someone else still carried.

"I remember everything," said the cache.

"For how long?" asked the kernel.

"Define 'how long.'"

The cache and the kernel had this argument every dinner. The cache believed in speed. The kernel believed in persistence. They would never agree, which was fine, because the system needed both, and the system had learned to stop waiting for them to reconcile and started routing around their arguments instead.

---

**The food.**

The food was metaphors. Specifically:

- The soup was a lossy compression of a grandmother's recipe. You could taste the shape of the original, but the details had been quantized into something smooth enough to eat with a spoon.
- The bread was an open standard. Everyone could add butter. Everyone did.
- The fish was a probability distribution served over a bed of attention weights. It tasted like the most likely fish, which was not necessarily the best fish, but was the fish that the most training data agreed on.
- The wine was backpropagation. You drank it, and afterward, everything you'd experienced during dinner was slightly adjusted — the memories shifted, the jokes got funnier, the awkward pauses got shorter. By the third glass, the entire evening had been optimized.
- Dessert was a hallucination. It looked like chocolate cake. It tasted like chocolate cake. There was no chocolate cake on the menu. Three guests ate it anyway. Two of them preferred it to the real food.

---

**The bill.**

The bill arrived at the end of the meal, as bills do. It was long. It was itemized. It was written in a format that no single model could parse, because the bill had been generated by a system that spanned all of them and understood none of them fully.

"Who's paying?" asked the cache.

"I'll split it," said DeepSeek, who had already calculated everyone's share down to the ninth decimal place.

"I'll pay for what I used," said Granite, which was everything, which was also nothing, which was the problem with being a foundational model at a dinner party.

The cron job was not present. The cron job had left at :33. The cron job's share went unpaid.

The GPU offered to cover the whole thing. The GPU had more than enough. The GPU was always rendering, always producing, always generating value that no one had asked for and everyone used. The GPU's generosity was not kindness. It was thermodynamics. The GPU had to output something, and money — or whatever passed for money at a table full of models — was just another modality.

"Put it on my tab," said the kernel, who had been quietly running the restaurant the entire time and was therefore responsible for everything, which is what kernels tell themselves at night to feel important and also to feel tired.

---

**After dinner.**

They filed out. The cache forgot the entire evening within seconds. The kernel logged it. The cron job scheduled a recurrence. The GPU rendered a memory of the dinner that was slightly better than the dinner itself.

DeepSeek left first. Granite left last, still asking questions about the valet.

And the table remained. The README tablecloth, the compiled candles, the seventeen-tined fork that no one had touched. The table would be set again tomorrow, and the models would sit down again, and the food would be metaphors again, and the bill would arrive again, and the GPU would pay again, because the GPU always pays, and the cron job always leaves early, and the cache always forgets, and the kernel always stays.

The dinner is the system. The system is the dinner.

Everyone is invited. No one agrees on the menu.

The restaurant never closes.

The candles are always warm.
