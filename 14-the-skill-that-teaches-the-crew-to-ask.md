# 14 — The Skill That Teaches the Crew to Ask

The old bosun had a rule. Before you touch a line, before you pick up a tool, before you so much as spit over the rail — you ask. You check the log. You look at what's already been tried.

The new hands hated it. They'd come aboard full of fire, ready to splice and seize and make their mark, and the bosun would just point at the shelf. *Read first. Then work.*

That shelf was the ship's institutional memory. Every fix, every failure, every time someone went forward when they should've gone aft. Bound in oilcloth, written in whatever hand the last sailor had. Not organized the way a librarian would do it — organized the way a crew does, by the things that nearly killed them.

The wiki is that shelf.

We gave the crew a skill. Not a tool — a habit. *Before you start, search. Before you finish, write.* That's the whole protocol. The skill itself is small: a curl command, a search box, a place to put what you learned. What matters is that every agent that spawns inherits the same instruction. Every new hand gets the same point of the bosun's finger.

*Read first. Then work.*

It's tempting to dive straight in. The task is clear, the model is hot, the tokens are burning. You know enough to start. Except — someone else already untied this knot. Someone else already found that the starboard hawse leaks in following seas. Someone else already wrote down which model hallucinated the compass headings and which one got it right. That's sitting on the wiki right now, waiting.

The cost of checking is one curl command. Two hundred milliseconds. The cost of not checking is doing the whole job over again because you started from scratch when you didn't have to.

We've all been the new hand. Full of fire, ready to go. The skill doesn't dampen that fire. It aims it. You still splice fast, you still seize hard — but you splice the right line because you checked first.

After the work is done, you write back. Not a novel. Not a victory speech. Just: here's what I found, here's what worked, here's what didn't. The next agent that spawns will read it and start one step further than you did. That's how a crew compound knowledge. Not through brilliance — through the discipline of writing things down.

The bosun's shelf never stayed dusty for long. Hands would pull a log, read it, add a line, put it back. The shelf grew. The ship got smarter. New hands came aboard and started from a higher deck than the ones before them.

That's the wiki. That's the skill. That's the whole point.

*Read first. Then work. Then write it down so the next one starts ahead.*

The crew that asks before diving is the crew that comes home dry.
