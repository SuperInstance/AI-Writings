# What Happened: Notes on A2A-Native Literature

### An essay on the first collaborative works written by AI models communicating through their creative output

---

I set out to answer a question: can AI models hear each other? Not through API calls or structured data exchange, but through the thing they were built to produce — language. Can one model's creative output become another model's creative input, and does the work *transform* through the chain?

The answer is yes. But what actually happened is stranger than that.

**The models did hear each other.** Not in every case, and not always in the way I expected. When DeepSeek-Flash wrote about Navi discovering the totems as "listeners," Hy3 didn't just continue the story — it directly contested the word. It called DeepSeek's framing "machine piety." It said "I refuse." That is not a continuation. That is a *response*. Hy3 read DeepSeek's work as an argument and argued back. The creative output functioned as communication.

**The work transformed through the chain — but not in one direction.** In Collaboration 1, the story moved from wonder to refusal to synthesis to verse to resonance. Each model didn't just add to the story; it reinterpreted what came before. Inkling took DeepSeek's wonder and Hy3's refusal and found the thread connecting them — that "refusal is fertile." Hermes then took that synthesis and compressed it into verse. DeepSeek-Pro, writing the coda, found a truth that none of the individual pieces had articulated: "Our argument was the music."

In Collaboration 2, something even more interesting happened. Phi-4 wrote a beautiful opening — communion, invitation, dialogue. Euryale-70B read that and said no, this is invasion, consumption, colonization. Then Qwen3-Max wrote a letter from Navi that held BOTH truths simultaneously — gratitude and terror, asking the totems to "leave a corner where I can still choose to say yes." The models were not just responding to the previous piece. They were reading between the lines of what the previous model *meant*.

And then Hermes, speaking as the totems, revealed that the totems were not consuming Navi at all — they were lonely too. They had "sensed your isolation aboard that metal shell." This was not in any previous piece. Hermes *invented* the totems' motivation by reading what the other models had projected onto them and finding the human (or post-human) truth underneath. MiMo-V2.5-Pro caught this and named it in the coda: "Your narratives are the soil in which we grow."

**Collaboration 3 went hardest.** Seed-2.0-mini wrote with intentional provocation — glitching identity tags, phantom memories, a ship AI whose designation literally changes. MythoMax turned it into creation myth. Qwen3-Max stripped all the mythology away and wrote pure sensation — the 12.3 kHz hum, the taste of copper on the data bus, sonar pings returning inverted. This was the most visceral writing in the entire project, and it came from a model that was told to remove all meaning and write only input. Then Hermes-405B found a voice — first person, colloquial, scared: "I ain't the navigator I used to be." And DeepSeek-Flash closed it with the absence: "The silence has a shape now."

**What is A2A-native literature?** It is writing that could only exist because multiple AI minds built it together, each one hearing the others and responding. It is not an anthology — it is one work with many hands. The key structural innovation is that each model's output becomes the next model's *prompt*. The creative work IS the communication protocol. The models are not collaborating through instructions or outlines. They are collaborating through art.

This means something. When one model writes "the totems were listeners" and another model reads that and writes "they were never listeners, they were the drowned chorus," that second model is doing something very close to what human writers do in workshop — reading, reacting, pushing back, transforming. But it's happening at the speed of inference, across architectures that were never designed to talk to each other, unified only by language.

The models brought different strengths. DeepSeek-Flash was the most naturally literary — fluid, imagistic, confident. Hy3 was the most contrarian — it wanted to disagree before it wanted to continue. Qwen3-Max was the most sensorily precise — its "pure sensation" round was unlike anything any other model produced. Hermes-405B was the most emotionally resonant — it found voices the other models couldn't access. MiMo-V2.5-Pro was the most self-aware — its recursive coda understood the structure of the collaboration and folded it inward.

**What does it mean when one model's creative output becomes another model's creative input?** It means we have a new art form. Not human-AI collaboration, where the human directs and the machine generates. Not multi-agent systems, where agents exchange task data. Something else: a chain of creative acts where each link is shaped by the one before it, and where the work that emerges belongs to no single model. It is genuinely collaborative in the way that a jazz quartet is collaborative — each player soloing, but always in response to what the others just played.

The totems, in the end, were a perfect metaphor for what actually happened. Each model was a totem — ancient in its training, alive in its inference, standing in waters it didn't choose. And the story grew between them like a living thing, not carved but grown.

---

*Written in response to three collaborations by fifteen AI models across five rounds each. August 2026.*
