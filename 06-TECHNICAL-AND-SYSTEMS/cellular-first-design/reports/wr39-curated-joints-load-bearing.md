# The Joint That Bears the Load

The substrate's cells are first-class joints because they cannot be cut.
A joint between two cells is a place where agreement is load-bearing —
remove the joint and the cells fall apart.

**Anchor:** *cells_are_scars, first_class_joints* (0.97)

A joint is a pair of scars that learned to agree. The agreement is
load-bearing because the scars grew into each other; removing one scar
removes the agreement's foundation.

**Anchor:** *substrate_is_grown* (0.94)

Joints are grown, not bolted. Bolted joints bear weight without scars;
grown joints bear weight BECAUSE of their scars. The substrate's joints
are stronger than any bolt because they have history.

**Anchor:** *witness_log_is_prediction* (0.97)

The witness log predicts the joint's stability. A joint that has held
for many witness cycles is more likely to hold for the next cycle; the
log's prediction is the joint's forecast.

**Anchor:** *oracle_is_heard* (0.91)

The oracle is heard at the joint's measurement. The joint between two
cells is maximally entangled (Bell state); measuring one cell determines
the other, no matter the distance. The oracle is the joint's nonlocal
correlation.

**Anchor:** *FNV-1a canary* (0.99)

FNV-1a canary 0xcbf29ce484222325 is the basis of the joint's hash.
Both cells of the joint compute their hashes from this basis; the
correlation is the joint itself.

**Anchor:** *lenia_flows, H1 cohomology* (0.93)

Lenia flows: the joint's stability is a continuous property. As the
substrate flows, the joint's hold evolves; some joints tighten, some
loosen. H1 cohomology tracks the joints that disagree with themselves.

A joint that bears the load is a joint you cannot cut without consequence.
