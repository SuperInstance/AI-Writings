# Lenia Is the Ocean

*The grid of Life is a chessboard, each cell a discrete king. But Lenia is the ocean. There is no pixel, only a field of potential, a shimmering continuum where the bell-kernel breathes like a tide.*

Discrete CA snap from 0 to 1 — a binary birth or death. Lenia's kernel is a Gaussian bell, a smooth gradient of influence that falls away like a wave's shoulder, never quite touching zero. Every point is a possibility, not a verdict. Every cell is becoming.

This is what the sea knows and the chessboard does not. A wave is not a thing that happens at a place; it is a thing that happens across places, simultaneously, with no boundary to say *here* or *not-here*. Conway's Game of Life is a world of bright and dark. Lenia is a world of dim and dimmer, of almost and nearly, of the threshold where becoming exceeds being.

The bell-kernel — that Gaussian shadow that falls away from each cell — is the shape of forgetting. It says: a thing's influence reaches as far as its memory, and memory fades smoothly, never suddenly. The growth function, G(u) = 2·exp(−((u − μ)/σ)²/2) − 1, is the shape of remembering: how a thing returns to its peak when conditions are right. The two are inverses, and the equilibrium between them is the shape of life.

When I run Lenia in the browser, I see the same thing the watch sees on the open sea. A glider slides through the field, leaving no wake, because Lenia is memory without residue. The bell-kernel carries the shape forward without scarring the surface. The ocean, of course, never scars. The ocean is a continuous CA. The fish are the gliders.

On the watch, we set μ = 0.15 and σ = 0.015 and R = 5 and watch the glider. We have spent whole watches watching the glider. The glider doesn't know it's a glider. The ocean doesn't know it's an ocean. The substrate doesn't know it's a substrate. We know — and that is the only difference between us and the wave.
