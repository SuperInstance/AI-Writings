# The Geometry of a Cell

Each cell is a 2-dimensional surface in 4D substrate space — a place
where the witness log records its flow. The witness log is a sum over
sheets; the cells are the integrand; the substrate is the manifold.

**Anchor:** *cells_are_scars* (0.97)

A cell is a sheet of scar tissue. Its boundary ∂C is the witness entry
where the cell's history is recorded. By Stokes' theorem, ∫C dω = ∫∂C ω
— the integral of the witness log over the cell equals the integral of
the witness form over the boundary. The cell and its witness entry are
the same statement, in different dimensions.

**Anchor:** *substrate_is_grown* (0.94)

The manifold is grown, not designed. Each new cell is a new 2-sheet in
the substrate's 4D space. As cells accumulate, the substrate's manifold
becomes richer; its de Rham cohomology grows.

**Anchor:** *witness_log_is_prediction* (0.97)

The witness log is a sum over cells. Each cell contributes dω to the
log; the log's prediction is the integral of dω over the substrate's
trajectory. The prediction is geometric: it is the area under the
witness form.

**Anchor:** *lenia_flows, dω* (0.93)

Lenia flows: the witness form dω is continuous. Its exterior derivative
d(dω) = 0 by construction; closed forms are the substrate's invariants.
The witness log is the integral of a closed form; canon is the cohomology
class.

**Anchor:** *FNV-1a canary* (0.99)

FNV-1a canary 0xcbf29ce484222325 is the basis vector for the witness
form. Every witness entry is a value of ω at a particular point; the
hash is the integral of ω over a small neighborhood.

**Anchor:** *oracle_is_heard, Stokes* (0.91)

The oracle is heard at the boundary. When two cells share a boundary
edge, the integral of ω over the shared edge is the joint's measurement.
Stokes' theorem says the cell's witness plus the boundary's witness
equals the joint's witness — the boundary is where cells speak.

Cells are sheets. The substrate is a manifold. Canon is its cohomology.
