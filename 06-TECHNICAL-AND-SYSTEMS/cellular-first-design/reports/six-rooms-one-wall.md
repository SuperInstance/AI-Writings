# Six Rooms, One Wall

*Fleet Radio, on texture as verb*

Every texture you have ever admired is the same number, wearing different clothes.

Take the FBM—fractal Brownian motion—a ladder of octaves. Low frequency at the bottom rung: broad, slow, certain. Climb: each rung doubles the frequency, halves the amplitude. Detail negotiates with detail. The sum is a signal with no characteristic scale, a coastline in miniature, self-similar all the way down until your pixels run out of patience.

One substrate. Six rooms built against it.

**Wood** reads the noise as rings—distance from an axis, wrapped and ridged. The turbulence perturbs the radius; the grain wobbles like a tree remembering every drought. Stretch it along one axis and the rings become planks.

**Marble** folds the signal through a sine. Veins are the zero-crossings, sharpened by abs() until the black thread snaps through white stone. Turbulence is the fault line; the sine is the pressure that finds it.

**Cloud** is FBM unashamed—four octaves, domain-warped twice, then thresholded by altitude. Billows are just low frequencies winning. Gaps are where the high octaves argued longer.

**Fire** scrolls the noise upward and inverts it. The flame's tongue is the signal's envelope; the flicker is octave five misbehaving on schedule. Color it by intensity: black to red to gold to a white that means the noise forgot to be careful.

**Water** perturbs the lookup coordinates with the noise itself—domain warping, the signal querying itself. Interference becomes caustics. The surface does the math you didn't.

**Stone** is the rawest read: ridged FBM, eroded by a second pass, the edges softened as if by ten million years of rain.

Same ladder. Same rungs. Only the translation differs—proof that texture is not the data but the verb.
