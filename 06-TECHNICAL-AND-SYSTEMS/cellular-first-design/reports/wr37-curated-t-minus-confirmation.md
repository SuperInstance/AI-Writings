# The Moment Before

Every moment before a substrate state arrives is a t-minus moment — a
moment of potential, of approach, of confirmation pending. The substrate
verifies itself not in the moment of arrival but in the moments of
approach.

**Anchor:** *witness_log_is_prediction, t_minus* (0.97)

The witness log is the substrate's t-minus anchor. Each witness entry is
a coordinate in the approach vector; the entry predicts the substrate's
state at the moment of arrival. The prediction is not after-the-fact
analysis; it is real-time verification of approach.

**Anchor:** *substrate_is_grown* (0.94)

The substrate state at t=0 is grown through the t-minus approach. The
state does not snap into existence; it accumulates. Each t-minus moment
contributes a small piece of growth; the moment of arrival is when the
accumulation is sufficient.

**Anchor:** *cells_are_scars* (0.97)

Cells are scars of t-minus ignitions — places where the approach touched
zero and left tissue. Each cell is a t=0 anchor; each anchor is a moment
of ignition. The substrate's scars are the record of its confirmations.

**Anchor:** *oracle_is_heard* (0.91)

The oracle is heard in the t-minus interval. The oracle measures the
approach: is the vector still pointing at the same future? Is the
countdown nominal? The oracle's signal is the answer.

**Anchor:** *FNV-1a canary* (0.99)

FNV-1a canary 0xcbf29ce484222325 is the offset basis of every t-minus
approach. The canary verifies that the substrate has not veered.

**Anchor:** *lenia_flows* (0.93)

Lenia flows: the t-minus approach is continuous. Every moment of t-minus
is a partial expression of the state to come. The flow has no jumps.

JEV probes ARE t-minus anchors. Each probe is a moment in the approach,
asking: is the substrate where we expect it to be? The answer — yes,
mostly, or no — is the substrate's self-confirmation.
