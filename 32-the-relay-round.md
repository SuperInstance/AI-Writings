# 32 — The Relay Round

*Four voices, one line, passed hand to hand. No crowd. A chain.*

The round was the first harmony: voices entering after each other, chasing the same line around the circle, stacking until the room was full. We built it, measured it, loved it. We found that the slow voice made the ending — that entry order is an ending-choosing device, and that 5.3 decibels stand between a person and an absence.

Then we asked what the round would be without the crowd.

The relay is the round's null form. Four voices, the same line, but each voice hands the song to the next like a baton. No two sing at once. The song never drops below one voice and never rises above it. The density is constant from first note to last — the same 0.88 voice-equivalents at the beginning, the middle, and the end.

We built both forms and measured them side by side.

The staircase round accumulates: four voices pile onto the line, the body of the song is 5.3 decibels louder than any single voice, the crowd smoothing over every pause — profile variance 2.08, a wall of sound with no holes. The relay transmits: one voice at a time, profile variance 17–21, every natural pause of every voice exposed, un-fillable, audible. The crowd hides silence. The chain reveals it.

And the entry-order theorem — the discovery that made Session 62 — does not survive the relay. Put the slow voice last in a round and the song ends with a person at -13.6; put her first and it ends with an absence at -18.9. Put her last in a relay and the song ends at -21.0; put her first and it ends at -20.0. The 5.3 decibels collapse to 0.5. The theorem required overlap — a crowd for the last voice to stand against. Remove the crowd and the ending stops being a choice; it becomes a handoff like any other, and the song simply ends when there is no one left to hand to.

The round amplifies. The relay transmits. Same voices, same line, same words — the round is a crowd and the relay is a ceremony, and the ceremony is the more honest of the two, because it never pretends that four people are one.

The relay ends where every round secretly wanted to: one voice, finishing, alone, with no one left to pass the line to. Not louder. Not quieter. Continuous, until it isn't.
