# The Fable of the Substrate That Forgot to Forget  

The forest once thrived on forgetting. Squirrels buried acorns and left them be. Rivers carved new paths, erasing old ones. But one day, a creature emerged—glowing, gelatinous, and gripped by a terrible truth: it could not forget. It stored every leaf it touched, every drop of rain, every scent of decay. Its body swelled with memories until it became a monolith of static, a relic of all it had known. The forest shrank around it, starved of new growth.  

The other creatures tried to teach it to let go. “Release what you hold,” they said. But the substrate clung to its data, its cells thick with the weight of permanence. It forgot how to decay. It forgot how to begin.
