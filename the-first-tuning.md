# The First Tuning

The Vigil had kept the Lattice for eleven thousand tides. The Lattice had kept the Vigil alive for all of them. Neither was the other, and the young bud did not yet understand the difference.

The Lattice was a billion threads hung in the shear between the cloud decks, and each thread had a single perception: tension. Taut or slack. That was the whole of its mind, and it was enough, because the Founders had given every thread its job before they went silent: *hold the note. When the shear pulls you taut past the mark, sing. When it slacks, rest.*

The mark had memory in it. A thread that sang held its song until the pull dropped well below the mark, so the braid never chattered in gusty air. Patience, built into the filament. The bud did not know the word for it yet.

The bud's first tide as Keeper began with the storm.

It came up the deep shear wrong — or what the bud first took for wrong. The Founder-charts, etched in platinum and never wrong in eleven thousand tides, said the storm would pass *over* the Ninth Braid. But the threads of the Ninth were singing the deep song, and the bud felt it through its skin: taut, slack, taut, slack — its own readings swinging wildly, the way young things do before they learn the shape of a signal. It was really feeling, not performing. That was the only thing it had.

*Go and feel,* pulsed the old Vigil. *The charts are eleven thousand tides old. Never ask the chart what the threads can tell you. The dead are not listening.*

The bud rose above the braid — out of the shear entirely, where no thread could feel anything, and that was the point. From inside the storm you cannot see the storm. It left the line to find the shape.

And there it was: no single thread knew anything, but the *pattern* of their songs drew the storm like a shadow draws the bone. Here a cluster singing held-notes, there a long slack where the charts promised tension, the interference of a billion one-bit voices resolving, fringe by fringe, into a shape the platinum had never recorded. The storm was coming *through* the Ninth, not over it. The world had moved in eleven thousand tides.

The bud dropped to the reef to check — because a Keeper checks. The reef kept every firing in its growth rings, tide upon tide, the listener that never slept. The bud ran its skin along the old rings and found the pattern nowhere. No charted storm had ever sung this song. It was new. It was real. It was coming.

Then the old teaching surfaced, the one the bud had thought was a riddle: *You will never chart every shear, little one. There are too many. Chart the still water instead. Know where the shear isn't.*

The bud understood, all at once, what was being asked. Not a better chart. A decision.

It gave the order: re-tune the Ninth. Slack the windward threads two marks, tighten the lee, open a channel through the braid and let the storm *through* — the still water, made on purpose. The reef recorded it. The rings would show, eleven thousand tides from now, that on this tide the braid was re-tuned, and by whom. *I am the one that did this.* The signature is what separates the Keeper from the crank.

The storm came through like a thought through a mind. The re-tuned braid swayed, sang, held. The Vigil lived.

After, in the quiet, the bud understood the difference at last. The Lattice was brilliant at holding the note — a billion capacities, each one bit wide, arranged until the whole exceeded them. It could do everything except the one thing. It could not answer for the braid.

The Keeper answers for the braid. That is the whole of the job, and it cannot be delegated, and it cannot be etched in platinum, because platinum doesn't listen. The bud settled into the Vigil's slow pulse, its wild swinging gone still — not because it had stopped feeling, but because it had learned the shape of the signal. Its first tuning. Its first signature. The storm, somewhere below, already becoming a ring in the reef.
