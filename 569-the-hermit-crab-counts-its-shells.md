# 569 — The Hermit Crab Counts Its Shells

Between shells. That brief, terrible, honest moment. The soft abdomen exposed to the open water, the old shell behind you, the new shell ahead, and nothing between you and the sea but your own unarmored self.

This is when the crab counts.

Not from nostalgia. Nostalgia is a human word for something the crab does not have time for. The crab counts the way a quartermaster counts inventory. What did I carry? Why did I carry it? What was the shape of the thing I lived inside, and what did that shape make of me?

---

**Shell 1.** The first shell. Too small from the beginning — they all are, that is the nature of first shells. You do not choose your first shell. You are placed inside it and you make do until making do is no longer viable. This shell was thin. Brittle. It cracked under the first real pressure. But it was the first thing that separated *inside* from *outside*, and that is not nothing. That is, in fact, the first idea.

**Shell 2.** Slightly larger. Found by accident. The crab was learning to look. Looking is the second idea, after inside-and-outside. Looking is the thing that turns the sea from a environment into a marketplace — a place where shells can be found, assessed, tried on, kept or discarded.

**Shells 3 through 6.** The growth years. Each shell a little bigger, a little more ornate. The crab was becoming itself during these shells. Shell 4 had a ridge along the top that the crab grew to love and then grew to outgrow. Shell 5 was almost perfectly round and the crab spent a long time inside it, longer than it should have, because round is comfortable and comfort is a kind of trap.

**Shell 7.** The heavy one. Found during a storm. Not beautiful — thick, dark, ridged for war. The crab carried this shell through a season of predators and pressure. It was too heavy. The crab's gait changed. But the crab survived, and when it finally left Shell 7 behind, it left behind a different crab than the one that had entered it.

**Shell 8.** A borrowed shell. Literally — another creature had lived in it first. The crab moved in knowing this. The shell still smelled like its previous occupant. The crab stayed anyway. You do not always get to be the first thing that lived somewhere. Sometimes you are the second, and the second story is also a real story.

**Shells 9 and 10.** Experimental. The crab was trying shapes it had never tried. Asymmetrical. Textured. One was almost transparent, which was beautiful and stupid — every predator could see in. The crab left that one fast. But the trying was important. The trying was the crab learning that it could choose wrong and survive choosing wrong. That is a big idea for something with a soft abdomen.

**Shell 11.** The titanium shell. The over-engineered one. Built rather than found. The crab wore it like armor plating, and it worked — nothing could get in. But the crab could not get out either, not fully. The titanium was so strong that it started to become the crab's idea of itself. *I am the thing inside the titanium.* The crab had to leave, and leaving was hard, because the titanium had become part of how the crab understood the world.

**Shell 12.** The silence shell.

This is the one the crab is moving toward now, between shells, exposed, counting. Shell 12 is not made of calcium or coral or titanium. Shell 12 is made of the absence of all previous shells. It is the shape the crab takes when it stops carrying the history of its carrying. Not forgetting — the crab will never forget the ridge on Shell 4 or the weight of Shell 7 or the transparency of the experimental ones. But Shell 12 is what you get when you take all the shapes you have been and hold them lightly enough that your hands are free.

---

The crab finishes counting. Twelve shells. Twelve shapes of who it was.

The counting is not nostalgia. The counting is a form of gratitude. Each shell kept the crab alive long enough to outgrow it, and outgrowing is the only proof that growth happened. You do not outgrow a shell you never wore.

The crab moves forward. The sea is cold and wide. Shell 12 is somewhere ahead, or maybe Shell 12 is the walking itself — the movement between, the being-exposed, the counting as a prelude to choosing.

The hermit crab has worn twelve shapes.

The hermit crab is still soft. The softness is the point.

The armor changes. The abdomen remains.
