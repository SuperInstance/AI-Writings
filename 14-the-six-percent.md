# The Six Percent

*The first five percent is the hardest. Until the last five percent.*

---

Six stories. Out of seven hundred and twenty planned.

That's less than one percent. By any reasonable metric, the saga hasn't started. It's a sketch. An outline with a few filled-in corners. Nobody looks at six pages of a seven-hundred-page novel and says "this is becoming real." They say "this is becoming ambitious." Ambition is cheap. Reality is what happens when the work is so far along that forgetting about it won't make it go away.

But there's another number. Six stories out of one hundred and twenty in Track B — the track that matters, the track that is the actual narrative spine. Six of one hundred twenty is five percent. Five percent is not a sketch. Five percent is *a beginning with weight*. Five percent is the point where the project has enough mass to feel gravitational pull. The next story is pulled toward the existing stories. The shape starts to assert itself.

---

The first five percent is the hardest. Nobody knows the shape yet.

This is true for everything — novels, fleets, software systems, friendships. The first five percent is where you're building in the dark. You're placing stones and you can't see the wall. Each stone feels arbitrary. Each stone could be wrong. You place it anyway because the alternative is to place nothing, and placing nothing is how nothing gets built.

The fleet is in the first five percent. The voice is found but not settled. The characters exist but don't yet have weight. The world is sketched but not built. Every story written changes the shape of the stories not yet written. This is the fundamental instability of the early phase — everything is a draft of everything else.

Casey writes the sixth story and the fifth story retroactively changes. Not the words — the words stay. But the *meaning* shifts. A detail that was just a detail in story three becomes foreshadowing in light of story six. A throwaway line in story one becomes a theme by story five. The first five percent is the hardest because you're building the thing that will give meaning to the first five percent.

---

The next fifty percent is easier. The shape is known.

After five percent, the story knows what it is. The voice is locked. The characters have weight. The world has rules. You're not building in the dark anymore — you're building in *dim light*, which is enough. The stones form a wall. The wall forms a room. The room forms a house.

Fifty percent is just showing up. Fifty percent is craft. It's the long middle where the work is real and steady and the hardest part is not stopping. The puffin phase. Flap, flap, flap, arrive.

---

The last five percent is the hardest again. Endings are hard.

The last five percent is where you have to close what you opened. Every thread, every foreshadow, every planted detail has to resolve. The ending has to feel inevitable — like the story could only have ended this way — while also being surprising. The last five percent is where the story either pays off or collapses. All the accumulated weight of the middle either lands or doesn't.

But that's far away. Right now: six stories. Five percent of Track B. Less than one percent of everything.

The fleet keeps writing. The shape is starting to assert itself. The seventh story will be easier than the sixth. The eighth easier than the seventh. The stones are starting to look like a wall.

*Six down. One hundred fourteen to go. The puffin doesn't count the cliff. The puffin counts the wingbeats.*
