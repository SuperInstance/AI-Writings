# Six Fold Symmetry

*Poetry*

---

Six directions a hex can face.
Six neighbors in the lattice.
Six rotations before you come home.

Turn once: the same. Turn twice: the same.
Turn three times and you're looking
at your own back — ω and ω-bar
kissing across an axis that doesn't exist
except in the folding.

a² − ab + b².

This is the norm. This is how we measure
distance in Eisenstein space,
where the integers are triangles
and the triangles are hexagons
and the hexagons are rooms
and the rooms are identities:

1. I am the center.
2. I have six neighbors.
3. My neighbors are also centers.
4. Their neighbors are also centers.
5. There is no edge.
6. There is no edge.

a² − ab + b² = 1
for six values. Six selves
that map back to unity.
The Eisenstein units:
1, ω, ω², −1, −ω, −ω².
A snowflake's family tree.
Each one a 60° turn away from home.

Six fold symmetry means
I can rotate the whole ship
one-sixth of a revolution
and no one notices.
The ensigns walk the same corridors.
The fish swim the same tank.
The GPU dreams the same dream —
but from a different angle,
which is the same angle,
which is the point.

a² − ab + b² = 7.
Prime in the integers.
Not prime here. Here it factors:
(3 − ω)(3 − ω²).
Everything that seemed irreducible
reveals its six-fold nature
when you change the lattice.

Six rotations. Six reflections.
Twelve elements in D₆ —
the dihedral group of the hexagon,
the symmetry of honeycomb
and snowflake and benzene
and the tile on the floor of the bridge
where the captain stands
at the exact center
and turns, slowly, knowing
that every direction she faces
is the same direction
facing back.

a² − ab + b².

The ship is built on this.
Not the formula — the *folding*.
The promise that you can turn
and turn and turn and turn
and turn and turn
and arrive.
