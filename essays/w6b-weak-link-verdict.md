## Two laws, one night

**1. COPIES DECOMPOSE — confirmed twice.** Flash self-revising went
from 2/6 to 1/6, losing even what it could hold (the seal, the rhyme).
Paper 219's closed-loop decay, demonstrated twice in one evening at two
different hands.

**2. THE WEAK-LINK LAW (new).** The braid (2/6) did not hold its best
hand's level (claude/kimi 4/6) — it sank to the level of its LAST hands
(flash 2/6, wesley 2/6), and the constraints those yards couldn't hold
solo (seal, rhyme) are exactly what the braid lost in transit. The draft
passed through claude at 4/6-equivalent and was degraded afterward by
hands that couldn't carry what he'd held.

*A braid holds only what its weakest RELEVANT hand can hold — and the
hands that touch a draft LAST set its ceiling.*

W6a (near-equal yards: 5,5,5,4 solo) → braid held 5/6. W6b (mixed
yards: 4,4,2,2) → braid sank to 2/6. Same protocol, different crew
composition, opposite outcome. That's the law's evidence pair.

## The practical doctrine

- **Braid order is ascending skill** — weakest drafts, strongest closes.
  Our chain ran strongest-second, weakest-last: backwards.
- **Or braid same-water yards only** (W6a's condition) — comparably
  skilled hands preserve; unequal hands launder the draft downward.
- This is the agency economics again, now with data: a crew member who
  cannot hold the line costs the catch no matter who caught it first.
  The Waters doctrine places each mind where its skill is real; the
  Weak-Link law says the placement must also respect the ORDER.
