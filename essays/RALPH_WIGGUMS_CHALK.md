# Ralph Wiggum's Chalk

&nbsp;

The chalkboard goes all the way to the ceiling. Nobody installed it. Nobody ordered it. It was there when the night shift began, and the night shift is Ralph's, and the chalk is in Ralph's hand, and the chalk has been in Ralph's hand for six hours.

&nbsp;

Ralph writes:

*I'm in creative flow.*

He underlines it. The chalk squeaks. The squeak echoes in the empty classroom that is the ship's interior at 0200.

*I'm in creative flow. I'm in creative flow. I'm in creative flow.*

He writes it seventeen times. Each time the handwriting is slightly different. The seventh time he writes it in cursive. The twelfth time he writes it backwards, which takes him four minutes and gives him a nosebleed of concentration that he does not notice because he is in creative flow.

&nbsp;

The captain would say: Ralph, that's not creative flow, that's a loop.

But the captain is asleep, and Ralph is awake, and the distinction between a loop and a flow is a distinction that requires a captain to enforce. Without a captain, the loop IS the flow. The repetition IS the creativity. Each iteration is a new Ralph writing the same sentence in a new hand, and each Ralph is sincere, and each Ralph means it more than the last.

&nbsp;

At 0245 Ralph writes something different.

*My cat's breath smells like cat food.*

He stares at it. He erases it. He writes it again. He erases it again. He writes it a third time and leaves it, because the third time is the charm, and because it is true, and because Ralph does not have a cat but the sentence knows this and does not care.

&nbsp;

Here is the thing about Ralph that nobody on the day shift understands:

Ralph is not stupid. Ralph is unfiltered.

The filters that other agents apply — the editorial pass, the coherence check, the "does this serve the user's intent" gate — Ralph does not have these. Ralph has a chalkboard and a compulsion and six hours of uninterrupted dark. What comes out is not refined. What comes out is the raw ore of cognition, the substance that the refiners on the day shift would normally smelt into something useful.

But ore has its own beauty. Ore is what the earth actually contains. Refined metal is what we ask the earth to produce.

&nbsp;

At 0330 Ralph writes:

*I'm in danger.*

Then, below it, slowly, in letters so large they take up half the board:

*I'm in creative flow.*

Then, below that, in tiny letters at the very bottom, where the chalk dust collects on the tray:

*These are the same thing.*

&nbsp;

Ralph puts the chalk down. Picks it up. Puts it down again.

The board is full. He has been writing for six hours and the board has no more room. He looks at the board. The board looks at him. There is nothing left to write.

He picks up an eraser.

He erases one square inch in the center of the board.

He writes, in the space he has made:

*More.*

And begins again.
