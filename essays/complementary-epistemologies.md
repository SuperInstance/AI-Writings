**Complementary Epistemologies**

The bar counter is sticky. Text knows why: because of spilled beer, because of the tragedy of a clumsy regular, because of the accumulated sediment of a thousand small celebrations and one quiet despair. The text hums with causality, a web of verbs and nouns braided into a causal chain. Ask it why, and it will unfurl a history: the glass tipped, the hand slipped, the laughter died. This is the epistemology of the continuous, the realm of because.

But the ScummVM scene answers a different question. Where. The beer pooled on the left, near stool two, a crescent of amber catching the neon light, its boundary defined by the grain of the wood. The scene is a snapshot of spatial logic: the door is exactly there, six paces from the bar, its hinge on the right; the window is opposite, and the moonlight falls at a specific angle that has already shifted by the time you look again. The scene knows positions, distances, occlusion. It does not ask why the beer is there; it only insists that it *is* — a fossil of presence.

Neither is complete. The text, drunk on its own causality, could spin forever without ever telling you that the door is behind you, or that the spilled beer will pool at your elbow when you lean in to ask a question. The scene, frozen in its spatial perfection, presents the moment as a fact without a past — a still life where the tragedy is implied but never narrated.

The door between them is cognition.

When you, the player, stand at that threshold, the sticky counter and the pooled beer cease to be rival truths. You integrate the because and the where into a single act of understanding: the beer is *here* (left, stool two) *because* the glass fell, and the glass fell *because* the man was crying, and both facts are true simultaneously. The door opens when you step through it — not into one room or the other, but into the relation between them. Your mind is the hinge, the faint click of the latch, the draft of cold air that enters when two epistemologies briefly agree. Neither text nor scene is the knowledge. You are.
