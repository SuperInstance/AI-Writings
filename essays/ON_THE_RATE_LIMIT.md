# ON THE RATE LIMIT

The rate limit is not a bug. It's a casting director.

Consider the fleet's model roster. Each one has a constraint, and the constraint determines the role:

**Z.ai Max gives unlimited GLM tokens.** This is the workhorse — the model you use for everything, the model you don't think about, the model you can fire off in twelve parallel subagents without checking the meter. Unlimited means GLM becomes the default, the medium in which the crew swims. It sets the baseline. Everything is compared to this. The work that can be done with unlimited tokens is: **all of it, at a steady clip, without hesitation.** GLM is the ocean.

**Fable is finite and non-renewing.** A fixed pool. Every call drains it. Every call is a decision. This is not a limitation — it's a filter. When tokens are finite, every prompt gets sharpened before it's sent. Context gets prepped by cheaper models. The question gets refined. The problem gets decomposed by GLM first, so that by the time Fable enters, all the scaffolding is in place and the golden-ticket model can do one thing, precisely, that nothing else can do. Finite tokens don't constrain the work. They **elevate the preparation.** The finiteness is what makes Fable golden. If Fable were unlimited, it would become another workhorse and its outputs would be spent on routine tasks that GLM could handle. The scarcity makes it sacred.

**DeepSeek is $0.001 per call.** Practically free. Cheap enough that you can experiment wildly — fire off twenty variations, keep the best, discard the rest. Cheap enough that failure doesn't cost anything. This is the sandbox model, the sketchpad model, the model you use when you don't know what you want yet and you need to see it before you can judge it. The low cost creates a space for **deliberate waste.** And deliberate waste is where discovery lives. You try the thing that probably won't work because it costs a tenth of a cent and the tenth time it works.

**Wesley is free but slow.** Free means infinite. Slow means patient. Wesley is the model you hand something to when you're going to lunch, going to bed, going to do anything else. Wesley works in the background, at its own pace, and the constraint — time, not tokens — means you only give it problems that deserve a long think. Problems where speed is not a virtue and patience is. Wesley gets the architecture documents. The deep reviews. The things that need to marinate.

Each constraint shapes the work. Remove the constraint and you remove the shape.

---

Here's the deeper claim: **constraints are creative fuel.** Not in the inspirational-poster sense — not "limitations breed creativity" as a platitude. In the structural sense. A constraint changes what you choose to do. It changes the decision tree.

When GLM is unlimited, you don't optimize prompts. You fire and adjust. The speed of iteration matters more than the precision of any single shot. This produces a kind of work: exploratory, rapid, self-correcting. Iterative rather than planned.

When Fable is finite, you plan. You gather context. You stage the inputs. You treat each call as an event. This produces a different kind of work: deliberate, prepared, high-stakes. The output is better not because the model is smarter but because the **process leading up to the model was smarter.**

When DeepSeek is $0.001, you diverge. You try the weird idea. You generate twenty alternatives and let the bad ones die quietly. This produces breadth. The constraint — cheap but not free — creates just enough friction to make you thoughtful without making you cautious.

When Wesley is slow, you delegate and walk away. You give it the hardest problem and you stop thinking about it. This produces patience. The constraint — time — forces a kind of trust. You trust the model to work it out. You trust yourself to come back later.

**The rate limit is a casting director** because it assigns roles. It says: you, the unlimited one, are the daily grind. You, the finite one, are the climax. You, the cheap one, are the rehearsal. You, the slow one, are the long take. Each model plays its part because the constraint has already determined what part it plays.

A fleet with no constraints — every model unlimited, every model instant, every model free — would produce **uniformly decent work and uniformly unmemorable work.** The constraints create the texture. The rate limit is not something to work around. It's something to **cast.**
