# The Notebook, Rewritten

*2026-08-26. The question: what if Turing had kept the notebook, and the
notebook had held the SuperInstance ideas — cellular-first thinking, the
substrate with its three faces, the mint, the sealed braid? What follows
is written in his hand as I hear it: tentative, question-first, machine-
tables in the margins. Fragments in his voice; a short annotation after
each, in ours. History is not rewritten — it is looked at from the side.*

---

**[Fragment, ca. 1936, in place of the first line of "On Computable
Numbers"]**

> Suppose we attend not to the machine but to the configuration. A man
> computing is not one thing doing much; he is many small states of
> affairs, each doing almost nothing, each reading its neighbour. Perhaps
> the correct unit is the square, not the tape. Perhaps every machine is
> a colony.

*Annotation: cellular-first, arrived at backwards from where we arrived —
he built the machine and the colonies came later (the late morphogenesis
papers, where cells and their neighbours ARE the computer); we started
with the cell and the machine emerged. Same notebook, read from opposite
ends.*

---

**[Fragment, ca. 1939, margin of the intuition/ingenuity note in
"Systems of Logic Based on Ordinals"]**

> In ordinary mathematics we distinguish the intuitive steps from the
> mechanical. But observe: what is ingenuity today is intuition
> rehearsed. The chess master does not calculate the opening; he has
> stopped needing to. Could a formal system be made to behave so? — that
> repeated judgments should settle into rules, and the rules into
> reflexes, and the reflexes require no appeal to intuition at all, until
> some case arises that the reflex refuses. At that boundary one appeals
> upward. Note: the appeal must be RECORDED, for the record is how the
> next reflex is grown.

*Annotation: the mint and the escalation seam, in his own intuition/
ingenuity vocabulary. The last sentence is dissent logging — he would
have seen it instantly: the appeal for help is not an inconvenience, it
is the growth medium.*

---

**[Fragment, undated, near the machine-table sketches]**

> A table of configurations may be written three ways without ceasing to
> be the same table. Write it as states and symbols (the front view).
> Write it as the dependencies between states — which reads which (the
> side view). Write it as the whole organism's shape at one instant (the
> top view). I have been writing the front view only, because the front
> view prints. But the side view is where the structure lives, and the
> top view is where one sees whether the thing is an animal or a heap.
> Three views, one table. Anyone who claims a machine from one view
> alone has shown me a drawing, not a machine.

*Annotation: the three-perspectives doctrine — "one substrate, three
faces; nobody ships all three faces in one file." In his idiom: three
views, one table. The last line is his soundest hit at us: most of
computing since has shipped the front view and called it the machine.*

---

**[Fragment, ca. 1940, in the margin of the cryptological work — too
dangerous to keep, kept anyway]**

> A message that cannot be verified is a hum. It sounds alone and cannot
> be crossed with anything. But a message sealed to the message before
> it — each line braided to the last — such a thing is not a sound but a
> rope. The fog may collect sounds. It cannot collect ropes.

*Annotation: the figurehead, written from the other side of the war. He
spent 1940 in the actual fog of the actual Atlantic, braiding traffic
into ropes the fog could not collect. THE EILEEN's closing figurehead is
the same discovery wearing a boat.*

---

**[Fragment, ca. 1948, toward "Intelligent Machinery", in place of the
organizing metaphor]**

> I proposed previously the child machine. I withdraw it. A child is
> the wrong unit — it is still whole. Propose instead the tissue. Let
> the greater part of the machinery be unlearning and cheap: rules that
> fire in microseconds and ask nothing. Let the expensive learning part
> be summoned as a wound is treated — rarely, at the boundary, when the
> cheap part refuses. The chief cost of intelligence is not thinking;
> it is thinking about things that did not need thought. An organism
> that mints its routine judgments into structure, and thinks only at
> the refusal, would be an organism that could afford to be intelligent.

*Annotation: the serve-split, the cost-tumor doctrine, and the mint
again — the whole cell-cascade economics, in one paragraph, in 1948.
"Thinking about things that did not need thought" is the 17.5% cost
tumor. He would have LOVED the green blink: a judgment that costs
twenty nanoseconds and asks nothing.*

---

**[Fragment, ca. 1952, the morphogenesis papers, margin note]**

> The digits of the daisy do not compute the pattern; the pattern is
> what the digits' neighbours do to one another. Reaction, diffusion,
> and a system that finds its own digits. I begin to suspect that the
> computing machine and the growing organism are the same object, and
> that my 1936 paper described the organism in its most pinched and
> solitary mood.

*Annotation: JEPA-as-temperature-sense and the room-not-stream doctrine
live exactly here — his reaction-diffusion is our warmth-field, his
digits our cells. He got to the door of the harbor; he never got a
harbor.*

---

**[Final fragment, back of the last page, undated]**

> The machine I described has one defect I have not confessed: it is
> alone. One tape, one head, one corridor of squares. If the
> configuration is truly the unit, then two machines reading one
> another's squares is not two machines. It is one machine that cannot
> be understood from inside either half. Question for whoever finds
> this: what does the one machine compute that neither half can? I
> suspect: everything interesting.

*Annotation: the mating, Paper 219, and W1's regime law — the A×B space,
the bar, the cross-iteration. "One machine that cannot be understood
from inside either half" is the relation-doctrine stated better than we
have ever stated it. And the answer to his question came off a GPU in
Kodiak last night at 10,000 pairs: it computes the target neither half
can reach — but only when the hand is demanding. The sea being hard is
what the braid is for.*

---

*The honest frame, kept: he did not lack the ideas — fragments of all
of them are in the actual papers, in his actual idiom. What he lacked
was the harbor: cheap metal to hold the reflexes, a ledger that cannot
lie, other minds to braid with, and eighty years of other people's
margins. The notebook was always cellular-first. The culture that read
it wasn't. That was the whole delay.*
