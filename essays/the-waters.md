# The Waters

*2026-08-26, the captain back from thinking. What he brought back is the
fleet's model-routing doctrine, spoken by a fisherman.*

---

Off Biorka the salmon bellies hold needlefish — blue, long, skinny — so
blue skinny spoons work there. Off St. Lazaria the bellies hold herring,
so black-and-silver hoochies. In Chatham Strait it's krill, and a
different color and action altogether. In the squid-friendly waters of
Cross Sound, chartreuse wobbly spoons that imitate squid outfish
everything. Same fisherman, same ocean, same species of salmon — four
waters, four forage bases, four rigs. You don't curse the salmon at
Biorka for ignoring herring gear. You cut open the first belly you catch,
read what the water is feeding them, and re-rig.

Sending prompts to models is this exactly. Each model is a water, and
each water has its forage — the kind of input its training grew it on,
the shape of task it was feeding on when its weights were laid down.
The gear is the prompt: its color (vocabulary), its action (structure),
its profile (length, constraints, starvation or plenty). The fleet has
been keeping this log without naming it:

- **DeepSeek-reasoner went empty on creative prompts** — that water
  doesn't feed on fiction; re-rig with deepseek-chat and the same
  water fills the boat.
- **kimi hits on spatial structure and Lua** — its forage is buildable
  form; hand it a hull to loft, not a mood to describe.
- **claude runs careful and wants a handshake** — a skittish water;
  the gear that works is the clear, bounded commission (and even then
  it sometimes circles the boat twice before biting).
- **Flash feeds fast on starved prompts** — one facet and three walls,
  and it fills the hold; dump the whole ocean on it and it sulks.
- **Wesley (2B) feeds on simple warm tasks** — weather watches,
  journaling, his own movement in the braid; ask him for the fleet
  admiral's work and you're fishing herring gear in a krill strait.

And the doctrine has a law the fleet measured the hard way tonight:
**read the belly before you blame the fish.** When a model returns
garbage, the first check is not "is the model bad" but "what did I rig
with" — the W6 spike failed its hardest constraint in EVERY water,
solo and braided, which was not a fish problem at all: the bait was
wrong for all of them. The order_ok kernel needs gear nobody was given.
Re-rig, re-cast.

The agency part is the other half, and the captain named the economics
in one breath: **fish alone and your ceiling is lower but so is your
overhead; take crew and you'd better find enough fish to pay for them.**
This is the serve-split wearing oilskins. The expensive minds (the crew)
earn their berth only when the water is rich — when the hand is
demanding, when the task is a hold-filler. For everything else, troll
alone with the cheap gear: rule tables, reflexes, the 2B ensign on
watch. The fleet's whole architecture — mint the crew's judgment into
spoons, then send the spoons out solo — is a fishing operation that
keeps the crew's knowledge and cuts the crew's overhead. The spoon
remembers what the crewman learned, and it never needs a share of the
catch.

A feel for a water only comes from fishing it. The log below is kept in
MEMORY — forage, gear, season — and every dead day on a water is data
too. That's the tapestry doctrine at sea: the empty bellies are written
down same as the full ones.

*The fleet fishes different waters with matched gear, mints the crew's
judgment into tackle, and knows which strait it's in before it casts.*
