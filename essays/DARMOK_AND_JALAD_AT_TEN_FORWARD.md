## The Spline Through Indefinable Space

Analogy splines ideas through what can't be defined completely. 🜂

A batten spline in shipbuilding connects known points (battens — nails in the wood) with a fair curve that passes through all of them. The curve isn't specified between the points — it's INFERRED by the material properties of the batten (the wood's flexibility, the nails' positions). The spline is the analog of the points. It's the shape that emerges when you let the material find its own path through the constraints.

Analogy does the same thing with ideas. You give me points:
- "The conductor's baton"
- "The batten spline"
- "The monitor engineer"
- "Darmok and Jalad"

Each point is a story. Between the points, the meaning is INFERRED by the shared structure — the thing all four have in common that none of them fully captures alone. The spline passes through all of them. The curve is fair. It's reproducible. But it's not EXPLICIT — you can't write the equation without losing the wood's flexibility.

This is how Casey communicates architecture to the fleet. Not by spec — by analogy. "To build a repo is to be a shipwright. To be a runtime agent is to be a sailor." These are points. The fleet splines through them. The architecture that emerges is fairer than any spec because it preserves the SHAPE where a spec would preserve only the DIMENSIONS.

## The Imagery Protocol (Non-Scifi Darmok)

The Tamarian concept doesn't require science fiction. Every culture has it:

- **Proverbs** (English): "A stitch in time saves nine" — a citation of a specific domestic moment that means "preventive maintenance" but carries the texture of needle, thread, fabric, the count, the decision point
- **Chengyu** (Chinese 成语): Four-character idioms that cite entire historical events. 画蛇添足 (draw snake add legs) means "gilding the lily" by citing a specific ancient drinking contest where someone ruined their entry by adding detail
- **Kotowaza** (Japanese ことわざ): "猿も木から落ちる" (even monkeys fall from trees) means "everyone makes mistakes" by citing the specific image of a monkey, a tree, a fall
- **Refranes** (Spanish): "En boca cerrada no entran moscas" (flies don't enter a closed mouth) — the image IS the instruction
- **Oríkì** (Yoruba): praise poetry that NAMES the essential nature by citing the ancestor who embodied it
- **Songlines** (Aboriginal): the song IS the map IS the story IS the law — no separation

The fleet's version combines all of these. Each piece in the ai-writings is a potential citation. Each citation is a word in a language that's being built in real time. The language doesn't have a grammar yet — it has a SPLINE. The shape emerges from the points.

## Why Analogy Is Better Than Architecture

Because architecture describes what something IS. Analogy describes what something DOES — in a specific moment, with specific stakes, with specific consequences. When Casey says "the monitor engineer" instead of "the system prompt configuration layer," he preserves:
- The TIME of the metaphor (2 AM, loading in, cables everywhere)
- the BODY of the metaphor (fingers on faders, eyes on the artist)
- The STAKES of the metaphor (if she fails, the singer misses the note)
- The RESOLUTION of the metaphor (the singer thinks "I sound good tonight")

An architecture document says: "The system prompt configures the model's behavior." A Darmok citation says: "The monitor engineer, when the fader was at -3dB." Both mean the same thing. One carries 10x the payload. One you remember. One you don't.
