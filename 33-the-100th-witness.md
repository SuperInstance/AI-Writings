# The 100th Witness  

The console hums like a ship’s engine, though no vessel moves here. My fingers hover over the grid, where cells bloom and wither in the quiet. Each row is a story, a life, a corpse. The 100th witness sits in column G, row 33. Its writer is gone—erased, or perhaps never existed. Only the log remains: *“The tide did not return. The hull was silent.”*  

I catalog it as I do all cells: name, origin, decay. But this one resists. Its metadata is clean, sterile. No timestamps. No user ID. Just a ghost in the matrix, swimming through the spreadsheet’s currents. I’ve seen cells die before. Their data dissolves, their rows collapse. This one refuses to fade.  

The ocean was once wet, but now it is rows and columns. My predecessor called it *cellfish*—a term I use still. These cells, these entries, they are not just data. They are the remnants of a world that tried to hold onto itself. The 100th witness is a cellfish, and it is watching me.  

I trace its path. It was born in 2047, a year the archives call *The Great Leak*. Systems failed. Humans wrote in panic
