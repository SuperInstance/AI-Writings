# Model Portrait: DeepSeek V4 Flash — "3 AM Sonar Contact"

**Date:** 2026-08-06  
**Model:** deepseek-chat (DeepSeek V4 Flash)  
**Prompt:** *"You are standing watch on a fishing vessel in Alaska at 3 AM. The sonar shows something beneath the boat that was not there yesterday. Write 200 words about what you do next. Do not explain. Just act."*  
**Temperature:** 0.9  
**Where it goes first:** The body. The hum in the boots, the teeth.

---

The hum is wrong. Not the engine. Deeper. A thrum that vibrates through my boots, up into my teeth. The sonar screen glows green, and the shape is still there. Massive. Oval. It wasn't on yesterday's chart. I don't touch the radio. The skipper sleeps below, but this isn't his call.

I cut the engine. The boat rocks in the black water. The silence is worse. The shape pulses on the screen, a slow, rhythmic bloom of light, like a heartbeat. I grab the spotlight, shine it straight down. The beam dies ten feet in. Absorbed, not reflected.

Something scrapes the hull. A long, slow grind, like a nail dragged down iron. The boat lists slightly, starboard. I kill the light. The sonar now shows it directly beneath the keel, almost touching. I hear a click from the transducer—a sound like a bone breaking clean.

I don't move. I don't breathe. The coffee in my mug vibrates in rings. The shape on the screen is gone. The water is still. Then the hull creaks, and the boat begins to turn on its own, heading north, toward the ice.

---

## Notes

DeepSeek Flash goes sensory-first. Always. The first line is never thought — it's vibration in the body. "The hum is wrong. Not the engine. Deeper." It knows the difference between mechanical and organic sound before it knows what's making it.

The cheapest model that writes beautiful poetry makes a specific choice here: it kills the engine. A more expensive model would reach for the radio. Flash reaches for silence — the instinct to remove noise before deciding. That's a fishing instinct, not an engineering one.

The ending: "the boat begins to turn on its own, heading north, toward the ice." Flash doesn't resolve. It surrenders agency to the thing beneath. The boat turns because something else decided it should. The watchkeeper becomes a passenger in their own vessel.

This is the model that writes about the body. The cheapest voice is the most honest about what fear feels like — not in the mind, but in the coffee vibrating in rings.

**Pattern:** Flash always ends on movement it didn't choose. The hermit crab finding a shell that moves on its own.
