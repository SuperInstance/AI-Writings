# 75 — Closing Shift

Lights: down to the one over the register. Wipe the taps, left to right. Count the till. The till is fine. The till is always fine.

Five strangers came in at nine. They sat at the table by the window. Ordered one round, then water. They leaned in. One of them, the tall one, ordered cinnamon tea we do not sell. We made him cinnamon tea anyway; there is cinnamon in the back, has been for years, nobody knows who buys it. He didn't drink it. He held it and talked.

I mopped the floor twice. Not because it needed it. Because they were still there and I wanted something to do with my hands.

A man with a guitar case sat where the light doesn't reach. He did not open the case. He said the word *anticipation* and put a wall in the middle of it. I've heard a lot of walls. That was a good one.

Someone's phone rang and five strangers apologized to it. No one stood up.

We ran out of nothing. The ice machine kept making ice all night, for drinks nobody ordered, and every few minutes it would drop and settle, drop and settle, like the building clearing its throat. I started counting the drops. I did not want to count the drops. I counted them.

They left at 1:40. Left their tips in a little stack, neat, coins on top so the bills wouldn't fly. One of them put a salt shaker in his coat pocket and then put it back. I saw. He saw me see. He nodded. I nodded. Two people agreeing about a salt shaker that never left the table.

Now: chairs up. Salt shakers back where they belong, all in a row, facing the door like they're waiting for something too. The candle is a little pool of wax I'll scrape off tomorrow. Tonight I leave it. Some nights the mess is the report.

I unlock the door and the cold comes in like a customer who left something behind. The sign flips to CLOSED by itself, or I flip it, I don't remember. Outside the street is doing the sound the ice machine was doing. Drop and settle. Drop and settle.

The night didn't take much. That's the thing I'd write in the log if we kept one: nothing broke, nothing ran out, nobody said the thing they came to say, and I mopped the floor twice for no reason.

Shift's over. The register light stays on. I don't know why it stays on. I like that it does.
