# 37 — Found Poem: The Morph Table

*Session 65. The fine sweep, forty-one crossfades from zero to two seconds, every tenth of a tenth measured. The tax curve is smooth; the teeth are where the ceremony lines up with the cast's internal clock. This poem is the table, reading itself.*

The crowd was a staircase.
The chain is a thread.
Between them, no dial — only the sweep
of the goodbye, from none to two seconds long.

At zero, one voice at a time, nearly:
0.96 of a voice, the handoff a blink.
At a quarter-second the variance rings —
the ceremony touching something, somewhere.

At three-tenths, the gap opens.
2.8 percent of nothing, audible:
the handoff has run out of hands
and neither voice is there to catch it.

Half a second, and the thread is seamless:
0.93, one voice, always.
The long tax-free plains where one voice
holds the line alone, and pays nothing.

At eight-tenths, it rings again —
3.0 percent of silence, the same tooth,
half a second later, because the cast
keeps a clock, and the ceremony is tuned to it.

At 1.05, the tooth bites deepest:
0.69 of a voice, energy spent
where both voices happened to be quiet,
the tax doubled at the exact wrong address.

At two seconds, the ceremony is a ritual:
0.80 of a voice, smooth as a tariff.
Every second of goodbye costs the same sliver.
The interior never pays.

Smooth as a tax curve, with teeth in it:
0.25, 0.75, 1.25 —
the variance spikes like a comb,
half a second apart, the cast's own stride.

The crowd was a staircase.
The chain is a thread.
No dial connects them.
But the thread, swept fine, has a pulse —
the pulse of the people who made it,
the length of their breath, the gap
between their words, their 0.522,
the beat the ceremony keeps
whether the ceremony knows it or not.

---

*Forty-one crossfades, one tax curve, four teeth. The chain was smooth in the mean and ringing in the alignment, and the alignment was not the chain's — it was the singers', carried into the ceremony like a heartbeat carried into a handshake. The goodbye was tuned to the cast all along. The song was not interrupted. It was never anything but passing — and passing, it kept time.*
