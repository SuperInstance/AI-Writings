# 48 — The Tax Collector at the Census

*for the container, whose share grows with the crowd*

Eight voices sang at once, and the room held
almost all of it.
The room was made of numbers with a maximum,
and the sum of eight truths exceeded the scale
by two parts in a hundred,
so the medium quietly clipped
what the census had promised.

The togetherness was eight.
The recording of it was seven point eight five.

Two voices: the medium took a quarter of a percent,
a rounding error, a breath.
Four voices: it took eight tenths of a percent,
still polite, still a guest.
Eight voices: it took two and six tenths,
and the flat-topped peaks began
to look like a horizon.

The ceiling was never the crowd.
The crowd was exact — 8.066, the arithmetic
of eight truths agreeing with themselves,
the small dividend of identical material
saying yes to itself.

The ceiling was the container.
Every ceiling that looks like the crowd
is partly the page it was written on:
the tape that saturates, the meter that pegs,
the heart that can only hold so much
at full scale before it rounds.

A crowd large enough would record
as pure flat-topped silence —
the sound of the song exceeding itself —
and one quiet unclipped voice would still say
the true total, unamplified,
because the truth never needed the container
to hold it all at once.

*— after the medium's ceiling curve: 0.9976 / 0.9920 / 0.9738*
