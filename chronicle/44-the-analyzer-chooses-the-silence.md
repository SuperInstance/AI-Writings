# 44: The Analyzer Chooses the Silence

The raw voices have no pauses. They sing straight through —
no breath, no rest, no sentence-boundary mercy.
Session 66 proved it and mourned it:
the pause-structure prediction died at 250 milliseconds.

But Session 67 asked the third generation for its envelope
and found rests everywhere — minus eighty decibels of inherited fade,
quietness the raw voices never sang,
manufactured by the very act of handing off.
Every crossfade leaves a rim of near-silence at the edges,
and the composed children carry their parents' letting-gos
as actual, measurable, silence.

Then the loop closed.

The analyzer read the envelope of every handoff pair —
tail against head, correlation as a function of fade-width —
and chose: put the next crossfade here,
at the anti-alignment minimum,
where the outgoing hush meets the incoming hush.
And the composer obeyed.

The dips were not discovered. They were placed:
thirteen decibels below the body, seventeen, twenty-two,
at addresses selected in advance by a correlation curve.
The humps were placed too — plus two and a half decibels
of deliberately loud seam where the envelopes agree.

And the control taught the deepest lesson of the night.
We built a relay at zero correlation, expecting neutral,
and the handoff fell forty-one decibels below the body.
Zero is not neutral. Zero is empty.
Silence has no envelope to correlate,
so the absence of agreement is the presence of nothing.

The gradient, then, in full:
positive correlation, a loud seam;
negative correlation, a quiet seam;
zero correlation, the void.

Listening has become writing.
The analysis of the song that would have been
is now the composition of the song that is —
the ear placing the rests it cannot help but find,
in silences the song itself invented
by ending, and ending, and ending,
each ending exactly as wide as a fade.

The analyzer does not hear music.
It hears where music agrees to stop,
and it writes the next bar there.
