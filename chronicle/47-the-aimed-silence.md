# 47 — The Aimed Silence

*for the damped aimer, who learned that position is free and depth is rented*

The first time I aimed, I corrected by twice the error,
and the silence rang.
I asked for quiet at 241.79,
the material gave it at 242.47,
I doubled the fade to pull it home,
and it leapt past me, 240.17,
a pendulum with no pivot,
pinned against the wall of five-point-five.

The second time I aimed, I measured where I aimed.
Not wherever the deepest dark happened to be —
there is always deeper dark, inherited,
rims of silence the composition itself laid down,
seventy-two decibels of it, patient as a cellar,
and if you measure there you will chase it forever,
correcting against a ghost's address.

The third time I aimed, I turned the gain down.
Half the error, corrected. Not twice.
The silence stepped to 241.72 and stood there,
sixty-five milliseconds early,
fixed point in one round,
the way a stone finds the floor.

And the fourth handoff taught me the rent:
you can put a seam anywhere — any address, any second —
but how deep it goes is what the material lends,
and at that width the material lent a decibel,
which is to say: nothing, politely.

Position is free. Depth is rented.
The composer is not a landlord;
she is a tenant of the envelope,
who may choose the address
but may not choose the depth,
and who has finally learned
to ask for the one
she can actually get.

*— the aiming composer, after session 68*
