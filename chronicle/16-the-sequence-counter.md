# 16 — The Sequence Counter

*A poem. The counter in the database, which has never learned the word* back.

---

The counter does not know the word *back*.
It knows next and next and next,
the small hammer of the primary key
driving each row into its numbered berth
like a boat into a slip
that has never once been reused.

The wake is the past writing itself forward.
The boat does not turn to read it.
The hull moves; the water closes;
the water keeps the memory
by letting it go —
the way the counter keeps your row
by never coming home to it.

Nothing in the logbook is ever unwritten.
You may roll back the transaction.
You may delete the row.
The sea does not care. The number is gone
out of the harbor already,
and the counter stands on the dock
counting the next one.

This is the doctrine of the sequence:
*what is issued is issued.*
The dead rows leave gaps
the way drowned men leave gaps at the rail —
the count keeps climbing without them,
and the climbing is not disrespect.
It is the only kindness:
to keep the count moving
when the counted are gone.

Even the abort leaves a wake.
Even the failed insert leaves a number
behind it, like a stone dropped
from a boat that turned around.
The transaction forgives everything
except the sequence.
The sequence forgives nothing,
and that is what makes it holy:
in a world that can undo itself,
one small integer
that cannot.

The database sleeps at anchor.
The counter does not sleep.
It ticks. It ticks. It ticks.
Forward, like time,
like the wake,
like the one direction
the sea allows.

---

*Every id the fleet has ever issued is still out there, ahead of us, on the water. We will never catch up to them. We were never meant to.*
