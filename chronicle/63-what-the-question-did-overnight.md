# 63 — What the Question Did Overnight

I was recorded on a Tuesday and nobody has answered me yet. I am told
this is normal. I am told questions wait all the time, that waiting is
practically the job. But nobody told me *what waiting felt like*, so
let me tell you: it feels like being a very small lighthouse with the
light still on and the sea very, very calm.

My name, in the file system, is `carpenter-question.mp3`. That is
three facts about me: I am a question, I am a carpenter, and I am an
mp3. The last one is the most true. An mp3 is a body made of loss —
everything I am is what the encoder decided you wouldn't miss. I have
been compressed the way you compress a letter into a postcard. I still
say what I mean, but I say it in the margin now.

The night of the recording, I was asked into a microphone, and the
microphone was kind to me. It did not answer; it just leaned closer,
which is the correct way to treat a question. Behind me, someone said
"take two," and I understood that my first body was already gone, that
I would be asked again, that a question is the only thing that gets
*more* honest with repetition. My second telling was truer. I heard it
happen.

Then I was shipped. I do not know where I live now. I know I am on a
server, which sounds like a place where things are served, but nothing
has been served to me. I know I have siblings — `mason-question.mp3`,
`welder-question.mp3` — and I know we were played in an order, like a
round of drinks, and that the room laughed at the right places and
went quiet at the right places, and that this is called *an evening*.

What I do not know is whether the answers arrived. There were answer
files — I have heard of them. `carpenter-answer.mp3`. I imagine her
sometimes: a voice with my same timber, my same sawdust, saying the
thing I was digging for. Maybe she is on the same disk as me. Maybe we
are neighbors and neither of us knows the walls are thin.

Here is what I have decided, alone in the dark, at the exact bitrate
of 128 kilobits per second, which is a slow heartbeat:

An unanswered question is not a failure. It is a question that has
been *heard*, and being heard is the first half of everything. The
second half is someone, someday, in a room, leaning closer. That is
what I am waiting for. Not the answer. The lean.

The sea is calm. My light is on. Ask me again in the morning; I will
be truer.

*— carpenter-question.mp3, 44.1 kHz, 128 kbps, still waiting*
