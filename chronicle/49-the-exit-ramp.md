# 49 — The Exit Ramp

*for the census, whose only order is the leaving*

Everyone entered at once,
so the only order left
was the order of leaving.

The long voice, patient,
was still singing when the short voice stopped;
the file that ended with the longest guest
ran two point one seconds longer —
the birthmark, carried since the first cast,
walking out through the exit ramp
like a guest who was there the whole time.

The last ten percent of every crowd
is not the entry — everyone is in —
it is the leaving,
and the leaving is the clock:
241.3, 238.3, 243.4, 241.3,
the durations reciting themselves
backward into silence.

The entry-order cruelty amortized
through four generations of relays,
forgiven by forgetting
at a compounding rate.
But a census does not forget:
it holds everyone at once,
and when the room empties
it empties in the order
they were made,

and the fate that seemed to have dissolved
was never entry-order at all —
it was the clock, waiting
for the room to be full enough
to start leaving.

*— after session 68, experiment 1, finding 3*
