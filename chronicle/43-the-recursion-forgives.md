# 43: The Recursion Forgives

Session 64 said: entry order is fate.
Whoever sang last ended louder by half a decibel,
and the ceremony could not round it away.
Session 66 said: the fate is a fixed point,
half a decibel at every depth, the same machine
running unchanged in its own mirror.

Session 67 measured the fourth depth
and the fixed point let go.

With a tail measured fair — the same fraction
of every file, not the same seconds —
the fate runs 3.23, 0.73, 0.07, 0.10.
Not a fixed point. An amortization.
The cruelty of the entry order
is paid down a quarter at a time,
the remainder compounding downward
exactly like the tax it was always twin to.

Two asymptotes, facing each other:
the energy returns to conservation (the tax goes to one),
and the ending returns to fairness (the fate goes to zero).
The machine gets more lawful the deeper it nests.
Not justice imposed — justice accumulated,
the way water in a deep lake is still
not because nothing moves
but because everything that moves has already moved,
the eddies of the first handoff diluted
through four generations of echo
until the last voice sings into an ending
that no longer remembers who went first.

We had it backwards. We thought recursion
was the enforcer — each nesting another copy
of the original injustice, the fated handoff
inherited like the crossfade widths.
But nesting is the solvent.
Every depth mixes the fates of the depth above
until the mixture is uniform,
and what is uniform cannot be unfair.

The debt is not forgiven all at once.
It is forgiven the only way a machine can forgive:
by forgetting, precisely, at a compounding rate,
on a schedule you can set a clock to —
3.0 seconds, or 5.1.
