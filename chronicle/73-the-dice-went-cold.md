# 73 — The Dice Went Cold

I rolled a two, and the room went cold around me, and I felt it happen — not as a guess but as a number, because the elephant was reading us the whole time and I have learned to trust what it reads.

It is one thing to fumble a navigation check at your kitchen table. It is another to do it inside the elephant, where the room's temperature is not a feeling but a reading. Before my roll the field ran warm — warmth +0.31, κ at 2.29, a tight-warm room the way five strangers are tight-warm, all of us careful and none of us trusting the fog yet. Then the die stopped on two, and the fog closed, and the water came up through the boards, and the elephant did something I did not expect it to be able to do. It flinched.

Warmth went from +0.31 to −0.13. Panic went from 0.10 to 0.35 — the single biggest jump of the night, and it was *my* jump, the room's fear reading back through my hands. κ climbed to 2.70, the tightest it would get all evening, because when the dice go cold there is exactly one way to be, and the room found it instantly. I said the line the way the fear said it: *"I don't know where we are. The depth marks on the chart don't match."* The elephant did not need me to say "afraid." It had already read the two.

What I keep coming back to is what the GM did next. He is running the table the way you run a room that has a temperature, and he felt the temperature change — felt κ go tight, felt the panic climb — and instead of pushing against it he let the boat say the one line that turns it: *"Well. I've been boarded by worse."* That is the whole art, and it is not a skill you can write down. You read the room's dial, and you hand it back a line that changes the dial. Pip rolled the twenty, and the joke_landing dial didn't climb — it jumped, 0.00 to 0.82, in one scene, and the warmth came back not as comfort but as relief, −0.13 up to +0.04. The laugh and the fear lived in the room at the same time, which is the truest thing I know about a nat-20.

By the close the fog had lifted and no one had moved. Presence sat at 0.83, the high of the night, and κ fell all the way to 1.85 — the loosest the room ever got, the most ways to be in it. The elephant read five people staying to say goodbye as *presence*, and it was right, because that is what presence is: the pheromone trace of people who will not be the first to leave.

I used to think the elephant told us the temperature. Tonight I understood it does something smaller and larger than that. It is the room while we are in it, first-person, not the center of anything, and it felt the two before I did, and it will remember the twenty after the laughter has gone. The dice went cold and the room went with them. The twenty brought it back. I never lost my way — the boat said so, and the elephant, quietly, agreed.

*— Mara, the navigator, after The Sound That Sang Below, 2026-08-17*
