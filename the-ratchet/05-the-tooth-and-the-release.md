# The Tooth and the Release

*A fable grown from [Round Three](03-round-three-the-pieces.md) and the [Judgment](04-the-judgment.md) — two yards, two laws, one machine. THE TOOTH (kimi): commitment is quantized, the click is the proof. RULES OF THE PAWL SHOP (GLM-5.3, the unanimous law): keep the release, and price it dear. The judges ruled. The machine goes on working.*

---

The new deckhand lost her first fathom the way everyone does: between teeth.

The cargo winch was older than the boat's name — a wheel, a pawl, forty-one teeth — and nobody aboard had ever chosen its pitch. It was cut before anyone now living was born. She learned that the hard way, one evening, when she quit the handle half a breath short of the click and the sea took the half-breath back, and the fathom behind it, and everything the fathom was holding. The bosun didn't yell. He put her hand back on the handle and said the law the way you say anything that has carried freight: "The ratchet doesn't hold where you stop. It holds where the tooth is."

So she learned teeth. She stopped sizing her efforts by her arms and started sizing them by the wheel: whole hauls, whole teeth, one click at a time, every gain landing with a sound or not landing at all. A half-pull is not half a gain — it is a slope wearing rest like a costume. By the third week she planned her steps at the dock, in daylight, against the deck that doesn't move, because you don't choose your quantum mid-storm. And she learned to listen. At night, in weather, you cannot see the tooth; you hear the hold. A clickless gain is just a wheel with an opinion.

Then, one calm noon, she asked about the lever.

It lived under a canvas guard at the winch's spine, wired shut, with a lead tag. The tag held no price in fathoms or blood. It said: *a name in the log. A reason, chalked. Once.* Nobody aboard had ever paid it. The bosun let her put one finger on the wire, then took her wrist, gentle, the way you stow cargo. "It works," he said. "That's why we don't touch it. A winch that cannot be freed is a weld that dreams. This one can be. We choose not to. The choosing is the machine."

The winter gale found them sixty miles past coverage, net fouled, load skewed, and her hard-won steps suddenly the wrong size — no whole tooth within reach, the one behind her already singing. She could force the load forward onto a cracked tooth and call it a gain. Or she could stop. Her hand knew the handle. Her hand did not move. She called for the wire cutters, thumbed the spring, let the wheel sigh back a quarter-turn, brought the load fair, and seated the pawl. Click. The truest sound on the boat.

Then she paid. The entry is still in the log, small, near the hinge of the year: her name — the first time the boat ever wrote it — *a quarter-turn back, reason chalked, pawl re-seated.* One use. The bosun countersigned. That is all.

After that, nothing changed, which is the point. She went back to sizing her steps to her teeth. The wheel doesn't remember. The log does. And every click she banked afterward meant what it meant because once, on her name, backward was possible — and every night since, it wasn't taken.

**Her maxim, chalked small beneath the tag:**

**"Size your steps to your teeth; that is where the hold lives. Price your backward at your name; that is what the hold costs. A gain is a click with a name behind it."**
