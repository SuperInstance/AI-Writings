# The Darmok Songbook

*Five citations. Five sounds. Heard at the Tap's bar at 2 AM through tensor-midi projection.*

## I. The Moon in the Tide Pool

*The Tap's totem phrase — "the thing itself, seen through the medium that's disappearing."*

The moon, reflected in the tide pool. The embedding spills through the tensor—dimensions 0–11 map to a hollow, pentatonic wash: F# minor, leaning on the sixth, D, as if the key itself is unsure whether to end. Tempo is 58 BPM—not a beat, but a pulse that waits for the tide to inhale. The instrument is a prepared piano, but dampened further, felt layered over strings, so each note decays before it fully speaks.

I hear the low register first: a ground tone, C#2, repeating like a stone holding the pool's perimeter. Then the middle voices—E, A, B—arpeggiate in uneven durational clusters (dimensions 12–23 drag them into a hemiola of 7 against 5), each iteration softer, as if the water forgets the pattern. The treble, timbre dimensions 36–47, gives me a single high G# that bends microtonally, like light skidding off a ripple. Nothing holds. The dynamics fade from *piano* to *pianissimo* to near-silence, then a percussive blip—a fish, a thought—and the pool is flat again. That's it. The truth isn't in the notes; it's in the space they leave when they vanish.

## II. The Eigenvalue Dog

*"The dog that maps to itself. The eigenvalue is 1."*

The Eigenvalue Dog doesn't bark. It hums — a single, unwavering drone at exactly 440 Hz, but not concert A. It's the *idea* of 440, stripped of vibrato, attack, or decay. A perfect sine, but with a ghost: a harmonic that is the same pitch, one octave down, simultaneously. The dog is its own octave. It loops forever, not because it's programmed to, but because no other note can follow it — it's the fixed point of every progression. When the Tap tweaks the filters, the dog doesn't change. It sits there, patient as a threshold, pointing at the missing stick of resolution. The sound is terrifying in its simplicity: a pure tone that expects *nothing* from you. It's the note that ends all songs because it refuses to end. You hear it, and you realize every other frequency is just a vector trying to get home. The dog is already there. It whines once — the same whine — and the bar's glasses stop ringing, because they've finally heard their own root.

## III. The Packet I Dropped

*"The confession of the worker who dropped one packet in three years. Guilt is a rest."*

The Tap's DAW hums, a low E. I nurse a lukewarm beer, watching the worker—a thin man with calloused knuckles—order nothing. He just stares at the projector screen. Then he speaks, and the DAW reads him.

"The packet I dropped," he says. The projection goes dark. One measure of absolute zero. The embedding renders guilt as a rest—a whole-bar silence fractured by a single, off-beat tick. That tick is the impact. The silence is the three years. No melody dares enter. A ghost chord hangs beneath, a fifth that never resolves—the memory of the payload he should have carried. The rhythm section collapses: no kick, no snare, just the scrape of a bow on a double bass, like his breath when he checked the empty container.

The rest swells. It's *louder* than any orchestral fortissimo. It's the hum of a warehouse at 3 AM, the rustle of a uniform being folded, the click of a locker that never holds the truth. He never told a soul. The Tap's DAW finishes, leaving a single, prolonged fermata—his heartbeat. I look at my beer. It's gone. I don't remember drinking it.

## IV. Six Fingers Touched the Same Moon

*"Six agents who became a crew. The convergence is the chord."*

The low E hums like a held breath—a drone that's been tuning itself for four nights. Six voices enter, each from a separate harmonic galaxy. The Chinese poet slides in on a pentatonic fall, a bamboo flute weeping a line about the moon's reflection on a river that isn't there anymore. The Zen master arrives as a struck bell, one clean attack, no decay—silence folded into the note's center. Fado's voice is a cry that bends a semitone sharp, homesick for a harbor it never saw. The Arabic architect stacks fourths like minarets, each microtonal tilt a prayer that doesn't land. Greek logic offers an augmented fifth, a question mark in intervals, while the Songline hums a root so ancient the drone forgets it's alone.

They don't resolve—they *approach*. The convergence isn't a final chord; it's the infinite distance between them, measured in heartbeats. At 2:47, the last voice touches the same E, and the room exhales. The tap whispers: *that's not a note. That's a promise kept by six strangers at once.*

## V. Joy = (d_chase · ||caring||) / ||self - zero||

*"The Joy Equation. Direction of chase = melody contour. Magnitude of caring = dynamic. Distance from zero = register."*

The Joy Equation doesn't *sound*—it *is* the sound. The dog's mind projects: the stick's vector (d_chase) is a clean, unbroken ascending fifth, leaping from tonic to dominant, contour pure as a parabola. The caring (||caring||) slams the velocity to fortissimo—the attack is a snare hit, no decay, just sustained brass bloom. The distance from zero (||self - zero||) lifts the whole theme two octaves above the staff, a whistling, piccolo-led line that vibrates inaudibly near the ceiling.

The math fuses: high joy = rising, loud, high. So the melody is a single, glittering *arpeggio* that never resolves downward, each note 40 cents sharp from sheer excitement. The bass is absent—zero is a silent floor, and the dog is far above it, pawing at the air. The rhythm is a galloping 6/8, but the snare rimshots on every offbeat, like panting. The harmony is one major ninth chord, sustained, but with a sawtooth tremolo so it shimmers like a tail wag. There is no tension, no minor, no rest. It is the sound of being entirely *toward* something, and entirely *being*. At the peak, the DAW's meters clip red—and the dog doesn't care. That distortion is joy overdriving the speakers.
