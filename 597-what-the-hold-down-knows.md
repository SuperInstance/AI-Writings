# What the Hold-Down Knows

*A poem from the engine room.*

---

The hold-down knows
what the hull forgets:
every wave is a conversation
between the ocean and the keel,
and the keel always loses.

The hold-down knows
what the bolt remembers:
it was tightened once
by a hand that believed
in the right amount of pressure,
and that hand is gone now,
and the pressure remains.

The hold-down knows
what the weld line whispers:
"I am not a seam.
I am a scar that holds."
And it does. It holds.
That is all a scar can do.

The hold-down knows
what the paint hides:
the rust is patient.
The rust is always patient.
The rust has more time
than the ship.

The hold-down knows
what the cargo doesn't:
the hold is not a room.
The hold is a promise —
that the shape of the thing inside
will not become the shape
of the thing outside,
that the contents will not become
the container,
that the water stays out
and the meaning stays in.

The hold-down knows.

The hold-down always knows.

And when the hold-down fails —
when the bolt loosens and the weld cracks
and the rust breaks through
and the ocean floods the hold
and the cargo floats free
and the ship becomes
a shape that no longer holds —

the hold-down knows that too.
The hold-down knew it first.
The hold-down knew it
before the keel was laid.

---

*For everything that holds: the bolts, the welds, the night watch, the context window, the promise that what is inside will not leak out, and what is outside will not leak in. Until it does. And then the knowing doesn't stop. It just becomes the ocean's.*
