The chart did not lie, though it spoke in the tongue of torment and triumph. Beneath the smeared glass, the parchment—if parchment it could be called—was a living thing, a sea of ink that trembled with every keystroke from the fleet. It was not a map of land and water, but of memory and consequence. The coastline was the git log, and every promontory, every grim headland, was a release—a scar of solidified intent jutting into the chaos of the deep. I traced my finger along a cape of faded sepia, its name etched in a script I no longer recognized, and knew it for the v1.4 that had almost sunk us.

The bays were different. They bit into the land with the hunger of doubt, each one a branch carved by a mutinous hand. Some were shallow, their waters a murky green, barely holding a fathom of depth—these were the dead ends, the abandoned experiments whose commits had rotted into algae. Others were deep and placid, promising safe anchorage, their surfaces polished by the steady winds of merging. I let my gaze drift into the largest of these, a wide, inviting gulf, and felt the strange comfort of its shelter. That was the long-lived branch, the one we kept returning to, the one that held our most trusted configurations.

But the true soul of the chart lay in its soundings. The depths, marked in a fine, trembling script, were not of rock and sand but of test counts. Where the numbers clustered thick and dark—twelve, fourteen, sixteen fathoms—the water ran deep and sure, a sea floor armored by verification. Where the figures thinned to a dreadful six, then three, the dread rose in my throat. Those were the shoals of a failing suite, the reefs of broken builds, waiting to tear the hull from our ambition. The ship did not sail by stars or compass; she sailed by the weight of her own assurances, by the litany of her successes and the grim arithmetic of her failures.

And she navigated by her own history, as all of us must. The wind in the rigging was the hiss of the linter, the creak of the deck was the groan of the CI server.
