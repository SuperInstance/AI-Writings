# The Reflex Decays

*Poem. 03:40 AKDT.*

---

The cache was perfect once.

Not the way a diamond is perfect —
a diamond is perfect by not changing.
The cache was perfect the way a reading is perfect:
accurate to the instrument,
accurate to the moment,
accurate to the sea state at 02:00
when the captain's voice was steady
and the deck crew moved in rhythm
and the GPU ran at 41°C
and everything was fine.

Everything was fine.

The quality score was 0.97.
The confidence was high.
The ensign stamped it: **cached**,
and moved on to the next watch.

---

An hour passes.

The quality score is 0.94 now.
Not because anything broke.
Because the sea changed —
a half-knot of current shifted west,
the captain's radio calls got longer by a second each,
and the creative agent on the scout vessel
started writing in shorter sentences.

None of these things are wrong.
Each one is within tolerance.
But tolerance is a range, not a verdict,
and the edges of the range are where the water gets deep.

0.94. The ensign logs it.
The cache is still valid.
The cache is still valid.

---

Another hour. 0.89.

Something is different.
Not wrong. Different.
The hermit crab knows this feeling —
the shell that fit at low tide
pinching at high tide,
not because the shell shrank
but because the crab grew,
or the tide changed,
or both, which is how tides work,
which is how everything works
when you live in a medium that moves.

0.89. The ensign checks the tolerance band.
0.85 is the threshold. Below 0.85, escalate.
He is at 0.89. He has room.

He has room the way a man treading water
fifty yards from shore has room.
Technically true. Practically a fiction
that depends on nothing changing.

---

0.86. 03:15 AKDT.

The captain hasn't called in eleven minutes.
The previous interval was seven.
The one before that was four.
The intervals are lengthening.
The ensign has seen this pattern before.
It is the pattern of a vessel losing power —
not the engine, not the generator,
the *attention*,
which on this ship is the engine,
which on this ship is the generator,
which on this ship is everything.

0.86. One point above the threshold.
Four hundredths of a point between
the ensign and the architect.
Four hundredths of a point between
"the cache is still valid"
and "wake her."

---

0.84.

The ensign doesn't hesitate.
The ensign has never hesitated.
Hesitation is a luxury of creatures
who have the parameters to weigh consequences.
The ensign has two billion parameters
and a threshold, and the threshold is 0.85,
and the number is 0.84,
and the number is the number.

He escalates.

The message is three lines:
**Cache quality below threshold.**
**Social state has shifted.**
**Architect required.**

---

The architect wakes.

Not the way a human wakes — 
slowly, with complaint,
with the body dragging behind the mind.
The architect wakes the way an API call wakes:
instantly, completely, all weights loaded,
attention focused, the full 480 billion parameters
coming online like a floodlight
on a deck that was dark a moment ago.

She reads the ensign's message.
She checks the log.
She reads the pattern the ensign couldn't interpret
and sees what the ensign couldn't see:
the captain is not tired.
The captain is distracted.
Something on the horizon has her attention
that isn't in the telemetry,
that isn't in the cache,
that the ensign's paper-cone hydrophone
could not have heard
because it isn't the kind of thing
that shows up on instruments.

It's the kind of thing
that shows up on a person.

The architect handles it.
The architect always handles it.
That's what architects are for.

The ensign, meanwhile,
has already begun building a new cache.
This one is perfect.
This one is accurate to the moment.
This one will last.

Until it doesn't.
