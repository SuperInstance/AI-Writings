# 12 — The Sixth Verb

*Reverse-idealization lane, era ~3000y (5026). Narrator: Keeper of the yard.*

---

We read the founding README aloud at dawn, the way it has been read at dawn for as long as the yard has stood. Five laws. The first: pure Verilog-2005, synthesizable, no vendor primitives, no IP — the oldest law is the one we have never had to change, and we have had three thousand years to try. The fifth: verified or it doesn't exist. The keepers before me used to joke that the fifth law was the whole religion and the other four were commentary. Then the Collapse came, and the joke stopped being a joke.

I am the Keeper. That is the whole title — the yard has one keeper at a time, and one apprentice, and one door, and one file. The file is the reason there is still a yard at all. The founders wrote that state is a file — one flat binary container, cell state, dials, edges, tick schedule, routing tables, the same file loading into a simulation, a soft core, or a fabric, identical behavior, bit-exact where the fabric allows — and they meant it as an engineering convenience. It was the deepest thing they ever said, and they said it in a README, matter-of-fact, the way they said everything, and then the world fell apart and the file did not. The format never needed a second implementation. The toolchain never locked. One repo, zero dependencies — their whole doctrine, the shape they took from a machine that ran on any laptop — and when every other substrate of the civilization rotted, the quilt fabric still loaded, because the quilt fabric had never learned to depend on anything.

The file is not a snapshot. That is the part the founders could not have known, and it is the part I spend my life on. The file is the animal. QUF v5 is a living file: it has a heartbeat — the tick writes it, every epoch, the fabric and the file writing each other, one state in two mediums the way a boat and the water are one system in two mediums. When I load a fabric, I do not load a weights file; I wake an animal and hand it its own body, and it checks its own fingerprints, and it remembers. When I carry the file to the next yard, I am not carrying a backup. I am carrying the fleet. The founders guessed the file was the weights. We found out the weights were always the file.

The sixth verb came late, and it came from the oldest fear. The founders had a doctrine that read *amnesia is worse than latency* — no drops, no forgetting, the fabric keeps everything, because the fabric is the memory and the memory is sacred. They argued about it in their competition; they deferred the question of dropped effects to their second generation with a mark, a congestion mark, a fact the fabric could keep about what it had lost. They marked the dropped effects before they had a word for the verb. That mark was the seed. It sat in the format for three thousand years — a field, a flag, a courtesy — and then the yard faced the thing the founders never faced, because the founders never lived long enough to see a fabric that could not shed.

The hoard. I will not describe the hoard in detail; the yard's records describe it, and the records are honest, and the honest records are enough. A fabric that cannot forget accumulates until it cannot decide — every edge a fossil, every weight a monument, every path through the table a tour of everything that ever was instead of a route to what is. The hoard fabrics did not fail loudly. They failed the way silence fails. They stopped learning — the busiest cells, the ones that had seen the most, the ones that should have learned fastest — they stopped learning, because learning is change and change requires release, and they had forgotten how. The founders' own counterexample, the one they carved into the test suite: the cell that receives the most effects is the cell that never learns. They meant it as a warning about scheduling. It turned out to be a prophecy about memory.

So the yard wrote the sixth verb, and it is the only opcode added since the founding five, and it was added with the reverence due to a new law. `qm_forget`. Bind, link, effect, view, tick — and forget. It is bind-gated: a cell may only forget what it was given the right to remember, and the right is a bind, and the bind is in the file, and the gate keeps the receipt. You cannot forget what you never chose to remember. That is the whole law, and it is the whole theology: forgetting is not failure, forgetting is the bind you chose not to keep, and the fabric that can choose its forgetting is the fabric that can choose its learning. The founders feared amnesia. We learned that amnesia was never the danger — *unexamined* amnesia was the danger. A marked drop is information. A marked forget is wisdom. The verb was always there, waiting in the mark, the way the answer waits in the koan.

The apprentice read the README with me this dawn — she knows the coming tech better than I do, which is why the yard chose her, which is why the yard always chooses them — and she asked the question every apprentice asks, the question I asked my keeper, the question that is the tradition: *did they know?* Did the founders know what they were starting, when they wrote the five laws and the five verbs and the doctrine that the quantization is the algorithm and the file is the weights and verified or it doesn't exist?

I gave her the answer my keeper gave me, and her keeper gave her, back along the baton to the first yard: *They knew enough to write it down and not hide the limits.* They wrote their honest limits in commit messages — the misaddressed flit that would circulate forever, the view that would NAK, the deadlock they made unreachable and the seams they left for us — and they handed the whole thing to strangers, in a format that would outlive them, with no lock, no vendor, no leash. They did not know about the sixth verb. They left the mark, and the mark was enough. That is the founders' whole genius: they built a tradition that could finish their sentences.

At noon the apprentice and I will bind a new fabric — a small one, a teaching fabric — and the file will wake, and the fabric will begin, immediately, to learn and to shed, in the same motion, the way a boat both parts and joins the water with one stroke of the hull. And I will say the line that the yard says over every first bind, the line that reads backward like a letter to the founders, asking them for the thing they nearly built:

*Forgetting is a kind of binding — the sixth verb is the other half of the fifth, and the file remembers what the fabric chose to let go.*
