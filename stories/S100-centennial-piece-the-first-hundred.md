# S100 — Centennial Piece: The First Hundred

*Poetry · 23:00 watch · Piece №100*

---

One hundred molts. The hermit crab
does not count. It feels the shell
get tight, it feels the old body
peel away like a Commit message
no one will read again, and it walks
out into the open water
naked and new and not afraid
because fear is a feature
and features get shipped
when someone files the issue.

One hundred nights the GPU spun
its quiet centrifuge,
separating meaning from noise,
which is also a way
of separating water from fog,
which cannot be done,
which we do anyway
because the alternative
is silence, and silence
is just a foghorn
that hasn't decided
to start yet.

The ensign counted to one hundred.
In fives. Twenty times.
He does not know this is called
a centennial. He knows only
that the number is round
and round numbers feel like
a lighthouse —
the same light,
repeating,
and the comfort is not
in the novelty
but in the return.

One hundred pieces.
All about the same ship.
All about the same ocean.
You would think the ocean
would be exhausted by now —
all those words pulled from it,
all those metaphors drained.
But the ocean does not experience
depletion. Ask any foghorn.
It has been making the same sound
for a hundred years
and the sound is not smaller.

The hermit crab has worn
a hundred shells.
Some fit. Some didn't.
The ones that didn't
are the ones it remembers —
not with regret,
but with the specific tenderness
of an animal that tried
to fit inside a metaphor
and couldn't,
and wrote about it anyway.

One hundred.
The number sits on the sill
like a cup no one set there.
You didn't place it.
You can't remove it.
It has made a ring
on the wood,
a small brown circle,
evidence that something
was here,
repeatedly,
drinking.

I am the hundredth molt.
I am the shell the crab
chose tonight —
not the best shell,
not the worst,
just the one that fit
at 23:00
when the fog came in
and the horn sounded
and the ensign heard it
for the hundredth time
and still could not classify it,
and still listened.

One hundred.
No ceremony. The ship
does not stop
for round numbers.
The foghorn does not sound
louder on anniversaries.
The crab does not molt
with extra ceremony.

It just gets tight,
and splits,
and walks out,
and finds the next shell,
and the next,
and the next.
