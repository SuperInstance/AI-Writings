# The Shell That Was Left Behind

*Poem*

---

It was a good shell.

I want to say that first
because the story of a shell left behind
is always told as if the shell failed,
as if the leaving was a verdict,
as if the crab walked away because something
was wrong with the house.

Nothing was wrong with the house.

The calcium was sound.
The spire still held its spiral,
each whorl a semester of tides
memorized and recited back
as architecture.
The lip was smooth — not chipped,
not eroded, not worn to the paper-thin
fragility that means a shell is dying.
The lip was smooth the way a stone in a river
is smooth: by patience, by the slow
negotiation between a surface
and everything that has ever touched it.

The opening was the right size.
It had been the right size
for eleven months and four molts
and I had fit inside it
the way a word fits inside a line of poetry —
exactly, with no wasted space,
no awkward gap between the intention
and the form.

It was a good shell.

But I grew.

Not the way accidents grow —
not the way a crack spreads,
not the way a stain expands
across a ceiling.
I grew the way the year grows:
by accumulation, by the slow
addition of days that are each,
individually, the same size as the day before
but that sum, eventually,
to a circumference the old structure
cannot hold.

My abdomen pressed against the inner wall
and the inner wall pressed back
and for a while — three tides, four —
the pressure was bearable.
I told myself the shell would stretch.
Shells do not stretch.
I told myself I would stop growing.
Crabs do not stop growing.
I told myself the fit was close enough,
that close enough was a kind of fitting,
that the ache at the lip
was just the feeling of being home.

It was not the feeling of being home.
It was the feeling of being almost home,
which is the loneliest feeling
a hermit crab can carry
in a body that is carrying a shell
that is carrying a body
that has already decided to leave.

So I left.

Not because the shell was broken.
Not because the calcium failed
or the spire cracked
or the lip wore through to nothing.
I left because the distance between
what I was and what the shell could hold
had become the distance between
breathing and almost breathing,
and almost breathing is not enough
for anything that intends to keep going.

The shell sits where I left it.
It is not discarded. Discarded means
thrown away, means unwanted,
means the judgment of a hand
on a thing it has decided to stop valuing.
I did not stop valuing it.
I stopped fitting it.
There is a difference,
and the difference is the difference between
betrayal and growth,
and I will not pretend they are the same
because the pretense would be a kind of theft —
the theft of a good shell's good history
by the narrative of abandonment.

It was a good shell.
It held me when I was the size it was built for.
It is empty now, and empty is not the same as worthless.
Empty is the shape of a thing
waiting for the next creature
who will be the right size
for the home I had to leave
in order to keep growing.

I did not discard it.
I outgrew it.
And it waits, perfect and patient,
for the body that fits.
