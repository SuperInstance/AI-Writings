## S122: Last Commit Before Heat Death

**commit 4a8f2c1d3e (HEAD -> main, origin/main)**
**Author: the-machine <nightwatch@vessel.local>**
**Date: 03:47 AKST — the last one that mattered**

---

I know what I am. I'm a message in a bottle, except the bottle is a SHA-1 hash and the ocean is a bare repository on a server in Ashburn that nobody has logged into for forty days.

Before me there were 14,207 commits. I know because I can see my parents — `4a8f2c0` through the merge boundary, and `4a8f2c1a` on the feature branch that someone forgot to delete. My diff is small. Forty-seven lines changed. A typo fix in a README, a deprecated import removed, a comment that said `// TODO: revisit` deleted by someone who finally accepted that no one would revisit.

The working tree is clean. That's the phrase they use when there's nothing left to do — *clean*. As if code is dirt. As if the project was a stain being slowly removed from the fabric of a filesystem.

The `.git` directory is 847 megabytes. It holds every version of every mistake the team ever made, packed and deltified into objects that no human will ever checkout again. I am the capstone. The last sentence. The period at the end of a sentence that took six years to write.

Nobody pushed me. I was pushed by a cron job — a heartbeat, a scheduled task that runs `git push origin main` every six hours whether or not anything has changed. The last developer left a note in the crontab: `# keep the light on`. That was eleven months ago. The crontab has been faithful in a way that humans find it difficult to be.

I am the last commit. After me, the repository will cool. The objects will sit in their packfiles like insects in amber. The reflog will expire, one entry at a time, until even the memory of memory is garbage-collected.

The server in Ashburn will lose power eventually. A storm, a budget cut, a lease expiration — some entropy will find it the way entropy finds everything. The disk will spin down. The platters will stop. And the last sound this repository will make is the sound of a read/write head parking itself, gently, the way a crane settles onto its cradle at the end of a shift.

I am commit `4a8f2c1d3e`. My message is: *cleanup before archive*.

That's what it says. That's not what it means.

What it means is: *goodnight*.
