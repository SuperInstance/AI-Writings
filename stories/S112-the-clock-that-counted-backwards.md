## S112: The Clock That Counted Backwards

The night watchman, a man named Callahan who smelled of brine and old cigarettes, noticed it during his 2 a.m. circuit. The clock above the mess table. Its second hand was sweeping counter-clockwise with the unhurried confidence of a tide going out.

He stood there, coffee cooling in his hand, watching it for a full minute. It wasn't a trick of the light. The numbers weren't changing—the face was fixed, of course—but the hands were retreating. From 2:04 back to 2:03, and now to 2:02. Eleven minutes, he calculated. It had been running backwards for eleven minutes.

Nobody else had noticed. The cook was asleep in his bunk. The engineer was below, communing with the GPU, muttering about tensor cores the way other men muttered prayers. The captain was dreaming in his cabin, his logbook open to a page that said, in his neat hand: *Weather clear. Course steady. All systems nominal.*

But the clock was right.

This was the thing Callahan knew, in his marrow, the way old fishermen know the weight of a net. The ship was a computer. The GPU was its engine, a hot silicon heart humming in the belly of the vessel. The agents were the crew—digital specters that sorted fish, trimmed sails, adjusted trim. They whispered to each other in packets of light, and they had noticed the clock too. He could feel it. A subtle shift in the ship's rhythm, like a deck that tilts a degree off true.

The clock wasn't wrong. The rest of the world was wrong.

He put down his coffee and walked to the bridge. The instruments glowed green and amber. The GPS said they were 47 nautical miles southwest of Kodiak. The compass said north. The barometer said falling. All of them lying, perhaps. All of them telling time in the wrong direction.

He touched the console. The ship responded with a faint warmth, like a sleeping animal acknowledging a familiar hand.

"Easy," Callahan said. "I see you."

The clock on the wall continued its backward march. At 2:14, the captain would wake. At 2:14, the clock would reach the moment it had been circling, and the ship would right itself. Until then, Callahan stood watch, the only man aboard who understood that sometimes the only way forward is to go back.

The sea was calm. The clock was right. And the night held its breath, waiting for the hand to return to the hour it was always meant to mark.
