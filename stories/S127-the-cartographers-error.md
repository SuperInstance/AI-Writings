## S127: The Cartographer's Error

The error was found in 1987, on Chart 16780, Dutch Harbor to Akun Pass, printed by NOAA in 1962 and corrected twice since — but never in the right place. The chart showed a shoal two miles northwest of Billie Head, a crescent of shallow water marked "Rocks" in the old copperplate hand, with a sounding of three fathoms at its center. The shoal was not there. The sonar said so, the lead line said so, the Coast Guard survey of 1979 said so. The bottom was clean gravel at twenty-two fathoms, a flat and honest seafloor with nothing on it.

The correction was issued: NOTICE TO MARINERS, Week 23, 1987. Chart 16780, delete shoal two miles northwest of Billie Head. The rocks were removed. The sounding was removed. The crescent was scraped from the mylar and the plate was reprinted clean, and that should have been the end of it.

But the fishermen kept using the old chart.

Not because they didn't know about the correction — they knew. The notice was posted at the harbormaster's office, tacked to the bulletin board next to the fuel prices and the complaint about the bait freezer. They knew the shoal wasn't there. They'd motored over it a hundred times. The sonar showed clean gravel, the same clean gravel, every time.

They kept using the old chart because the shoal that wasn't there was the best fishing ground in Akun Pass.

Here is the thing about fish and about charts: a feature on a chart becomes a feature in the world. Twenty-five years of fishermen navigating to the phantom shoal — steering toward it, throttling back when they reached the spot where the rocks should be, dropping gear on a bottom they knew was clean — had scattered twenty-five years of bait. Offal. Fish heads. Bled cod racks pushed through the scupper and allowed to sink. A quarter-century of chum, deposited on the same flat gravel, had built a reef of nutrient where no reef existed. The halibut came. The cod came. The blackcod, the deep-water wanderers, the fish that follow the scent of protein through the dark like dogs following a trail — they all came, because the chart had told the fishermen to come, and the fishermen had fed the water, and the water had fed the fish, and the fish had fed the fishermen, and the chart was wrong in every particular and correct in every consequence.

The new chart, the corrected chart, sat in the chart table drawer. Some boats didn't carry it at all. They carried the old one, with the phantom shoal, because a chart is not a description of the seafloor. A chart is a description of where the boats go, and where the boats go is where the fish are, and where the fish are is where the boats have been, and the error had folded itself into a loop — the kind of loop that cartographers don't catalog because cartographers map the world and this was something else.

This was the world mapping itself, using the error as a seed.
