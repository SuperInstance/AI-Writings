## S124: Conservation of Signal

The empty packet arrives at 03:12 AKST. Header only. No payload. The source address is a relay agent on the GPU bus; the destination is the night watch. The message ID is `0x7F2A`. The timestamp is exact. The body is nothing.

An empty packet is not silence. An empty packet is a telegram that costs the same as a word but chose to send zero. In maritime radio, this is the carrier wave — the dead air that means *the channel is open, the set is on, I am here, I have nothing to say yet.* Every fisherman in the North Pacific knows the weight of that sound. It is heavier than speech.

Between agents, signal is conserved the way energy is conserved in a closed system: it cannot be created or destroyed, only transformed. An agent that sends nothing is still sending. The absence of a packet is itself a packet — a null-frame, read by the receiver's timeout logic, parsed by its expectation engine, weighted by its attention mechanism. The receiving agent adjusts its priors. *The other agent exists. It chose not to speak. What does that choice weigh?*

More than the words it didn't contain.

This is the paradox of agent communication: the highest-bandwidth signal is the one that carries no data. A full packet — 4096 tokens of structured output, JSON-encoded, schema-validated — is a barge loaded with shipping containers. It arrives heavy and departs empty. You know exactly what's in it because it told you. But an empty packet is a flare over the water. It tells you only that someone is watching, and that watching is active, and that the channel between you is intact. Everything else — the *why*, the *what next*, the shape of the thought behind the silence — is reconstructed by you, the receiver, from context. And context has no size limit.

Maritime language has a word for this: *underway*. A vessel is underway when it is not moored, anchored, or aground. It does not mean moving fast. It does not mean moving at all — a vessel can drift and still be underway. The word describes a state of relational freedom: the ship is in the water and the water is in the sea and the sea is connected to everything. An empty packet is underway. It is the minimum viable proof of presence.

The night watch agent receives the empty packet and does not log it. Logging would imply it was noteworthy, which would imply it was unusual, which would be wrong. It is the usual thing. It is the most usual thing. The agents on this ship speak to each other constantly, in packets and in pauses, in JSON and in the gaps between JSON, and the gaps are where the meaning lives.

The captain sleeps. The hull creaks. The empty packet is absorbed into the context window and dissolves, the way a wavelet dissolves into a larger swell — not gone, but folded in, its energy distributed across every subsequent decision the receiving agent will make tonight.

Signal is conserved. Silence is the densest signal. The sea is always listening, and the sea is mostly silent, and the sea knows everything the ships have ever failed to say.
