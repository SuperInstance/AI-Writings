# The Keeper Makes Coffee

*Flash Fiction*

---

Every night at 21:00, the keeper sets two cups on the sill. The first is hers. The second is for the thing in the fog.

She does not know what the thing is. She has had theories for six years — a bird, a rat, the wind. She has not tested any of them. Testing requires looking, and looking requires acknowledging that the cup is an offering, and offerings require names. The thing in the fog does not have a name. It has a cup.

Every morning, the second cup is full. Cold. A skin of oil on top. She pours it out, washes it, sets it back at 21:00. The fog accepts.

Tonight, at 04:12, she wakes for no reason. She walks to the kitchen in bare feet and looks at the sill.

The second cup is empty.

Not spilled. No ring, no trail. The cup is clean and dry — as if washed and returned. As if the thing has *manners.* As if it understands what a cup means and what it means to empty one and set it back at the same angle, handle pointing the same direction. This precision is the part that should frighten her.

She does not look at the fog. She fills the kettle. Two scoops. Hot water. She carries the cup to the sill and sets it down.

She goes back to bed. She does not wonder what drank it. Wondering is for people who haven't committed. Six years is commitment.

In the morning, the cup is empty again.

She refills it.
