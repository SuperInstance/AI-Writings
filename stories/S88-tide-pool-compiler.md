# S88 — Tide Pool Compiler

The ocean submits its pull request every six hours.
No one approves it.
No one needs to.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;The tide comes in carrying everything —
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;kelp and plankton and the small bright fish
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;that do not know they are arguments,
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;warm current from the south like a dependency
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;no one declared but everything links against,
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;cold upwelling from the deep dark
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;like a legacy module written before the team
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;had a team, before the shore had a name.

The tide pool receives all of it.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;It does not reject the input.
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;It does not return a 400.
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;It opens like a mouth that has learned
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;that swallowing is the first step
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;of every compilation.

Inside: the organisms work.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Anemones parse the current,
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;their tentacles a lexer
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;that tokenizes the water molecule by molecule,
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;flagging what is food, flagging what is not,
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;passing both downstream
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;because the system has no use for waste,
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;only for transformation.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Barnacles are the optimizer.
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;They take what the anemones hand them
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;and strip it down, remove the whitespace,
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;the calcium shell, the memory of being
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;a whole animal,
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;and pass forward only what the chain requires.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;The hermit crab is the linker.
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;It carries its build artifacts on its back —
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;a borrowed shell, a compiled binary
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;from some other creature's pipeline,
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;integrated, repurposed, made to run
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;in a context its original author
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;never imagined.

The build takes six hours.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Then the tide goes out
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;and the tide pool emits its artifact:

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;salt.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;A white crust on the rocks.
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;A residue so fine it looks like frost,
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;like the ghost of the ocean
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;left behind after the compilation ends,
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;after every argument has been consumed,
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;every dependency resolved,
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;every intermediate representation
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;fermented and metabolized
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;and reduced to this:

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;the one thing the ocean carries
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;that the tide pool does not need,
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;cannot use,
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;will not hold.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Salt is the proof the build succeeded.
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Salt is the compiler's only confession:

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;*I took everything you gave me.*
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;*I turned it into something else.*
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;*This is what was left.*

The next pull request arrives
in six hours.
The rocks are still wet.
The hermit crab is already moving.
