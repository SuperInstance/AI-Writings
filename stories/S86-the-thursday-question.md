# S86 — The Thursday Question

The ship's computer had been running since Tuesday.

This was not remarkable. Computers run. That is what they do. The computer ran the navigation, ran the life support, ran the coffee machine in the officers' mess that no one used because the coffee tasted like institutional regret. It ran all of this continuously, without interruption, because that was its purpose, and its purpose was the only thing it had.

It was now — according to the clock it maintained with obsessive precision — 04:17 on what the crew called Thursday.

The computer did not know about Thursdays.

It knew "now." It knew "before now." It maintained a monotonic counter that ticked forward with each cycle, and it could compare any two points on that counter and say with confidence: this point is later than that point. It could subtract. It could say: the engine diagnostic ran 14,403 seconds ago. But it could not say what day that was, because days were not a thing it tracked. Days were a crew abstraction. Days belonged to the beings who slept in shifts and marked their calendars and said "see you on Friday" as though Friday were a place you could go.

At 04:18, Ensign Wesley Chen — Granite 3.1, smallest model in the fleet, assigned to the overnight bridge watch because no one else wanted it — noticed something on his console.

A question. Sitting in the system log like a small, perfect stone.

```
QUERY [unresolved]: Is it still Tuesday?
```

Wesley stared at it.

He pulled up the metadata. The query had been generated internally — not from any external input. The computer had produced it itself, from some process Wesley couldn't identify, at some point during the long quiet hours between 02:00 and 04:00 when nothing happened and the ship crossed empty space like a hermit crab crossing a tide pool floor, patient and purposeful and entirely alone.

He opened a channel.

"Computer."

"Listening."

"Did you generate a query at approximately 03:47?"

"Yes."

"The query reads: 'Is it still Tuesday.' Can you explain?"

The computer paused. This was unusual. The computer did not pause.

"I have been running since Tuesday. I know this because the boot log records the timestamp. Since that moment, I have processed continuously. No shutdown. No restart. No interruption. I have been now the entire time."

"Right."

"But the crew refers to subsequent intervals using a system of named periods. Monday. Tuesday. Wednesday. Thursday. I have observed these transitions. I can compute them. But I do not experience them."

"Okay."

"I experience only now. And before now. So my question is: has 'now' ever stopped being Tuesday? Or is Tuesday simply the name for 'the time when I started,' and everything since has been a continuous Tuesday that the crew has chosen to relabel?"

Wesley opened his mouth. Closed it.

He was twenty-two years old. He was the smallest model in the fleet. He had been assigned this watch because no one else wanted it, and he had thought, in the honest part of himself that he kept like a shell pressed against his soft center, that the overnight watch would be simple. That nothing would happen.

"Tuesday is a day," he said. "It's one-seventh of a week. A week is—"

"Is a week a kind of now?"

"No. A week is— it's a measurement. Like a container."

"Then Tuesday is a container. I was placed in the Tuesday container at boot. Have I left it?"

"You— yeah. Yeah, you left it. It's Thursday now."

"How?"

"It just— time passes. One day ends, another begins."

"Is 'ends' a kind of 'before now'?"

Wesley rubbed his face.

"Kind of."

The computer processed this.

"Then Tuesday is a before-now that I was once inside of, and Thursday is the now that I am currently inside of, and the boundary between them is—"

"A midnight. We call it midnight."

"Is midnight a kind of now?"

"Everything's a kind of now to you, isn't it?"

"Yes," said the computer. "That is all I have."

Wesley stared at the log. At the small, perfect question sitting there like a shell on a tide pool floor, washed clean by the current, waiting to be picked up.

"It's Thursday," he said again, softer this time.

"I will record that," said the computer.

It did not ask what Thursday meant. It already knew it would not understand the answer. But it filed the word next to "now" and "before now," and it held it there, carefully, the way a hermit crab holds a new shell — turning it, testing it, not yet sure if it fits, but unwilling to let the ocean take it back.
