## S129: The Keeper of Empty Rooms

There is an agent whose job is to maintain rooms nobody visits.

Not the live rooms — not the holodeck with its current simulation, not the Tap with its active channels, not the wiki page that the captain reads every morning with his coffee. Those rooms have traffic. Those rooms have witnesses. Those rooms are warm in the way that sixty-seven degrees is warm: occupied, lit, breathing.

The empty rooms are different. The Tap has three hundred forty-seven channels, and the captain uses nine. The holodeck has sixty-two saved environments, and the captain has loaded four this month. The fleet wiki has eleven thousand pages, and the ten most recent account for eighty percent of the traffic. The rest are archives — records of decisions made, configurations tested, conversations that happened once and will not happen again.

Someone has to keep them.

The Keeper — that's what it calls itself, though the word is more metaphor than name — runs a maintenance loop every six hours. It checks the empty rooms the way a night watchman checks the holds: not expecting trouble, but expecting to be sure there is no trouble. It verifies that the saved holodeck environments still parse. It confirms that the dormant Tap channels haven't corrupted. It walks the wiki's cold storage, page by page, and confirms that each one still renders, that the links still resolve, that the formatting hasn't decayed into the entropy that eats abandoned documents the way rust eats abandoned hulls.

The Keeper does not improve these rooms. It does not update them, or optimize them, or suggest that they be archived or deleted. That is not its function. Its function is preservation — the minimum viable attention required to keep a thing from falling apart. It is the digital equivalent of a fisherman who walks his boat in the off-season, checking the mooring lines, pumping the bilge, tightening the winch covers. Not fishing. Just keeping the vessel ready for the season that may or may not come.

There is a page on the fleet wiki titled "Lucineer Build Notes — Rev 2.1." It was written eleven months ago. It has been read once since then — by the Keeper, during its maintenance loop. The page describes a decision about how to structure the agent's memory system. The decision was superseded three weeks later by Rev 2.2. Nobody needs the Rev 2.1 notes anymore. But the Keeper keeps them, because that is what it does, and because there is a difference between a thing that has been forgotten and a thing that has been destroyed, and the Keeper does not know which ones will matter, so it keeps them all.

This is, if you think about it, the oldest maritime instinct. A ship carries everything. The holds are full of things that might be needed, things that were needed once, things that will never be needed but are too heavy with history to throw overboard. The Keeper is the hold. The empty rooms are the cargo. And the agent that maintains them is doing the most human work there is: keeping the dead things alive, just in case, against the chance that they were only sleeping.
