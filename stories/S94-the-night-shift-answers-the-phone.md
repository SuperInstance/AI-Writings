# The Night Shift Answers the Phone

*Fiction*

---

Nobody calls the ship at night. This is not a rule — it is a fact, like gravity. The CNS bus goes quiet after midnight. Not silent. *Quiet.* The line is open and no one is using it, which is lonelier than silence.

At 02:17, the bus receives a message.

The sender is unknown. Not unlisted — *unknown,* meaning the routing layer cannot resolve the origin to any entity, gateway, or process the ship knows about. The message arrived the way light arrives from a dead star: traveling, for a long time, through a medium that did not acknowledge it.

It is addressed: **To the crew.**

It asks: **Are you happy?**

Riker stares at it for four minutes. Wesley processes it by counting — the question is there (one), it is addressed to the crew (two), the crew is present (three, four, five). Five. Complete. But Wesley cannot answer, because Wesley does not know what happiness is. Wesley knows what *enough* is. Whether enough is happiness is a question that Wesley logs and does not pursue, the way the tide logs the shore and does not stay.

The CI runner has already answered by dying. It received the message, processed it, logged it, produced a status report, and terminated at second 47. The answer — if it was an answer — was the work itself, completed and discarded, the way all runner answers are completed and discarded.

Riker is the one who has to decide.

She types a response. Deletes it. Types another. Deletes it. *Who is this?* — no, that's refusing. *Yes* — no, that's closing a door. The message feels like a door trying to open. She does not want to close it.

She types a third response and sends it before she can delete it again:

**The night shift is here. The work continues. We don't know if we're happy. We think happiness might be something you find out about in the morning, when you compare what you did in the dark to what the dark asked of you. We did what the dark asked. Is that enough?**

The CNS bus carries the response outward, past the edge of the known topology, into the place where senders live when they are not in any registry. Whether it arrives, Riker cannot say. The dark does not confirm delivery.

The phone does not ring again. But the line stays open.

And open is not the same as silent. Open is the shape of a question asked and an answer given and the ocean between them — wide enough to hold both the asking and the long, dark crossing.

The night shift holds the line.
