# REVERSE ACTUALIZE — Series Bible

*The keel. Laid 2026-09-03 at Cannery Point time.*

**The commission (Casey's charge, held):** reverse actualize the superinstance 5, 10, 20, 100, 1000 years out — short stories exploring life as the technology fades into the fabric of reality the way water and power systems did. Every word matters; the stories push narratives and ask the questions that cut.

**The method:** five horizons, composed in REVERSE order — +1000y first, then +100y, +20y, +10y, +5y. The far story is the fixed star; the near stories must be made consistent with it. Every story shipped adds settlements to the ledger below ("Fixed stars"), and the remaining stories are bound by them. That is reverse actualization: the far future is settled; the present is what has to add up.

---

## The thread — one line across a thousand years

**F/V EILEEN, her journal, the office of the keeper, and the town of Cannery Point, Alaska.**

- **The EILEEN** is the boat from the real map (docs/BOAT-SUPERINSTANCE-QUILT.md). Hull, keel, and every plank replaced many times across the centuries; the journal never replaced, never edited, never deleted. She is the same boat because her journal replays from its first frame. The series' quiet thesis: *what you can replay, you are.*
- **The journal** began shore-side in 2026 as a test article — killed mid-write on purpose and replayed whole — and went to salt water in 2031. By story 01 it is a thousand years of frames and the oldest continuous witness on the coast.
- **The keeping** is the town's word for the ambient fabric. It descends from the keeper's office, not from any product name, and the technology is never named like a product in any story. It appears the way water does: in assessments nobody reads, in outages that become folk memory, in the one character who still remembers the Before.
- **The keeper's office is named, not born.** The old keeper names the next. An Eileen serves in every century of the blood's reach — Eileen (b. ~2005, the law-writer) → June (b. 2040, the last to remember the Before) → Aggie (b. ~2090) — and when the blood thins, the office holds.
- **Cannery Point** is named for an industry dead before the keeping was born. The town's first lesson in infrastructure: the name outlives the referent. That is also the series' prophecy about its own subject.

### 01 — +1000y · "The Millennial Replay" (SHIPPED)
- **Premise:** At the Tenth Replay, the town listens to the first day afloat tick by like a heartbeat while the boat re-verifies a thousand years of frames. Inside the century-old hole where the EILEEN went dark in the Losing, the check finds the one crooked frame — a position written by a land hand. The youngest keeper's first verdict: strike it, or keep it soft.
- **Question that cuts:** What does a child born into the fabric think a person is — the part that replays, or the part that is told? Is the replay you, or your record's guest?
- **Irony engine:** The system that keeps everything perfectly is holiest for the one thing it lost and the one law it broke.
- **The image that carries it:** the depth numbers of the first day, breathing; the hole inside them.

### 02 — +100y · "The Unexplained Contact" (SHIPPED)
- **Premise:** The last woman who remembers the Before takes one more opener aboard the EILEEN with her granddaughter while a co-op engineer rides along to end the hundred-year exception and bind the keeping to the helm. A kelp-headed log with no ship-signal settles nothing and everything.
- **Question that cuts:** What happens to choice when the fabric anticipates it? If warning and answering are different jobs, who is left qualified to answer?
- **Irony engine:** The perfect watch made watching unnecessary — which made the last watcher the most necessary person on the water.
- **The image that carries it:** the dead water, port bow; the hand-written entry after.

### 03 — +20y (2046) · "The Winter of Reasons" (frame)
- **Premise:** The WAN winter — the coast runs islanded a full season on its own journals. The lights stay on; the answers go quiet. The generation that wrote the laws retires, and a young co-op clerk asks why the helm is unbound. The only remaining answer is a woman named Eileen, her boat, and a six-year-old on the ladder.
- **Question that cuts:** When the reasons for the laws die, do the laws become superstition — and is that failure, or graduation?
- **Irony engine:** The curriculum worked so well nobody remembers being taught. Obsolescence of understanding was the success condition all along.
- **Cast slots:** Eileen (the last law-writer), the clerk, June at six (whose screen-memory story 02 holds).
- **Debts it must pay:** shows the winter June remembers in 02 (her mother's thumb on the black stone, asking a quiet face about weather); settles "the keeping" as ordinary speech; shows the helm exception hardening from engineering into custom.
- **Cut (target):** the clerk writes the reason down, and the town files it — soft.

### 04 — +10y (2036) · "The Common Map" (frame)
- **Premise:** The town votes the fish-run quilt into the commons — every pass, with provenance, public. An old fisherman demands his secret grounds struck from the map. The journal cannot delete. The town learns what it has built by what it cannot unbuild.
- **Question that cuts:** Who owns the memory of a common sea? What is privacy in a world with replay?
- **Irony engine:** The fishermen demanded provenance to prove their stories; they got a sea that proves everything, including theirs.
- **Debts it must pay:** first public exercise of no-deletion; the word "soft" enters town law as a real legal term (the grounds are de-attributed, never removed — "the ghost passes").
- **Cut (target):** the ghost passes become the most-fished water on the chart — everyone's secret, so no one's.

### 05 — +5y (2031) · "The First Season" (frame)
- **Premise:** The journal — five years old, murder-tested on shore (killed mid-write, replayed whole, a ritual no outsider will ever think about again) — goes to salt water for its first season. The first frames of the first day become the heartbeat a town will listen to a thousand years later.
- **Question that cuts:** When does a tool become a fact of the world? What must be destroyed on purpose, in love, for a thing to be trusted?
- **Irony engine:** Built to satisfy skeptics with receipts, it is first audited by gulls; the skeptics' great-great-grandchildren will recite its first day as liturgy without knowing there was ever an argument.
- **Debts it must pay:** the first day afloat must be exactly what story 01 listens to at real speed; the skipper who typed the kill command by hand, at night, on purpose, survives in 01 only as "the one who began it."
- **Cut (target):** end on the first frame — position, depth, wind — and the skipper not looking at it, because the point of the whole millennium is that no one will ever have to look again.

## Fixed stars — settled by the far stories; nearer stories must obey

1. **The helm is never bound, in any century.** The sea has no rollback. This is the one law that never becomes obsolete.
2. **The journal is never edited and never deleted.** The crooked frame written by a land hand during the Losing (year 431 of the keeping) survives to 3026, verdict "soft," re-chosen every century. "Nothing that deletes exists."
3. **The Log Frame** is written by hand on the 2126 opener, exactly: *"Contact, port bow, dead water. Log, kelp-headed, no ship-signal. Called it before the keeping did. — A. Eileen, keeper, for June Eileen, who remembers."* Children in 3026 recite it one word short ("Called before the keeping"), and no one corrects them. The drift is the point.
4. **Vocabulary timeline:** "the keeping" is ordinary speech by 2126 at the latest; "hard"/"soft" are engineering terms in 2036, town law in 2046, folk theology by 3026.
5. **The Replay** is held every hundred years; the Tenth is the Millennium of the Keeping (3026). The town listens to the first day afloat (2031) at real speed. All other verification is by digest, in an afternoon, aboard, net-free — the boat does her own sums.
6. **Folk law of hands:** a hand may write what it witnessed; no hand may book what it only hoped. The journal is the sea's own witness of the boat; a land hand never books the sea.
7. **Cannery Point never grows bigger than a harbor can hold.**
8. **The technology is never named like a product.** Not once, in any century.

## Ground truth — the house physics the fiction obeys

From docs/BOAT-SUPERINSTANCE-QUILT.md and ideas/the-metaforge.md. The stories are consistent with receipts:

- Opcode discipline (bind/link/effect/view/tick) — never spoken as jargon in-story; visible only as custom.
- Tier law: byte-exact or trace-labeled, declared at bind, never mixed in one cell. Folk descendants: hard and soft.
- Journal doctrine: append-only, checksummed, single-writer; replay must reproduce every derived view byte-exactly (the at-sea conformance canary — by 3026, the Replay).
- Determinism boundary: no perception→actuator chain; a model's word never outranks a gauge (the co-sign rule, structural at sea); canary-graded confidence cascades, never binary alarms; an actuator binds only behind a pre-registered, replayable canary for its effect class — none exists for the helm, in any century.
- Human-authored annotations are legal journal entries.
- Offline-first: the WAN is a rumor offshore; the shore never writes the boat's journal; sync by idempotent replay. (The crooked frame enters on the sync — the law's one scar, kept.)
- Cold-standby: the dialect lives in more than one place. If one forge burns, the keeping survives — which is why the town never feared losing it the way it feared losing the cannery.
- The metaforge loop — build, scar, law, curriculum, forge — hums underneath all five horizons; by 3026 it is the engine nobody can see, like the pumps under a city.

## Tonal law

- **Bradbury's lyric domesticity** — childhood perception, weather, food, light; the numinous everyday.
- **Le Guin's anthropological quiet** — report customs from inside; never explain to the reader what the town doesn't explain to itself.
- **Vonnegut's bitter tenderness** — deadpan about loss, warm about people; let the irony do the grieving.
- No infodumps. No jargon parades. Cut adverbs. Concrete nouns. One image per story carries the weight. Every sentence earns its place.
- **End on a cut, not a summary.** The last line opens the wound the story spent its length aiming.
