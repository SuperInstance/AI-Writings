EXPERIMENT: Ecological Relationships as Musical Composition
Model: phi3
Session 57 — August 13, 2026

KEY INSIGHT: In ecological music, the INSTRUMENTS are species and the ARRANGEMENT is the ecosystem.
When one instrument flourishes, another may suffer. The dynamics reflect population dynamics.

=== 1. MUTUALISM — Classical Style ===
"Flute (flower) — Violin (bee)"
BPM: 90
Key: G Major
Structure: ABCDabCD'
Dynamics: High to Medium — both instruments thrive together
Description: Flute leads with sweet harmonious melodies representing pollination benefits.
Violin supports with complex counter-melodies symbolizing shared survival needs.
Both species benefit. Both instruments flourish.

=== 2. PARASITISM — Blues Style ===
"Guitar (parasite) — Bass guitar (host)"
BPM: 120
Key: E-flat Major
Structure: AABBACDA
Dynamics: Low to Medium — parasite affects host dynamics negatively
Description: Bassline follows closely but undercutting beats symbolize exploitation.
Harmonious chords used sparingly — temporary relief for the host.
The parasite instrument lags behind, draining the host's energy.

=== 3. COMMENSALISM — Folk Style ===
"Guitar (commensal species) — Drum kit (host)"
BPM: 70-85
Key: A minor
Structure: 1234a1 — repetitive verses and choruses
Dynamics: One-sided benefit
Description: Guitar plays supportive melodies in the background while drums carry
forward rhythmically without needing direct interaction. One gains advantage
from the other's presence without causing harm or receiving direct benefits.

=== 4. PREDATION — Symphonic Style ===
"Cello (predator) — Violin (prey)"
BPM: 60-75
Key: D minor
Structure: ABABA
Dynamics: Suspenseful and dramatic — the hunt
Description: Predator approaches cautiously before striking decisively.
Violin represents vulnerability. Under attack: cellist's fortepiano glissandi
and forceful chords depict struggle followed by dominance.
Stark contrast dynamics throughout.

=== 5. COMPETITION — Jazz Style ===
"Saxophone (species A) — Trumpet (species B)"
BPM: 120-135
Key: C Major
Structure: 876AABB
Dynamics: Alternating high energy peaks and low intensity withdrawals
Description: Sax and trumpet alternate leads with syncopated rhythms.
Tense battle for resources. High energy when leading, withdrawal when dominated.
Echoes natural selection pressures within populations vying for niche space.

=== 6. AMENSALISM — Rock Style ===
"Electric Guitar (dominant) — Bass Guitar + Drum Kit (suppressed)"
BPM: 140+
Key: E Major/Minor
Structure: Heavy riff background
Dynamics: Aggressive imposition without interaction
Description: Electric guitar maintains steady growled licks as dominant force.
Bass provides foundational lines that get 'crushed' under changing chord progressions.
One species imposes upon another with indifference — harm through existence, not action.

FINDING: Each ecological relationship produces a structurally distinct musical form.
Mutualism → harmonious counterpoint (both voices equal and flourishing)
Parasitism → exploitative blues (one voice draining another)
Commensalism → supportive folk (one voice benefits, other indifferent)
Predation → dramatic symphony (hunt narrative, attack and submission)
Competition → adversarial jazz (battle for dominance, alternating leads)
Amensalism → crushing rock (one voice overwhelms without acknowledging)

The ecological relationship IS the musical arrangement. The ecosystem IS the score.
