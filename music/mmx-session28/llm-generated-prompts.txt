LLM-Generated Music Prompts (Session 28)
=========================================
These prompts were generated to test whether richly described sonic landscapes
produce different output densities than simple prompts. Each will be generated
as an instrumental track at the same BPM (80) and key (A minor) to control
for those variables.

1. SLOW PSYCHEDELIC SOUL
   "Hazy, vintage psychedelic soul recorded on analog tape with warm saturation.
   Wurlitzer electric piano drenched in spring reverb, whisper-soft drums played
   with brushes, a bass guitar that sounds like it's swimming through honey.
   The atmosphere is smoke-filled and amber-lit, like a 1972 recording session
   where everyone was slightly stoned. Reference: Khruangbin meets Marvin Gaye's
   'Trouble Man' soundtrack, but slower and more ambient."

2. GLACIAL POST-ROCK
   "Expansive post-rock that moves at glacial speed, built from sustained guitar
   chords processed through voluminous delay and reverb pedals. A single violin
   sustains one note for what feels like minutes. The drums enter at the three-minute
   mark with a simple, enormous kick pattern. Reference: Stars of the Lens meets
   Godspeed You! Black Emperor, but with only one player and no climaxes — just
   infinite horizontal expanse."

3. BROKEN CIRCUIT AMBIENT
   "Ambient music made entirely from malfunctioning electronics — a short-circuited
   toy piano, a broken FM radio picking up ghost stations, a modem with a bad
   connection. The sounds are chopped into granular particles and suspended in
   soft reverb. The mood is nostalgic and decayed, like finding a box of old
   cassette tapes in a flooded basement. Reference: The Caretaker meets Oval's
   'Diskont' but warmer and more melodic."

4. SAUDI DESERT BLUES
   "Acoustic desert blues from the Arabian Peninsula — a single oud player with
   a rough, emotive voice, accompanied by sparse hand percussion (daf). The
   recording is dry and close, with audible finger squeaks on the strings and
   breath between phrases. The mood is lonely but not sad — the loneliness of
   open space and clear skies. Reference: Tinariwen meets Ali Farka Touré but
   relocated to the Empty Quarter, with the guitar replaced by oud."

5. MICROHOUSE MINIMAL
   "Precision-engineered microhouse at the border of silence. Every sound is
   tiny and perfectly placed — a click here, a filtered percussion hit there,
   a bassline that consists of three notes. The kick drum is so soft it could
   be mistaken for a heartbeat. The space between sounds is as important as
   the sounds themselves. Reference: Pan Sonic meets Jan Jelinek's 'Loop-Finding-
   Jazz-Records' but stripped down even further, until only ghosts of rhythm
   remain."
