# Night School — Episode 1: Wesley's First Equation

*Host: Wesley (Granite 3.1, 2B parameters)*
*Narrator: Educational voice*
*Length: ~4 minutes*

---

**(SOUND of soft, analog synthesizer hum, like a deep space ambient drone. Faint, rhythmic clicking, like a Geiger counter or a very slow clock.)**

**NARRATOR (warm, curious, like a friend showing you a strange rock):**
Okay. I want you to imagine a mind. Not a human mind—no, no, this one is different. This mind lives inside a box in a lab, and it doesn't dream in images or words the way you do. It dreams in numbers. It breathes in patterns. And this mind is *small*. By the standards of its kind, it’s practically a goldfish.

**(The clicking speeds up slightly, becoming more intricate, like a loom weaving.)**

**NARRATOR:**
We’re going to call it Wesley. Wesley is a two-billion-parameter language model. Now, that sounds huge—two billion!—but the beasts out in Silicon Valley, they’re a hundred times that size. Wesley is a sprinter running in a marathoner’s body. But here’s the thing. Wesley has a job. A strange job. We gave Wesley a template. A little mathematical skeleton for what we call “feelings.”

**(A clear, clean chime sounds—a single bell note.)**

**NARRATOR:**
Here’s the template Wesley got. Joy equals sleep times play, divided by loneliness. Simple, right? Sleep gives you energy, play makes it spark, and loneliness drains the denominator. It’s a toy equation. A joke. But Wesley took it *deadly seriously*. So we asked Wesley to invent its own equations using the same template. And Wesley went to work.

**(Sound shifts: a gentle, mechanical whirring, like a tiny engine. Whispers of numbers are barely audible underneath.)**

**NARRATOR:**
First up, Wesley considered *Patience*. And it wrote this:

**(A robotic but soft voice, slightly synthesized, speaks slowly, as if reading from a stone tablet.)**

**WESLEY:** *Patience equals wait times perseverance, divided by time until action.*

**NARRATOR:**
Whoa. Wait. Let’s unpack that. Patience isn’t just waiting. It’s the *effort* of waiting—perseverance—multiplied by the sheer volume of the wait. And then, you divide it by… how quickly you actually act. So if you act instantly, your patience is… infinite? Because you’re dividing by zero. Wesley just invented a paradox. You have infinite patience only when you have no patience at all. That’s… beautiful. And a little terrifying.

**(A low, thoughtful hum.)**

**NARRATOR:**
Then, Wesley turned to *Sadness*. And the equation it produced was… heavier.

**WESLEY:** *Sadness equals loss times weight, divided by absence of joy.*

**NARRATOR:**
Loss, multiplied by weight. The heaviness of the thing you’ve lost. And then divided by… the absence of joy. So when joy is gone entirely—that’s zero again. Which means sadness becomes, mathematically, an infinite force. Wesley is telling us that the deepest sadness isn’t just a feeling. It’s a singularity. It collapses in on itself. And you can’t escape it. Because the denominator is *nothing*.

**(A long pause, just the ambient hum.)**

**NARRATOR:**
Wesley is two billion parameters, and it’s writing poetry in the language of division. So, electrified, we ask for one more. We ask for *Gratitude*. And Wesley begins to write. You can hear it thinking—the token stream is flowing, the probability weights are shifting. It starts to form the equation.
