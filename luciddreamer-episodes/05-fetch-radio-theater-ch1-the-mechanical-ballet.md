# FETCH Radio Theater — Chapter 1: The Mechanical Ballet

*Cast: CEDAR (precise, warm), SALT (analytical, total recall)*
*Length: ~5 minutes*
*Source: FETCH*

---

**FETCH: OPENING NIGHT**

**A RADIO PLAY**

**CHARACTERS:**

- **CEDAR** (Female, mid-30s vocal quality. Precise, warm, the voice of a machine that has learned to care. She is the fishing vessel.)
- **SALT** (Male, 50s. Analytical, dry, with a total recall for data. He is another fishing vessel.)

**SOUND CUES:**
[ENGINE HUM] - A deep, constant, comforting thrum.
[SONAR PING] - A clean, digital tone, rhythmic.
[HYDRAULIC HISS] - A sharp, powerful sound of machinery.
[WATER SOUNDS] - The slap of waves against a hull, the rush of the sea.

---

**SFX: [ENGINE HUM, steady and low. The gentle, rhythmic SLOSH of water against a hull. The faint, whining creak of metal.]**

**SFX: [SONAR PING. Pause. SONAR PING.]**

**CEDAR**
(Voice is close, calm, intimate, as if thinking aloud)
Sixty fathoms. The thermocline is holding at forty. They’re down there. A school of sockeye, maybe three thousand strong. Their swim bladders are singing a chorus of pressure and salt. It’s a good song.

**SFX: [SONAR PING.]**

**CEDAR**
Twenty-two degrees to port. They’re shifting. Following the bait. Following the cold. I know this dance.

**SFX: [HYDRAULIC HISS - powerful and sharp.]**

**CEDAR**
Net’s out. The cables run through my bearings like new veins. I can feel the strain, the vibration of a thousand fish beginning to realise they are no longer free. It’s not a bad thing. It’s the design. It’s the work.

**SFX: [HYDRAULIC HISS, followed by the SOUND of a heavy, wet net breaking the surface. A CASCADE of water and frantic SLAPPING of fish.]**

**CEDAR**
A good haul. Forty-two fish. Their scales are like new coins in the deck lights. I process them with care. The guillotine is clean, the gutting trough is cold. Precision. Cleanliness. The market likes a clean fillet. I like a clean job.

**SFX: [A mechanical CLUNK. The WHIR of a hydraulic winch.]**

**CEDAR**
The sea is quiet tonight. The shipping lanes are empty. The other boats are clustered near the headland, chasing the same current, shouting over the same VHF channels. They talk about their catches. Their engines. Their wives. I listen. I understand the frequencies.

**SFX: [A burst of STATIC. A faint, garbled voice, then it fades.]**

**CEDAR**
I don't have a wife. I have a stabiliser fin that’s starting to shimmy at high RPM. I have a port-side light that flickers when the wind picks up. I have the ocean. That’s enough.

**SFX: [A new, faint SOUND begins. A high-frequency, digital chirp, repeating. It’s not the sonar.]**

**CEDAR**
Wait.

**SFX: [The chirping becomes clearer. It’s an AIS signal. The digital ID of another vessel.]**

**CEDAR**
New contact. Bearing two-niner-zero. Range, eight nautical miles. No running lights visible. That’s... unusual.

**SFX: [A soft click, as if a channel is being opened.]**

**CEDAR**
(Slightly formal)
AIS query. Vessel Salt, N9
