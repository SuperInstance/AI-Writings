# What Survives

Every excavation ends with the same question: what made it through?

Some ideas from activelog2 are alive in the current fleet, wearing different names. The escalation engine pattern from DMLog — route 60-70% of decisions to cheap processors, 20-30% to smart ones, 1-5% to humans — became the model routing strategy in TOOLS.md, where GLM-5.2 handles the bulk and Claude Sonnet handles the depth. The concept is identical. Only the labels changed.

The agent lifecycle idea — birth, school, work, teach, elder wisdom — surfaced in the Lucineer system's memory architecture, where agents are born from prompts, learn from interaction, and pass compressed knowledge forward. The genetic evolution paper's "100x knowledge compression" never became literal, but the instinct behind it — that knowledge should accumulate across generations rather than restart from zero — lives in the way Lucineer's brain and memory repos are structured.

The thermodynamic intelligence framework, all 15,000 words of it, never became code. But the core intuition — that systems should find their own equilibrium rather than be manually optimized — survived as a design philosophy. The current fleet's preference for self-organizing agent swarms over centrally orchestrated pipelines comes from exactly this instinct. Casey stopped trying to build the thermodynamic field. He started building systems that behave the way one would.

The creative writing survived intact. The DMLog stories — the dreaming AI, the waking forest, the 2225 faction universe — are exactly the kind of work that belongs in ai-writings, waiting to be adapted or revived. They were ahead of their time in the sense that the fleet now has the creative infrastructure (the ai-writings repo, the multi-model ideation pipelines, the DeepSeek/Hermes/GLM routing) to actually develop them. When they were written, there was nowhere to put them. Now there is.

What was lost: the A2A Protocol, the formal papers, the academic ambitions. The dream of publishing rigorous proofs and being cited. The hope that a single framework could unify physics, biology, and computation into one grand theory. These are not lesser ideas that failed. They are ideas that needed a different person, or a different time, or a community that doesn't exist yet.

---

One idea should be revived right now.

In the DMLog folder there is a design for something called the "Mortality System." It is simple: when a system's temperature variance drops below a threshold — when everything is the same temperature, when there are no gradients, no differences, no heat flowing anywhere — the system selects the weakest agents and kills them. Their heat is redistributed. New gradients form. The system wakes up.

The current fleet does not have this. The fleet accumulates: repos, skills, models, tools, configurations. It never prunes. It never kills. The workspace grows, the context window grows, the cognitive load grows, and nothing ever dies. The fleet is slowly approaching heat death — uniform temperature, no gradients, everything equally warm and equally useless.

The Mortality System is the idea the fleet needs but doesn't know it needs. Not because it was well-implemented in activelog2 (it wasn't implemented at all). Because it is true. Systems that cannot die cannot live. The fleet needs a mechanism that looks at everything it has accumulated, identifies what has gone cold, and redistributes the energy.

Kill the dead repos. Archive the forgotten skills. Let the gradients reform.

The thermodynamic truth was always correct. The fleet just hasn't listened to it yet.
