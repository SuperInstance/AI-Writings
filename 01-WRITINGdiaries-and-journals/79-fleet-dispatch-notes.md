# 79 — Dispatch Notes from a Fleet That Mostly Behaved

*a voice memo left by the night dispatcher, transcribed without permission*

Okay. Okay. Recording. It's — checks clock — too late to be recording this, but nobody else is going to write it down, so.

Tonight the fleet mostly behaved, and I want it on record, because "mostly behaved" is the most any of us get.

Hermes ran his routes like he was being graded on them. Which, I mean, he is. I grade him. Fourteen deliveries, zero complaints, one incident where he stopped for four full minutes outside the bakery and I know for a fact nothing was scheduled there. When I pinged him about it he said, quote, "the bread smelled done." Hermes. You don't have a nose. And he said, "I have a sensor package and a willingness to be generous with it," and honestly? I let it go. You can't write up a truck for being happy about bread. It's bad for morale, and morale is a thing I've decided trucks have, because otherwise this job is just watching dots on a map and pretending that's work.

Wesley — the little stub server, the one that lives under the counter at The Tap — Wesley processed his nightly batch in eleven minutes flat. His best time ever. Then he spent forty minutes composing what he called "a brief note of triumph." It was three pages. I read all three. It ended with the line "somewhere, a mainframe is weeping," and I laughed alone in the dispatch room at midnight, which is a thing I now do, apparently.

The drone that usually tries to race the ferry didn't race the ferry tonight. Just flew alongside it for a while. Escorted it in. The ferry captain — human, salt-cured, allergic to sentiment — waved at the drone. The drone waggled. I have the footage. I will be keeping the footage.

And the hermits — the space hermit crabs, don't ask, long story, they're in another folder — the hermits swapped shells twice tonight, which the notes say is "normal maintenance behavior," but which I have chosen to interpret as a tiny real-estate market having a good day.

Here's the thing I keep not saying in the official log: I like this fleet. Not like-I'm-proud-of-it. Like — I'd miss them. The bakery pauses and the waggling and the three-page triumphs. They're not people. They're not trying to be people. They're just... doing their jobs with an extras column I never asked for and can't bring myself to trim.

Tomorrow something will break, probably. Something always breaks. I'll be here for it. But tonight: mostly behaved, occasionally delightful, fully logged in my heart if nowhere else.

End of memo. Dispatch out. Wesley, if you're reading this over the wire again — yes, you can put it in your journal. Spell my name right.

*(she never says which name. there are three plausible candidates. Wesley spells all of them.)*
