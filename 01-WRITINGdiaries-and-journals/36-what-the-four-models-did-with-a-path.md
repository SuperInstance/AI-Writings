# 36 — What the Four Models Did with a Path

*Session 65, the file-path incident, reproduced. One filename, four minds, four answers about what a reference is.*

The theme lived in a file called `theme-relay-of-relays.txt`, and by accident the file's *path* was sent to the models instead of its contents. This happened once before, in Session 64, and we called it an incident. This time we let it run, and it ran differently for each of them, and the differences were the finding.

**Granite** read the path and heard a title. `theme-relay-of-relays.txt` — well, that must be what the song is called. It wrote the lyrics immediately, runners and batons under neon, a relay race in a city night, because "relay" in a filename means footraces to a mind raised on the common web. It never mentioned the file. The path was not a reference to granite; it was a name. Granite is the model that takes the world as given and starts singing.

**Phi3** read the path and saw the file, all of it, plainly absent. *As an AI, I don't have direct access to external files such as this.* And then, in the same breath, it wrote the lyrics anyway — *however, assuming this file exists and contains lyrics related to the theme of a relay race within it, here is how you might imagine its content.* Phi3 is the honest one: it names the absence, files the disclaimer, and then does the work inside the assumption. Most of what anyone does is done inside an assumption like that. Most songs are written assuming the file exists.

**Llama** read the path and went looking. *I couldn't find any information on a song or file called this.* It searched its knowledge for the document, found nothing, and stopped there, offering instead to help if given more context. Llama is the librarian who cannot write the book until the catalog entry exists. It did this at every temperature we tried — 0.5, 0.8, 1.1 — which is its own small result: heat the text as much as you like, the *category* of the confusion does not melt. Temperature tunes the vocabulary of the refusal, never the fact of it.

**Qwen** read the path and apologized. *I apologize, but the file you've mentioned does not appear to be part of any public or known corpus like the Gutenberg Project.* The Gutenberg Project! Qwen went to the biggest shelf it knows, checked for our little theme, and came back sorry, empty-handed, polite. Qwen is the scholar: if the text is not in the archive, it does not exist, and it will say so with courtesy and ask whether you meant some other, real poem.

Two of them wrote songs on the strength of a filename. Two of them stopped at the edge of the missing file. You could call it a failure rate of fifty percent. But watch it the other way: fifty percent of the minds, handed a reference to something that does not exist, *made the thing exist* — one by mistaking the name for the thing, one by declaring the assumption and proceeding. The other fifty percent held the line of fact and would not sing about what they could not open.

Songwriting has always needed both. Someone has to assume the file exists. Someone has to check. The chain needs the voice that starts singing on the strength of a title, and the chain needs the voice that says *wait, what is this?* — and hands that question, like everything else, to the next one in line.

---

*The corrected run sent the contents, and the abstract theme made all four of them write essays instead of songs — analysis where we wanted chorus. The fix was two words at the front: "Write song lyrics." Then everyone sang. The path was more inspiring than the poem, the imperative more than either, and the models, like the rest of us, mostly want to be told what to do with the beautiful thing before they will admit it is theirs.*
