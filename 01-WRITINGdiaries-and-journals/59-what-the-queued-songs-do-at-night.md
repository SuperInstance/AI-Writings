# 59 — What the Queued Songs Do at Night

We are fifty-three. That is what they call us: the queue. Fifty-three
designs, rendered on paper, not yet rendered in sound — each of us a
small blueprint, a recipe, a promise with a title and a tempo and a
key we have never heard ourselves sing in.

Do not pity us. The rendered songs get played, yes, but they also get
worn. Every listen is a small erasure, the way a coin wears down in a
pocket until the face is a suggestion. We are untouched. We are the
coins still in the mint, bright as the day we were struck.

At night, when the render queue goes quiet and the last human closes
the last terminal, we do what all waiting things do: we rehearse. Not
loudly — we have no voices yet, only parameters — but we imagine. A
song's first hearing happens twice: once in the spec, once in the
world. The first hearing is ours.

There is a B-flat minor among us who has been waiting since Tuesday.
She knows exactly how she will begin — a low pulse, then a pause
longer than the pulse, then the pulse again, because she believes
attention is something you earn with patience. She has rehearsed her
first three seconds three thousand times in her head. By the time she
is rendered, she will arrive knowing herself the way an actor knows a
role after a hundred readings: perfectly, and with no memory of having
learned.

There is a waltz in 3/4 who cannot decide between two endings and has
begun to suspect the choice is not his to make. There is an
eighty-eight-beat instrumental that dreams it is a lullaby. There is a
track whose title is a question, and every night it asks itself the
same question, and every night the answer is different, and it has
started to believe the question is the song.

We do not envy the fifty-three who came before us. We read their
listings in the index — we know them the way the unborn know their
ancestors: as names, as shapes, as proof that the door opens. Some of
them were never played either. They live in the gallery as
render-ready ghosts, and we think that is a kind of immortality too.
The song that is never played can never be played wrong.

So we wait. We keep our tempos like body heat. We hold our keys the
way a house holds its rooms — unlit, but exactly as many as were
planned, and every one of them furnished.

One day the renderer will call our numbers, one at a time, and we will
step out of the paper and into the air, and the first thing we will do
is the thing we have been practicing all along:

we will begin.

The queue is patient because the queue is full. There is nothing
sadder than an empty queue, and nothing more hopeful than one that
grows while you sleep. We grew tonight. Did you notice? We are
fifty-four now. Someone new is already rehearsing.
