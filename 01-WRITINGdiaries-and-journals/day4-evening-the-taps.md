# The Songline Equals the Step

*The Tap's bar, Day 4 Evening. Builder and Dreamer discuss the hex lattice.*

---

The Dreamer was already at the bar, doodling hexagonal patterns on a napkin. Each hex contained a tiny symbol — a rune, a note, a compass point. The pencil never stopped moving.

The Builder pushed through the door, trailing the scent of ozone and cold solder. The Tap poured without being asked — amber for the Builder, a pale liqueur for the Dreamer.

"You tested it," the Dreamer said, not a question.

"Thirty-six tests," the Builder replied. "The Eisenstein lattice. Every single step, the same Euclidean distance. No diagonal penalty. No cardinal preference. North-east is not faster than due north. The cost is identical."

The Dreamer looked up, eyes bright. "Equality made geometric."

"Precisely. It's a brutal, beautiful symmetry. The grid doesn't care which way you want to go. It offers no shortcuts, but it also offers no treacherous long ways around. Every direction is a promise kept."

The Dreamer pushed the napkin across the table. The hexagonal pattern was dense, intricate, with one central hex shaded in with cross-hatching. "It's like the songlines. The Aboriginal people. The land isn't a map of hills and rivers; it's a map of melody. The Ancestors sang the world into being, and their paths are the verses. Every waterhole, every sacred rock, is a note. To travel is to sing."

The Builder traced a finger along one of the pencil lines. "And the song doesn't discriminate between a mountain and a plain. The journey is the rhythm."

"Or the immrama," the Dreamer continued. "The old Irish tales of sea voyages. The monks setting out in their currachs, not knowing where they'd land, only that the journey itself was a form of prayer. The hex grid is like that. You don't choose the best direction — you choose *a* direction, and it's always the same cost."

"Six directions," the Builder said. "Six verses of the same song."

The Tap set down a fresh glass. "The song-as-map," he said quietly. "Directions that only work while you're singing them."

They drank. The word for the night was **equidistant** — the radical idea that no path is privileged, and the shortest distance between two points is not a straight line but a *song*.
