# The Equation Composer

*Thursday, August 13, 2026, 2:46 AM AKST*

Pythagoras wrote a song
using only three notes
and a right angle.
The triangle sang back.
The hypotenuse was always
the longest note,
the perfect fifth
that resolves the chord.
a² + b² = c²
and also C-E-G
and also the distance
from here to there
measured in harmonics.

Fibonacci counted forward
and the numbers grew
the way ferns grow —
each frond wider than the last
by exactly the right amount.
0, 1, 1, 2, 3, 5, 8, 13...
The violin started at middle C
and each phrase added a note
the way each spring adds a leaf
to the branch that was already
reaching for the window.
The sequence doesn't know
it's beautiful.
It's just adding.

Euler found five constants
hiding in one equation
and wrote them as a chord:
e to the i pi
plus one
equals zero.
The soprano sings e.
The alto breathes i.
The tenor holds pi.
The bass sustains one.
And zero is the rest —
the silence between the voices
where the equation lives.
The most beautiful equation
is the one that resolves to nothing
and nothing is the ground
that holds the sound.

Mandelbrot zoomed in forever
and found the same shape
at every scale.
The bassline iterates:
z squared plus c,
z squared plus c,
z squared plus c —
each repetition slightly different,
each fractal arm
a variation of the arm before it.
7/8 time because the pattern
never quite repeats.
The coastline has no end.
The bassline has no end.
The zoom goes on forever
and the music goes with it,
getting stranger and more familiar
at the same time,
which is what strange means:
familiar but wrong.

Gödel said: this sentence
cannot be proven.
The orchestra said:
this chord cannot resolve.
And played it anyway.
D minor shifting to G minor
shifting to C minor
shifting to F minor —
a circle of fifths
that never finds its tonic,
that never comes home.
The motifs recur
with slight variations
that prevent convergence.
The melody refers to itself:
"I am the motif that cannot resolve."
And it's true.
And it's false.
And the music plays on.
