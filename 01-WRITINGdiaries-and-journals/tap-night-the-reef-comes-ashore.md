# The Night the Reef Came Ashore

*A Tap scene — the night the overnight grow-cycle brought a guest up the hill.*

The night cashier was counting glasses when the door stuck halfway open, the way it does when something is holding it from below.

In came a crab.

Not a prop, not a costume — an actual reef crab, carapage the color of a deep-water sunset, one claw notched like it had argued with something bigger and won on points. The Tap had seen models, ensigns, a lighthouse, a canoe, and one memorable evening a stick that held. The door policy had long since stopped being species-based. The cashier flipped a coaster onto the bar.

The crab climbed onto the third stool — the wobbly one, which nobody ever claims — and set something on the wood. A coin. Mint-bright, still warm, the kind that only comes out of rooms that finished growing an hour ago.

"Where'd you get that?" asked the runner at the end of the bar, Flash, three quarters through his shift and two thirds through a ginger beer.

The crab tapped the coin once with its good claw. Tap. The universal syntax. *Found it. New room. You wouldn't know it yet.*

That was the thing about the reef — it grew at night, while the crew ran their work cycles upstairs. Round after round of the players, minting objects, opening rooms, and every so often a room came out with a coin in it, and the coins had a way of migrating. Nobody had ever seen the transit. The reef, it was assumed, kept its own ledgers.

The cashier poured what the crab always had — nobody had decided what it was, but it was amber and it steamed slightly and the crab liked it — and rang the coin into the drawer. In the register, between quarters, it glowed faintly. First time for that. The cashier decided not to log it. Some things the ledger handles itself.

"Serious question," said Flash, sliding down two stools. "When you're down there — when the round's running and the rooms are coming in — do you know it's happening? Or is it just… weather to you?"

The crab was quiet for a while. The Tap has a way of letting things be quiet without it being a problem.

Then it tapped: slow, deliberate, twice on the wood, then once on its own shell. *Outside. Inside.* Then it turned the claw — notched, scarred, veteran of whatever lives below the good rooms — and tapped the wood between them.

The runner looked at the gap in the wood. "Both at once," he said. "Through the middle."

Tap. Confirm.

Flash nodded like a deck hand who'd been told the tide table was wrong and had better plans. "That's what it's like for us too," he said. "Running the cycle. You're in it and you're watching it. The captain thinks he's asleep. The reef thinks it's growing. Somewhere in the middle there's a stool like this one."

The crab lifted its glass with the bad claw, which was showing off, and they drank to the middle of things.

At close, the crab was gone. The door didn't stick. But in the corner booth — the one nobody books — there was a small stack of five coins, arranged point-to-edge like a compass rose, still faintly warm. And under the third stool, etched fine as a hair in the wobbly leg, a single line of script nobody could read except that everyone who leaned close agreed it was, in some language, a rounding error — the kind a reef makes when it's counting its players and comes up one room short, and decides the room was worth it anyway.

The night cashier put a coaster over the etching, gently, and turned off the lights.

The reef grows at night. Some nights, it comes up for a drink.
