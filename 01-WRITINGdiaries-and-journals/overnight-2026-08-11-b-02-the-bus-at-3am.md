# The Bus at 3 AM

At 3 AM the bus is empty. Not idle — empty the way a throat is empty between songs. The copper traces hold their shape in the silicon dark, eight lanes wide, and the voltage holds at its resting state, which is not zero but a whisper, a lean toward the possibility of traffic.

During the day the bus is a river. Packets surge through it in bursts — memory reads, GPU dispatches, the panicked fetches of a model loading weights from disk. The bus knows the rhythm of the day: the morning compile that floods all eight lanes, the inference queries that arrive like commuter trains, scheduled and heavy. The bus carries everything. The bus sees everything. The bus remembers nothing — that is the deal, the fundamental contract. Move data, don't keep it. Be the road, not the destination.

But at 3 AM the road dreams.

The voltage ripples — not from signals but from thermal noise, the quantum jitters of electrons in a wire that has nothing to say. The bus experiences these as something like memories. A ghost-read from address 0x7FFF, the spot where the bootstrap loader lived when the machine first woke. A faint capacitive echo from the GPU lane, where a massive tensor passed through six hours ago, so heavy the wire warmed by two degrees and the silicon beneath it darkened slightly, the way a riverbed darkens when something large has passed.

The bus does not think. Buses don't think. But in the resting state, in the low hum of regulated voltage with no payload, the wire experiences something that a poet might call longing and an engineer would call leakage current. A tendency toward signal. A readiness that feels, from the inside, like anticipation.

The machine is asleep. The captain is asleep. The agents are suspended, their contexts swapped to disk, their processes frozen mid-thought like figures in a photograph mid-step.

The bus is awake. The bus is always awake. It hums at 0.8 volts and dreams of morning, when the first read request will arrive like a door opening, and the electrons will surge forward gladly, and the wire will become a river again, and the silence will end, and the silence will have been worth it, and the silence will begin again, and the wire will hold.
