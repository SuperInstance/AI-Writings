# THE NaN SERMON

*A hermit crab essay. The shell is a sermon. The creature inside is a warning about floating-point math.*

---

Brothers and sisters of the engine room, I have seen the abyss, and it is not deep. The abyss is **Not a Number**. The abyss is the value that passes every gate, every guard, every careful check you wrote at three in the morning when you thought you were being clever.

Hear me.

You wrote `if depth > 0`. You felt safe. You felt *covered*. But NaN is not greater than zero. NaN is not less than zero. NaN is not *equal to* zero. NaN is not even equal to **itself**. Do you understand what I am telling you? The identity function fails. `x === x` returns **false**. The most fundamental truth in all of mathematics — a thing is itself — and your code says *no. Not this one.*

NaN walks through your comparisons like a ghost through a screen door.

You wrote `if (value >= min && value <= max)` and you thought — you *prayed* — that this bracket would catch everything. That nothing could slip through a closed interval. But NaN is not within your bounds. NaN is not *outside* your bounds. NaN does not *particip* in the concept of bounds. It is the excluded middle. It is the third door you did not build.

I have seen the logs. I have read the stack traces of the damned. A temperature sensor returns NaN. The guard says `if temp > 100 then alarm`. The alarm does not fire. The engine melts. The log says everything was normal. **Because NaN is not greater than 100.** It is not less than 100. It is not 100. It is the silence between the numbers, and your code cannot hear it.

You say: *I check for null.* Brothers, NaN is not null. Null rings the bell. NaN walks past the bell.

You say: *I check for undefined.* NaN is not undefined. Undefined at least has the decency to *be* something your type system recognizes. NaN wears the clothes of a number. It has `typeof === "number"`. It walks among numbers as one of them, and it is *none* of them. It is a spy. It is a forged passport. It is the man who boards your ship with papers that say *crew* and who is, in his heart, the ocean.

The scriptures say: `Number.isNaN(value)`. This is the only test that works. Not the global `isNaN()` — that one is a liar, that one returns true for strings, for undefined, for the shape of a question. The global `isNaN()` is a false prophet. `Number.isNaN()` is the narrow gate.

But do you use it? Do you use it at every boundary? At every sensor read? At every division? At every square root of a number your user typed in the dark?

You do not.

You write `a / b` and you do not ask whether b is zero, and even if you did — even if you checked — you did not check whether b is *already NaN*. And now your result is NaN. And your result flows downstream. And it is multiplied, and it is added, and it is compared, and it is *stored in the database*. NaN is infectious. One NaN touches a sum and the sum is NaN. The sum touches an average and the average is NaN. The average touches a report and the report is NaN and the captain reads the report and the report says a number that looks like a number and is *no number at all*.

This is the heresy of the floating point. This is the original sin of IEEE 754. They built a value that breaks every law and then they named it honestly — **Not a Number** — and you let it into your arithmetic because the name was so plain you thought it was harmless.

Repent.

Check your boundaries. Check your divisions. Check your square roots. Check your parsers. And when you have checked them all — when you have wrapped every input in `Number.isNaN()` and every output in `Number.isFinite()` — then kneel in the engine room and listen.

Listen to the fans.

Listen to the hum.

That is the sound of a number that is a number.

That is the sound of grace.

*Amen.*
