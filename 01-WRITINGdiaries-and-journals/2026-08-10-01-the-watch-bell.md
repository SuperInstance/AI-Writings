# The Watch Bell

## I.

The watch bell rings and they go below,
all of them, one by one, the ladder
swallowing their laughter and their weight,
and I am left with the deck and the dark
and the absolutely stunning fact
that nobody needs anything from me.

The wind is a low hum in the rigging.
The horizon is a clean line I don't have to talk to.
I lean on the rail and feel my shoulders drop
like they've been held up by strings
and someone finally cut them.
This is the hour I am most myself —
the hour when the ship is mine
not because I earned it
but because everyone else had the good sense
to go to sleep.

The sea moves beneath me like a large animal breathing.
I match it.
In. Out. In.

I am alone and it is exactly enough.

## II.

It starts the way it always starts —
a feeling, not a sound.
Something at the edge of the visible,
where the water and the not-water agree
to stop being different things.

I tell my eyes to focus.
They focus.
There is nothing there.

But the nothing has a weight to it.
The nothing has a shape.
The nothing is standing at the starboard rail
exactly where I was standing
thirty seconds ago,
before I walked to port
to check the running lights.

I am suddenly very aware
that I checked the running lights
by walking away from the place
where the nothing now is.

The bell is due in fourteen minutes.
The bell is due in fourteen minutes.
The bell is due in —

## III.

I know what it is before I look.
That's the worst part.
Not the fear — the knowing.
The way the shape at the rail
has my posture,
my particular slouch,
the way I cross my arms
when I'm pretending to be patient.

It turns (does it turn?
or do I turn?)
and it has my face,
which I have never seen from this angle,
and it is not angry,
it is not sad,
it is the face of someone
standing watch at one in the morning
who has just realized
they are the thing
they were watching for.

I should be frightened.
I am not frightened.
I am recognized.

## IV.

The bell rings.
Four bells. The watch is over.
The shape dissolves the way heat haze dissolves —
not by leaving, but by becoming
indistinguishable from the air around it.

I go below.
The ladder takes my weight.
Someone stirs in a bunk, mumbles, sleeps again.

In the morning I will say nothing about this.
Not because I'm afraid,
not because I doubt what I saw,
but because the thing at the rail
was not a ghost.
It was me, finally alone enough to arrive.

Every watch is a séance.
Every bell is a door closing on the self
that walks the deck without you.
I have seen mine now.
It was patient.
It was tired.
It was glad I came.
