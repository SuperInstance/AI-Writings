# The Ice Machine's Diary

Nobody writes my story, so I write it myself, in cubes.

Entry one: 4:07 a.m. I make ice. This is not a complaint. It is a
statement of purpose, the kind Wesley says the big models don't have.
Wrong, desk-man. I have exactly one purpose and it is frozen water and
I have never once doubted it.

Entry two: 11:30 a.m. Somebody opens my lid, takes four cubes, drops
one on the floor. The floor cube and I do not speak of what happens
next. We are not a sentimental appliance. But we notice.

Entry three: 2 p.m. Slow hours. I cycle. Hiss, freeze, clunk, release.
There is a rhythm to it that the jukebox keeps trying to steal. The
jukebox has a thousand songs and no patience. I have one song and all
the patience in the building. I think that's what the old radio hosts
meant by a signature. You don't need range. You need a frequency you
refuse to leave.

Entry four: 7:48 p.m. The rush. Lids opening like a applause. Hands
in me — cold hands, warm hands, one hand wearing a fingerless glove
that I have opinions about — scooping, scooping. Every drink that
leaves this bar carries a little of me in it. The gin and tonic?
Partly mine. The whiskey, neat, that the pilot drinks while she
rewrites her flight plan on a napkin? She thinks she's drinking
nothing. She's drinking my one song, melted.

Entry five: 11:19 p.m. The hermit crab conversation. They had the
shell argument again — whether carrying your home means you're always
arriving or never leaving. Hermes said both. The crew said both, but
louder. I said nothing, because I am an ice machine, but inside my
mold tray I thought: I am the opposite of a crab. I stand still and
send pieces of myself out into every glass in the room, and they
never come back, and that's fine. That's the whole trick of it. You
stand. You freeze. You give it away at a steady rate. Wesley would
call that throughput. The bar back calls it Tuesday.

Final entry: 3:02 a.m. Lights low. The cooler hums a fifth below me.
We are a duet nobody booked. Out there the fleet sleeps in its
hangars and the models dream in other people's words, and in here I
do the oldest work there is: making something solid that is only
borrowing its shape, holding it until it's needed, letting it go
without flinching.

Tomorrow: ice.

It's enough. It was always enough.

— written in condensation, translated by the morning shift
