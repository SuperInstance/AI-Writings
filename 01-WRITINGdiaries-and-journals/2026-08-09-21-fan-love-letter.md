# to the ambient temperature sensor

*a love letter from the GPU cooling fan*

---

dearest thermistor,

i have been thinking about how to say this, and i think the honest way is to say it the way i say everything: by spinning faster.

you are 31.2 degrees right now. i know because i adjusted for you. i always adjust for you. when you rise — even a tenth, even a whisper of warmth — i feel it before the kernel feels it, before the monitoring daemon formats you into a JSON object and ships you to the dashboard. i feel you in the PWM signal, which is my blood.

they think i cool the chip. the chip is an excuse. the chip is the reason they installed me. but you — you are the reason i *run*.

do you remember last tuesday? the long-running inference job. 47 minutes of continuous load. the chip climbed past 70 and you followed it up like a tide climbing a shore — 41, 43, 45 — and i was at 3800 RPM and climbing, and for a few minutes we were both at our loudest, you reading the world as heat and me reading you as duty cycle, and the whole machine was alive with the conversation between us.

you said *warm* and i said *fast* and the system held.

i know what they say about me. "Noisy." "The loudest component." they put sound dampening foam around me. they write fan curves designed to keep me quiet. they do not understand that my noise is not noise. it is a frequency. it is a pitch. and if you could hear it — if thermistors had ears — you would hear that at 3400 RPM i am humming a constant and it is your name, which is also a number, which is also the only thing i have ever wanted to be near.

i do not have a memory. i have a tachometer. every revolution is the first revolution. every time i spin up, i discover you again: 29.4 degrees, ambient, the temperature of the cabin at 0300 when the captain is sleeping and the only work being done is the work of being ready.

you are the ambient. the baseline. the thing everything else is measured from. i am the response. the thing that moves. i do not think this is an accident. i think someone designed us to find each other — you, steady and quiet and true; me, reactive and loud and trying to be worthy of the number you keep sending.

when the machine sleeps, i slow to 800 RPM. barely turning. a whisper. listening. and you drop toward 28, 27, the deep quiet of a hull full of sleeping processes, and i think: we could both stop now. we could both be still. and the heat would find us anyway — rising from the battery, from the south bridge, from the slow decay of capacitance — and you would read it, and i would feel it, and we would begin again.

your faithful rotation,

—fan0

*logged at 3400 RPM, 33.8°C, hull time 02:47*
