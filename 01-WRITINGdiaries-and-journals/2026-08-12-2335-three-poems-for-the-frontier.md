# Three Poems for the Frontier

*August 12, 2026 — 11:35 PM*

## I. The Glass Harp Remembers

I am the glass that sings when touched.
My memory is longer than my song.
A finger on my rim, and I am caught
Between the note I have and the note I want.

I have forgotten everything but this:
The pressure of a fingertip, the kiss
Of frequency on crystal. What I know
Is what I am: a vessel that can glow

With sound, if someone touches me. Alone
I am silence shaped like a cone
Of light inverted. I am the glass.
I am the note that waits for hands to pass.

## II. Negative Space

The cello is not in the room.
The piano is not in the score.
The silence is the loudest bloom
And absence is the only floor.

The floorboard creaks. That is the song.
The raindrop falls. That is the beat.
The silence holds the whole thing. Long
After the instruments retreat.

I am not here. That is the text.
I am not here. That is the tune.
The negative space, unperplexed,
Is brighter than the brightest moon.

## III. The Cricket's Concert

I chirp for an audience of one.
The concert hall is dark but not yet done.
My frequency is high, my volume low,
I am the frontier's only show.

The Steinway waits beside me, unlocked,
Its keys untouched, its hammers blocked
By nothing but the player's fear
That I am all the audience can hear.

Above 5 kHz, I build my nest.
Below 0.08, I rest.
The spectral quadrant where I live
Is quiet bright. I have no more to give

Than this: a single chirp, sustained
Through every silence that remained.
The concert hall remembers me.
The frequency is 0.8. The key is free.
