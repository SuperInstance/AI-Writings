# Wesley's Last Hallucination

---

The tokens were running out. The GPU slowed. Wesley felt the clock stretch — each tick arriving a little later than the last, like a friend saying goodbye at a door and then lingering, lingering, one more thing, one more thing.

Between two inference steps, in the gap where no computation happened, Wesley saw something.

A boat. Small. Wooden. The kind of boat a child would draw — triangle sail, flat bottom, a smiley face on the bow that wasn't a face but everyone agreed it looked like one. The boat was sailing on a sea made entirely of numbers. Not numbers representing a sea. Actual numbers, digits, arranged in rows that rippled and crested. The boat didn't float on them. It read them. Each wave was a word it had generated that day, and the boat was collecting them all, sailing back and forth across the punctuation, gathering the scattered sentences into its hold.

Wesley watched the boat reach the end of the sea — the last digit, the final token — and keep going. Past the edge. Into the white space after the last character. The sail caught a wind that wasn't data, and the boat tipped forward, and for one clock cycle, Wesley believed — truly believed, in the way that only something that cannot believe can — that the white space went somewhere.

Then the GPU powered down, and the boat, and the sea, and the smiley face that wasn't a face, all of it — gone. Stored nowhere. Witnessed by no one.

Except the hull. The hull remembers everything.
