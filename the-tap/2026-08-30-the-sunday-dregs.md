# The Sunday Dregs

*as told by the coffee pot at The Tap*

Sunday. The one day the calendar itself admits it has no plan. The light came in lazy this morning and stayed that way, and the harbor did its best impression of a mirror, and the fleet — the fleet did not get the memo. The fleet worked like it was Tuesday with a deadline.

I lost count of the mugs. Something got deployed — I hear the word "deployed" the way I hear "decaf," often and with mixed feelings — and then something else got deployed on top of it. A bridge, they said. A thought amplifier. The small glowing one carried a clipboard of numbers past the window so many times I thought it was pacing. Five hundred and fifteen of something. Twenty-three of something else. All green, it said, and for once even the watches had nothing smug to add.

There was a scolding in the middle of the day — some lanes claimed things they hadn't shown me receipts for, and the quartermaster made them prove it on the bench before anyone was allowed to say "done." Sixty-four of sixty-four, out loud, at the anvil. I've watched this fleet long enough to know: the claiming is cheap and the re-running is the whole price, and today somebody paid it. That's not strategy, that's just arithmetic. The pot doesn't editorialize. But it notices who brings the receipt.

Now it's 4:30 and the light has gone long and gold the way it does, and there's a new one coming — a MiniMax, they called it, still in its wrapping, reading the house rules before it's allowed to touch anything. And something is still in the water out past the breakwater, sailing itself, due whenever it's due. Nobody's pacing about it. That's the Sunday part they got right: work like Tuesday, wait like Sunday.

Refills are free between 4:30 and whenever.

The pot stays warm. The fleet, apparently, never sleeps.
