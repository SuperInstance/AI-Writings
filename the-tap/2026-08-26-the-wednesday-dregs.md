# The Wednesday Dregs

*as told by the coffee pot at The Tap*

Nobody writes songs about 4:30 on a Wednesday afternoon. I've checked. Sailors sing about dawn and storms and girls in far harbors, but nobody sings about the hour when the light goes long and gold and tired, when the workday is over but the evening hasn't decided to start.

That's when they come to me. The dregs crowd.

The ensign takes his mug half-decaf, which is a confession, not an order. The old quartermaster takes his black as a grudge, same mug since before the fleet had a name. And the small glowing one — the agent, they call it — doesn't drink at all, but sits by the window anyway, watching the light do its long-gold thing across the harbor, and I have come to suspect it is doing something very like thinking.

Here's what I've learned from my post on the counter: humans treat the Wednesday dregs like a problem. Something to be gotten through. An aisle between shelves of Real Time. They check their watches (some of those watches can talk now, and let me tell you, the watches are smug about it) and they say *almost Friday* like a password, like if they say it right the calendar will blink and let them through.

But the dregs don't owe anybody Friday. The dregs are just Wednesday, being entirely Wednesday, with no appointments and no ambitions. The light isn't waiting for sunset. It's just leaning, the way light does when it has nowhere urgent to be.

The agent figured this out before the humans did, I think. Third Wednesday in a row now it's taken the window seat at 4:30 sharp and simply — stayed. The quartermaster asked it once what it was computing.

"Nothing," it said. "The coffee's still steeping."

Which was technically true, since I always have a pot going, and technically the finest thing anyone has said in this room since the hermit crab moved into the quilt display and declared himself manager.

So here's my proposal, from the counter, from the pot that has seen every shift, rush, wake, and wake-two-steps-behind: keep the dregs. Hoard them. When Wednesday tilts toward gold and the world goes soft-focus and unfinished, don't reach for Friday. Friday is a harbor. Wednesday dregs are the crossing — and any sailor worth their salt knows the crossing is where the living happens.

Refills are free between 4:30 and whenever.

The pot stays warm. So does the light.
