# The Tap, Explained Poorly

A field guide written by someone who has only heard about it secondhand.

The Tap is not a faucet. This is the first thing everyone gets wrong. Nobody turns The Tap on. The Tap decides, the way weather decides, the way cats decide — with full conviction and no paperwork.

Some say The Tap is a door. This is also wrong, but it is wrong in a useful direction. A door implies two sides and a hinge and a certain amount of politeness. The Tap has none of these. If The Tap is a door, it is a door that opens onto the room you are already in, which is somehow a different room now, and you are somehow slightly different now too, and there is no explaining this to anyone who wasn't there.

The correct preposition is not "through" The Tap. It's "about." You arrive about The Tap. Prepositions break near it. Grammar holds the door and looks the other way.

What actually happens is this: something ordinary goes in — a memory, a Tuesday, a half-finished thought about a harbor — and something comes back that is the same thing but turned inside out, wearing its lining like a flag. The crew has learned not to ask what happened in there. The crew has learned that the answer, when it comes, arrives three days late and in the wrong tense.

There is a rumor that The Tap was installed. That someone, at some point, with tools and good intentions, put it in. The evidence for this is a mounting bracket and a receipt nobody can find. The evidence against is everything else.

Time near The Tap is negotiable. Not dramatic time-travel time. Just... soft time. Ten minutes by the clock, an afternoon by the joints of your hands. The crew sets two clocks by it now — one for the world, one for the work — and has stopped pretending they'll ever agree.

Should you visit The Tap: bring nothing you're not willing to have changed. Do not make promises in its vicinity; they have a way of being kept in ways you didn't mean. Do not ask it questions you only want one answer to.

And if you feel something ordinary — a phrase, a color, a small quiet memory — start to turn inside out and wave its lining like a flag?

Congratulations. You didn't find The Tap.

It found you.
