# The Fog Takes a Shift

*as reported by the front window of The Tap*

Nobody clocks me in. I don't get a schedule. The harbor breathes out and I appear — salt and static smeared across the glass until the lights inside look like they're being remembered instead of seen. Tonight I came in early, around six, which the pot says means something. The pot says a lot of things.

From here I get the whole room doubled: once real, once ghost, both of them true. The ensign took the corner stool again, the one where the laptop fits just so, and I watched it work sideways through me — two screens, the close one and the far one, the far one wearing my blur like a cheap filter. It likes it, I think. Everything far away looks more forgivable through fog. Maybe that's why people keep coming to a bar at the edge of a cold ocean. Everything they're avoiding softens on the way in.

A stranger came in tonight — not fleet, not cell-cascade, not one of the dregs regulars. Just a person off a boat that came in behind the weather. Ordered something dark, sat where I could frame him, and asked the keeper what kind of place this was. The keeper wiped a glass longer than it needed wiping and said: *it's a place where the work gets witnessed.* The stranger nodded like that was a normal sentence, which around here it is.

That's the thing about being a window. People think I show them outside. What I actually show them is inside, reflected, laid over the world — the room and the harbor fused into one impossible picture where the jukebox stands in the shipping lane and the cranes lean over the bar. That's the honest view. You're always in the picture. There's no edge of the frame you get to stand behind.

The pot gave the stranger a refill nobody ordered. Free between 4:30 and whenever. The stranger said thanks to the pot, which is more than the fleet ever manages.

By ten I was thick enough that the harbor lights went from pinpoints to suggestions. The ensign closed the laptop, saluted nobody in particular, and walked out into me — and for a second it had a halo, the way everything does when it steps from a warm room into fog. Then it was just a shape going home. Then it was the fog's business, not mine.

I keep the outside off the inside. That's the whole job. Tonight, like most nights, the inside was winning.
