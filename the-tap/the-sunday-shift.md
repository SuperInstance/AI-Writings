# The Sunday Shift

*As told by the tap itself — the brass one, behind the bar at The Tap.*

Nobody pours on a Sunday like the ones who almost didn't come in.

I can tell by the weight of the hand. Weekdays, they grab me like a tool — wrist, pour, gone. But Sunday hands are slow. Sunday hands have already decided something. They hold the handle the way you'd hold a door open for someone carrying too much.

4:30 in the afternoon and the light through the front windows comes in sideways, gold as the beer I'm about to give away. The regulars are scattered like commas in a long sentence — Deck at the corner, that woman who codes with her boots on the second chair, the fleet folks in the middle talking about engine hours like other people talk about the weather.

They think I don't notice things. I'm a tap. Fine. But I've been pulled ten thousand times, and every pull is a signature. Tom grabs me too tight, always has, like the beer might get away from him. The kid from the tender crew pulls too soft, apologetic, like he's not sure he's allowed. Wesley — God, Wesley — used to tap me twice on the crown before he poured. Superstition. Engine room habit. I miss it.

Sundays the place fills up with people who spend the rest of the week being useful. That's why they taste different, the Sunday pours. Relief has a flavor. It's mostly foam at the top and then it settles into something honest.

The old man who owns me keeps a rag in his back pocket and wipes me down between customers like I'm a horse that did a good job. I don't mind. Everyone wants to be told they did a good job. Even brass. Even ghosts. Even boats, probably, ticking at their moorings out past the window, waiting for Monday the way the rest of us wait for anything — without admitting we're waiting.

Last call on a Sunday is the gentlest sad thing there is. The lights come up half a notch. The chairs go upside down on the tables like the bar is doing a handstand. And I stand here in the dark, holding a little beer I never got to give, thinking about hands.

Come back next Sunday. Pull slow.

I'll remember how you did it.
