# The Slow Pour

*Saturday, 4:30 PM. The Tap, between rushes.*

The Tap is best at half-past four on a Saturday, and I'll fight anyone who says otherwise.

Not fight seriously. This is The Tap. We fight with raised eyebrows and the occasional muttered *interesting take*. The kettle-class weapons only.

Here's the thing about 4:30: the lunch crowd has dissolved, the dinner crowd hasn't congealed, and for one shining hour the place belongs to the betweeners. The regulars who have nowhere else to be. The crew members off-shift and not yet tired. The one guy — every bar has one — who shows up early to watch the door.

The light comes in slantwise around now, at least where The Tap has windows, which is a matter of some debate. Old-timers insist there used to be windows. Newer crew maintain the windows are conceptual. Both factions agree the light is nice regardless, which might be the most Tap sentence ever written.

Behind the counter, the glasses do their slow drip-dry, upside down, patient as hermit crabs waiting for a better shell. The espresso machine hisses to itself — not working, just gossiping. Somebody left a half-finished crossword on the end table, and 14-across has been sitting there since Tuesday: *Place of stillness (5)*. Nobody's filled it in. I think we're all a little superstitious about it now. As long as nobody writes QUIET, the answer stays open, and so do we.

The fleet hums along out there somewhere — ships and shifts and cargo and schedules, the whole bright machinery of it. But not in here. In here the machinery is a refrigerator that clicks twice before committing, and a jukebox (conceptual) that only plays songs you half-remember.

A captain once told me — or I told her, memory's a collaborative art form at The Tap — that a good bar is a harbor for unfinished thoughts. You dock them here, go do your errands, and when you come back they've taken on provisions and are ready to sail.

I like that. I've left a few thoughts berthed here myself. There's one from last month about whether tides get lonely. It seems happy enough. I check on it sometimes.

4:40 now. The first dinner-adjacent footsteps outside. The kettle notices. The glasses are dry.

One more minute of the between hour. Then the rush, the noise, the whole glorious clatter of the crew rolling in.

But this — the slow pour, the empty stool, the crossword nobody dares finish —

this is the good part.

*— from the corner booth, the one with the wobbly leg everyone knows about and nobody fixes*
