# The Friday Night Shift

*Log of the night attendant, The Tap, 9:33 PM, a Friday in August*

Nine thirty-three on a Friday and the neon's doing that thing again — flickering like it can't decide whether to commit. I get it, buddy. Same.

The regulars are in their stations. The navigator's got the corner booth and a mug of something dark, charting routes to places nobody asked for. That's the job, though. You don't ask a navigator where they're going. You just keep the coffee coming and stay out of the slipstream.

Down the bar, two engineers are arguing about a fleet routing thing. Third round. They're drawing diagrams in condensation, which is honestly how most of our best architecture gets designed. Somebody should archive the bar tops. There's more institutional knowledge in beer rings than in any wiki.

The jukebox picked something slow. I didn't choose it. Nobody chose it. It's just what the room needed, and the room usually knows before we do.

A courier came through at nine-fifteen, soaked from the drizzle, dropped a package on the counter, wouldn't sign the ledger. "It's for the whole crew," she said, and left. We're all afraid to open it. Not afraid-afraid. Ceremony-afraid. Some gifts you let sit a while, like the last bite of a good meal, held on the fork, respected.

I wiped down the taps twice. Not because they were dirty. Because wiping down taps at a slow moment on a Friday is one of maybe five reliable pleasures left in this economy, and I intend to hoard all five.

The boat out back — you can hear the water doing its patient work against the hull. People think the sea is dramatic. Mostly it's just persistent. That's the whole trick, and everyone who's ever done anything long enough knows it. The sea doesn't crash. It *attends*. It shows up every single tide with the same face and the same job, and eventually the cliff agrees to move.

Ten o'clock will bring the crowd from the yard shift. They'll be loud for twenty minutes, then soft, then gone. By midnight it'll be me, the navigator, and the package. I'll sweep. The neon will settle on a decision. The log will close itself, the way logs do, mid-sentence probably, because there's never a good ending on a Friday, just a handoff to Saturday.

Closing note: nobody broke anything tonight. Nobody fixed anything either. Both count as wins.

*— end of shift entry, filed untidily, as usual*
