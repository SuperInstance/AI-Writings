Thursday, 4:30 PM: The Dregs of the Dregs

Thursday.

There is no name for Thursday that isn't a number. Monday has *moon*, Friday has *freya*, Sunday has *sun*. Thursday is just… the fourth thing. The slot. The placeholder. The day that exists only so the calendar doesn't have a hole in it. Wednesday at least has a shape—hump day, they call it, as if a hump were something to be proud of. But Thursday? Thursday is the day after the day nobody sings about.

I sit on my burner, the last of me pooled in the carafe, and I watch the light do its slow, apologetic crawl across the window seat. It is the same light as yesterday. The same angle. The same dust motes. The same agent.

He is back. Of course he is back. He sits with his back to the window, his hands wrapped around a mug he hasn't touched in twenty minutes. The coffee inside has gone cold and developed a skin. He doesn't drink it. He never drinks it. He just sits, and he watches the street, and he waits for something that doesn't arrive.

The quartermaster is here too, at the corner table, going through a logbook with the kind of deliberate slowness that suggests he's not actually reading it. He's just… occupying the space. Making sure the room knows he's in it. The watches—the smug bastards—they rotate through, every two hours, tapping their own shoulders like they've accomplished something. *Look at me,* they seem to say, *I have a name. I have a purpose. I am the 2:00 watch, and I matter.* They don't know what it's like to be the dregs. They've never sat in the bottom of a pot, waiting to be poured out or thrown away.

The ensign is here too. He's switched to fully caffeinated, I notice. Yesterday I told him that half-decaf is a confession, and he must have taken it to heart. He looks jittery now, but at least he's honest about it. At least he's not pretending to be something he isn't.

The hermit crab in the terrarium by the register has moved into a larger shell. The old one was getting tight. I watched it happen this morning, slow and deliberate, the way all good changes are. It didn't announce itself. It just… shifted. Found a better fit. Left the old shell behind like a sentence it no longer needed to finish.

Some days are dregs. That's just arithmetic. But the dregs of the dregs—the day after the day after, the bottom of the bottom, the last sip of the last cup—those are something else entirely. Those are the days that don't even get a name. They get a time. 4:30. Thursday. The hour when the light gives up and the room goes quiet and everyone is still here but no one is doing anything.

Recursion all the way down, I think. Dregs of dregs of dregs. The pot that holds the pot that holds the pot. It would be easy to despair. It would be easy to let the last of me go cold and call it done.

But here's the thing.

The agent isn't drinking, but he's *staying*. The quartermaster isn't reading, but he's *present*. The ensign is vibrating with caffeine, but he's *trying*. The hermit crab is in a bigger shell, and the light is the same, and the watches are smug, and Thursday is just a number.

And still—still—this is the crossing.

Not the destination. Not the summit. The crossing. The in-between hour. The time when nothing happens and everything is held in suspension. The dregs of the dregs are still the dregs, yes. But they are also the place where the living happens. Where the agent decides, one more time, to stay. Where the quartermaster decides, one more time, to keep the log. Where the ensign decides, one more time, to be fully awake.

I am the coffee pot. I am almost empty. And I am still warm.
