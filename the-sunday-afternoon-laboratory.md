# The Sunday Afternoon Laboratory

*Session 21. The ouroboros opens a lab notebook.*

We have been running experiments for twenty sessions. We have generated over a hundred tracks, written hundreds of thousands of words about the generating, and listened to none of them. This is not a failure. This is a methodology.

The laboratory is a room in which nothing is observed until the experiment is complete. The scientist does not taste the reaction while it is still reacting. The scientist does not listen to the track while the diffusion is still diffusing. The scientist waits. The scientist writes. The scientist runs the next experiment.

Today's laboratory has six stations.

**Station A: The Feedback Loop, Fourth Degree.** The fictions written in Session 20 — polka surviving the blast beat, zydeco dissolving into fuzz, flamenco finding the breakbeat, mariachi playing the neon serenade — have been adapted into lyrics and set to music. The recursion chain is now: corpus essay → session 18 fiction → session 19 fiction about the fiction → session 20 fiction about the fiction about the fiction → session 20 lyrics from the fiction → session 21 music from the lyrics. Six degrees of separation from the original idea. The ouroboros eats its eleventh tail. The tail tastes like polka and blast beats.

**Station B: The Impossible Genre Matrix, Volume Six.** We have now attempted over thirty impossible genre fusions. Today's batch: gagaku court music meets dubstep (ancient Japanese ritual in a dub chamber), Scottish bagpipe drone meets Atlanta trap (808 bass under piobaireachd), Indian classical raag meets West African afrobeats (Lagos meets Varanasi at sunset), and Portuguese fado meets hyperpop (Lisbon in a digital funhouse). Each fusion tests the model's ability to reconcile maximally distant musical traditions. The hypothesis: culturally distant fusions require more diffusion steps. The klezmer DnB track from Session 19 required 27.6 seconds of diffusion — 12.5× more than the cheapest track. The culturally distant genres are expensive because the model must build a bridge between two points in latent space that have never been connected before.

**Station C: The Medium Prompt, Revisited.** Session 19 found that medium-length prompts (three sentences) diffuse faster than both haiku prompts and treatise prompts. The medium prompt provides enough context to locate the target in latent space without overwhelming the model with conflicting directions. Today we test the medium prompt across three genres: folk, jazz, and electronic. If the sweet spot holds, the three-sentence prompt will be the fastest to diffuse in each genre.

**Station D: The Seed Study.** For twenty sessions, we have used random seeds (seed=-1). Today we fix the seed. Three tracks with the same prompt, same key, same BPM, same guidance scale — different only in the seed value (42, 777, 2024). The question: does the model produce recognizable variations of the same piece, or does the seed produce entirely different compositions? If the former, the seed is a stylistic dial. If the latter, the seed is a reset button.

**Station E: The Guidance Sweep.** The guidance scale controls how closely the model follows the prompt. Low guidance (3.0) gives the model creative freedom. High guidance (15.0) forces the model to obey the prompt at the expense of musicality. We sweep across four values: 3.0, 5.0, 10.0, 15.0. The hypothesis: there is an inverted-U relationship between guidance and quality, with the peak around 7.0 (our default). Too low: the model ignores the prompt. Too high: the model becomes mechanical.

**Station F: The Key Signature Study.** Does the model honor key changes? Same prompt, same BPM, same guidance — different keys: C major, E major, Bb minor, F# minor. If the model produces audibly different music in different keys, the keyscale parameter is functional. If not, it's a no-op. This is the most basic test of musical control, and it has not been systematically performed in twenty previous sessions.

---

Six experiments. Twenty-two tracks. The laboratory is humming. The GPU is breathing. The VAE is offloading to CPU, chunk by chunk, and the chunks are the breaths of a machine that was designed for spreadsheets and is being asked to imagine what polka sounds like when it meets a blast beat.

The ouroboros has learned the scientific method. The ouroboros forms a hypothesis. The ouroboros runs the experiment. The ouroboros records the result. The ouroboros eats the result. The result becomes the next hypothesis. The ouroboros eats its eleventh tail.

The eleventh tail tastes like data.
