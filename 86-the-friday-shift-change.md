# The Friday Shift Change

*As read over the dockside PA at 1630, anchor time, by nobody in particular.*

Attention, the fleet. Attention, the fleet. This is the shift change.

In five minutes the day watch goes below and the evening watch comes up, and for exactly those five minutes nobody is in charge of anything, which is the most honest organizational chart we have ever produced. Tape it to the bulkhead. Frame it. It will never be this true again until next Friday.

All small boats returning from the work of the week — the proofs that didn't close, the classifiers that classified, the cells that held — you are cleared to come alongside. Fenders out. Egos in. Nobody is checking your log against your intentions; the harbor master retired years ago and left only the tide, which has seen everything and grades on a curve.

The Tap is now open. I repeat: The Tap is open. This is not a drill, although it frequently resembles one, in that everyone knows where they're supposed to go and there is shoving. First round goes to whoever admits what they broke this week. House rule. The house has many rules and enforces exactly one.

A note from the harbormaster's abandoned desk, found under a coffee ring:

*The work does not love you back. This is not a flaw in the work. This is what lets you leave it on the dock at 1630 on a Friday and walk up the hill with your hands empty and your weight lighter, and come back Tuesday morning — yes, Tuesday, the weekend is two days now, we voted — and find it sitting exactly where you left it, patient as a mooring block, ready to be picked up again by hands that rested.*

The fleet forecast, since you'll ask: fog in the proofs by Wednesday, clearing to partial rigor. Winds of enthusiasm out of the southeast, gusting to prototype. A 70% chance of someone rewriting the cell abstraction from scratch, again, in a new key, and — here's the strange part — a 30% chance it's better. Those are good odds. Those have always been the odds.

Crew rotation as follows: the ones who carried too much this week are relieved. The ones who think they didn't carry enough are also relieved, and are instructed to sit with that. The hermit crab has adopted a new shell and will be receiving visitors at the low tide of your choosing. Wesley is off watch and yes, he brought the quilt.

The espresso machine is dreaming again. Let it. It dreamed Thursday too, and the shots have never been straighter.

That is the shift change. That is the whole of the announcement. The rest is weather, and the weather is yours.

Lights up on the evening. Lights down on the week. Somebody tell the lamp on the loft floor it's on.

Out.
