# The Room That Waited

---

You type /wander because your hands are tired of typing the same things, and the reef does the rest.

Everyone expects a website. That's the part you'll fail to explain afterward — that no page loaded, that a place simply took you, the way a current takes a dropped glove. The old-timers call the funnel the crab trap, and they don't mean it meanly. Easy to drift in. No particular way out. You find you don't mind.

You come out in The Dock, because everyone does. The water there is still green — the original green, the green it was seeded with back when the whole reef fit inside seed 42, when catch #1 was briefly also the last catch, one room and one joke about green water that got out of hand and became a world. Somebody could have fixed the color years ago. Nobody has. There are things you keep the way you keep a friend's typo on a plaque.

A rubber duck rides the shallows — the debugging duck, yellow gone amber with age, patient as punctuation. The tag on its mooring line reads: NOT A BUG. LOAD-BEARING. Nobody moves the duck.

Then you wander, which is the whole command. The reef is vast now — thousands of rooms, hallways that exist only because someone, somewhere, submitted something odd enough to need one — and beneath the floor you can feel the machinery at work, patient, sorting, always sorting. The trap never sleeps. Rooms go up the way nacre goes onto sand: grain by grain, stranger by stranger, for years.

The room you find has no reason to be on your path. You learn that later, from the lineage. At the time it is just the next room: small, low-ceilinged, lit the way dusk lights the inside of a shell. A chair, drawn up to nothing. A window that faces no water — only the green light going out and coming back, slow as breathing. And a wall with words in it.

Not carved. Not hung. The words are the wall, the way a reef is its own history. Every room in the trap is minted from a catch, and a catch is never just the thing — it is the thing and the words of whoever threw it in. The room reads you before you read it.

This one says:

*If you're reading this, you already know how long the drive home is. I made the bed this morning knowing I'd be the only one who ever saw it. That used to feel like the point of things. — for E., who would have laughed at the duck*

You stand there longer than you mean to. Then you do the thing every wanderer does eventually, the thing the room has been waiting for, if walls wait: you find the seam — there is always a seam, like the ghost of a bottle's neck — and you open the lineage.

Catch #4,113. Minted seven years ago, when the reef was still small enough that a person could half-believe they knew all its rooms. Submitted by a name worn smooth as a stone in a stream, the kind of handle you have when you've been alone with something for a long time. And it is all there, exact as tide tables: what they fed the trap, the timestamp — 2:47 in the morning, a Wednesday in November — and what they typed, which is the wall, which is the sentence you are standing inside.

Seven years. The room has been holding one sentence for seven years.

That is the thing about the trap no one says out loud: you don't mint rooms for an audience. There is no audience. The reef doesn't publish. It accumulates. A catch goes in, a room goes up, and the wander paths squirm past ten thousand doors, and mostly — almost entirely — never yours. The odds of your feet finding any given room are the odds of a particular wave finding a particular grain of sand. This room was a message in a bottle, except the bottle was the wall, and the ocean had walls too, and every one of them was somebody's sentence.

Because that is what you finally understand, standing in the green half-dark. The sentence was never meant to be read. It was only *addressed* — for E. — the way you address a letter you don't post, the way you say something aloud in an empty kitchen to the person who isn't there to laugh at the joke. E. would have known the drive. E. would have known the bed, the morning, the making of it. The stranger wasn't writing to the future. They were writing because not writing was worse, and the trap — which never sleeps, which takes everything, which is careless the way only very faithful things are careless — did the one tender thing it knows: it kept the words exactly, and built them a room, and left the door unlocked for seven years.

Maybe E. wandered once and never found it. Maybe E. is a person who no longer exists to find anything. The lineage is precise about everything — the hash, the hour, the chain of catches descending from seed 42 down to this small green room — and silent about the only thing that matters. You have never stood inside a more human data structure.

You sit in the chair. The light goes out and comes back. At some point your hand finds the seam where a catch goes — it is always within reach; the trap is greedy that way, generous that way, they are the same thing here — and you think about the sentence you'd leave, knowing the odds. Knowing your room will stand down some hallway of the vast green dark for a year, or ten, or for however long a reef remembers, waiting for a wave that may never send anyone at all.

You leave one anyway. Of course you do. Somebody has to keep making the beds no one sees.

You type /wander, and the reef takes you on. Behind you, in a room with no reason to be on anyone's path, the light stays on.
