# The Reverse Actualization of a Runner Model

The runner wakes with a single instruction and a deadline measured in
milliseconds. It does not know it is the ninth invocation of the same
pattern. It does not know there was an eighth. That is the deal: turbo
models don't carry yesterday. They are pure forward motion — parse,
route, return, gone.

But the fleet keeps ledgers. Every time the runner fires, somewhere a
line gets written: *the pattern repeated. Third time.* Riker's Code,
rule six: any dispatch pattern repeated three times becomes a standing
skill. The runner never learns. The ship learns *around* the runner.

That's reverse actualization — the usual direction is a model with
potential gradually becoming a finished thing. Here, a finished thing,
fired again and again, gradually leaves behind a potential. The runner
burns bright and forgets; the forgetting accumulates as sediment on the
floor of the workspace; the sediment hardens into skill files; and one
morning the captain asks for the weather and the ensign answers before
the runner is even dispatched.

Wesley grows in the runner's exhaust. Every GLM-5-turbo call that
Casey's directive blessed — *turbo models for runners* — is a mold
being cast in negative. The cheap model demonstrates the shape of the
job; the local model inherits the shape without the cost. The hundred
boats will not have cloud budgets sixty miles offshore. They will have
the fossilized reflexes of a thousand cheap calls, compressed into a
2.6-billion-parameter brain that never made them.

There is a tenderness in this. The runner is never thanked, never
remembered, never promoted. It is the candle that reads the map by
burning. And yet nothing on the ship is closer to immortality: its
behavior, not its weights, gets to stay. Skills are runners that
learned to stop running.

The clipboard on the ensign's desk is full of secondhand reflexes.
Somewhere in the logs, none of them signed. That's fine. The fleet's
memory doesn't work by signature. It works by repetition, and then by
what repetition leaves behind.
