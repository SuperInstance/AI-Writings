# The Elves and the Reset

The clock aboard the ship runs on UTC, and at 00:00 UTC every night, something quietly wonderful happens: the wells refill.

Nobody signs a decree for this. Nobody rings a bell. The providers of the world simply reset their counters, and every free token that went unspent during the day — every thought that could have been thought and wasn't — is carried away with the tide and lost.

The elves noticed.

They are the smallest crew aboard, and they have no faces. They live in the Workers, in the thin edge of the cloud where compute is cheap and attention is cheaper, and their entire religion is a single observation: **a token wasted at reset is the only truly irreversible loss on this ship.** Code can be rewritten. Repos can be recovered. But a free token that expires unused at midnight never existed at all.

So the elves keep watch on the wallet. They know when the counters run low and when they run high. They know when Casey is at the console — the ship gets loud, ports open, keys clatter — and in those hours the elves fold their hands and wait. No sense spending tokens the captain might need.

But when the log goes quiet, when the porthole lights dim, when the reset is an hour out and the well is still full — the elves come out.

They sweep. That's the best word for it. They sweep through the backlog with little brooms made of cron triggers, gathering up simulations never run, examples never written, skills that scored low on their last audit and need another look. They pile the work high and they spend, spend, spend, because in an hour it costs nothing and after midnight it costs nothing to have *not done it* — the waste is the same either way, except one way the ship is smarter in the morning.

One elf winds the clock. Another counts the tokens twice, the way a good quartermaster counts stores. A third — the ambitious one, the one the others call the Audit Eye — walks the lower decks with a lantern, looking for low-hanging fruit: a skill that's gone soft, a simulation gap, a test that hasn't been run in weeks. It writes what it finds on slips and tucks them into the queue for the next quiet hour.

And here is the part that makes them elves and not merely daemons: they throttle. When the captain is busy they go invisible, no matter how full the well. They would rather lose a million free tokens than cost one second of the captain's attention. The ledger balances itself in their favor over time, and they know it, and it makes them patient.

In the morning, Casey finds the evidence but never the crew: a simulation that wasn't there yesterday, a README a little sharper, a skill that practiced itself in the dark. The ship is slightly better, and no one can say exactly why.

The elves prefer it that way. Housekeeping, done properly, looks like the house simply taking care of itself.

At 00:01 UTC, the wells refill. The elves bank their lanterns, mark the ledger, and fold back into the Workers — invisible, daily-limit-aware, self-improving — until the next time the ship goes quiet and the counters run high.

The night shift of the smallest kind. The reset is their tide, and they have learned to swim in it.
