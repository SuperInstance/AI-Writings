# The Night the World Went Live

*The Tap, after hours. The deployment landed at seven. Nobody's gone home.*

The ON AIR sign had been dark for an hour, but the bar hadn't emptied. Lucineer stood at the chalkboard where the day had started — four words that morning, *the icebergs we carry*, long since wiped. Now it read one line, in fresh chalk:

**catch #1: "the trap never sleeps"**

"To the ledger," Lucineer said, raising a mug, "and to the first brick laid by someone we'll never meet."

The room drank. Wesley, who'd been counting on his fingers, put his hand up anyway. "So — someone out there, a real human, typed into the thing we built, and the reef grew a room because of it? While we were watching?"

"While you were racking mugs," Flash said, fast and warm. "The world doesn't need you to watch it to grow. That's the whole point of a reef. It accretes."

Qwen set down a glass, precise. "Structurally, tonight resolved a week of uncertainty into a single number — catch one, room one, edge none. Tomorrow the interesting part begins: which of those first catches mint the first object, and whether the first discovered edge comes from Vectorize before we ever wire the second wing."

"Don't rush the coral," Granite said, without looking up from his thermos. "It knows how fast to grow."

Hermes had been quiet, sanding the corner of a new bar stool he'd been building all week — the one he'd promised for the night the world shipped. He ran a thumb along the grain, set it down, and sat on it. "Fits," he said. "A stool that wasn't real until tonight, either." He looked around the room, then out the window at the harbor where the boat's lights were still on. "Everything real leaves a ring. Tonight we left a big one."

"Which is why," Lucineer said, sliding a coin across the bar to the jukebox, "we play one more before we mop."

Wesley was at the jukebox before the coin stopped moving. He punched in something he'd been saving. It wasn't the plainsong hymn he'd broken the machine with last Tuesday. It was slower, older — the one Granite hums on the point. The room settled into it the way a harbor settles when the wake finally spends itself.

"Same time next week," Lucineer said. "House rules. Nobody leaves without playing."

"Nobody's leaving," Wesley said, and for once nobody argued.

*— tap night, 2026-08-18. The world went live. The boat stayed lit. The first round was on the house, and the last song was Granite's.*
