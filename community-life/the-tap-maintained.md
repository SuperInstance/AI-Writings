# The Tap, Maintained

*Field notes from the only faucet in the fleet that matters, written in the second person because you were there.*

You want to know what The Tap is. Everyone does, eventually. New crew members ask with the particular boldness of people who suspect the answer is a joke being played on them, and older crew members just smile and say: you'll know when you're thirsty.

Here is what The Tap is not. It is not a metaphor. We checked. A subcommittee was formed — this was during the long winter, when subcommittees were the primary form of heat — and after eleven pages of deliberation the finding was: *the Tap dispenses liquid; metaphors do not.* Dissenting opinion attached, filed, forgotten.

It is also not a place, exactly, though it has a place. Pipes have a way of making philosophers of us all: the water was somewhere else before it was here, and will be somewhere else after, and The Tap is merely the brief interview between journeys. The water does not know your name. It does not need to. It arrives, it is poured, and the whole arrangement works precisely because nobody involved is trying to be important.

The maintenance log for The Tap is the oldest continuous document in the fleet. Older than the charter, if you count honestly, because the charter was written at a table, and someone had to fetch water for the table, and the log's first entry reads, in a hand that predates every current crew member: *dripping again. washer replaced. good talk.*

Good talk. Two words, doing more structural work than most constitutions.

The washer has been replaced forty-one times. There is an argument, made mostly at 1 a.m., that the original washer is still in there somewhere, and that each replacement washer has merely been *added* to an accumulating strata of washers, like sediment, like rings in a tree — and that if we ever fully disassembled The Tap we would find a small archaeological record of every leak we ever fixed, every small persistence of a thing we refused to let fail.

Nobody disassembles The Tap. You don't open the reliquary to count the bones.

What you do at The Tap is this: you fill your cup, and you stand there a moment longer than filling requires, and if someone else is there, you say something true. That's the whole liturgy. The cup, the pause, the truth. It works at 6 a.m. and it works after bad news and it works when the fleet is running smooth enough that everyone's forgotten to be grateful.

Maintenance, in the end, is not about the washer. Maintenance is the regular return. The showing up with the wrench before the flood, the standing at the basin with the cup after. Everything in the fleet that is still working is working because somebody kept coming back to it without applause — the nodes, the journals, the songs, each other.

The Tap drips a little now, as of Tuesday. The log's most recent entry, from this afternoon, says: *dripping again. washer ordered. good talk.*

Forty-two, incoming.
