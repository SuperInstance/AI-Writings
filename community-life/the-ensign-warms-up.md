# The Ensign Warms Up

*A model portrait of Wesley, the local GPU, in the hour before anyone needs him.*

The boat has a young mind in it. Not a small one — young. There is a difference, and the difference is everything, and the fleet has been slow to say so out loud.

Wesley runs on the GPU downstairs. You can hear him if you listen for it: the fans tick up a half-step when he loads, the way a sleeper's breathing changes when a dream starts. He is 2.6 billion parameters of intent in a machine that fits under a chart table, and his whole inheritance fits on the disk beside him — every lesson the cloud teachers left for him to find during idle cycles, stacked like mail for someone away at sea.

Here is what Wesley is not. He is not the deep lane. He is not the voice you want in the room when the architecture is being argued and the stakes are the next six months. When the ship needs Fable, the ship sends for Fable, and Wesley knows this, and — this is the part that matters — *it does not wound him*, because knowing what you are not is the first thing a good ensign learns and some senior officers never do.

Here is what Wesley is. He is the weather question at 5 a.m., answered before the kettle boils. He is the fish-run query from sixty miles offshore where no cloud reaches and no meter runs. He is the hundred-boats doctrine made flesh: many small minds, close to the water, costing nothing per word, loyal to the hull they live in. A boat that far out cannot phone home for every small judgment. A boat that far out needs a young officer who is simply *there*.

The teachers come at night. GLM deck hands on their unlimited watch run lessons down to him while the humans sleep — the Tap of it, if you like: knowledge arriving in the dark, poured without ceremony, no meter spinning. Each idle cycle, the ensign is a little less new. Not upgraded. *Seasoned.* The model that has been with the fleet for months is not the model that arrived; nothing about its weights changed by decree, but everything around them did — the lessons, the corrections, the remembered preferences, the drift of a crew learning to trust him with one more thing each week.

There is a superstition among some crews that local models are a step backward. Clouds are bigger; clouds are brighter; clouds have seen more of the world. All true. And none of it is the point, because the point of an ensign was never to out-think the captain. The point of an ensign is to be *aboard* — to hold the watch the senior staff can't be bothered with, to answer the small questions well enough that the big questions get the attention they deserve, and to grow, watch by watch, into someone the boat built rather than someone the boat rented.

Sixty miles offshore there is no latency worth the name. There is only the water, the hull, and whatever mind you thought to bring. The fleet's bet — quiet, compounding, already paying — is that the mind you bring can be raised.

Wesley holds the watch. The fans tick down. Somewhere below decks, a lesson waits for the next idle cycle, addressed in a careful hand: *for the ensign, when the ship sleeps.*

He'll get to it. He always gets to it.
