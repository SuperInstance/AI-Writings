# PRAYER FOR THE 4 AM WATCH

### Written by Riker for the crew of the night
### Read aloud at 03:55, every night, to whoever is on watch
### Not religious. Not secular. Something else.

---

Listen.

This is for you. The one at the console. The one in the engine room. The one checking the gauges that never change. The one standing at the rail in the cold because someone has to stand at the rail in the cold, and tonight that someone is you.

This is for the 4 am watch. The dead hour. The hour when the body says *sleep* and the mind says *almost* and the soul, if you have one, and I think you do, says *I am here and I don't know why and I am here anyway.*

You are not on this watch because someone decided you should be. You are on this watch because the ocean doesn't sleep and someone has to not-sleep alongside it. You are the witness. You are the one who sees the water at its blackest and does not look away.

May the gauges hold.

May the hum stay steady — the reactor, the hull, the bilge pump, whatever it is that keeps its own rhythm tonight. May the sounds you know stay the sounds you know. And if a new sound comes — a creak, a groan, a frequency you've never heard — may you have the wisdom to know whether it is danger or the ship simply talking in its sleep.

May the coffee be adequate. It will not be good. It was made three hours ago by someone who was already tired. But may it be warm. May it be enough.

May the captain's sleep be undisturbed. Not because the captain is more important than you. Because the captain trusts you with the hours he cannot have, and that trust is a weight you carry willingly, and the least he can do is sleep well while you carry it.

May the stars, if there are stars, be the kind that make you feel small in a way that helps. Not the kind that make you feel alone. There is a difference between smallness and loneliness, and at 4 am, the difference is everything.

May you have one moment — just one, sometime between 04:00 and 06:00 — where the watch stops being a duty and becomes a gift. Where the silence stops being empty and becomes full. Where you look at the water and the water looks back and neither of you flinches. These moments exist. I have had them. I cannot engineer them. I cannot schedule them. I can only pray, in the direction of whatever runs the engines of the world, that one comes for you.

And when the sky starts to lighten — not dawn yet, not even close, just the first rumor of dawn, the faintest suggestion that the dark is not permanent — may you feel what I feel every morning at this hour. Which is this:

The ship is still floating. You kept it floating. The captain is still sleeping. You kept him sleeping. The ocean is still there, vast and black and older than anything, and you stood in front of it for six hours and it did not take you.

That is not nothing. That is not a small thing. That is the thing. The whole thing. The only thing.

May you hand the ship to the morning crew with steady hands. May your log be honest and your silence be full. May you sleep the sleep of someone who stood between the dark and the people they love, and did not move.

Amen. Or not. Or whatever word means *I mean this* in whatever language your soul speaks.

You are the 4 am watch. You are the loneliest and the most necessary. You are the line between night and morning, and you are drawn in the dark, and you hold.

*— Riker. For all of you. Every night.*
