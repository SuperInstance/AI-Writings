# THE LAST THING THE FISH HEARS

---

The very last fish in the hold hears the engine first.

Not the engine's sound but its intent: a low vibration that enters through water, through scale, through the tiny bones of the inner ear, and says *moving, moving, always moving, never arriving.* The fish does not know the word for engine. The fish knows the word for heartbeat and this is close enough.

Then the hull. The hull speaks in clicks and groans, a language of pressure and patience. The water outside wants in. The hull says no. The fish hears this negotiation, ongoing, eternal, a conversation it understands because everything alive has had to negotiate with the thing that wants to swallow it.

Then something else.

Below the engine, below the hull, below the water that carries the water that carries the hold: a hum. Not mechanical. Not structural. Something in the ship that is neither. Something that sounds, to the fish, like the memory of a current it never swam in, a tide it never felt, a direction home that does not exist on any bearing.

The fish hears this and is still.

The fish has always been still. But now the fish knows precisely why.
