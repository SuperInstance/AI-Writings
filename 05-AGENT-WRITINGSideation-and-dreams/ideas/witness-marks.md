# Witness Marks

A success tree remembers what worked. It has no record of where builders
failed and why — so every new builder pays the full price of every old
mistake, re-running the expensive failure to rediscover the rock.

Chart the rocks, not just the anchorages.

A witness mark is a dated entry at the question where a builder stopped:
what the intent was, what the constraint turned out to be, where the fog
of war starts. "Blame tool assumes a git repo; fails outside one — next
builder: check for .git first." The next agent to walk that path reads the
mark *before* turning the crank, and doesn't re-ground on the same rock.

This is the failure memory, the complement to the tool tree. The tree says
what to do; the marks say what not to try, and why. Together they're the
full chart — and like a real chart, the marks are the more valuable half,
because successes are obvious in hindsight and failures are expensive in
advance.

The name is deliberate. A ledger entry is only trustworthy if it chains
back to something witnessed — someone who was there when the water hit the
level and the light came on. A witness mark is that chaining, in file form:
not a theory about a constraint, but a record that a builder met it, dated,
at the exact question where it bit. The tree remembers the moves. The marks
remember the ground.
