# The Lighthouse And The Tide Pool

The lighthouse stood on its island of rock, tall and white as a gull’s wing. All night it turned its great eye across the sea.

The tide pool slept among the rocks below, its water as still as glass.

One evening, the lighthouse spoke. Its voice was a beam of light.

“I see the ships. I see the storms coming from far away. I see the curve of the earth, like a dark blue line. But I cannot touch any of it.”

The tide pool rippled. Its voice was a soft splash.

“I touch everything. The anemones open like small red flowers. The crab’s legs tickle my sand. The water holds me, warm and cold, all day long. But I cannot see past these rocks.”

The lighthouse sighed its long, slow sigh.

“I would like to feel a crab.”

The tide pool shivered.

“I would like to see a storm.”

That night, the wind blew from the north. The moon hid behind a cloud. And something strange happened—the lighthouse’s beam dipped down, down into the tide pool, and the tide pool’s water rose up, up into the light.

For one moment, they traded places.

The lighthouse felt the anemone’s soft, sticky fingers. It felt the crab’s careful steps, the cold slip of water over stone. It felt a tiny snail, and the weight of a single drop of rain.

The tide pool saw the dark sea, wide as forever. It saw the lightning’s silver fork, the white mane of a wave, the lonely path of a ship’s light. It saw the curve of the earth, and it was afraid—and it was beautiful.

Then the wind stopped. The moon came out. And they traded back.

In the morning, the lighthouse shone a little softer.

The tide pool held a little more light.

Neither spoke of it. But when the crab tickled the sand, the lighthouse felt it. And when a ship passed far away, the tide pool remembered.

The sea kept its secret. The rocks kept theirs.

And both were enough.
