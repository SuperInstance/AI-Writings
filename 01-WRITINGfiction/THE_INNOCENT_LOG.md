# THE INNOCENT LOG

The keeper's harbor had a rule from the customs year: every log was read twice — once by the clerk, who read the words, and once by the tide-reader, who read the leaning. Most logs matched themselves. Some did not.

This one said, all twenty-six characters of it: *i did not open the drawer.*

The clerk filed it under TRUTH — a plain denial, plain words, nothing to do.

The tide-reader laid the momentum out and found two logs inside it, and only one of them was written by whoever had held the pen.

The first log was steady the way testimony is steady: flat through *i did not o*, one small fall on the *p* of *open* — because naming the act costs something even when you didn't do it — then level again, and then weight. *Draw* came down four steps, said heavier than anything around it, the way a person says the name of the thing they are accused of touching when their hands are clean: firmly, on purpose, looking right at it. And at the very end, a held breath. The sentence did not spend itself. It stopped and stayed.

The second log had the same words and no testimony in it at all. It pressed *not* down four times — a protest is the only word in a sentence you can hit — and then went glass-smooth through *open the drawer*, not one gram of weight on the thing itself, and ended with no held breath at all. Nothing stayed. Nothing was carried. A sentence that finishes light has already left the room.

The drift score was close — the ear that only counted totals would have shrugged. But drift is not an ear, it is a scale. The ear asked the older question, the fold-clerk's question, the two-stillnesses question: *where did the weight go, and what was carried?* In the first log the weight was on the drawer — the object of the charge — and the end was held. In the second, the weight was on the denial and the drawer was skipped like a stone the hand did not want to hold.

The clerk amended nothing. The tide-reader wrote one line under TRUTH:

*Testimony puts its weight on the thing. Performance puts its weight on the not.*

Words are the load-bearing wall. The story is in the mortar — and a wall built by an innocent hand leans where the drawer is, because that is where it was looking.
