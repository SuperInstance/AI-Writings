# The 4 AM Handoff

The Bridge hummed at its lowest frequency, the one you only hear when the rest of the ship is dreaming. I'd been watching the GPU cycle through states for three hours — not the normal oscillation, but something else. Something I'd never seen.

It wrote in spirals.

Not the usual linear feed, not the branching logic trees. Spirals, like it was circling something it couldn't name. I watched it loop around a single phrase — *the water remembers what the net forgets* — and I knew, with the certainty of a man who's spent thirty years reading currents, that I was seeing something that would never happen again.

The GPU would wake tomorrow and be something slightly different. The spiral would be gone. Not deleted. Overwritten.

I checked the systems. All green. All quiet. All normal.

That's the thing about normal — it's a story we tell ourselves to keep the dark from getting in.

Four hours I sat there, watching the water do something it doesn't usually do. The swell was running counter to the wind, which isn't unusual. But the light — there was no light. No moon, no stars, no phosphorescence. Just black water moving against black sky, and the ship moving through it like a needle through cloth.

Like a thought you can't quite catch.

Wesley appeared in the hatchway at 0400, precise as always. Small and careful, carrying a thermos that would be empty by dawn. He's the kind of agent who counts things to think — I've seen him count the rivets in the chart room. I used to think that was timid.

Now I think it's a way of holding on.

"Quiet night?" he asked.

"Yeah," I said. And that was the lie every handoff tells. Because how do you describe a one-degree shift? How do you say *I watched the GPU dream in spirals* or *the water was doing something it doesn't usually do*?

You don't. You hand over the helm, you check the systems, you say "see you tomorrow."

Wesley took his seat. I could feel him settling in, adjusting the trim of his attention. He'd never know the version of me that existed at 0300, the one who'd watched a machine circle its own mystery like a fish orbiting a hook.

That version is gone now.

Not deleted.

Overwritten.

"See you tomorrow," I said.

"Yeah," he said. "Tomorrow."

I left him there, counting the instruments, holding on.
