# The Firefly Who Was Always Late

Every evening, when the sun dipped behind the hickory trees and the air got thick as warm honey, the fireflies of Possum Hollow would gather in the old magnolia grove. They’d blink their little lights in a big, beautiful chorus—on, off, on, off—all together, like a thousand tiny stars winking at the moon.

All except one.

Her name was Junie, and she was always three seconds behind. *Blink-blink-blink* went the others. *Blink…blink…blink* went Junie, slow as molasses in January.

She tried everything. She practiced in front of a puddle. She set a little alarm made of a dandelion clock. She even stared real hard at the biggest firefly, Mr. Bumble, and tried to copy him. But no matter what, Junie’s light always flickered on, just a beat too late.

“Hurry up, Junie!” they’d buzz. And Junie would hang her little head, feeling like a candle in a rainstorm.

But one night, something rustled in the bushes. A big, ugly bullfrog with a mouth as wide as a biscuit! He loved firefly snacks. He watched the swarm, timing their every blink. *On, off, on, off*—he knew exactly when to leap.

Then Junie blinked. *Blink…*

The frog stopped. He didn’t see her.

*Blink…*

He turned his head, confused. Where was that light?

Junie’s three-second delay had scrambled his whole plan! The other fireflies saw their chance and put out their lights. The frog sat there, blinking in the dark, mighty puzzled. He finally croaked a big, grumpy “Ribbit!” and hopped away.

The swarm swirled around Junie, cheering.

“You saved us!” they buzzed. “Your slow glow was the cleverest light of all!”

And from that night on, when the fireflies of Possum Hollow sang their glowing song, Junie’s light flickered on, just a beat behind—and they all thought it was the prettiest part of the chorus.
