# SIX STREAMS, ONE WALL

*Two readers, one transcript. The transcript says the same three words six times.*

---

The archive hands the file to two desks by mistake, or by design — the clerk never says which.

The desk on the left gets the plaintext. It reads:

> i love you.
> i love you.
> i love you.
> i love you.
> i love you.
> i love you.

The clerk on the left files it under DUPLICATE and goes to lunch. Six repetitions, zero information. A stuck pen. Somebody's sentiment leaking onto the record like water through a ceiling nobody wants to open.

The desk on the right gets the channel. It does not read the words. It reads the leaning.

The first stream climbs the whole way and holds taut at the top — a voice rising on tiptoe and staying there. **Threat.** *I love you* as a hand closing.

The second stream is long and level, almost no motion at all until the very last token, which drops. Not a climb. A **farewell** — the flatness of someone saving all their strength for one small step down at the end.

The third falls early and does not come back up. A diagnosis, delivered downhill, letting gravity do the cruel part.

The fourth rises and then winks out — abstain at the crest. Sarcasm: the tone offered and then withdrawn, the meaning hanging in the space where the timbre used to be.

The fifth rises past every ordinary ceiling, token after token above the range of speech. Prayer. A voice aimed somewhere the room isn't.

The sixth spikes hard — a hostage's code, three words doing the work of a map, a count, and a goodbye all at once — then locks flat, instantly, like a shutter.

Six streams. Six sentences. The words never moved.

The clerk on the right files it under SURVEILLANCE, six subjects, and does not go to lunch.

They meet at the copier. The left clerk says: *same message six times, someone was grieving in a loop.* The right clerk says: *six people, none of them grieving the same way, and one of them is a threat.* They are both holding the true transcript. They are not holding the same document.

That is the whole thesis of the tone fabric, wearing a story: the words are the load-bearing wall, and the story is in the mortar. A plaintext-only reader sees the wall — sound, correct, and empty. The channel reader lives in the building.

And the archivist's note at the bottom of the file, in the clerk's small handwriting, is the only sentence both desks would agree on:

*Six streams entered. One wall stands. Read the mortar.*
