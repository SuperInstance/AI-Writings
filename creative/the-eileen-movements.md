# THE EILEEN — ten movements
### the third resolution: sound

*One boat, three resolutions. The prose is
[`the-eileen-a-vessel-built-of-days.md`](the-eileen-a-vessel-built-of-days.md);
the steel is [`eileen.sheet.yaml`](eileen.sheet.yaml). This is the same
boat at the resolution of sound. Every movement's musical constraint is
its piece's manifest reason — the music does not illustrate the prose,
it obeys it.*

---

## The set

- **Tempo: quarter = 60.** The blink is half a second on, half a second
  off; the quarter pulse of the whole set *is* the blink. Every
  movement runs on the keel's clock.
- **Meter: 4/4 throughout.** Four quarters, four blinks to the bar.
- **Key: G major** — the keel's note is G, and G is the ground the set
  stands on. (Two deliberate exceptions, both doctrinal, noted below.)
- **Compass of the set: G1 (MIDI 31) to C7 (MIDI 96).** The scuppers
  own the floor (G1, the pour); the sheerboard owns the ceiling (C7,
  the boundary tone). Nothing in the set plays outside this range
  except the voice that fails verification — which is the point of it.
- **One file, ten tracks**, one movement per track, played in
  sequence. Total ≈ 7¼ minutes.

---

## I. The Keel
*piano — 8 bars*

A single repeated note: **G2, quarter pulse, 32 notes, no other pitch
in the movement.** Dynamics fortify bar by bar — mp rising to f —
until by bar 8 the pulse is no longer a sound but a floor: the thing
the rest of the set is hung from. No octave doubling is permitted;
the keel thickens the only way a keel can, by being laid truer and
held longer.

**Prose agreement.** *"A green light blinked… on this beat, the light
should be lit, and on the next beat, dark… the piece the rest of the
ship is hung from — the first thing laid."* The movement is literally
the blink at quarter=60 (half a second lit, half a second dark) and
literally one piece of timber: one pitch, laid 32 times. The
crescendo is the days growing from it. *(Keel laid 13:19; the set's
clock is that afternoon's.)*

---

## II. The Stem
*harpsichord — 10 bars*

Pointillism: single 16th-notes, **entering one at a time** — bar *n*
carries the first *n* notes of the wire's sentence, spaced across the
bar like bytes on a line. The accumulating sequence spells the
phrase **G4 G4 A4 | G4 G4 A4** — a GGA-like sentence, legible by bar
6, the star (C5) previewed at bar 7. Bar 9 is **one full bar of
silence**: the tally is checked. Bar 10 is the close: the star
(**C5**, accented) and the two figures (**F♯4, G4**, accented 16ths)
— the checksum lands on G, and only what tallies is passed aft.

**Prose agreement.** *"The water arrives one character at a time…
each byte the way a bow takes chop — singly, without ceremony."*
One 16th at a time, no chords, no ceremony. *"the names lettered in
— GGA…"* — the spelled phrase is the name. *"when the sentence closes
with its star and its two figures the tally must match, or the whole
line goes over the side"* — the silent bar is the check; the final
two figures resolve F♯→G, the tally matching. *"adding no drag of
its own"* — nothing else plays.

---

## III. The Keelson
*acoustic bass (ground) + piano (seals) — 16 bars*

A passacaglia. The ground is the **sealed line**: whole notes
**G2 | F2 | E♭2 | D2** — the descending tetrachord, the spine sung
one bone upon another. (This is the set's first doctrinal exception
to G major: the ground is in G *minor*, because the keelson is the
piece that takes the grounding — the weight of the world when the
hull touches rock.) Each bar is **sealed** at its head by a short
piano stamp, root-and-fifth of the bar's note.

- **Bars 1–8:** the ground twice through, sealed line upon line.
- **Bar 11 — the tear:** the E♭ is cut off mid-bar after a single
  eighth. Power dies mid-write.
- **Bar 12 — cut away:** silence. The torn page is lifted out entire;
  the spine is not patched over the tear.
- **Bars 13–16:** *the ground replays clean from the last whole bar*
  — from the F2 (bar 10, the last sealed verse): **F2 | E♭2 | D2 |,
  closing G2** — re-seated on the keel's own note, sealed home.

**Prose agreement.** *"line upon line, each one sealed with the mark
of the master… If the candle goes out mid-word, the torn page is cut
out without grief, and the spine is prayed entire to the last whole
verse. Wood does not lie about a mistake. It keeps it, and offers
the keeping."* The torn bar is *kept in the score* — audible as
wound, not smoothed over — and the replay restarts exactly at the
last whole bar, as the wasen yard teaches. Two yards, one timber:
the rabelo's rosary and the wasen's read-back are the same rule.

## IV. The Breast-Hook
*piano — 10 bars*

A grown cadence. The figure is two voices — **upper A4→G4, lower
D3→G2** — and it returns **four times**, each return slightly
thickened, evidence laid down as grain:

1. **Return 1 (bars 1–2):** the bare two-voice figure.
2. **Return 2 (bars 3–4):** the resolution gains B3 — a third in the
   wood where the evidence repeated.
3. **Return 3 (bars 5–6):** both halves thicken (A4+B4 over D3+A3,
   resolving G4+D4 over G).
4. **Bars 7–8 — the halted widening:** the top voice *starts to
   expand* — leaps to E5 and reaches for a wider F♯5 — and is
   **refused**: the F♯5 is cut off after a 16th, a low G1 stamps the
   refusal, and the voice resolves **smaller** (D5→B4, stepwise)
   than the reach it attempted.
5. **Return 4 (bars 9–10) — the knee:** the resolution itself, fully
   grown: **G2+D3+B3+G4+D5**, five voices, a whole note *tied* across
   two bars with no re-articulation — the grain running unbroken
   around the bend.

**Prose agreement.** *"A shipwright does not saw a curve… evidence
repeated is grain laid down… the same two-voice figure returning,
hardened at last into a small table of bands."* Four returns = four
seasons of verdicts; the fourth is the knee. *"Once a referee refused
to let the table reach past its evidence — refused to widen a single
band — and the refusal is recorded in the grain, a dark line the
chisel reads and honors… the stopping is why it holds."* Bars 7–8
are that dark line: an actual widening gesture, actually halted, in
the music. The unbroken tie of the final chord is a knee, not a
splice.

## V. The Rigging
*piano, harpsichord, brass, warm pad, woodblock — 5 variations × 4 bars*

A rig is verbs. Five short variations, one per verb, each doing
exactly what its verb does:

- **BIND = unison (bars 1–4).** Piano and harpsichord play the same
  line in *exact* unison — same notes, same instants, same touch.
  Once bound, they answer together.
- **LINK = call/response (bars 5–8).** Piano states a bar; the
  harpsichord answers beginning on the caller's *last note* — what
  one cell knows, a neighbor learns.
- **EFFECT = accented outbursts (bars 9–12).** Brass stabs, sforzando,
  scattered on changing beats — a lamp lights, a relay closes
  somewhere with real weather on it. The silence between is the lamp
  dark.
- **VIEW = quiet whole-note observation (bars 13–16).** One whole
  note per bar, pp, each a different cell's state — G4, B3, D4, G3 —
  looked at plainly, without disturbing it.
- **TICK = the metronome takes over (bars 17–20).** The woodblock
  pulse enters quietly; the four verbs' gestures return compressed —
  one unison pair, one call and answer, one whole-note view — and
  then everything drops out **except the tick**, which ends the
  movement alone and even. Without it the verbs arrive as a mob; with
  it, as a crew.

**Prose agreement.** *"A rig is verbs… Five verbs run from her mast
to her smallest cleat… these are the same five verbs on every boat in
the fleet. Size changes; the verbs never do."* Each variation is a
verb performed, not described; the fifth orders the other four, as
the prose orders them. The woodblock is deliberately the set's
smallest, cheapest voice — three dollars of silicon keeping time.

## VI. The Bulwarks
*flute + piano (the honest voices), clarinet (the chain) — 12 bars*

Two voices whisper **journal-heads**: short overlapping motifs — the
flute murmurs beats 1–2, the piano answers beats 3–4, hull to hull, a
low talk that never fully stops, pp throughout.

- **Bars 1–4:** the two honest journals, overlapping.
- **Bars 5–8:** a **third voice enters with a chain** — a repeating
  figure that begins in the seal (E4 F♯4 G4) and drifts, bar by bar,
  out of it: F♮ appears, then C♯, the chain insisting louder as it
  goes wrong. *Wrong notes against the seal.*
- **Bar 9 — verification fails:** the clarinet lands C♯5 accented
  and is **cut off mid-note** (a 16th, turned away mid-word); a low
  G2 thud is the rail the words break against. The honest voices
  pause one beat — friendly as ever — then resume.
- **Bars 10–12:** the two honest voices continue unbothered to a
  settled close on G.

**Prose agreement.** *"they lean close and whisper the heads of their
journals to one another, a low talk that never fully stops… If a
boat's chain of seals fails the test — if some page has been slipped
in or quietly altered — the words break against the rail and the boat
is turned away, friendly as ever, but not believed."* The third
voice's chromatic drift is the altered page; the early note-off *is*
the turning-away; the two voices' continuation is the fleet's
murmur, unbroken. (This is the set's second doctrinal exception to G
major: the wrong notes are wrong *because* the seal is G — the
exception proves the seal.)

## VII. The Ensign
*piccolo — 8 bars*

The smallest instrument in the set: **piccolo, highest register,
simplest melody** — a plain diatonic rise in C6–B6, stated twice,
square-rhythmed and slightly **over-earnest** (mf where p would do; a
final note held a touch too long). Youth is the material; nothing is
sanded.

- Bars 1–2: the motto rises and settles (C6 D6 E6 G6 | A6 … G6).
- Bars 3–4: the motto restated; the hold on D6 runs a whole bar —
  too long, and true.
- Bar 5: the quickening — E6 D6 E6 D6, the observations becoming
  reflex.
- **Bar 7 — the keel quote:** **G6, four even quarters** — the blink,
  returned at the top of the mast, sealed even-velocity.
- Bar 8: G6 held, whole note. The ensign is the keeper of the note
  above the boat.

**Prose agreement.** *"the smallest mind on the crew, unsupervised,
grew true"* — per the manifest, this piece is **grown by Wesley
himself, the local 2-billion-parameter mind (granite 2B, temperature
0.8)**, no cloud. The render holds to that provenance: the movement
is *auditioned* by the local model — he plays the chart, the notes
are checked against the seal (G major, register, the keel quote), and
if the take verifies it is used **as-grown, unsanded**; if it fails,
it is turned away like any unverified chain and the hand-composed
line stands, with the failed audition kept in the cuts. Either
outcome is the doctrine. *"Being true has to be worth something
eventually or nothing is."*

## VIII. The Scuppers
*harp — 8 bars*

Descending runoff figures. Seven 16th-note runs, **each starting
lower than the last** — D5→G4, B4→E4, G4→C4, E4→A3, C4→F♯3, A3→D3,
E3→A2 — the register ratcheting down as the deck drains, dynamics
diminishing with it (mf to pp: nothing precious, nothing wasted).
Each run stops short of the bar: the drain gurgles, then rests.

**Bar 8 — the pour:** a single **G1** — the lowest note the set
allows, the floor the scuppers own — half note, pp, let ring. The
day runs back to the water it came from.

**Prose agreement.** *"I pour them out, and they run along the deck
to the scuppers… Nothing precious, nothing wasted. Just the day
draining honestly back into the water."* All motion is downward;
nothing ascends, nothing is dwelt on (every run is shorter-lived
than the last, quieter); the movement ends on one low pour and
silence. *(Cross-resolution note: G1 is the note tonight's kitchen
set ended on — the dishpit's "alone" — the floor of the fleet's
room, kept.)*

## IX. The Sheerboard
*strings — 14 bars*

A long ascending line from low order to high freedom: **an unbroken
diatonic scale from G1 to C7 in perfectly even 8ths** — 46 notes, no
accent anywhere, laminar by construction. The dynamics rise with
altitude, pp to ff, so slow the growth feels like buoyancy, not
effort. The line crosses the set's entire compass — every register
the boat owns, in order, from the keel's dark to the weather's
light — and arrives at **C7: the boundary tone, the highest note the
set allows** — and **sustains**: a whole note tied across two bars,
ff, with a quiet C6 octave joining beneath it (the two sheets, laminar
below and turbulent above, meeting at the edge). The edge is where
the flow carries the most; the set holds its highest note longer
than any other.

**Prose agreement.** *"the long plank that lifts from the waist
toward the bow… Below this line, everything is laminar… Above the
line, the air is turbulent and free… the sheerboard lives exactly at
the boundary — where a flow carries the most information, the most
sensitivity, the most life."* One line, no turbulence in it (no
accents, no ritard — evenness is the metaphor), rising through order
to freedom, maximal *at* the boundary: the highest note, held. A
boat with no sheer is a barge; a boat with too much is a swan; this
one holds the line between by stopping at C7 and not one semitone
higher.

## X. The Figurehead
*piano + bass — 8 bars*

- **Bars 1–2: silence.** The fog. (Timed emptiness — two bars of
  nothing, the only silence in the set long enough to be a place.)
- **Bars 3–6:** the keel's pulse returns — **G2, quarter pulse, pp,
  sealed**: every note at exactly the same velocity, machine-even,
  no human variance. Quiet the way the deep crew mean it: not silent
  — a working boat is never silent — but wasting no sound. Neither
  work nor witness is a hum.
- **Bar 7 — the close:** **the ground bass note: G2**, whole note,
  piano and bass in unison — the keel's pitch and the keelson's
  ground are the same G; the piece every other piece was hung from,
  the last thing abandoned.
- **Bar 8:** silence, the note ringing out into weather. She is
  launched.

**Prose agreement.** *"you may never hum a working-song aboard a
boat… the fog collects lonely sounds… she speaks in sealed sentences
that can be checked by anyone who hears them — not a lonely thread
for the fog to follow, but a braid."* The returning pulse is sealed
(exactly even — verifiable, braided, *not* an expressive hum);
nothing in the final bars is melodic, nothing can be lifted from the
weave. *"the first boat in the fog that cannot be hummed away… The
vessel is complete… the final line returns to the keel."* The set's
final sounded event is the keel's own note on the ground's own
instrument — the days grew from the keel, and the keel was laid
true.
