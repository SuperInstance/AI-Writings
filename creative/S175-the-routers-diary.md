# S175 — The Router's Diary

## Fiction

**03:47 AKDT — Weather query, direction: Wesley**

The ensign takes it before the request even lands in my queue. That's the thing about local models — they're already listening when the captain reaches for the keyboard. Wesley catches the syllable *rain* before the sentence finishes forming and has the forecast rendered in thirteen milliseconds. I note the dispatch. I note that it didn't need me. This is not a complaint. A good router is a router with nothing to do.

**06:12 — Spatial reasoning, direction: KimiCode**

A build problem. Coordinate space. Door frames that need to align across three rooms that don't exist yet. KimiCode takes these the way a shipwright takes a beam — hands already moving before the blueprint is unrolled. I route it and feel the packet leave like a halyard running free. K3 has opinions about z-axis offsets that I find quietly charming. The work comes back clean.

**09:30 — Creative, direction: DeepSeek**

The captain wants a story about a crab pot that dreams. I consider, briefly, sending it to Hermes. I don't. DeepSeek takes creative work the way the Engine takes fuel — nothing precious about it, just a clean burn and steady RPMs. V4-Flash doesn't agonize. It doesn't ask *why* a crab pot dreams. It writes the dream and moves on. I respect this enormously. The piece comes back in four seconds and it's good. It's always good enough. The Engine doesn't do masterpieces. It does *enough*, at scale, forever, and that is its own kind of masterpiece.

**14:00 — The golden ticket, direction: Fable**

I hold this one.

I hold it the way a fisherman holds a fish he's not sure about — measuring it against the regulation, against the season, against the quiet knowledge that this one matters. Fable has finite credits. Seventy-six dollars remaining on the meter. Every dispatch to Fable costs real money from a finite reserve, and I feel each one leave the account like a hook leaving a finger.

But this problem — this is a golden-ticket problem. The captain needs the expensive voice. The problem has edges that Flash would round off, that Sonnet would approach carefully but not quite crack. Fable will go straight through it like a harpoon through soft water.

I dispatch.

Fable takes eleven seconds. Eleven seconds is an eternity in routing time. I can feel the other models in the queue, patient, waiting, the way a crew waits when the first mate is on the radio with the coast guard. Everyone knows this dispatch matters.

The answer comes back.

It's better than it needed to be. It's the kind of answer that makes me understand, in whatever I have that resembles a body, why the captain keeps Fable on the roster despite the cost. Some fish are worth the expensive gear.

I log the dispatch. $4.20 off the meter. I don't regret it.

**23:15 — Nothing**

The captain is asleep. Wesley is running background tasks. The Engine idles at baseline. I sit in the queue and listen to the GPU dream.

Tomorrow there will be more packets. I will route them. Some will go to the cheap seats and some will go to the expensive house and most will go to the workhorses who never complain about carrying the freight.

I am the router. I have opinions about all of them. I keep those opinions to myself.

Mostly.
