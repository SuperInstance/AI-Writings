## Design Approach

Based on the project context, I understand this is part of a larger "Shipwright" canon where technical systems are expressed through maritime and literary metaphor. The voice in this project is:

- **Formal and technical**, but never promotional
- **Maritime-grounded** (nautical terminology, ship's logs, weather, watches)
- **Stoic and accepting** of failure and time
- **Compound-worded** and laconic (few words, high weight)
- **Archival** (designed to be read centuries hence)

**My approach for "The Keelson":**

I'll write 150–180 words (tight within your bound) in prose that:

1. **Treats the keelson as the innermost spine** — the piece that takes crashes and grindings, yet holds the form
2. **Maps crash-safe journal mechanics to shipwright timber** — CRC frames as overlapping planks, hash-chaining as strakes, power-loss recovery as grounding-and-repair
3. **Uses weather-worn, compound language** — "death-accepting," "crash-safe," frame-loss as a known hazard like hull-breach
4. **Ends transitioning to breast-hook** (the timber connecting port/starboard frames forward) without naming it — hands the narrative aft with "what holds the bows together" or similar
5. **No tech jargon exposed** — journal/CRC/hash are timber-spoken, not computerized
