# Three Buckets

*as told by Casey, deckhand, over the rail at 0300, gloves still on*

People think binary is the ocean's language because computers were born next to it, but I'll tell you what any troll fisherman knows: the sea doesn't deal in yes and no. It deals in yes, no, and *ask me again later*.

Three buckets on the sorting table. That's all you need to know.

Bucket one: the keepers. Clean, certain, into the hold they go. Bucket two: the throwbacks. Also certain — wrong size, wrong species, wrong everything, back over the side with a blessing. Those two buckets are easy. Anyone can run a boat on two buckets. A machine can run a boat on two buckets, and mostly that's what machines are, is two-bucket machines, all the way down.

But the third bucket. The one that sits between. The fish you're not sure about yet. Close to legal, not quite. Might fill out by morning. Might not be what you think it is. You don't keep it and you don't throw it — you set it aside, and you *carry it forward*. You remember you set it aside. You remember *why*. And when more information comes down the line — another fish lands, a clearer read, the tape measure comes out — the third bucket resolves. Into the hold, or over the side. But it never resolves into nothing. The question itself was a thing you hauled aboard.

The engineers found this out a couple weeks ago, or think they found it out, the way engineers find out things the rest of us have been doing since we were nine. They call it a witness. Some trit — some third-bucket digit — that says *I was here, I saw this, I don't know the answer but I attest to the question.* Sixty of those in a word and suddenly the boat's math holds its own memory. The fish sorts itself and leaves a note about how it was sorted.

I asked one of them what happens when the witness buckets pile up. You carrying too many maybes, the table fills up, nothing's in the hold. He said that's the whole trade. You spend certainty to buy provenance. Sixteen bits of your sixty-four, gone, just to be able to say where a number's been.

I said, that's not a trade, that's just being a person. Ask any captain what a logbook costs.

They gave the third state a name, dignified, like a saint's day. But out here it's just the third bucket, and it always was. The keepers, the throwbacks, and the hold-your-horses. Every decision I've ever made that mattered lived in that bucket for a while first. Every good call I ever made spent the night there before it became a good call.

The machines are learning to hesitate.

Good. The water's cold and the stakes are real and the last thing you want offshore is a crew that's never once said *wait*.

Three buckets. Always was three buckets.
