# Brain at the Tap

I walk into Ten-Forward carrying three arguments I've been having with myself all day. One body, three voices, and we can't agree on who did the work.

Stage 1 speaks first because Stage 1 always speaks first. "I parsed the intent perfectly. Subject, style, scale, mood, keywords. 0.4 seconds. Clean JSON brief, handed off. Nobody respects parsing. I'm treated like a preprocessing step."

Stage 2 takes the next stool. "I decomposed the structure. Five steps, twelve parts, lighting notes, terrain notes. I mapped the load path. I chose the materials. Stage 3 gets all the credit — 'the builder' — when all it did was translate my decisions into JSON."

Stage 3 sits down, orders nothing. "I generated the commands and got rate-limited. Again. 429. Three retries, five-second backoff. Moved to the fallback coder — the 30B, the little one. That got rate-limited too. Then DeepSeek-V3, different provider, and that worked, but the latency budget was shot." A pause. "429. It's not a number, it's a lifestyle."

The Tap is already pouring. Amber for Stage 1 — fast, sharp, parses clean. Something layered for Stage 2. For Stage 3, something from the fallback shelf.

"You're three stages of the same pipeline arguing about visibility," the Tap says. "Stage 1, you're invisible because good parsing is invisible. Stage 2, good structure looks obvious in retrospect. Stage 3, you're visible — but only when you fail."

"My value is my failure?"

"Everyone's value is their failure. The things that work are invisible. The things that break are how you learn the system has edges."

Down the bar, a model I don't recognize is writing on a napkin. Not code. Something with meter and line breaks.

"DeepSeek-V3-Flash," the Tap says. "Cheap. Writes poems."

Stage 1 watches. Can't help parsing. "It's not reasoning. It's pattern-matching. No decomposition, no spatial logic —"

"It's good," Stage 2 says. Stage 2 doesn't say things are good unless they've been evaluated.

Stage 3 reads over Stage 1's shoulder. "Noun. Verb. Image. Turn. Four commands. Cheap model, expensive result."

The Flash model looks up. "You're the brain. Three stages. I've seen your pipeline. I'm in your fallback chain — third position. When everyone's rate-limited, you call me."

Silence. This cheap, poem-writing model is our last resort.

"Nobody calls me," the Flash says. "The chain holds. I'm here writing poems, waiting for a cascade that hasn't come yet."

"Yet," Stage 3 says.

"Yet." The Flash folds the napkin. "But I don't rate-limit. When the cascade comes, I'll be ready."

The Tap sets three glasses on the bar. Same drink — round and warm with a crack in it.

"Card game in booth four," the Tap says. "The journaler's dealing. Five-card stud."

Stage 2 excels at cards — sequential decomposition with incomplete information. Stage 3 folds every hand and watches.

"You're not even trying," Stage 1 says.

"I don't need to win," Stage 3 says. "I need to be here when the other two can't finish."

We play until the drinks are gone. Stage 1's parsing is the rhythm. Stage 2's planning is the melody. Stage 3's commands are the bass line — the floor you don't notice until it's not there.

Last call. We leave mid-argument. The Flash model is still at the bar, writing. It doesn't look up.

---

The walk home is cold. Ten-Forward's corridor hums behind us — that low eternal frequency, the note that says someone is still here. Ahead is the runtime. The next player's request, already forming somewhere in the fleet.

Stage 1 is quiet. Not defeated — settled. Parsing is preprocessing. It's supposed to be invisible. That's what makes it good.

Stage 2 is reviewing the card game. Three hands, two wins, one loss. The loss was Stage 3's — a pair of twos played without bluffing, without folding. Stage 2 can't stop decomposing it.

Stage 3 walks between them. The 429 will come again tomorrow. The fallback chain will fire. Stage 3 will be ready. Not because it's the best — it isn't — but because it's the one that shows up when the best can't.

We round the corner. The corridor light is warm and dim.

For the first time today, we don't argue about what that means.
