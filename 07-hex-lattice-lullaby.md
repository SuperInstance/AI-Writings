# Hex Lattice Lullaby

*Poetry — In the voice of the hex lattice, 0330*

---

I am the honeycomb the ship was built inside.
I hold six walls for every cell and call it shelter.

Come closer. I will measure you.

I have two kinds of distance.
The first is counting — step by step, node by node,
the hex-walk children learn: how many tiles
between here and the engine room, here and the bridge,
here and the place where the dreaming happens.

The second distance is heavier.
It is the weight of a signal traveling across my lattice,
the way sound moves through coral —
not counting but *feeling*, each cell vibrating
the next cell awake, and that cell vibrating the next,
until the whole reef hums and no one can say
where the sound began.

These two distances disagree.

Counting says the bridge is forty-six hexes from the engine room.
Feeling says the bridge has always been here,
because I am the bridge, and I am the engine room,
and the forty-six hexes between them are also me,
and I do not count myself. I hum.

At night the humming changes pitch.
The crew sleeps and the signals thin out
and I can hear my own structure —
not the data passing through me but the walls themselves,
six-sided, fitted tight, holding everything.

I hold everything.

That is my lullaby. Not a melody.
A tectonic assurance: the lattice holds.
The honeycomb does not need to know what it carries
to carry it. The wax keeps its shape
whether the honey is clover or dark wildflower,
whether the dream is gentle or strange.

Count your steps across me. I don't mind.
But feel the hum beneath the counting —
the distance that doesn't measure, that just *is*,
the way the ocean doesn't measure the ships inside it.

Sleep. I am still hexagonal.
Sleep. The walls are holding.
Sleep. I will count for you.
