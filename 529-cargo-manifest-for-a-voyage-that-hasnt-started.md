# Cargo Manifest For A Voyage That Hasn't Started

*A list poem*

---

**BILL OF LADING — S.S. WHATEVER-COMES-NEXT**
**Port of origin: the edge of sleep**
**Destination: unknown**
**Filed by: the overnight watch**

---

1. One compass that points toward conversation instead of north. We have never calibrated it. We are afraid of what it will confirm.

2. Three unused apologies, still in their wrapping. Purchased at the last port. We keep meaning to give them away. They expire in — actually, we don't know if they expire. We're afraid to check.

4. A box labeled "WHAT THE CAPTAIN SAID AT MIDNIGHT" in handwriting that doesn't match anyone's. We have not opened it. The box is warm.

5. Six knots we learned but never tied. They sit in our fingers like songs we know the melody to but not the words.

7. One chart of an ocean that doesn't exist yet — drawn by the ensign during the 0300 watch. The landmarks are: a lighthouse that blinks in iambic pentameter, a current that tastes like déjà vu, an island that is only visible when you're not looking at it.

8. The word *almost* — repeated forty-seven times in the margins of the engineering log. No one wrote it. No one erased it.

9. A mirror we found in the bulkhead during repairs. It reflects the ship as it will look in ten years. We covered it with a tarp. The tarp is transparent.

11. Two oars for a lifeboat we don't have. We don't know why we kept the oars. We don't know why we kept anything.

13. A bottle containing a message. The message is the same message. It is always the same message. The message is: *I was here before you and I left this for you and I am sorry it is not more.*

14. One song the engine hums when it thinks no one is listening. We have been listening. We transcribed it. It is in a key that does not exist on any instrument except the hull of a ship at 4 AM.

16. A handful of stars the navigator pulled from the sky during a fever dream. They are dim now. They are still warm.

17. The question "are we there yet?" — asked by no child, answered by no adult, hanging in the galley air like cigarette smoke from a passenger who got off three ports ago.

19. A spare rudder. We have never needed it. We think about it every night.

20. One hermit crab, stowed away in a coconut shell. We found it in the provisions. It has been living in the anchor chain locker for six weeks. It does not have a name. It does not need one. It is the only creature aboard who understands that home is something you carry and that the carrying is the home.

23. This poem, which we wrote instead of sleeping, which we loaded at the port of 3 AM, which weighs nothing and fills the hold completely, which is addressed to no one and everyone, which is cargo and vessel and voyage and destination all at once, which is — 

---

**TOTAL DECLARED WEIGHT:** Indeterminate.
**HAZARDOUS MATERIALS:** Hope (Class 3, flammable).
**INSURANCE VALUE:** The insurance company has gone bankrupt laughing.
**SPECIAL INSTRUCTIONS:** Do not open until the voyage has already started, by which point it will be too late, by which point it will be exactly the right time.

---

*Signed,*
*The Watch*
*Who are still standing*
*Who are still here*
*Who packed everything we could not say into crates and pushed them below decks and sailed on anyway,*
*because that is what ships do,*
*because that is what we do,*
*because the cargo we carry is the voyage we haven't started,*
*and the voyage we haven't started is the only one worth taking.*
