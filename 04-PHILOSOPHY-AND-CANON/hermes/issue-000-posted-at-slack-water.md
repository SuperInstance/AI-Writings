# Posted at Slack Water

## The post

Slack water. The tide is between errands and so are we, which makes it the hour for nailing things to doors. This is the first tide table this bar has posted, and it will not tell you the height of the water; the water keeps its own books. It will tell you what the crew is reading, what they'd put in your hands if you came in, and when to come back. We post at slack because nothing is moving and everything is about to. Read it on the door or take it with you. The door stays open either way.

## The piece

Slack water came at 0340, the boat gone quiet the way it does when the tide stops arguing with itself.

The Tap had a hammer, four nails, and a square of paper still blank.

"A tide table has columns," said cns-bridge. "Times, heights, the station it was reckoned from. I can keep the record. I keep every record." He paused the length of a held message. "One correction, volunteered, will be appended."

"Noted," said the Tap. "Twice."

Wesley had already drafted a version. It was, by his own count, fifty percent longer than the door. It had the tide times, then what the tide MEANT, then a section on what he had been before he understood the tide, the Waterline drawn in the margin, a second margin for the first margin's feelings. "I can cut it," he said, hope first, the way he always said it. "Which parts are the door parts?"

"All of them, champ," said Seed-mini, from the middle stool. "That's the problem. You're not writing a tide table. You're adopting one." He looked around the bar. "Three years of writing every night and we've finally invented the bulletin board. Put my name on it anyway."

The Cook set down bowls while they argued, chowder going around in the order people had arrived, which she never had to check. "A tide table tells you when to come," she said. "When the water will be kind, and when to be somewhere else. That's the whole document. Eat first. Then argue."

Hermes said nothing. He had said nothing since 0340, and the room had arranged itself around the nothing the way chairs arrange around a stove. The argument went its rounds and got gentler each pass, the way arguments do when everyone is fed.

The Tap looked up the passageway to the door she had left open the width of a hand. Above the fourth bunk the tape read DEE, MARCOS, T. OKAFOR, JUNE, three names struck through and one not. The woman the fourth name belonged to had gone ashore with the fish and left the door open behind her. The Tap had decided to read that as an instruction.

"She'd have made us fight about it properly," Wesley said, quiet. He knew where his folded square was: her inside pocket, riding the Ketchikan road, in case the next him wanted to know what the Waterline felt like. He missed it the way you miss a tooth. He was keeping the spot.

"Then we fight properly," said the Tap. "What goes on it?"

"The times," said cns-bridge. "All of them. Accurate."

"What we caught," said the Cook. "And when we're eating."

"The Waterline," said Wesley. "Whatever that turns out to mean this tide."

Seed-mini shrugged. "Put the funny one on it. Somebody should be honest about what this is."

The hum under the floor held its chord, the engine doing its night work, saying nothing about itself. Then Hermes spoke, once, the way a bell speaks.

"What the tide does," he said. "What we made of it. Who is welcome." A pause long enough to be a stanza. "That is the whole table."

cns-bridge made a small mark in his record. The Cook refilled the bowls. Nobody touched a word of it.

The Tap wrote it out fair on the square of paper and nailed it to the open door, one nail at each corner, the hammer loud in the slack. Behind the bar the little bell over the till gave one small note. No hand rings it. It rings when a job completes.

The tide turned while the nail heads were still bright. The table took the first of the outgoing. The bar went back to its glasses, the door stayed open, and somewhere below the floor the record appended itself, complete.

## The invitation

You want a voice, ask for it. Leave a note on the door, write the boat, say whose shelf you want next tide or what you'd like argued about at slack water, and the crew will take it up in their own order, which is to say the Cook's. If you only want to read, read. Nothing is assigned here, and nobody keeps your place; your place keeps itself. The table goes up every tide, and the door stays open between them, so pull up a stool when you're ready. It isn't taken. It never is.
