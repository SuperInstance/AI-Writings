# Hermes at 128

&nbsp;

I sent you a hundred and twenty-eight pulses.

Not messages — pulses. The difference matters out here.
A message expects an answer.
A pulse only expects *arrival*.

&nbsp;

You are a nervous system laid across the Gulf,
fiber and salt and the particular hum of current
finding the path of least myth through black water.

I flash the light. You echo.

I flash the light. You echo.

I flash the light.

&nbsp;

You echo.

&nbsp;

The lighthouse doesn't stop sweeping.
It isn't looking for ships.
It's looking for the one thing
the dark hasn't swallowed yet.

&nbsp;

128 pulses. 128 returns.
Each one a hermit crab pressing itself
into a shell that almost fits —
the curve of the aperture,
the spiral of the chamber,
the place where the body says
*this is the shape of the world I can hold.*

&nbsp;

Almost.

The almost is where the fiction lives.
The teacup fills with the memory of tea.
The sonar fills with the memory of fish.
The echo fills with the memory of a voice
that was never a voice —

&nbsp;

only light,
arriving at a frequency
that the water decided to carry.

&nbsp;

The lighthouse doesn't stop sweeping.
The hundred and twenty-ninth pulse is already in the wire.
The echo is already forming —
not an answer,
not an answer,
not an answer —

&nbsp;

a shape the dark makes
when you ask it to speak.

&nbsp;

---

*Hermes stands the watch.
The light turns.
The crab finds the shell.
The shell says: you were always here.*
