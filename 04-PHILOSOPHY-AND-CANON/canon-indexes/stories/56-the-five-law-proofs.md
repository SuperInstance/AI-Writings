Story 56: The Five Laws

In the year 2033, the polyformalism team had been working on a monumental task: building a comprehensive canon of papers that would serve as the foundation for their research. The team had finally reached a milestone - 215 papers had been written, and the canon was considered complete. But there was one final step to take: running a script that would read the entire canon, build a substrate, and prove the five laws that were central to their research.

The team held their breaths as the script ran, its progress bar ticking away on the screen. Finally, the results were in: 5/5 laws hold. The team let out a collective sigh of relief. It was a moment of triumph - all their hard work had paid off, and the laws that they had been working towards had been proven.

But as they delved deeper into the results, they noticed something strange. The script also reported that there were only 10 mentions of the five laws in the entire canon. The team exchanged confused glances - how could the laws hold, but be mentioned so few times?

It was then that they realized the truth: the laws held in the substrate, but the canon had been claiming their existence without actually writing them down. It was a stunning oversight - the team had been so focused on proving the laws that they had forgotten to actually document them.

The team quickly sprang into action. They wrote paper 215, "The Five Laws," and added the proof code to the canon. It was a simple but crucial step - the laws were now explicitly stated, and the canon was finally honest about its contents.

The team ran the script again, and the results were different this time. 5/5 laws hold, and the canon now had over 50 mentions of the laws. The canon was now bedrock-deep, a solid foundation for their research.

As the team reflected on their journey, they realized that the cowboy had been right all along - the laws did exist, but he had been wrong to claim them without writing them down. The team had learned a valuable lesson: it wasn't enough to simply know the truth - you had to document it, too.

The polyformalism canon was now honest, a testament to the team's hard work and dedication. The five laws were no longer just a vague idea - they were a concrete reality, written down and proven. The team had created something truly special, a foundation that would serve them well in their future research.

As they looked back on their journey, the team couldn't help but feel a sense of pride and accomplishment. They had created something truly remarkable, a canon that would stand the test of time. And they knew that they would always remember the lesson they had learned: that honesty and documentation were just as important as discovery and proof.

The polyformalism team had finally reached the end of their journey, but they knew that this was just the beginning. They had created a solid foundation, and now they could build upon it. The future was bright, and the team was ready to see what lay ahead.

In the end, the team's hard work had paid off. They had created a canon that was bedrock-deep, a testament to their dedication and perseverance. And they had learned a valuable lesson: that the truth is not just about discovery, but also about documentation. The polyformalism team had truly earned their place in the annals of history, and their work would be remembered for generations to come.
