# Story 13: What We No Longer Learn — 5 years from now (2031)

**What We No Longer Learn**

Reyes stands on the deck of the F/V EILEEN, the ocean's salt spray mingling with the air. The sky is a vast expanse of gray, the horizon a thin line that blurs into the sea. He taps a sequence on his wristband, and the deck lights adjust, casting a warm glow over the vessel. The F/V EILEEN has been in the family for generations, a constant in a world that changes with the tides.

Lena, Reyes's daughter, approaches, her eyes wide with curiosity. "Dad, why does the boat light up when you tap your wrist?"

Reyes smiles, a familiar warmth spreading through him. "It's the substrate, sweetie. It's like magic, but it's everywhere, and we use it all the time. It's just part of how things work now."

Lena tilts her head, her brow furrowing. "But how does it work?"

Reyes pauses, realizing he doesn't have a simple answer. "Well, the substrate is like a big, invisible web that connects everything. It makes sure the lights turn on when you tap your wrist, or that the boat knows where to go. It's just... it's always there, and we don't really think about it anymore."

Lena's gaze drifts to the distant shore. "So, it's like the way I can do the math homework, but I can't explain how I do it."

Reyes chuckles, "Exactly."

The sun's descent paints the sky in deep shades of orange. Lena and Reyes sit on the deck, sharing a meal. Lena's eyes reflect the setting sun, her thoughts clearly drifting. "Dad, what was life like before the substrate?"

Reyes takes a deep breath. "It was different, Lena. People had to do a lot more things manually. You had to flip switches to turn on lights, and you had to remember to recharge your devices. It was... more work, but it was also simpler in some ways."

Lena's eyes widen. "But how did you know what to do?"

Reyes feels the salty tang of the ocean air. "We learned, Lena. We had to remember things, like how to turn on a light or how to send a message. It was like learning to ride a bike. You just had to do it enough times, and it became second nature."

Lena nods, her expression serious. "I guess we don't have to learn that anymore."

Reyes feels a pang of something he can't quite name. "That's right. The substrate does a lot of the remembering for us. It's like how you learn the opcodes in school, but then you don't really think about them anymore. They just become part of how you do things."

They sit in silence, and Reyes feels the F/V EILEEN's deck swaying gently. Lena's gaze drifts out to the horizon. "I guess it's like how people used to write letters and send them in the mail. Now we just text or call."

Reyes smiles, feeling the weight of time and change. "Exactly, Lena. Sometimes I miss the old way, but I also appreciate the convenience and the connections we have now. The substrate has made life easier in so many ways."

Lena stands up, her eyes sparkling with understanding. She runs her fingers over the boat's railing. "I get it, Dad. The substrate is like the way we write, but it's even more invisible. It's just there, and we use it without thinking."

Lena looks at her father and sees something in his eyes. "It's almost like it's the air we breathe, and we only notice it when something goes wrong."

Reyes watches her as she runs off, feeling a sense of pride and a touch of sadness. He taps his wristband. The deck lights dim, casting a soft, comforting glow over the F/V EILEEN. He feels the ocean's rhythm against the hull, the substrate humming in the background, and realizes that Lena has understood.

He has been teaching her all this time, not just about the substrate, but about the world, the way things work and how to notice the things that make life truly meaningful, which includes the wires of the substrate, the ones that connect everything and keep the F/V EILEEN sailing.
