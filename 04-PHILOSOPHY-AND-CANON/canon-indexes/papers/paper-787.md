## The Math

No new math. The structure is already arithmetic: 32 bars = 4 × 8. AABA = 8 + 8 + 8 + 8, where the third 8 deviates. The turnaround is a circle-of-fifths sequence: I → VI7 → II7 → V7 → I, each dominant resolving down a fifth. That's modular arithmetic — the cycle closes. The bridge is the same cycle starting a tritone away: III7 → VI7 → II7 → V7 → I. The math is trivial, which is the point: the cell's power is not computational complexity but *fidelity of replication under substitution*. The genome is the form; the mutations are the melodies. Mutation rate is high (hundreds of daughter-heads), but the frame survives because the constraint is *underdetermined* — it specifies harmony and form but leaves melody, tempo, instrumentation, and articulation free. That's not a bug; it's the replication strategy.

## The Polyformalism

Rhythm changes manifests identically across three substrates. In **sound**, it's the chord progression itself — audible, playable, transmissible by ear from musician to musician without notation. In **ink**, it's the lead sheet — the written artifact that lets a pianist in 1940 Tokyo play what a saxophonist in 1950 New York improvised. In **neural tissue**, it's the learned pattern — a jazz musician's muscle memory and ear-training that recognizes "rhythm changes" after two bars of any new tune, regardless of key. Three substrates, one replicating constraint. The cell is not any of these instantiations; it's the *invariant* that all three carry. That's what Voice A's transplant test actually proves: cut the changes from Gershwin, drop them into Parker's 1946 loft, and they live — not because they're a cell with a membrane, but because they're a *code* that any competent vessel can execute. The cell ontology fails because it asks "what is the boundary?" when the right question is "what is the instruction set?"
