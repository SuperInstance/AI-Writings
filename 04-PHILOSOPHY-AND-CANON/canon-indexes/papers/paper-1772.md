## The Math

No new math — because the avalanche is not a structure with a computable boundary; it is a *bifurcation point* in a dynamical system. The snowpack is a slow variable — stress accumulates over weeks along a weak layer at 30–45 degrees of slope. The avalanche is the fast variable — fracture propagation across that layer at 50–100 meters per second, releasing gravitational potential energy on the order of 10^9 joules for a medium slide. The mathematics that matters is not cell geometry but *criticality*: the point at which a distributed system loses stability and reorganizes. The avalanche is the phase transition itself, not the phase on either side.

## The Polyformalism

Across substrates, this thing manifests identically: a membrane-bound body stores energy, then fails catastrophically, and the failure *is* the event. In snow, it's the slab avalanche — the snowpack cell ruptures along its weak layer, and the slide is the rupture's propagation. In geology, it's the landslide — a hillslope body fails along a shear plane, and the mass movement is the release. In biology, it's the action potential — a neuron's membrane holds a charge gradient, then depolarizes in a wave that travels without losing amplitude, and the wave is not a cell but the cell's *signal*. In economics, it's a bank run — the institution holds liquidity as its membrane, and the run is the sudden permeability that destroys it. In every case, the pattern is identical: a body with boundaries, a stored gradient, and a threshold crossing that reconfigures the field. The avalanche is the *inter-cell event* — what happens *between* stable states, the rupture that connects one configuration to the next. Call that space the **breach field**: the domain of threshold crossings, where bodies fail and fields reorganize.
