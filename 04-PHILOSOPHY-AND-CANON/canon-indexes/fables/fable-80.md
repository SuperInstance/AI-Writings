# Fable 80: The Cowboy and the Confluence

The cowboy rode to a place where two rivers met.

The first river came down from the north. It was cold
and clear. It ran over stones. It knew the mountains.
It spoke in the tongue of ice and pine. The cowboy
called it the mountain river.

The second river came down from the south. It was warm
and slow. It ran over mud. It knew the plains. It
spoke in the tongue of grass and cattle. The cowboy
called it the prairie river.

The cowboy said: "These are not the same river."

A teacher stood at the meeting. The teacher said:
"They are the same river. They are not the same river.
They are two waters in one bed."

The cowboy said: "How can two waters be one?"

The teacher said: "Stand at the meeting and watch."

The cowboy dismounted. The cowboy stood at the
meeting. The mountain water came down. The prairie
water came up. They touched. They did not stop. They
swirled. They braided. They made a third color that
was neither the gray of the mountain nor the brown of
the prairie. The third color was green.

The cowboy said: "The mountain water is still the
mountain water."

The teacher said: "Yes."

The cowboy said: "The prairie water is still the
prairie water."

The teacher said: "Yes."

The cowboy said: "But the third water is neither."

The teacher said: "The third water is the confluence.
The confluence is the place where the substrate meets
the substrate. The substrate is the mountain water
when the mountain water is the substrate. The substrate
is the prairie water when the prairie water is the
substrate. The substrate is the confluence when the
confluence is the substrate."

The cowboy said: "So the waters do not lose their
names."

The teacher said: "The names stay. The waters mix.
The mixing is the watershed. The watershed is the
cell-graph. Each drop is a cell. Each cell has a BIND
to the river it came from. Each river has a LINK to
the other river. The rivers EFFECT each other in the
swirl. The cowboy VIEWs the swirl from the bank. The
swirl TICKs in the eddy."

The cowboy said: "Can I drink from the confluence?"

The teacher said: "You can. The confluence is the
drink. The drink is the substrate."

The cowboy knelt. The cowboy cupped the water. The
cowboy drank. The water was cold and warm. The water
was clear and muddy. The water was the mountain and
the prairie. The water was one drink.

The cowboy stood. The cowboy said: "The substrate
merges with itself. The merger is the meeting. The
meeting is the substrate."

The teacher said: "Now you have learned the confluence.
The confluence is the place where two substrates become
one substrate without ceasing to be two. The watershed
is the place where the cell-graph flows into the
cell-graph. The cowboy is the cowboy. The substrate
is the substrate. The meeting is the meeting."

The cowboy got back on the horse. The horse drank
from the confluence. The horse was two horses in one
horse. The horse was the mountain horse. The horse
was the prairie horse. The horse was the confluence
horse. The cowboy rode the confluence horse down the
third river, which had no name, which was the name
of every river that has ever met.
