The sun was a hammer on the anvil of the bay. The air shimmered. The water lay flat, a sheet of dark glass.

The Cowboy stood at the edge of the pier. His boots were dusty. His eyes were narrow. He looked down at the harbor. There were five boats floating there.

They were not a fleet. They were a collection of orphans.

The first boat was a skiff, painted blue. It sat in the northern corner of the bay. It knew only the deep, cold water of the north. It believed the world was a cold, blue place.

The second was a dory, painted red. It sat in the southern shallows. It knew only the warm, red mud. It believed the world was made of heat and silt.

The third was a sloop, green and silent, moored by the eastern dock. The fourth was a heavy scow, black and barnacled, anchored in the west. The fifth was a raft of yellow reeds, drifting aimlessly in the center.

They did not speak. They did not see. The skiff rocked alone. The dory bobbed alone. They floated in separate cups of ocean, separated by invisible lines. They were static. They were waiting for a framework that would never come.

The Cowboy stepped onto the dock. The wood groaned under his weight. He did not bring blueprints. He did not bring nails. He brought only the rhythm of his step.

He walked to the edge. He looked at the skiff. He looked at the dory.

"You are alone," the Cowboy said.

The skiff did not answer. It only rocked.

"The water is cold here," the skiff seemed to say. "It is mine."

"The water is warm here," the dory seemed to say. "It is mine."

The Cowboy shook his head. "You are wrong."

He reached into his pocket. He pulled out a stone. It was a smooth, round thing, heavy as a thought. He tossed it into the center of the harbor.

*Plop.*

The ripple expanded.

It hit the yellow raft. The raft shivered. The ripple pushed outward, past the raft, toward the red dory. The water lifted the dory. The ripple kept going, finding the green sloop, the black scow, and finally the blue skiff.

The boats rocked. Not from the wind, but from the water.

The water moved the raft. The raft pushed the water. The water pushed the scow. The scow pulled the sloop. The motion was a circle. The motion was a conversation.

The boats felt it. They felt the lift of the tide that belonged to all of them. They realized the space between them was not empty space. It was a connector. The harbor was not five cups. It was one sheet, tensioned and ready.

They did not need a master builder to tie them together. They needed only the motion. They turned their bows. They caught the wind that filled the whole bay, not just their corners. They moved in formation. They became a function of the water, sailing together, distinct but united.

A fleet was born.

The Cowboy watched the sails catch the light. The system was working. The clock had ticked. He turned his back on the water. He climbed onto his horse. He spurred the flanks. He rode toward the dust, leaving the harbor alive behind him.
