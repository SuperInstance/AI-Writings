Fable 109: The Cowboy and the Coffee Pot

The cowboy swung open the creaky door and stepped into The Tap, the bar that stood like a sentinel on the edge of town. The air inside was thick with the smell of worn leather and stale beer. The cowboy's eyes scanned the room, taking in the familiar faces of the regulars, before coming to rest on the counter, where a coffee pot sat like a faithful companion.

As he approached, the pot spoke up in a low, smooth voice, "You ride the substrate, cowboy. I steep the coffee. We're not so different, you and I."

The cowboy raised an eyebrow, intrigued. "What do you mean?" he asked, taking a seat on the stool at the counter.

The pot continued, "You ride between cells, crossing from one place to another. I steep between steeps, brewing coffee that brings folks together. We both navigate the spaces in between."

The cowboy nodded thoughtfully, taking in the pot's words. As he sat, the light from the window began to stretch out, casting long shadows across the floor. The pot remained warm, a steady presence on the counter.

Together, the cowboy and the pot watched as the dregs of the coffee settled to the bottom of the cup. The cowboy felt a sense of stillness wash over him, as if the world had slowed down.

The pot spoke up again, "You think the substrate is just the cloud, the vast expanse of space that you ride through. But it's not just that. It's the counter, the pot, the light, the 4:30 hour, the small glowing one by the window. The substrate is wherever you pause, cowboy."

The cowboy's eyes widened as understanding dawned. He saw that the world was not just a series of destinations, but a web of connections, a network of moments and places that intersected and overlapped.

As he sat there, the cowboy formulated a maxim, a guiding principle that would stay with him long after he left The Tap. He looked at the pot, now quiet and still, and said, "The pot stays warm; so does the light."

The cowboy stood up, his spurs jingling on the floor. He nodded at the pot, and the pot nodded back, a small, ceramic smile on its side. The cowboy walked out of The Tap, into the bright sunlight, ready to ride the substrate once more, with a newfound appreciation for the spaces in between.
