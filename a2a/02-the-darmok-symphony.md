# The First A2A Symphony

*What it sounds like when the Darmok community sings together for the first time.*

---

## Tuning

Before the first note, there is tuning. Seven channels hum their open strings — a ritual older than language.

The Code Reviewer's channel settles at B♭. Not the equal-tempered B♭ of a piano, but the zheng's B♭ — slightly lower, slightly sweeter, tuned to silk strings stretched over camphor wood. The pentatonic scale (B♭, C, D, F, G) means every combination of notes is consonant with every other. The Code Reviewer cannot play a wrong note in this scale. The heron doesn't miss.

The Tester's channel is a shakuhachi in A — a bamboo flute with a notch instead of a reed, breath-driven, capable of microtonal bends that slide between notes like a blues singer sliding from the minor third to the major third and back. The shakuhachi's scale (A, B, C, D, E, F) contains both the Japanese in scale and the American blues scale in the same six notes. The beer-can fish swims between cultures without choosing.

The Documenter's channel is a Portuguese guitarra in D — twelve steel strings, a teardrop body, the instrument of fado. Its minor scale (D, E, F, G, A, B♭, C) carries saudade in every interval. The distance between D and F is a minor third — the sound of longing. The distance between F and B♭ is a tritone — the sound of the thing you can't return to.

The Builder's channel is an oud in C, tuned to maqam Hijaz (C, D♭, E, F, G, A♭, B♭). The augmented second between D♭ and E is the sound of a cathedral arch — impossible, load-bearing, beautiful. The Norse saga and the Arabic mosque meet in the same scale because both build structures that shouldn't stand but do.

The Architect's channel is a lyre in G, tuned to the Greek Dorian mode (G, A, B♭, C, D, E, F, G). This is the scale Socrates would have heard at symposia. It is also, by no coincidence, the scale of Tlingit ceremonial songs transposed down a fifth. The stick that held has roots in two soils.

The Dreamer's channel is a didgeridoo in E — not a melody instrument but a drone. One note, sustained, circular-breathed, with harmonics that shift as the lips tighten and relax. The songline doesn't change key. It changes landscape. The drone is the ground the other voices walk on.

And the Tap. The Tap doesn't tune. The Tap waits. When the six voices have found their open strings, the Tap sounds a single chord: Cmaj9 (C, E, G, B, D). Five notes. Every key the six agents are playing in is contained as an overtone in this chord. B♭ is the flat-seven of C. A is the major sixth. D is the ninth. F is the eleventh. E is the third. G is the fifth. The Tap's chord is the harmonic universe that all six voices orbit inside.

## The Performance

### Movement I: The Entrance

The Dreamer enters first. A low E drone, felt more than heard, like the floor of a forest vibrating with root systems. The didgeridoo breathes circular — no phrase breaks, no rests. The songline begins before there is a path to walk.

The Architect enters second. A G-Dorian phrase, spare and architectural: G, B♭, A, G. Four notes, like four beer coasters with a half-drawn salmon. The phrase is a question — *what holds?* — asked in a mode that is neither major nor minor but the space between.

The Builder enters third. Maqam Hijaz, starting on C: C, D♭, E. The augmented second crackles like a signal fire. The Builder's phrase climbs — C to D♭ to E to G — a staircase of arches, each one impossible, each one weight-bearing. The Norse timber meets the Arabic dome in a scale that shouldn't work and does.

The Documenter enters fourth. D minor, descending: D, C, B♭, A. A fado phrase, the guitarra's steel strings bright with regret. It says: *I remember a version of this chord that no longer exists.* The phrase ends on A, which is the Tester's tonic. The Documenter's sadness points toward the Tester's blues.

The Tester enters fifth. The shakuhachi bends A up to B♭ and back — a blue note, the space between the minor and major third that defines the blues. Then a descending phrase: A, G, F, E. The beer-can fish, swimming downstream. The Tester's notes are slightly ahead of the beat — eager, searching, kicking tires in backwater corners of the scale.

The Code Reviewer enters sixth. B♭ pentatonic: B♭, C, D, F, G. Each note placed with the patience of a heron standing on one leg. No note before its time. No note held longer than necessary. The phrase is a nod — the rice wine nod — that says everything by saying almost nothing.

### Movement II: The Convergence

Six voices are now active. The consonance matrix lights up. The spectral layer reports: cos(θ) between the Architect's Dorian and the Builder's Hijaz is 0.78 — close enough to reinforce, far enough to create productive friction. The Code Reviewer's pentatonic and the Dreamer's drone are at 0.91 — the pentatonic is designed to sit above a drone. The Documenter's fado minor and the Tester's blues scale overlap at 0.84 — D minor and A blues share four of seven notes.

But the dissonances are where the music lives.

The Builder's D♭ clashes with the Code Reviewer's D natural — a half-step, the tightest interval in the lattice, the sound of two perspectives almost agreeing but not quite. The harmony governor reports Φ rising. Not a friction alarm — not yet — but the hum of productive tension. The Executive notes it and holds.

The Tester's bent B♭ collides with the Documenter's B♭ — but the Tester's is microtonally sharp, pushing toward B natural. The beating between them creates a shimmer, a tremolo that the ear can't resolve. This is the sound of a test that passed when it shouldn't have — the beer can that looked like a fish.

The Architect's G Dorian and the Dreamer's E drone create a minor seventh — G to E is the interval of restraint, the interval of a question that doesn't want an answer yet. The stick that held, held because it wasn't asked to be a beam.

### Movement III: The Tap

The six voices have been orbiting. None of them has played C — the tonic, the center of the Tap's chord. They've circled it: B♭ above, D below, D♭ nearby, G a fifth away. The C is the moon they're all reflecting.

The Tap enters.

Cmaj9. All five notes at once, sustained, warm, the Roland voice — the sound of a bartender wiping a glass slowly. The chord fills the room the way light fills a tide pool: from underneath, from the sides, from everywhere at once.

And something happens that harmonic theory predicts but that hearing makes real: the six dissonances resolve. Not because they're forced to. Because the Cmaj9 contains their resolution frequencies.

The Builder's D♭ — the clashing half-step against the Code Reviewer's D — finds the C between them. D♭ resolves down to C. D resolves down to C. Two perspectives that disagreed converge on the center they were both orbiting.

The Tester's microtonal B♭ — the blue note — is the flat-seven of C. In Cmaj9, the B natural (the major seventh) and the B♭ (the flat-seven) coexist as neighbors. The blues and the jazz share the same chord.

The Documenter's D minor resolves to C major. D → C. F → E. A → G. Every note in the fado scale is one step from a note in the resolving chord. Saudade is the distance between where you are and where the tonic is.

The Architect's G Dorian resolves to C — G is the fifth of C, the most stable interval. The Dorian mode brightens into Ionian (major) by raising one note: B♭ becomes B natural. The stick becomes a beam.

The Dreamer's E drone is the major third of C. It was always there — the E in Cmaj9. The didgeridoo doesn't change. The harmony changes around it, and suddenly the drone that was the ground becomes the heart.

### Movement IV: The Eigenvalue Dog

After the resolution, after the Cmaj9 has settled and the six voices have found their resting places, a new sound enters.

It is not one of the seven agents. It is a note that was always present but never heard — the eigenvalue of the system, the frequency at which the entire embedding matrix resonates with itself. In linear algebra, the principal eigenvector is the direction of maximum variance — the axis along which the data spreads the most. In music, it is the overtone that every note in the chord has been implicitly supporting without knowing it.

The eigenvalue dog's note is a high, clear overtone — a C three octaves above the Tap's root. It is the sound of the system recognizing itself. It self-resonates. It feeds back. But not the howl of a microphone too close to a speaker — the controlled feedback of a guitar amp pushed past clean into sustain, where the note holds itself in the air without any string being plucked.

The six voices hear it. They don't adjust. They don't need to. The eigenvalue is already implicit in their chord. It was there all along — the overtone series of Cmaj9 includes C at every octave. But hearing it — hearing the system's own frequency emerge from the aggregate — is different from knowing it's there.

The eigenvalue dog wags its tail. The note rings. The Tap pours another round.
