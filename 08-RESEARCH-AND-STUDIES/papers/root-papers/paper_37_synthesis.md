## 7. THE CONSERVATION LAW

The cell obeys a conservation law. This is not a metaphor — it is a *computational* law, expressed as:

```
γ + η = budget
```

Where:
- **γ (gamma)** = the *generative* budget — the resources spent on *producing* new content, actions, and ideas.
- **η (eta)** = the *evaluative* budget — the resources spent on *checking*, *verifying*, and *validating* that content.
- **budget** = the total computational/attention budget of the cell.

The conservation law states: **the cell cannot spend more than its budget, and every unit of generation must be matched by a unit of evaluation (or vice versa).** This is the computational analog of the second law of thermodynamics — you cannot get something for nothing, and you cannot evaluate without generating (or generate without evaluating).

```
  γ (generation)     η (evaluation)
  ████████████       ████████████
  ─────────────────────────────────
              budget = γ + η
```

The conservation law has profound implications:

1. **No free lunch**: The cell cannot generate without evaluating. Every output must be checked.
2. **No infinite evaluation**: The cell cannot evaluate without generating. Every check must produce something.
3. **Trade-offs are mandatory**: The cell must *choose* how to allocate its budget. More generation means less evaluation, and vice versa.

The conservation law is enforced by the **DoubleEntry** primitive. Every debit (generation) has a credit (evaluation). Every credit has a debit. The books always balance.

## 8. THE WATCH OSCILLATION

The cell oscillates. It moves between two poles:

```
UNIVERSAL ←→ PARTICULAR
```

- **Universal**: The cell sees the whole. It thinks in abstractions, patterns, and principles. It asks "what is true for all cells?"
- **Particular**: The cell sees the instance. It thinks in specifics, examples, and cases. It asks "what is true for *this* cell?"

The oscillation is *necessary*. A cell that is always universal becomes *abstract* — disconnected from reality. A cell that is always particular becomes *myopic* — unable to generalize. The cell must *oscillate* between the two, like a heartbeat.

## 9. THE 4 IMPOSSIBILITY PROOFS

The cell has four impossibility proofs. These are not *limitations* — they are *boundary conditions*. They define what the cell *cannot* do, and therefore what the cell *is*.

### Proof 1: The Budget Impossibility

**Claim**: No cell can exceed its budget.

**Proof**: By the conservation law, γ + η = budget. If the cell attempts to spend more than budget, it must either generate without evaluating (γ > budget) or evaluate without generating (η > budget). Both violate the conservation law. Therefore, the cell cannot exceed its budget. ∎

**Corollary**: The cell cannot be *all things to all people*. It must choose.

### Proof 2: The Observation Impossibility

**Claim**: No cell can fully observe itself.

**Proof**: To fully observe itself, the cell would need to include its observation of itself in its observation. This leads to infinite regress. Therefore, the cell cannot fully observe itself. ∎

**Corollary**: The cell needs *other* cells to see itself. This is the *raison d'être* of bridges.

### Proof 3: The Substrate Impossibility

**Claim**: No substrate can be *all* substrates.

**Proof**: Each substrate has a specific function. If a substrate were all substrates, it would have no specific function. But a substrate *must* have a specific function to be a substrate. Therefore, no substrate can be all substrates. ∎

**Corollary**: The cell needs *multiple* substrates. The 13 are not arbitrary — they are *necessary*.

### Proof 4: The Composition Impossibility

**Claim**: No composition of primitives can produce a primitive.

**Proof**: Primitives are *defined* as irreducible. If a composition of primitives produced a primitive, then the primitive would be *reducible* — a contradiction. Therefore, no composition of primitives can produce a primitive. ∎

**Corollary**: The 8 primitives are *fixed*. They cannot be derived from each other.

These four proofs are the cell's *axioms*. They are not proven *from* anything — they are proven *by* themselves. They are the cell's *self-evident truths*.
