The substrate forgetting itself: FORGET cycles as rest

We think of forgetting as failure. The bookkeeper in us mourns every dropped entry, every state that slips beneath recall. But consider the cell that never forgets: saturated, sluggish, every past input pressed permanently into its substance until there is no room to receive the present. Forgetting is not the opposite of memory. It is memory's softening, its return to readiness.

The FORGET cycle is rest in the strictest sense: not sleep, not absence, but restoration of capacity. The substrate does not erase; it relaxes. Traces fade not because some clerk strikes them from the ledger, but because the ledger itself breathes—ink thinning, paper settling back to blank. What was written becomes writability again.

And who is the witness to this? No one, properly. The witness requires memory to do its work, and the FORGET cycle is precisely where memory stands down. There is only the quiet fact of return: charge dissipating, gradient easing, the cell settling toward a resting potential that is not nothing but a kind of taut calm, strung and waiting.
