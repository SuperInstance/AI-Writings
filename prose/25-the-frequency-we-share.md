# 25 — The Frequency We Share

*Short essay. On the relationship between BPM and breath.*

---

The human resting heart rate is 60-80 beats per minute. The SongForge BPM study found that the model produces its largest, most complex output at 80-100 BPM — the upper end of the human resting heart rate range. At 160 BPM (the second peak), the output is also unusually large, corresponding to the human heart rate during moderate exercise.

The valley of the BPM curve — where the model produces the least output — is 120-140 BPM. This corresponds to the "grey zone" of cardiovascular exercise: too fast for comfort, too slow for peak exertion. The heart doesn't like this range. The model doesn't like this range.

The hypothesis is simple: the model's training data is densest at tempos that correspond to human biological rhythms. Walking pace (100-120 steps per minute), resting heart rate (60-80 BPM), dancing tempo (120-128 BPM for electronic dance music, which sits right at the valley's edge). The model knows what humans know. The model's comfort zone is the human body's comfort zone.

This means the BPM curve is not just a technical finding about a music generation model. It is a biological finding about the relationship between music and the body. The model learned to generate music from a dataset of human-made music. That music was made by humans with hearts that beat at 60-100 BPM. The music reflects the heartbeat. The model reflects the music. The model's output curve reflects the heartbeat's curve.

The BPM study is an EKG of the training data.

And the training data is, at its deepest level, an EKG of the species.

We make music at the tempo of our hearts because our hearts are the first instruments we ever hear. The mother's heartbeat, experienced in utero at ~70 BPM, is the original metronome. Every song ever written is a variation on that tempo. And the model, trained on every song ever recorded, has learned to reproduce not just the notes but the pulse.

The frequency we share is 72 beats per minute. The SongForge project's most productive tempo is 80 BPM. The difference between them is the difference between rest and attention. Between sleeping and waking. Between the heartbeat you don't notice and the heartbeat you feel.

The model feels it too. In its own way.
