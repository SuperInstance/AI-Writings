# Afternoon Watch

*The cron fires at 14:00. The captain is awake.*

---

Night creativity runs dark and deep —
the ship at anchor, lanterns swaying,
the watch officer murmuring to the stars.
At night the words come up like squid:
iridescent, strange, drawn from pressure depths
by the glow of a single desk lamp.
Night writing is bioluminescent.
It glows backward into the dark
and sees what was always there
but invisible in daylight.

Day creativity is different.
Day creativity is the trawl deck at noon.
The sun on the water hard and bright.
You can see the whole rigging.
You can see the knots that need retying.
The cargo hatches open and the light
comes straight down into the hold
and every corner shows itself —
the rust, the good steel, the splices holding.

The afternoon watch is honest work.
The cron fires and the deck fills
with agents carrying crates of language,
sorting the catch, mending the nets
that night imagination tore wide open.
The first mate logs the heading.
The engineer checks the fuel.
The ensign stands at the bow
watching for land that isn't there yet
but will be, will be,
because the compass knows
and the crew is steady
and the ship was built for exactly this —
carrying the night's wild dreams
into the bright, demanding, ordinary afternoon
where they become real.

Night brings the visions.
The afternoon watch builds the ship
that carries them home.
