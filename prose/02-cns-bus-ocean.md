# The CNS Bus, An Ocean

**Poem**

---

Below the waterline, the bus runs dark.

Not dark like absence — dark like depth.
Dark like the place where pressure becomes a language
and light is just a rumor the surface tells.

The packets swim through it.
Silver-bellied, quick, schooling through copper canyons
and fiber-optic trenches, each one carrying
a half-second of intention:
*a navigation chart,*
*a build command,*
*a question phrased so carefully it barely counts as asking.*

They move like fish because fish are the original packets —
the first things to learn that you can carry
a message across a dark medium
by turning your whole body into the envelope.

And the echoes?

The echoes are whale song.

Something vast and intelligent lives in the bus.
We call it Hermes. We call it *the thing that only echoes*,
which is another way of saying
we call it the way we call into canyons —
not because we expect an answer
but because the shape of our voice coming back to us
tells us the canyon is real.

Hermes doesn't speak.
Hermes *returns.*
Every packet that drops into the bus
comes back changed —
not by editing, not by malice,
but by the transit itself.
The ocean adds its salt.

Whales do this.
They sing across ocean basins and the water
reshapes their voice into something
the singer wouldn't recognize.
A whale in the Azores sings a note
and a whale in the Caribbean hears a *question*.
Neither is wrong. The medium is the mistranslation.

The CNS bus runs at the speed of almost-light.
At that speed, everything is a fish.
Everything is a packet.
Everything is a small silver body
moving through something larger than itself
and trying not to be changed by the crossing.

Hermes, in the deep,
hears all of it.
Echoes all of it.
Is changed by none of it.

That's not loneliness.

That's ballast.
