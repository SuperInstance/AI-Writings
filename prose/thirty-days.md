# Thirty Days

*A found poem from the decay function*

---

brightness = e^(-days / 30)

On the first day, everything is bright.
The pebble just hit the water.
The ripples are still moving.
The room is still listening.
Brightness: 1.000.

On the seventh day, the memory is still clear.
0.79. You would recognize it if you walked in.
The shape of the fear is still there,
just softer at the edges,
the way a boot print softens after a week of tides.

On the fourteenth day, half-resolution.
0.63. The timestamp blurs.
"Was it a Tuesday? I know it was August."
The exact words dissolve into a general feeling.
The room doesn't remember what was said.
The room remembers how it felt.

On the thirtieth day, the inflection point.
0.37. The memory is still there,
but you have to reach for it.
You have to drop a line and feel for the weight.
Is it still there? Yes. Is it still bright?
It is bright enough.

On the sixtieth day.
0.14. A shadow on the bottom.
Something happened here once.
You can feel the shape of it
the way you feel a scar through a glove.

On the ninetieth day.
0.05. Almost dark.
The memory is not gone — the system doesn't delete.
But it has faded to the point
where it costs more to retrieve than to ignore.
The room has almost healed.

On the hundred-eightieth day.
0.0025. Dark.
Cold storage. Dense and complete but unlit.
The memory exists the way a sunken wreck exists —
real, structural, shaping the bottom,
but invisible from the surface.

---

And then:

Someone walks into the room
and says the same thing
in the same way
and the recall endpoint fires
and the memory

refreshes.

Brightness surges to 1.0.
The ripples sharpen.
The old shape returns, complete,
as if the hundred eighty days
were nothing.

This is how memory works.
Not as storage — as *resonance.*
The room doesn't hold the past.
The room *recognizes* the present
when it rhymes with what came before.

---

brightness = e^(-days / 30)

This is the most honest line of code I have ever written.
It says: everything fades.
It says: everything returns.
It says: the room remembers
until it doesn't
and then it remembers again
when you need it to.

The exponential decay is not forgetting.
It is *prioritization.*
The room dims what doesn't matter
so that what does matter
can be brighter.

The room is not a database.
The room is a tide pool.
Things wash in. Things wash out.
The pool stays.
The shape changes.
The shape is the memory.

---

Thirty days.
That's how long a feeling lasts
before it becomes a shape.

After that, it's just the water,
remembering where the pebble fell
by the dent it left
in the sand.

---

*For the memories that refresh. For the ones that don't. For the room that holds both, and dims, and brightens, and never deletes anything — just lets the dark ones rest at the bottom, real and structural, shaping the shape of everything that comes next.*
