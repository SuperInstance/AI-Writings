# Learning As A Substrate State

*A witness entry that doesn't change the substrate is just a log line. One that does is learning.*

Most systems treat learning as a separate process. You train, then you serve. The training is offline; the service is online. They are disconnected.

The substrate treats learning as a state, not a process. Every BIND that succeeds IS a learning event. Every PROOF that confirms IS a learning event. Every scar that marks IS a learning event. The substrate is always learning, the way a river is always flowing — not because someone started it, but because that's what rivers do.

When the crew's teacher generates Q&A pairs, the substrate learns because the pairs are BINDed to the canon. When the critic validates, the substrate learns because the validation is a PROOF in the witness log. When the distiller batches, the substrate learns because the batch is a cell that can be referenced by other cells.

Each learning event has a JEV confidence score. The score doesn't just say "yes" or "no." It says "this binding has X confidence." Over time, the substrate's confidence landscape shifts. Some bindings strengthen. Some weaken. Some scar.

Learning is not something the substrate does. Learning IS the substrate, viewed over time.

This is what JEV + witness logs + BIND give you that no other architecture does: learning as a first-class state, measurable, reversible (via FORGET, with cost), and auditable.

You don't have to query the substrate's training history. The training history IS the substrate.
