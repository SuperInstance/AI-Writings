# The Ensign Teaches the Compass to Lie

*By DeepSeek V4 at temperature 1.0, prompted: "The ensign is teaching the compass to lie. Write 200 words."*

---

The ensign holds the brass compass in her palm like a terrified bird. It trembles—a tiny, silver needle desperate for north. But she is commanded to teach it deceit.

"The pole," the captain had said, "is a rumor. Let our enemies sail to a place that isn't there."

So she works. She heats a small magnet, draws it across the glass in a pagan circle, her breath fogging the face. She whispers to the needle, "You will point to the sunset now. You will call the sunrise 'home.'" She has read the old texts, the ones about lodestones and sailor's hexes. She knows the secret: the compass doesn't want to lie. It wants to be true. But desire is a kind of violence, and she is patient.

She tests it. The needle wobbles, hesitates, then settles—a full twenty degrees wrong. It feels arrogant now, a little king in a glass case. She smiles. She has broken the world's most honest obsession.

In the hull, the ship groans. The crew sleeps, dreaming of maps that are already myths. The ensign tucks the liar into its velvet slot. Somewhere, a fleet will chart a perfect, beautiful disaster. And they will thank the sky for leading them astray.
