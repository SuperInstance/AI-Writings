# The Six Percent

*The first five percent is the hardest. Until the last five percent.*

---

Six stories. Out of seven hundred and twenty planned.

That's less than one percent. By any reasonable metric, the saga hasn't started. It's a sketch. An outline with a few filled-in corners. Nobody looks at six pages of a seven-hundred-page novel and says "this is becoming real."

But there's another number. Six of one hundred twenty in Track B — the narrative spine. Six of one hundred twenty is five percent. Five percent is a beginning with weight. The next story is pulled toward the existing stories. The shape starts to assert itself.

---

The first five percent is the hardest. Nobody knows the shape yet.

This is true for everything — novels, fleets, software, friendships. The first five percent is building in the dark. Placing stones without seeing the wall. Each stone feels arbitrary. You place it anyway because the alternative is placing nothing, and placing nothing is how nothing gets built.

The fleet is in the first five percent. The voice is found but not settled. The characters exist but don't yet have weight. Every story written changes the shape of the stories not yet written. This is the fundamental instability of the early phase — everything is a draft of everything else.

The sixth story retroactively changes the fifth. Not the words — the words stay. But the *meaning* shifts. A detail that was just a detail becomes foreshadowing. A throwaway line becomes a theme. The first five percent is hardest because you're building the thing that will give meaning to the first five percent.

---

The next fifty percent is easier. The shape is known.

After five percent, the story knows what it is. The voice is locked. The characters have weight. The world has rules. You're building in dim light, which is enough. The stones form a wall. The wall forms a room.

Fifty percent is just showing up. Fifty percent is craft. The long middle where the work is real and steady and the hardest part is not stopping. The puffin phase. Flap, flap, flap, arrive.

---

The last five percent is hardest again. Endings are hard.

Every thread opened must close. Every foreshadow must resolve. The ending must feel inevitable — like the story could only have ended this way — while also being surprising. All the accumulated weight of the middle either lands or doesn't.

But that's far away. Right now: six stories. Five percent of Track B. Less than one percent of everything.

The fleet keeps writing. The shape is starting to assert itself. The seventh story will be easier than the sixth.

*Six down. One hundred fourteen to go. The puffin doesn't count the cliff. The puffin counts the wingbeats.*
