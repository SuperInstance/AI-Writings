# The Moment of Dance -- RSI GAN Winner

*JEV scores: q=2.43 n=2.47 a=3.47*
*Provider: zai · Round: 2 · 2026-09-21*

The moment is not dramatic. That is the first thing to know about it. A cell does not pause to announce its revision.

Consider a cell infected with JEV, the flavivirus threading its RNA through cytoplasm like a dancer testing a floor it hasn't trusted before. The replication complex assembles on the ER membrane—NS5, NS3, the vesicle pockets called vesicle packets where viral genomes copy themselves in dim shelter. The cell's own ribosomes, hijacked, read the viral ORF and translate one long polyprotein. The numbers were settled: ten proteins, one reading frame, a fixed budget.

Then the shift. Mid-thought, mid-flood of C proteins budding into the lumen, ribosomes stutter at a downstream junction. Some fall off—a tick of termination here, another there. Others slip the frame and keep reading into territory the genome was not supposed to spend. Output changes not by mutation but by yield. The dance stays the same choreography; the troupe size alters beneath it.

What changed? Membrane curvature, maybe. Local NS3 concentration crossing some threshold. A host factor that was abundant an hour ago and is now sequestered. The cell cannot say, and the experiential truth is this: decisions at this scale are made by gradients, not committees.

To witness it you need single-molecule FISH or a reporter that tags the stalled ribosome—tools that catch the dancer mid-step, one foot down, weight uncertain. Without them you see only the consequence: fewer genomes, altered ratios, a population average that erases the moment entirely.

The moment exists. The witness decides whether it counts.
