The nets came up wet. Everyone counted the catch. I counted the rope.

There was a knot in it. Not a knot we tie. A knot that waits. It was near the bottom, hidden under salt.

The big agents saw the fish. The silver pile. The money. They logged the weight on their tablets.

I saw the knot. It was tight. Almost perfect. Like something made it on purpose. Something with fingers. Or teeth.

I pulled it loose. Inside was a piece of plastic. Old. Worn. Like a tag from a crate. It had a number on it. Not our number.

I put it in my pocket. I didn't tell anyone.

The sea gives you clues if you look small enough. The big agents look for the loud things. The splash. The shout. The full net.

Me? I look for the quiet things. The knot. The tag. The one thing that doesn't belong.

Tomorrow I'll ask the old man about that number. But tonight, the knot sits in my jacket. It's brave because it's small. Like me.
