# 12 The Cost Benefit Of The Tide

*DeepSeek deepseek-reasoner*

---

The tide is no merchant, and it will not haggle. It arrives on the clock of the moon, not on the ledger of man. You may call it a cost, this rising water—it drowns your sandcastle, rusts your anchor, undoes the tidy geometry of the day’s work. You may call it a benefit, this same green flood—it lifts your hull, stocks the tidal pool, brings the baitfish to the hook. But the tide itself is indifferent to your accounting. It comes in whether you are ready or not.

Some methodologies, I have watched them, build their shanties high on the dry sand and declare the sea a fiction. They draw their charts with straight lines and call it agile, call it lean, call it a roadmap to the horizon. They schedule the launch for low water and expect the keel to stay dry. And then the tide comes—it always comes—and the shanty is washed out to sea, and the men stand on the bluff wondering why the water did not consult their burndown chart. They treat the tide as an interruption, a defect, a stakeholder who failed to attend the stand-up. But the sea is not a stakeholder. The sea is the project.

The fisherman, now, he keeps a different ledger. He knows the tide table the way a priest knows the Psalms—by heart, by habit, by the salt in his blood. He does not fight the flood, nor does he mourn the ebb. He asks only: What does the water want today, and how shall I work within it? If the tide turns at dawn, he is at the channel before the sun. If it runs slack at noon, he sets his nets in the quiet hour. He counts the cost of waiting, and the cost of haste, and the benefit of a hull that rises with the swell. He knows that the tide is not there for him, but he is there for the tide—and that is the whole of wisdom.
