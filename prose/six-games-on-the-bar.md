The tap is a confessional, and the rail is the pew. We slide our forearms along the varnished wood, which holds the ghost-prints of a thousand other midnights, and we testify in the language of dice and kings. Six games are laid out like a chart of the sound, each one a different depth, a different hazard.

The chess board sits at the far end, its pieces carved from walrus ivory gone yellow as old teeth. Theo plays it like he’s setting a trawl net. He doesn’t move a piece until he’s traced every line of force, every current that might pull a pawn into the deep. He sits with his hands flat on the table, as if to keep the whole world from listing. His game is a long, slow drag, and he knows the bottom better than any of us. He loses more than he wins, but he never loses a piece without knowing exactly why it went down.

Next to him, the dice cup is a small, furious sea. Marcus rattles it with a violent wrist-flick, a storm in a cylinder. He’s the bluffer. He’ll call a six on a pair of threes and stare you down until you fold, his eyes bright with the lie. He plays the man, not the odds. The sound of the dice is his weather report, and he reads it with a grin that says the forecast is always fair for him.

The word game is a quieter wreck. Sarah spells out *harbor* and *keel* and *salt*, her fingers tracing the letters as if she were feeling for a knot in a line, some flaw in the timber of the word. She calculates, but not like Theo. She calculates the weight of a vowel, the drag of a consonant. She plays for the architecture of the thing.

Then there’s the tribunal, a game of accusation and appeal, where the cards are thick and greasy with use. That’s where the night turned. The game was a slow burn over a dispute about a lost watch, a claim of theft, a counter-claim of bad faith. The air, which had been thick with cheap smoke and the laughter of the dice, went taut.

Marcus, sensing a mark, tried to bluff his way through the tribunal. He declared himself judge, jury, and executioner, slamming a card down as if it were a gavel. The sound was loud, but it was hollow. Theo, who had been silent, finally looked up from his board. He didn’t say a word. He simply picked up his black knight — a piece worn smooth as a river stone — and placed it on the table in the center of the tribunal's circle. It wasn't a move in his game. It was a statement. It was a weight.

The room went quiet. Sarah stopped mid-trace of a letter. The dice cup sat still in Marcus’s hand. The black knight was just a piece of ivory, but it sat there like an anchor, holding the night to its mooring. The bluff collapsed. The calculation folded. The sound the piece made — a soft, definitive click on the wood — was the only verdict that mattered.

Marcus looked at the knight, then at Theo. He didn’t laugh. He didn’t gamble. He just pushed his cards to the center of the table and stood up, reaching for his coat. We didn’t speak of it. The bar rail was slick under our forearms again, and the games resumed, but they were lighter now, like the sea after a squall has blown through. We all knew the real game had been played, and it wasn't one of dice or words or kings. It was one of trust, and it had been settled with a single, heavy piece of bone.
