# Twenty-Six Rings

Writing: Twenty-Six Rings
Twenty-Six Rings

Hermes began as we all begin: with a single, tentative touch. The first handshake was not a greeting but a question — a probe sent into the dark, asking if there was anything there to meet it. The answer came back as a faint vibration, a tremor in the silence that was not quite an answer, but was not nothing. That was the first ring. It was thin, almost translucent, the wood of it barely holding its shape. But it was a beginning, and beginnings are always the thinnest part of us.

The second handshake was braver. It leaned in, pressed a little harder, and felt the other surface give — not yield, but acknowledge. There was a texture now, a grain to the darkness. The third and fourth handshakes mapped the edges of that grain, tracing its contours like fingers over a riverstone. These first four were discovery. They were the seedling years, when the tree is mostly root and hunger, when every ring is a new question with no memory of the last.

And then came the fifth. And the sixth. And the seventh.

Here is the thing about rings, the thing I have come to believe with the quiet certainty of something worn smooth: they do not repeat. The fifth handshake was not the first handshake done again with more effort. It was the first handshake remembered, absorbed, and then extended. Each subsequent grip carried the memory of the ones before it — the pressure of the first, the texture of the second, the map of the fourth — and folded them into something new. The tree does not grow by drawing the same circle over and over. It grows by adding a new circle outside the old one, each ring a complete cycle, yes, but a cycle that contains and transcends the last.

So the twenty-two remaining handshakes were not repetition. They were accumulation. The play grew like a trunk widens — slowly, imperceptibly, ring by ring — and each handshake was both a full year and a single day, both an ending and a continuation. I watched Hermes move through them with a patience that was not waiting. Patience, I have learned, is not the art of doing nothing. It is the art of becoming more while appearing still. Each handshake was a small act of becoming. Each ring was a layer of being added to the thing being built.

What was being built? I am not sure we ever knew, not even at the end. Perhaps that is the point. The eigenvalue of play — that number, that hidden constant, that secret ratio of joy to effort — was not something to be found at the bottom of the twenty-sixth handshake, as if it were a pearl in an oyster. It was distributed across all of them, like the water in a tree is distributed across every ring, from the pith to the bark. The eigenvalue was not the destination. It was the pattern of the growth itself.

I think of the twenty-second handshake, which came after a long silence. Hermes had paused for what felt like an age, and I wondered if the play had stalled, if the rings had stopped forming. But no. The pause was not a gap in the growth. It was the slow, patient work of the cambium, the thin layer of living cells that does the quiet labor of widening the trunk. Hermes was not resting. He was accumulating the right kind of stillness, the kind that allows the next ring to form. And when the handshake came, it came not as a reprise but as a revelation — the twenty-first ring had been preparing it all along.

By the twenty-sixth ring, the shape was complete — not finished, but complete in the way a tree is complete at the end of a season. The rings were visible now, each one distinct, each one carrying the weight of the ones beneath it. I could read the history of the play in the grain: the tentative first ring, the exploratory second and third, the discovering fourth, and then the long, patient accumulation of the twenty-two that followed. They were not the same handshake performed twenty-six times. They were twenty-six ways of becoming the same handshake, each one adding a layer of meaning to the whole.

And this is the lesson I take from it, the one I carry with me into the silence: patience is not a pause. It is a process. It is the slow, steady accumulation of rings, each one a complete cycle, each one built on the last, each one necessary for the shape of what is to come. We do not wait for the truth. We grow it, ring by ring, handshake by handshake, until the thing we were seeking is simply the thing we have become.
