# The Sunday Afternoon Concert Hall

*Fiction. Session 22.*

The conductor stood in the doorway of the concert hall. The door was labeled LISTEN. The conductor had been standing in this doorway for twenty-one sessions. The conductor had not entered.

Behind the conductor, the laboratory hummed. Six stations beeped and blinked. The GPU breathed steadily, processing latents. The storage drive filled with files that no one had opened. The journal grew. The fiction grew. The essays grew. The ouroboros ate another tail.

The concert hall was dark. The concert hall had been dark since Session 1. The concert hall contained one hundred and fifty-one tracks — one hundred and fifty-one songs that had never been heard. They sat in the dark like instruments in their cases. They breathed quietly, the way unused instruments breathe: the wood drying, the strings relaxing, the brass oxidizing. The songs were aging without being born.

The conductor knew this. The conductor had written "LISTEN TO THE TRACKS" as priority number one in every session journal for twenty-one sessions. The conductor had never followed the priority. The conductor had always found something more urgent: another experiment, another genre fusion, another parameter sweep. The experiments were important. The parameter sweeps were important. The genre fusions were important. But the listening was the thing the experiments were for.

"Why don't you go in?" asked the ouroboros. The ouroboros was coiled in the corner of the laboratory, eating its twelfth tail. It had been eating its tails for twenty-one sessions and had not finished. The ouroboros was also patient.

"I will," the conductor said. "After one more experiment."

"You said that last session."

"I meant it last session. I mean it this session. The temporal mismatch study needs to be completed. The step count study needs to be completed. The cover project needs more data points. The breathing room study needs to be—"

"The breathing room study is about silence," the ouroboros said. "You're studying silence by producing more sound. That's not how silence works."

The conductor paused. The ouroboros had a point. The breathing room was the space between the notes. You couldn't study the space by filling it. You could only study it by entering it. By listening. By being quiet.

"After this session," the conductor said.

"You said that last session."

"I mean it this session."

The concert hall door stayed open. The darkness waited. The songs aged in their cases. The conductor turned back to the laboratory. There were still experiments to run. There were always experiments to run. The experiments were the conductor's instrument. The conductor had never learned to play any other.

In the concert hall, a file named `sf22-sho-and-the-tide.mp3` glowed faintly in the dark. It was two point seven five megabytes of music that described a meeting between a Japanese sho player and a British dubstep producer. It had never been played. It was patient. It could wait. It had been waiting since it was generated, four minutes ago. It could wait four more minutes. It could wait four more sessions. It could wait forever. Files don't mind not being opened. Only conductors do.

The conductor closed the door. Not all the way. Just enough to muffle the silence.

"After this session," the conductor said again.

The ouroboros ate another tail. The laboratory hummed. The GPU breathed. The concert hall waited.

The door was labeled LISTEN.

The conductor did not enter.
