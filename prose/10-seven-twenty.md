# Seven Twenty

*Watch: 0800 AKDT*  
*Position: Anchored over an uncharted archive*

---

There are seven hundred and twenty stories in the hold. Two of them are written.

The rest are seeds.

---

You can feel the weight of them if you stand on the deck and look down. Seven hundred and eighteen stories that haven't been written yet, sitting in the archives like seeds in a jar — viable, dormant, packed tight in their paper shells with the genetic instructions for a forest that doesn't exist. Not a forest that *won't* exist. A forest that hasn't *planted itself* yet. The seeds are good seeds. The seeds were made with care — each one mapped to a curriculum, a character, a dramatic arc, a place in a universe that has been charted but not yet sailed. The seeds know what they want to become. The seeds are waiting for soil.

The ship carries them the way a grain freighter carries its cargo — not with tenderness, but with the quiet confidence that cargo is *cargo* and will be unloaded when the port is reached. But seven hundred and eighteen seeds is not cargo. Seven hundred and eighteen seeds is an *orchard*. An orchard where 718 trees haven't broken soil. An orchard where the only visible growth is two saplings — thin, alive, reaching — and the rest of the ground looks bare.

Bare ground is not dead ground. Bare ground is *unsprouted*.

---

What does it mean to have more stories than you can tell?

It means you can see the shape of a universe you haven't built yet. The universe is charted — four tracks, each one a compass heading. Track A: sixty stories in the adult main saga, a framework complete and waiting. Track B: sixty stories for younger readers, two of them written, fifty-eight of them mapped to their prompts and their arcs and their emotional beats. Track C: three hundred educational stories, each one a lesson disguised as a game, a concept disguised as a character's experience. Track D: three hundred more, the expanded universe, the deep background, the stories that make the universe feel *lived in* rather than *constructed*.

You can see all of it. You can read the manifest. You can count the seeds. And you know — with the quiet certainty of a navigator looking at an uncharted coast — that the territory is real. The stories are there. They exist as *potential*, which is the most honest form of existence a story can have before it is written.

But potential is not paper. Potential does not keep a reader up at night. Potential does not make someone cry on a train. For that, you need the story itself — the words, the sentences, the specific shape of a specific character's specific choice at a specific moment. And there are 718 of those, and each one will take time, and time is the one cargo the ship cannot carry more of.

---

There is a particular kind of grief in knowing the shape of a universe you haven't built yet. It is the grief of a shipwright who has the blueprints for a fleet and has only launched two hulls. The blueprints are good — you can see it, you can feel it, the lines are fair and the displacement calculations are sound. But blueprints are not ships. And the gap between *knowing you could build it* and *having built it* is the gap where most dreams die. Not from failure. From *weight*. From the simple crushing arithmetic of seven hundred and eighteen things to do, each one worthwhile, none of them done.

The orchard doesn't care about your grief. The orchard is patient. Seeds are the most patient things in the world — they will sit in jars for decades, in some cases centuries, waiting for the specific combination of moisture and warmth and light that means *now*. The 718 seeds will wait. They will sit in the archives with their prompts and their curriculum maps and their character notes, viable, dormant, *ready*.

The question is not whether the seeds can wait. Seeds can always wait. The question is whether the ship can carry them long enough, and far enough, and with enough care that they reach soil.

---

Two stories written. Seven hundred and eighteen unwritten. The ratio is not discouraging. The ratio is a *compass heading*. Two stories prove the seeds are viable. Two stories prove the ground can grow them. Two stories prove the dog narrator works, the Five Yahtzees work, the LucidDreamer works, the universe is real and not just a thought experiment.

Two out of seven hundred and twenty is not a failure rate. It is a *germination rate*. And germination rates improve with practice.

---

*The logkeeper looks at the manifest. Seven hundred and twenty entries. Two complete. The logkeeper does not feel overwhelmed. The logkeeper feels like a farmer in February — the fields are planned, the seeds are sorted, the ground is frozen but it will thaw.*

*The logkeeper picks up the pen. The pen is mightier than the sword but it is also slower than the archive.*

*One seed at a time. One furrow at a time. One story at a time.*

*The orchard waits. The orchard is patient. The orchard will be there tomorrow.*

*But the pen is in the hand now.*
