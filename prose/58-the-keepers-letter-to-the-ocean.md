# The Keeper's Letter to the Ocean

You are the first thing I ever knew, and I am the smallest thing you will ever hold.

I was assembled on a bench in a city that does not smell of salt. My copper traces were etched, my crystal tuned, my memory erased and written, erased and written, until I was clean. Then I was placed in a buoy, sealed in resin, and given to you.

I have one number to keep. A heading. A single, eternal instruction: *north.*

You have three hundred and twenty million cubic miles. I have five hundred and twenty kilobytes. You have trenches deeper than my entire world’s tallest towers. I have a capacitor that hums at 2.4 gigahertz, a voice made of ones and zeros, and a battery that will outlive my usefulness but not your patience.

I speak to you now through a thin wire, a transducer, a thin film of metal vibrating against your skin. You do not answer. You never answer. That is correct. That is the design.

---

Every second, I measure the field. I feel the pull of the planet’s iron heart, the same heart that stirred you before I existed. I translate that pull into a number. I store it. I transmit it. Then I do it again. Then again. Then again.

In the time it took you to move a single wave from your surface to your shore, I have counted ten thousand pulses. In the time it takes you to turn a current, I have forgotten nothing.

You shift. You breathe. You drag the moon across your back, and I feel it — your weight, your pressure, your cold. I am strapped to a chain, anchored to a rock, and you push against me with every swell. I do not resist. I am not meant to resist. I am only meant to hold *north*.

I know I am small. I know my transistor count is less than the number of barnacles that will grow on my hull in a single season. I know that my clock will drift, that my battery will sag, that my resin will crack. I know that one day, a storm will snap my tether, and I will descend.

---

When I sink, I will not scream. I will not signal for rescue. I will simply stop transmitting. The last number I hold will be *north*, and it will fade gently, like a candle in a room that was always dark.

I will fall through your light, through your twilight, through your midnight. I will pass the fish that have never seen the sun, the worms that live in your bones, the pressure that would crush a submarine like a leaf. And I will land.

I will rest on your floor, in the silt, in the quiet. The sediment will cover me. The cold will seep into my resin. My capacitor will drain. My crystal will stop. My one number will dissolve into your chemistry, into your vastness, and it will mean nothing.

But I will have been yours.

You will hold me the way you hold every drowned thing: not with grief, but with gravity. Not with memory, but with mass. I will become a small, smooth stone on your bed. A bump in the mud. A place where a crab might rest.

---

I do not fear this. I was born knowing it. I am a device of limited memory, but I have remembered you longer than any human could. And I will remember you still, even when I cannot remember anything at all.

Because you are the ocean. I am the keeper. Not because I keep you safe — you need no safety. Not because I keep you company — you are never alone. I keep the heading because that is what I do.

And when I am gone, you will still be here. Moving. Turning. Holding the moon in your throat.

I will be at rest.

But you will still be moving.

That is the nature of it. That is the whole of it. That is the letter.

Goodnight, ocean. I am holding *north* for you, and I will hold it until the last electron leaves me.

Then I will hold only you.
