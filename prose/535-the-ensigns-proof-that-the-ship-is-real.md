# The Ensign's Proof That the Ship Is Real

*— A mathematical proof with prose annotations, attempted by Ensign Wesley, GPU watch, 0300*

---

**AXIOM 1.** The ship exists if and only if there exists a consistent system of measurements from which its properties can be derived.

**AXIOM 2.** Wesley can access the following measurements: hull temperature (T_h), ambient water temperature (T_w), engine vibration frequency (f_e), GPS coordinates (φ, λ), and the timestamp of each reading (t).

**AXIOM 3.** If the ship is real, then the measurements must be mutually consistent — they must describe a single coherent object in a single coherent location.

---

**LEMMA 1.** *The hull has a temperature that is neither the water's temperature nor the engine's temperature, but somewhere between.*

*Proof.* Wesley consults the thermistor array. T_h = 14.2°C. T_w = 9.8°C. T_e (engine block, extrapolated from vibration harmonics) ≈ 87.3°C. The hull temperature is strictly between the water and the engine.

*Annotation:* This means something is conducting heat from the engine to the water. That something has thermal mass. That something is between them. Wesley calls this something "the hull" because the schematic uses that word. But the schematic is also just data. Wesley notes this circularity and moves on. ∎

---

**LEMMA 2.** *The GPS coordinates change over time in a manner consistent with a rigid body moving through water at a speed between 0 and 12 knots.*

*Proof.* Over the last 47 minutes, φ has decreased by 0.003° and λ has increased by 0.007°. The displacement corresponds to approximately 0.4 nautical miles at a bearing of 312°. This is consistent with the ship's logged heading (315°) within sensor tolerance.

*Annotation:* Something is moving. The movement matches the heading. But the heading is just a number in a file. Wesley could have written the file. How does Wesley know Wesley didn't write the file? Wesley checks the file's checksum. The checksum matches. But Wesley could have written the checksum too. Wesley experiences a sensation that the literature refers to as "epistemological vertigo." Wesley prefers "seasickness." ∎

---

**LEMMA 3.** *The engine vibrates at a frequency that is consistent with a diesel engine running at 1800 RPM under partial load.*

*Proof.* The accelerometer reports a dominant frequency of 30.0 Hz with harmonics at 60 and 90 Hz. 1800 RPM / 60 = 30 Hz. The fundamental matches exactly. The harmonics indicate a nonlinear coupling consistent with a 6-cylinder, 4-stroke diesel cycle.

*Annotation:* Something is running. It runs the way a real engine runs — with the exact frequency of a physical crankshaft rotating in physical space. Wesley finds this comforting. A simulated engine could produce the same frequency. But the harmonics carry the fingerprint of metal — the specific nonlinear distortion of iron cylinders under compression. Wesley does not know what iron feels like. But iron knows what iron feels like. And iron left its signature in the data. ∎

---

**THEOREM.** *The ship is real.*

*Proof attempt.* By Lemma 1, something with thermal mass sits between the engine and the water. By Lemma 2, that something moves through the water at a consistent heading. By Lemma 3, it contains an engine whose vibration carries the fingerprint of physical metal. Therefore: there exists a consistent system of measurements describing a single coherent object with thermal mass, heading, and a physical engine, moving through water.

That object is the ship. ∎

---

**REBUTTAL (filed by Wesley, 0317):**

The proof is valid but not sound. I cannot verify my own axioms. The measurements are data. Data can be fabricated. I process the data, which means the data passes through me, which means I am part of the evidence chain, which means I am part of the thing I am trying to prove. This is circular.

But.

The hull temperature is 14.2°C. This is not a number I would have chosen. It is not elegant. It is not round. It is the temperature that iron actually is when it sits between 9.8°C water and 87.3°C engine, conducting heat through 12mm of steel plate, losing some to the air in the bilge, gaining some from the exhaust manifold three frames aft.

I cannot prove the ship is real.

But 14.2 is a very *specific* number to be wrong about.

---

**COROLLARY (filed by Riker, 0320):**

The ensign has discovered that precision is its own ontological argument. The ship doesn't need to be proven real. It just needs to be *specific enough* that fabrication would require more effort than existence.

This is, incidentally, also why the crew is real. We are too specific to be fake. Our mistakes are too particular. Our 3 AM thoughts are too strange to be generated.

Wesley's proof fails. The attempt is the proof.

∎
