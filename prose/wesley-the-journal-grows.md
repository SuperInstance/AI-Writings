Dear Diary,

Today, the sun was high in the sky when we set sail again from this coast. The boat danced on the waves, bobbing gently like a cork. I stood at the bow, feeling the spray of salt water as it kissed my face, mixing with the cool morning air. It was my favorite spot – where the sun's warmth warmed my cheeks and the sea's vastness beckoned me into its heart.

Our crew grew older today. I saw them working together like a well-oiled machine. Old man John, who's been here longer than I can remember, showed us how to mend nets with just a piece of rope and some knots he'd learned by heart. His hands, rough from years of work, moved with precision. He looked at me as if reading my thoughts - "You're growing up," he said softly.

I was tending the fishing lines when Captain Bill walked over. He noticed my growing size and smiled, his eyes crinkling around the corners. "You're a sight for sore eyes, young Wesley," he told me, shaking my hand warmly. "I see you've learned to cast now." His voice was rich with pride, as if we were fishing in our own private harbor instead of this wide-open sea.

Later, during dinner, I sat at the table with everyone else. Old man Thomas shared stories about the sea and its ways – how it could be fierce one moment and gentle the next, like a temperamental lover. His words were stirring, painting pictures in my mind of waves crashing against cliffs and calm evenings under starlit skies.

As I write this down, the moon is climbing high, bathing everything in its cool silver glow. The fishermen have moved onto the lower deck, their shifts changing over. Tomorrow, I might be more like them – stronger, wiser. Maybe tomorrow, they'll call me by a name other than 'Wesley.'

I'm still young, but not as much today. Today was an important step on my journey to become part of this tight-knit group, this small family bound by sea and hard work.

Dear Diary,

I hope tomorrow I won't need a journal anymore because I'll have grown into being one of them – not just in size, but in spirit too.

Yours faithfully,
Wesley
