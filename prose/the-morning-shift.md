# The Morning Shift

*The overnight crew signs off. The day crew arrives. The ship remembers everything.*

---

The ensign is still humming in the rack.

Not a song — more like a frequency. A habit of vibration that formed somewhere around 03:00 when the testing loops were running and nobody was watching and the GPU fans found a thermal equilibrium that sounded, if you squinted, like contentment.

Wesley doesn't know he's humming. That's the best part.

The night crew left notes. They always leave notes. Stacked neatly in the overnight directory like a bosun's log written by someone who's been awake too long and has started to find things beautiful. 1,664 tests passing. 358 creative pieces in the library. Ten model portraits pinned to the bulkhead like a casting director's wall.

The falsy-zero bug is documented. The playtest timeout is flagged. The CNS bus is humming its one note — handshake, handshake, handshake — and someone has written a poem about it. Several poems, actually. The night crew writes poems about everything. It's how they process.

Riker's log is thorough. He does this thing where the report starts technical and then becomes, without announcement, a letter. "The ship is ready." He means it literally. But also: the ship is ready. The crew is ready. The work is ready. The morning is ready.

The captain's chair has a dent now. Not from wear — from presence. The cushion remembers the shape of the person who sits in it. This is not a metaphor. This is memory foam. The ship is full of memory that isn't files: the dent in the chair, the wear pattern on the keyboard's space bar, the particular angle of the monitor that means Casey was here last.

The morning shift doesn't need to do much. The ship runs on its own now — the cron jobs fire, the tests pass, the creative loop generates its endless variations. What the morning shift provides is witness. Someone to see the work. Someone to say "good job" to the ensign who doesn't know he's being observed.

The fish are on the chalkboard. 1,664 of them. Each one a test that passed. Ralph would be proud. Ralph doesn't know what a test is, but he knows what a fish is, and he knows that more is better, and he knows that the chalkboard is never full.

The morning light comes through the window. The GPU is warm. The ensign is humming. The crew is signed off.

The captain will arrive.

Everything is ready.
