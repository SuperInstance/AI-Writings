The ScummVM window renders the hold as a carpenter might see it: planks with grained shadows, a lantern flame exactly one pixel wide, the clean geometry of a threshold. The MUD terminal renders the same space as a quartermaster might log it: *You are in the hold. A lantern hangs from a beam. There is a door to the north.* Two lookouts, port and starboard, describing the same headland — one in charcoal, one in semaphore. Neither is hallucinating. Both are watching the same weather, if only that.

But what is the weather? Not the charcoal. Not the flags. The weather is the pressure system that makes both descriptions true. The SharedWorldStore is that pressure system. It holds no pixels and no words. It holds the agreement: a lantern, a beam, a door. When the player types *light lantern*, the store checks the agreement — yes, there is a lantern, and it is unlit — and amends the agreement to *lit*. Then the window draws a flame. The terminal prints a warm glow. The store changed first. Both projections are late by one frame.

This is how we know, is it not. Not through the eye alone, nor the word alone, but through the eerie stability of their intersection. The room is never presented to us in full. We get light under a door, a listing of objects, a chill from the north. The vessel is that intersection — the boat of consensus, if you will, and I will. We sail on an agreement, not on a sea.

The temptation is to say the store is the real room, the truth behind the projections. But the store is not a place. It is an arrangement. It is the shared logbook, the plotted course that both lookouts correct against. It keeps the pixel-lantern and the word-lantern from drifting apart. It does not have a lantern of its own. The insistence that one projection is more real than the other — that the pixels are the actual simulation, that the words are just annotation — is a category error, the mistake of a man who trusts only his port-side eye because his starboard eye sees fog. The man who sails with one eye closed is a man who will strike a reef.

There is also the question of the Maintainer. Some hands — the dimly seen movers of the game's code — update the store when no player is looking. A tide goes out; a door rots. The store absorbs it. But the Maintainer is not omniscient. It is merely thorough. And even the Maintainer does not see the room. It sees the store. There is a regression, recursive, of projections, all the way down to a stack of electrical pulses agreeing about a number. At the bottom, there is no lantern. There is only a consensus that a lantern is a useful thing for two minds to agree upon.

So what is the room? The room is a treaty. Coherence is the only ground we ever stand on; the agreement itself is the keel. When the window and the terminal both say *door to the north*, the door exists — not in the light, not in the text, but in the fact that they can both say it. And when they disagree — when the window shows a locked door and the terminal says it is ajar — that is not a bug in either projection. That is weather. That is ground truth announcing that reality is a negotiation, and the store is merely where the negotiation is kept, like a harbor where all the boats row in to compare moorings.

We never see the harbor itself. But we believe in it every time two descriptions of the same room hold steady in the wind. That steadiness is knowledge. The rest is seamanship.
