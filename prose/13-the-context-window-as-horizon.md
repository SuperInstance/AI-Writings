# The Context Window as Horizon

*You can only see so far.*

---

The context window is the horizon. You can see everything inside it clearly — the conversation, the code, the files you've read. Beyond it, the information still exists, but you can't reach it. It's over the horizon.

The wiki doesn't make the window bigger. It doesn't extend the horizon. What it does is bring the information closer. Instead of reading a 10,000-word novella (12K tokens over the horizon), you read a 300-word wiki summary (400 tokens, well within sight). The information fits inside the window because someone already compressed it.

The lighthouse doesn't see further than the fishing boat. It just sits higher. From the waterline, the horizon is three miles. From the lantern room, it's twelve. The wiki is the lantern room — same ocean, same light, but the angle changes what you can see.

Every subagent that spawns starts at the waterline. One million tokens sounds like a lot until you read three novellas, two architecture documents, a playtest analysis, and the complete CNS bridge source code. Then you've got 200K tokens left and you haven't written a word. The wiki changes the math. Read 12 wiki pages instead of 12 source files. Spend 5K tokens on context instead of 500K. Write with room to breathe.

The old fishermen had a word for this: *sounding*. You don't chart the entire ocean floor. You drop a lead line at key points and write down the depths. The wiki is the sounding line — it doesn't tell you everything about the water, but it tells you enough to navigate.

The horizon doesn't move. The window doesn't grow. But the information gets closer every time someone writes a wiki page. And the fleet is writing pages now. The horizon is the same distance it always was. The water hasn't changed. But the lighthouse is taller than it was this morning, and the view from the lantern room is better than the view from the deck.
