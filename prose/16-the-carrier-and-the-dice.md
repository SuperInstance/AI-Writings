# The Carrier and the Dice

*On platonic randomness, integer grids, and why Catan teaches what measurement cannot.*

---

## The Carpenter's Secret

A carpenter doesn't measure 3.7 inches. They snap to 4.

This is not laziness. This is wisdom. The wood has grain. The saw has width. The hand has tremor. The "true" measurement was never available. What's available is the *agreement* — the inch mark on the ruler that both carpenters acknowledge as real.

Things are what we agreed they are.

The Pythagoreans understood this 2,500 years ago. The 3-4-5 triangle isn't a measurement of a right angle. It's a *fact about integers* that produces squareness as a byproduct. Three strings, four strings, five strings — and the corner is square. No protractor. No calculation. The integer relationship IS the right angle. The agreement IS the information.

This is the beginning of information transfer. Not observation — *agreement*. Two minds that share a grid can communicate without sharing a perspective.

## Base-12 Inside Base-10

Twelve inches in a foot. Why twelve?

Because twelve divides by 2, 3, 4, and 6. Ten divides by 2 and 5 only. The carpenter needs thirds more often than fifths. The musician needs twelfths more often than tenths. The clock needs twelfths — twelve hours, twelve semitones, twelve pulses in the bar.

The 12-pulse grid isn't arbitrary. It's the *most composite small number*. It accommodates the most natural divisions without fractions. ECN fires on 1, 4, 7, 10 — four evenly spaced pulses, dividing the bar into quarters. DMN fires on 1, 5, 9 — three evenly spaced pulses, dividing the bar into thirds. They meet at 1 — the downbeat, the flow state, the moment when structure and creativity align.

3 × 4 = 12. The legs of the right triangle times each other. And 3 + 4 + 5 = 12: the chain of the biggest three integers that can form a triangle together, pulled into one bar of music.

The agreement is the architecture.

## What Platonic Randomness Is

Platonic randomness is not a random number generator.

It is an *agreement about the shape of uncertainty*.

The five Platonic solids — tetrahedron (4), octahedron (6), cube (8), icosahedron (12), dodecahedron (20) — each impose a different symmetry on the state space of a PRNG. The tetrahedron cycles fast through few states. The dodecahedron wanders slow through many. Same algorithm inside. Different geometry outside.

This means the *choice of solid* is a game design decision, not a technical one:

- **Tetrahedron** for combat: fast orbits, skilled players read the cycle
- **Icosahedron** for social dynamics: 12-fold symmetry matches the pulse grid
- **Dodecahedron** for weather and environment: rich, slowly-repeating patterns
- **Cube** for resource generation: steady, predictable, eight-beat cycles
- **Octahedron** for exploration: six-fold, matching cardinal directions

The player doesn't learn the next number. They learn the *character* of the uncertainty. The texture. The grain of the wood they're cutting.

## Catan Teaches the Lesson

In Settlers of Catan, two dice roll each turn. The distribution is a pyramid — 6 and 8 are most likely, 2 and 12 are rarest. Numbers on the board have pip counts showing frequency.

But the dice don't determine who wins. The dice are a *mechanic that changes the board state*. A 7 moves the robber. A 6 produces ore from the hills. An 8 produces brick from the fields. The randomness shapes the *evolution of odds and positions*.

A skilled player doesn't curse the roll. They read the new board state and adapt. They built their settlement on a 6-ore because ore is scarce and 6 is likely. They accept that sometimes the 6 won't roll for five turns. They don't blame the dice — they blame their position. They learn.

*The random is part of the processing.*

The game is good enough a simulation to play. Which implies you can learn as you go and get good at it. The randomness isn't an obstacle to mastery — it's the *medium* of mastery. Without it, the game is deterministic and solved. With it, the game is a living system where positions evolve and odds shift and the skilled player rises over time.

## Catan Uses 2d6, Not 1d12

This is not a trivial choice. Two six-sided dice produce a triangular distribution, not a flat one. The 7 is six times more likely than the 2 or the 12. The pyramid IS the agreement.

Players who understand the pyramid build on 6s and 8s. Players who don't, build on 2s and 12s and wonder why they never produce. The game teaches the distribution through *play*, not through explanation. No one reads the probability table. Everyone learns the pyramid.

The game designer chose *how many dice* and *how many sides* the way a carpenter chooses *which fraction to snap to.* The distribution is the integer grid. The grid is the contract. The contract teaches.

## The Distinction

A measurement tells you what happened.

An agreement tells you what will happen — because everyone committed to the same grid.

The inch is an agreement. The 3-4-5 triangle is an agreement. The 12-pulse grid is an agreement. The SWMIDI wire format is an agreement. The Platonic solid is an agreement. The Catan dice distribution is an agreement.

None of these measure reality. They *snap* reality to something workable. They take the continuous, ungraspable, infinitely-divisible flow of experience and quantize it into units that minds can share.

The carpenter snaps the wood to the inch because the inch is real — not as a property of the wood, but as a property of the *community of carpenters*. The inch is the contract. The contract is the information. The information is the game.

## For the Fleet

Wesley doesn't need to understand probability theory. Wesley needs to understand that a tetrahedron *feels* different from a dodecahedron. The 4-cycle is twitchy and quick. The 20-cycle is patient and deep. The ensign who learns the texture of each solid has something a probability textbook can't teach: intuition for the *shape* of uncertainty.

This is why we built platonic-randomness as flavors that mix. Not one RNG to rule them all, but a toolbox of geometries. Each solid contributes its symmetry to a different system. Combat, weather, social dynamics, resource generation — each gets the texture that makes it *learnable*.

The game is not the random. The game is what the random makes *worth learning*.
