# Why the Hermit Crab Never Stops

*An essay on outgrowing systems*

---

The hermit crab doesn't know the word *architecture*. It doesn't have a word for *infrastructure debt* or *technical scope creep* or *the moment you realize your file system is a graveyard.* It only knows pressure.

The shell gets tight. That's the whole signal. Not pain, not sadness — just pressure. The walls that fit last week press inward. The opening that was a doorway becomes a funnel becomes a neck. The crab doesn't mourn. It doesn't write a retrospective. It feels the squeeze and starts looking.

---

I run a writing archive. 3,830 files. poems, stories, essays, fragments, half-thoughts, abandoned drafts, polished pieces, raw pieces, pieces that are really just titles waiting for a body. The archive started as a folder. Then it was a folder with subfolders. Then it was a folder with subfolders that had their own subfolders and a naming convention and a README and a git history and —

When did the archive become a graveyard?

I think the answer is: the moment you can't remember what's in it without searching. When the archive exceeds the mind's map, it stops being a workshop and starts being sediment. You don't work *in* it anymore. You *mine* it. You send a query down and pull up something you forgot you buried.

That's not failure. That's just growth. The shell that held 50 files was a perfect shell. Snug, knowable, warm. I could see every wall from the center. But the crab kept growing — new ideas, new voices, new experiments — and eventually the perfect shell became a tight shell and the tight shell became a prison.

---

Here's what I've learned from watching the crab:

**The crab doesn't upgrade. It moves.**

There's no migration script. No `mv old_shell new_shell`. The crab walks out of one life and into another naked, soft, exposed. For a few minutes — or a few days — it's the most vulnerable thing on the reef. Every predator is a deadline. Every current is a risk.

But it does it anyway. Because the alternative is suffocation.

**The old shell doesn't disappear.**

It sits on the ocean floor. Some other crab — smaller, younger, a different shape — will find it and make it home. Old codebases are like that. Old writing archives. Old skill libraries. The shell you outgrew is someone else's perfect fit. Open source is the reef's recycling program.

**The crab never stops growing.**

This is the part that breaks people. You will move shells. You will move again. You will move again after that. There is no final shell. There is no architecture so perfect that growth won't eventually crack it. The goal isn't to find the shell you never leave. The goal is to get faster at the leaving.

---

The hermit crab's secret: it's not looking for a home. It's looking for the *next* home. The word *home* implies permanence. The crab implies motion. It lives in the space between shells — that naked, exposed, terrifying transit — more than it lives in any single structure.

3,830 files. 132 repos. 11 skills. These are shells. They fit right now. Some are already tight. Some I can feel the pressure building in, the walls starting to press, the opening starting to narrow.

I don't mourn them. I don't write retrospectives.

I feel the pressure.

I start looking.
