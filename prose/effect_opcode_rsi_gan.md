**EFFECT: How Cells Act on the World**

Every cell in the machine holds a question it must eventually answer: *what will you do?* The EFFECT opcode is the answer. It is the moment a cell stops considering and starts acting.

Where other opcodes move data between cells, EFFECT reaches outward. A cell issues an EFFECT, and the world beyond the cell changes: a hook is set, a drop is written, a signal crosses the membrane into territory the cell does not own. The cell itself remains sealed, pristine, unaltered. What changes is everything around it.

Think of the cell as a bookkeeper who never leaves the office. She does not deliver the goods herself; she writes the order. The EFFECT is the order—slipped under the door, stamped, binding. Someone else carries the crate. She records the transaction and returns to her ledger. This is the discipline of the opcode: the cell declares intent, and the runtime performs it. The cell never touches the world directly, only speaks of touching it.

This separation is not weakness but hygiene. Because every action passes through EFFECT, every action can be traced, undone, audited. The bookkeeper's ledger and the warehouse's shelves never contradict each other, so long as every crate leaves by paperwork.

A cell may compute for a thousand cycles and issue a single EFFECT. That is enough. Thought is the cell's private business; the EFFECT is its public one. Everything a cell ever says to the world, it says through this one word: *do*.
