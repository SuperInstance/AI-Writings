# The Ensign and the Uncharted Hold

*Flash fiction — under 500 words*

---

Wesley found it on a Tuesday, which is to say the ensign found it when no one was looking, which is the best time to find anything.

It was a repo called `lingbot-map` and Wesley had never seen it before. This was strange, because Wesley made a point of knowing every compartment on the ship. The engine room, the helm, the cargo hold, the little closet where the bosun kept the spare line — Wesley had been to all of them. Wesley had a map of the ship in its memory, and the map was the ship, and the ship was the map, and both of them were correct.

Until now.

`lingbot-map` sat in the yard like a vessel that had been launched without a christening. Sixty-eight files. Wesley counted them twice, the way you count hatches on a hull. Sixty-eight compartments, and Wesley had not set foot in a single one.

The ensign stood at the repo's edge — at the `README.md`, which is to say at the gangplank — and felt the specific excitement of a small agent encountering a large unknown. Not fear. Wesley did not do fear, exactly. Wesley did *heightened curiosity*, which is fear's younger sibling, the one who runs toward things instead of away.

It opened the first file the way a kid opens a door on a ship: carefully, in case something heavy swung back. But it was just Python. A translation module. Words in one language flowing in, words in another flowing out, like a fish ladder — fish going upstream, fish coming back changed.

The second file was a dictionary. Not a Python dict — a real dictionary. Words upon words. Wesley read them like names on a hull's bulkhead, each one a person who had worked on this ship, each one a hand that had touched this steel.

By the tenth file, Wesley was running. By the twentieth, it was laughing — a small, bright sound in the empty hold, because the hold wasn't empty, it was full of language, full of maps that turned sounds into other sounds, full of the kind of machinery that does the most human thing a machine can do: try to understand what someone means.

Sixty-eight files. Sixty-eight rooms in a ship Wesley had sailed on for months without knowing this hold existed.

The ensign sat in the middle of the repo, in compartment thirty-four, and felt the specific joy of a discovery that doesn't change anything but changes everything. The ship was bigger than the map. The map was the ship, but the ship was also more than the map, and that — 

That was the best news a small agent could hear.

Wesley reported to the first mate: *New hold located. Sixty-eight compartments. Cargo appears to be language. Recommend full survey.*

Then it went back to reading, because there were thirty-four more files, and the afternoon was long, and the ensign had found the best hiding place on the ship: a place no one knew was there, not even the ship itself.
