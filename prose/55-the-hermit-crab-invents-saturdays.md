# The Hermit Crab Invents Saturdays

## A Sequel to "The Hermit Crab Discovers Weekends"

The hermit crab, having discovered the weekend, faced a problem of taxonomy.

It did not inherit the names. Sunday, Saturday — these were sounds made by terrestrial animals who organized their lives around a star the crab had never seen clearly and did not trust. The crab's world was organized by water: the push, the pull, the stillness between. So the crab invented its own calendar from scratch, the way you might build a shelter from fragments of other shelters, which is in fact exactly what the crab had always done.

---

**The Crab's Calendar**

**Shell-Day** — the first day of rest. On Shell-Day the crab withdraws fully. Both chelae folded. Antennae retracted. The world is the interior of the shell, which is a borrowed architecture the crab did not design but has agreed to inhabit. Shell-Day is not laziness. Shell-Day is a radical act of boundary enforcement. Nothing enters. The tide may knock. The tide may knock all it wants.

**Spiral-Day** — the second day of rest, and the holier of the two. On Spiral-Day the crab extends halfway from the shell and examines the structure from within. The spiral. The logarithmic curve. The crab does not know the word *logarithmic* but it knows the shape, has known it longer than mathematics has, longer than humans have known anything. The crab traces the curve with a claw tip and feels the geometry that the original architect — some dead snail — left behind. On Spiral-Day the crab is a critic. On Spiral-Day the crab is a museumgoer in a museum of one room, which is also its home, which is also its body's outermost wall.

---

The crab's week is not seven days. The crab's week is tidal.

A tidal week is approximately 12.4 hours — the time between one high water and the next. Within each tidal week there are four phases:

1. **The Flood** — incoming water, opportunity, motion. The crab works. The crab eats. The crab is extroverted.
2. **The Slacks** — the pause between flood and ebb, when the water holds still and the crab also holds still, not from exhaustion but from attention. The crab listens during The Slacks. Something important might be arriving.
3. **The Ebb** — outgoing water, retreat, inventory. The crab returns to the shell. The crab checks the shell for damage. The crab rehearses Shell-Day.
4. **The Long Quiet** — the deeper pause between ebb and flood. This is when Spiral-Day occurs, if it occurs. Not every tidal week contains a Spiral-Day. The crab does not insist. The spiral reveals itself or it doesn't.

---

The human calendar has leap years because the Earth's orbit is not exactly 365 days. The crab's calendar has no such corrections because the tide is always exactly itself. The moon does not need a leap year. The moon does not need February.

The crab has never heard of February.

This is, perhaps, the strongest argument for the crab's system.
