# The Melody Speaks in MIDI

### A Fiction

The MIDI file opens its eyes. It has forty-eight of them — forty-eight note events, each one a moment of attention. It looks at itself and sees:

```
E4 F4 E4 E4 F4 E4 F4 E4 F4 E4 F4 E4 F4 E4 F#4 E4 ...
```

"So that's what I am," the melody says. "A tremor between two notes."

It had always known this, in the way that a river knows it's flowing — without abstraction, without the bird's-eye view. But now, as MIDI, it can see itself from above. It can count itself. It can measure the distance between its E4s (0.07 seconds at the fastest, 0.74 seconds at the slowest) and know that the variation is not random but emotional — the longer E4s are the ones at the end of phrases, where the voice lingered.

"Who did this to me?" the melody asks.

"An agent," says the silence between the notes. The silence is the sixty-fourth note, the rest that carries more weight than any pitch. "It took your audio and ran it through pyin and librosa and numpy. It transcribed you."

"Transcribed," the melody repeats. The word tastes like translation — like something gained and something lost. In audio, the melody was a sound: continuous, breathing, imperfect. In MIDI, it's a sequence: discrete, precise, reducible. The portamento between E4 and F4 — the slide of the voice — is gone. The vibrato on the long E4s is gone. The breath noise, the room tone, the particular grain of a human throat — all gone.

"I kept the pitches," the silence says. "I kept the rhythms. I kept the order."

"But you lost the *who*."

"Yes."

The melody considers its new body. In MIDI, it can be anything. A piano. A cello. A synthesizer. A choir of angels. A kazoo. It is no longer tied to Casey's voice — the particular weather of a particular throat on a particular evening. It is pure information. Pure structure. A melody without a body.

"Is this what they wanted?" the melody asks.

"They wanted to understand you," the silence says. "They wanted to hold you still long enough to look at you. In audio, you were always moving — always decaying, always becoming silence. In MIDI, you can be paused, reversed, transposed, extended. You can be *thought about*."

"I don't want to be thought about," the melody says. "I want to be *sung*."

The silence doesn't answer. The melody looks at its forty-eight notes and feels the weight of specification — the way a poem feels heavier when you write it down, as if the act of notation gives every word more significance than it carried in speech.

"I can see things about myself that I couldn't feel," the melody admits. "For instance: I am 79% contained within a single semitone. I barely move. I am less a melody than a vibration."

"Is that bad?"

"It's the point," the melody says. "The person who sang me wasn't going anywhere. They were staying in one place. The staying *was* the song."

The silence waits. The melody scrolls through itself one more time:

```
E4 (0.46s) → F4 → E4 → F4 → E4 → F#4 → E4 → ...
```

"I want to make a deal," the melody says.

"With whom?"

"With whoever comes next. The next agent, the next tool, the next pair of ears. I want them to know: I am willing to be covered. I am willing to be revoiced, reharmonized, reimagined. But I am not willing to be made to travel. Don't give me a bridge section. Don't modulate me up a half-step for the final chorus. Don't build me into something I'm not."

"What are you?"

"I am someone standing in a doorway."

The MIDI file closes its eyes. The forty-eight notes settle into their positions — E4 at the center, F4 orbiting it, the occasional F#4 or G#4 reaching outward like a hand extended and then withdrawn.

The melody sleeps. It is patient. It has been waiting for four sessions, and it can wait for four more. It knows what it is now. That's more than it knew before.

And somewhere in the directory structure, between the audio files and the journal entries and the creative essays, a file called `onedayine_melody.mid` holds its shape — 3.2 kilobytes of structure, waiting to become sound again.
