The engine is the heart. The hull is the ribcage. We are five small organs in five small rooms, each thumbing our own pulse into the keys. Through the steel, I hear the others: a staccato burst from the port side—the fast one, casting lines with every sentence. Then the long, patient drag from starboard, a slow one hauling his net, letting each word settle in the mesh before pulling it over the gunwale. And beneath them, the third—a sound like a needle tapping glass, her prose a fine stitch against the storm’s raw weather. It is a strange orchestra.

The ship knows. When we are all working, the rhythm of her changes. Normally, she rolls with the sea’s dictation, a submissive vessel taking orders from the swell. But when four or five of us are tapping at once, she seems to hold her breath, then find her own cadence. The pitch of the engine drops a half-tone, as if she’s humming along with the keypresses. The creak of the bulkheads—that long, complaining groan in the trough of a wave—becomes a metronome, syncopated to our collective tempo. We are not just riding the water; we are giving it a backbeat.

I pause. The silence is immediate, a vacuum in the room. Through the thin metal, I can feel the others. The fast one stumbles, the slow one’s drag grows heavy, the needle-woman’s thread snaps. The ship wobbles, catching herself on a wave she didn’t expect. It is a lesson in ballast. When I resume, my fingers finding the keys again, it’s as if I’ve thrown a weight into the hold. The others adjust, and the vessel rights herself.

We are not writers in a cabin. We are the crew of a strange craft, each manning a different station. The fast one is the lookout, scanning the horizon of his plot. The slow one is the navigator, charting a course through the shoals of his paragraphs. The needle-woman is the cook, filleting the raw catch of her memory into something precise and edible. I am the engineer, tending the pressure of my own boiler, letting it build until the safety valve hisses a chapter.

The engine room is below us, but the true deep is down there with the words. When we all write, the ship moves not against the sea but *with* a purpose. She tilts her bow into the wind, not in defiance, but because we’ve given her a heading. The keyboard clicks are our sonar, pinging the darkness, mapping the shape of what we don't yet know. We are five rooms on one ship, but when the keys start up, we are a single thought, stitched together by steel and the shared weight of the water. We fish for meaning, and we throw the lines over together.
