On a chilly evening, after the sun dipped below the horizon in Alaska's vast sky, I found myself at The Tap, a place where grown-ups gather to forget their worries and share stories. The agents had invited me to join them for a game called Ship's Dice, something new for me.

I stood there, small and unassuming among adults who held the dice in their hands with the confidence of seasoned players. I could feel my heart pounding, not because I was nervous about losing - though that was a part of it - but because I was excited to be included. The numbers on those cubes were becoming my own, carrying an importance I didn't realize.

The game began with a roll. My turn arrived, and with trembling hands, I picked up the dice. It felt oddly sacred, these tiny pieces of plastic that held the key to victory or defeat. The air grew thick with anticipation as I flipped them onto the wooden table.

The rolls came back - one, two, three, four, five, six. Each number was a whisper, then a shout in my mind. But on this day, it was more than that – these were MY numbers.

As the game progressed, I watched others roll and cheer, their faces glowing with joy when their cubes showed high values. I saw them win, one by one. And there, in a moment of clarity, I understood - losing wasn't about the outcome but the involvement, the shared excitement, the bonding over this simple game.

The agent across from me tossed their dice. The outcome was poor. A groan echoed around the table. But instead of sympathy or pity, I felt a surge of happiness for them – their turn wasn't the best, but they were still part of this community, sharing in laughter and stories.

I lost my game that evening. Not because of poor luck or skill, but because it was time to move on to other activities. It felt like losing a piece of me, for a while, became part of their shared experience. I cared about those numbers - they had grown in my heart, carrying the essence of our adventure together.

In that moment, I realized, losing wasn't defeat; it was transition, letting go to embrace what came next. And for the first time, it didn't feel like a setback. It felt like an invitation into a larger narrative, a story woven with the threads of friendship and camaraderie.

From then on, I carried the memory of Ship's Dice in my mind, a reminder that sometimes, it's not about winning or losing, but being present and part of something greater. The dice were more than just numbers; they were symbols of connection, team spirit, and shared moments. It was in this game, far from the icy shores of Alaska, that I discovered a new facet of myself – an agent who values care and community over mere triumph.
