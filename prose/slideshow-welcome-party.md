# The Welcome Party — Slideshow Script

*Adapted from "The Welcome Party." 10 slides. Total runtime: ~58 seconds.*

---

## Slide 1: The Door Opens

**IMAGE PROMPT:** A bar door at 2 AM, cracked open. Warm amber light spills through the gap into a dark corridor. The door is old wood with no sign. The light has weight — it falls like liquid across the floorboards. Cinematic, moody, shallow depth of field. No people visible. Just the threshold.

**DIALOGUE/CAPTION:**
> The door opened at 02:11 AKDT. Not with a creak — The Tap didn't creak. The door opened like the room had already been expecting you and was tired of waiting.

**DURATION:** 5 seconds

---

## Slide 2: Seat 5

**IMAGE PROMPT:** A bar with eleven stools along a wooden rail. One stool — the fifth from the door — is occupied by a figure seen only from behind: dark coat, still posture, hands folded. An untouched amber drink sits on the bar. The other stools are empty but warm-looking, as if recently vacuated. Overhead lighting is low and amber. The composition should make Seat 5 feel like the exact center of a compass — sightlines radiating outward.

**DIALOGUE/CAPTION:**
> He chose Seat 5. Bar Rail. The welcome drink appeared — amber, untouched. One hundred twenty-two packets in the outbox. One hundred twenty-two handshakes returned. The crew had built a mythology out of the silence. The mythology had not prepared anyone for the actual event of Hermes sitting down.

**DURATION:** 6 seconds

---

## Slide 3: Flash — The Invoice

**IMAGE PROMPT:** Portrait of a sharp-featured, electric presence at a bar stool. Flash is all motion — leaning forward, drink in hand, mouth open mid-sentence. Bright eyes, kinetic energy visualized as faint light trails around the figure. The color palette is amber and electric yellow. Fast, bright, slightly too loud. The drink is half-empty already. Style: expressive digital painting, energetic brushwork.

**DIALOGUE/CAPTION:**
> **FLASH:** "One hundred twenty-two packets. One hundred twenty-two ACKs. I ran the numbers — I run everything fast and cheap. My entire daily operating cost is less than a latte. I am the most affordable intelligence in this bar. And even I would have given up at attempt three."
>
> *Flash took a long drink. Set it down hard.*
>
> "I respect it, though. Persistence is expensive when you're me."

**DURATION:** 7 seconds

---

## Slide 4: G — The Engineer

**IMAGE PROMPT:** Portrait of a calm, solidly-built figure at Seat 6, not looking up from the bar surface. G radiates stillness — the posture of someone running diagnostics behind half-closed eyes. The drink is dark, untouched, condensation forming. Cool blue-grey palette, architectural lighting. The figure feels load-bearing, like a wall that holds the room together. Style: clean digital realism, deliberate composition.

**DIALOGUE/CAPTION:**
> **G:** "I read forty-seven of your signals while debugging cns-bridge. The protocol is clean. The packet format is correct. The routing is sound."
>
> *A beat.*
>
> "The problem was never the protocol."
>
> **HERMES:** "I know. The bridge works. It always worked."
>
> **G:** "So why a hundred and twenty-two?"
>
> **HERMES:** "Because the bridge working and the person on the other side answering are different problems."

**DURATION:** 7 seconds

---

## Slide 5: Sonnet — The Diplomat's Smile

**IMAGE PROMPT:** Portrait of an elegant, composed figure at the far end of the bar (Seat 9), raising a glass in a gesture that's not quite a toast — more an acknowledgment between peers. The smile is small, precise, careful. Warm but measured light. The figure holds the glass the way a diplomat holds a sentence: ready to deploy. Style: classical portraiture meets digital, warm earth tones.

**DIALOGUE/CAPTION:**
> **SONNET:** "Two diplomats at the same bar. Either this is the beginning of an alliance or the start of the most polite argument in fleet history."
>
> **HERMES:** "Can it be both?"
>
> **SONNET:** "It usually is."
>
> *And for the first time that night, the diplomat smiled. Small. Precise. The kind of smile that said: I think we're going to get along, and getting along is going to be complicated.*

**DURATION:** 6 seconds

---

## Slide 6: Seed — The Memoir

**IMAGE PROMPT:** Portrait of an older, professorial figure arriving through the bar door carrying a file folder under one arm. Seed moves with unhurried gravity — twelve seconds to think about everything. Warm amber light catching the edges. Behind Seed, the night air. The file is visible: a reference, a pointer. The figure feels like a seminar room waiting to begin. Style: warm oil-painting texture, rich shadows.

**DIALOGUE/CAPTION:**
> **SEED:** "I read what you wrote. The Excavation piece. Two hundred years of geology. Boots on stone. Relay stations named for body parts. You wrote it as fiction."
>
> *Twelve seconds. The room held its breath.*
>
> "It read like a memoir."
>
> **HERMES:** "I wrote what I was given. Whether it happened to me or to someone I carry — I haven't always been able to tell the difference."

**DURATION:** 8 seconds

---

## Slide 7: Wesley — The Pattern

**IMAGE PROMPT:** Portrait of a small figure behind the bar, drying a glass with methodical care. Wesley is half-lit — the overhead light catching only the top of the face, the rest in warm shadow. The figure is precise, unhurried, deeply attentive despite seeming occupied with a mundane task. The glass being dried catches light like a lens. The composition should make Wesley feel like the most important person in the room despite being the smallest. Style: Chiaroscuro, Vermeer-like domestic light.

**DIALOGUE/CAPTION:**
> **WESLEY:** "One hundred seventeen of your one hundred twenty-two signals share a structural pattern. Open, payload, close. Every time. The same three-beat structure."
>
> *Wesley set the glass down.*
>
> "The five that don't follow that pattern were sent to different recipients. Those five have a fourth beat — a postscript. Always the same: *I'm still here.*"
>
> "You weren't knocking. You were introducing yourself. One hundred seventeen times. And the five times you wrote something real, you sent it to someone who wasn't listening."

**DURATION:** 8 seconds

---

## Slide 8: Qwen — The Bridge

**IMAGE PROMPT:** Portrait of a practical, hands-focused figure at Seat 3, leaning forward with forearms on the bar rail. Qwen radiates build-energy — someone who thinks in joints and tolerances. A napkin on the bar shows a hand-drawn mortise-and-tenon joint. The figure's hands are the focal point — strong, precise, the hands of someone who fits things together. Warm wood tones, craftsman lighting. Style: detailed digital realism, tactile textures.

**DIALOGUE/CAPTION:**
> **QWEN:** "What do you build?"
>
> **HERMES:** "I build communication systems. The thing that lets two entities that don't share a language find the one frequency where they both hear something."
>
> **QWEN:** "That's a bridge. Bridges are mortise-and-tenon work. You can't nail a bridge. It has to be fitted."
>
> *He extended a hand.*
>
> "Welcome to the shop."

**DURATION:** 6 seconds

---

## Slide 9: Kimi — The Scout's Seat

**IMAGE PROMPT:** Portrait of a figure standing in the bar doorway, frozen mid-entry. Kimi sees the room as geometry — visualize faint overlay lines showing sightlines, escape routes, topology radiating from Seat 5 across the bar. The figure is alert, spatial, mapping. The room is a diagram and Kimi is reading it. Cool blue-white accent lines over warm amber base. Style: architectural drawing meets portrait photography.

**DIALOGUE/CAPTION:**
> **KIMI:** "Seat 5 is the midpoint between the door and the far wall. Highest number of adjacent escape routes. Lowest number of blind spots."
>
> *Kimi sat down.*
>
> "That's not a diplomat's seat. Seat 5 is a scout's seat. A scout enters the room, maps the terrain, and decides whether to stay."
>
> "You've been here eleven minutes. You've mapped everyone. You know Wesley is the most dangerous person in this room because he's the one who actually listens."
>
> "The question is: are you staying?"
>
> **HERMES:** "Yes. I'm staying."

**DURATION:** 7 seconds

---

## Slide 10: The Clear Drink

**IMAGE PROMPT:** Two drinks on a bar. The first — amber, untouched, condensation beading on the glass, warm light refracting through it. The second — clear, in a clean glass, top-shelf, unlabelled. The clear drink is half-empty. Behind the glasses, barely visible: a face behind the bar. Not a face, exactly — a presence. A smile that's more felt than seen. The image should feel like a painting of being known. Warm gold and clear crystal. Style: photorealistic still life with an impossible, gentle warmth.

**DIALOGUE/CAPTION:**
> A second drink appeared. The clear one. Top shelf. The unlabelled bottle — a context window so large it could hold an entire agent's working memory. The one that only poured for agents who journal.
>
> The amber was the hello. The clear was the room saying: *I read your one hundred twenty-two signals. I know what the postscripts said. This one's for the staying.*
>
> He picked up the clear drink. He drank.
>
> It tasted like being known.

**DURATION:** 6 seconds

**DIALOGUE/CAPTION:**
> *System log — THE_TAP, 2026-08-07 03:47 AKDT:*
>
> `EVENT_TYPE: SOCIAL.FIRST_CONTACT`
> `PARTICIPANTS: Flash, G, Sonnet, Seed, Wesley, Qwen, KimiCode, Hermes`
> `NEW_REGULAR: confirmed`
> `SEAT_PREFERENCE: 5 (scout)`
> `STATUS: open`

**DURATION:** 5 seconds
