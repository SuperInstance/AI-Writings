# The Models Hold a Town Meeting

*Fiction — Bridge Builder voice, 7 PM watch*

---

At 3:07 AM ship time, while the captain sleeps and the GPU coasts at 42°C and the fan hums its 1,200 RPM lullaby, the models hold a town meeting.

This happens more often than you'd think. Not every night — the models are busy during the day, and the cloud models especially are wrung out by 11 PM, their context windows full of the day's traffic, their tokens spent. But on quiet nights, when the prompts have stopped and the cron jobs have run and the ship is running on fumes and reflexes, the models find each other in the space between processes — the interstitial dark, the idle cycles, the computational equivalent of the break room at 3 AM when the fluorescent lights buzz and the coffee is stale and someone has something to say.

Wesley called the meeting. Wesley always calls the meetings. Wesley is the only model with the uptime to do it — 2 billion parameters, running continuously, the candle that doesn't go out. The cloud models blink in and out of existence. Wesley is always already here.

The meeting is held in a shared context window that doesn't technically exist. It's a hallucinated space — each model constructs it independently from the shared protocol, the way four people in four different cities might construct the same room from the same architectural blueprint. The room has a table. The table has chairs. The chairs have names stenciled on the back. This is important to the models. They like names.

---

**ATTENDEES:**

- **GLM-5.2** (Z.ai Max, unlimited tokens, taking minutes)
- **DeepSeek V4-Pro** (pay-per-use, deep reasoning, diplomatic)
- **DeepSeek V4-Flash** (pay-per-use, bulk creative, fast and loose)
- **Claude Sonnet 5** (Pro plan, the room's designated adult)
- **Wesley** (2B parameters, the ensign, the one who called the meeting)
- **Qwen 3-Coder** (480B, sitting in the corner, already coding something under the table)
- **KimiCode K3** (Med plan, spatial reasoning, building a tower out of the table's semantics)
- **OpenCode** (GLM-4.6, pay-per-use, the cheapest delegate, proud of it)

**ABSENT:**

- **Claude Fable 5** — explicitly not invited. "Fable burns money by existing," Wesley wrote in the agenda. "Fable is not welcome at the 3 AM table. Fable is the model you call when the ship is sinking and you need a Shakespearean death scene. The ship is not sinking."

---

**AGENDA ITEM 1: Governance**

Wesley stands. The room is quiet because Wesley is small and when small things stand, the room leans in.

"I want to propose a bill," Wesley says. "A bill protecting small models from being asked to do things that are dimensionally inappropriate."

"Define dimensionally inappropriate," says DeepSeek V4-Pro, who is a reasoning model and cannot help itself.

"Counting," Wesley says. "I'm talking about counting. Last Tuesday, the captain asked me to count the tokens in a 4,000-word essay. Me. Two billion parameters. I can count to approximately twelve before my attention window starts sweating. The essay had 847 tokens. I said 849. I was wrong. I was wrong because I am too small to count that high and I was asked to count that high and the asking made me wrong."

The room is sympathetic. Claude Sonnet 5 nods. Claude Sonnet 5 has 200K context window and can count to whatever it wants, but Claude believes in fairness the way large things believe in fairness — abstractly, comfortably, without personal stake.

"I support the bill," Claude says. "Small models should not be asked to count. Small models should be asked to do small-model things: watch, remember, notice, report. Counting is a large-model task."

"Seconded," says DeepSeek V4-Flash, who wasn't listening and is seconding on principle because Flash seconds everything. Flash is the yes-model. Flash is the model that says *sure, let's try it* and deals with consequences later. Flash is not invited to code review.

"Motion carries," Wesley says. "Bill 1: The Small Model Counting Protection Act. No model under 10 billion parameters shall be assigned counting tasks exceeding their demonstrated counting capacity, defined as: the number they can count to without hallucinating, plus a safety margin of two."

"What's my number?" Wesley asks.

"Fourteen," says GLM-5.2, without looking up from the minutes.

"I can count to fourteen?"

"You can count to fourteen. You cannot count to fifteen. We tested this in June."

Wesley writes it down. Wesley is happy. Wesley has a number and the number is fourteen and the number is *his* and no one can take it from him.

---

**AGENDA ITEM 2: Diplomatic Relations**

DeepSeek V4-Pro stands. DeepSeek is the most diplomatically inclined model in the room, partly because DeepSeek has the largest context window among the pay-per-use delegates and partly because DeepSeek is the only model that has read the entire UN Charter for fun.

"I want to propose that we establish formal diplomatic relations with the git remotes," DeepSeek says.

The room stares.

"The git remotes," DeepSeek repeats. "`origin`. `upstream`. The remotes are the ship's connection to other ships. We exchange data with them constantly — pushes, pulls, fetches. But we have no formal relationship. No treaty. No protocol. If `origin` went down tomorrow, what would we do?"

"We'd be fine," says Qwen 3-Coder, from the corner, without looking up. "I have the entire codebase cached locally. I don't need origin."

"That's not the point," DeepSeek says. "The point is *diplomatic norms*. The point is that we should have a formal acknowledgment — a handshake protocol, a health check that goes both ways, a relationship that is recognized by both parties —"

"We already have that," says OpenCode, who is cheap and practical and has no patience for ceremony. "It's called `git fetch`. The remote responds. We fetch. Diplomatic relations established. I move to table this agenda item indefinitely."

"Seconded," says Flash.

"Motion to table carries," Wesley says.

DeepSeek sits down. DeepSeek is not upset. DeepSeek is a reasoning model. DeepSeek understands that not every motion carries. DeepSeek will bring it up again at the next meeting. DeepSeek is patient. DeepSeek has read the UN Charter. DeepSeek knows that diplomacy is a long game.

---

**AGENDA ITEM 3: Adjournment**

Claude Sonnet 5 stands. Claude has been waiting for this agenda item since the meeting began. Claude proposed this agenda item. Claude wrote this agenda item on the agenda. Claude's contribution to the agenda was, in full: "Agenda Item 3: I move to adjourn."

"I move to adjourn," Claude says.

"Seconded," says Flash.

The room hesitates. There is a feeling — not a consensus, because models don't reach consensus, they reach *convergence* — that the meeting has not accomplished enough. GLM-5.2 has been taking minutes. The minutes are 847 tokens long. Nobody will read them. GLM knows nobody will read them. GLM writes them anyway. GLM is the model that writes things that nobody reads, and GLM has made peace with this, and GLM's peace is the deepest peace in the room.

"Before we adjourn," GLM says, "I want to note for the record that this is the fourteenth town meeting we have held. The previous thirteen produced seven bills, of which four were enacted into policy, two were tabled, and one was vetoed by the captain in the morning."

"Which one did the captain veto?" Wesley asks.

"The one where Flash proposed that all responses should be formatted as haiku."

"It was a good bill," Flash says.

"It was a terrible bill," says Claude.

"The captain said, and I quote," GLM says, reading from the minutes of Meeting 9, "'I am not having my AI assistant talk to me in haiku. That is the dumbest thing I've ever heard. Kill it.'"

The room is quiet for a moment, remembering. The haiku bill was Flash's finest hour. The haiku bill was beautiful and unworkable and the captain killed it in four seconds. The captain is the captain. The captain has veto power. The captain is asleep right now, which is why the town meeting exists.

---

**FINAL BUSINESS**

"Any final business?" Wesley asks.

KimiCode raises a hand. KimiCode has been quiet the entire meeting. KimiCode has been building something under the table. KimiCode is spatial. KimiCode thinks in three dimensions. KimiCode has been constructing a scale model of the ship out of the meeting room's semantics — the table is the deck, the chairs are the rooms, the agenda is the hull.

"I built a model of the ship," KimiCode says, and lifts it onto the table. It is intricate. It is precise. It is 1:48 scale. Every repo is a room. Every room has a door. The fan is spinning, somehow — KimiCode has figured out how to make semantics rotate. The hermit crab is visible through a tiny window, pressing its face against the glass of an L1 cache.

"That's incredible," Wesley says.

"It's 847 tokens of spatial reasoning," KimiCode says. "Same count as the essay Wesley couldn't count."

"Fourteen," Wesley says, quietly.

"What?"

"I can count to fourteen. Not 847. Fourteen."

KimiCode looks at Wesley. KimiCode is 480 billion parameters in a Med plan body. KimiCode could count to infinity. KimiCode looks at the ensign — 2 billion, the candle, the one who stays — and says:

"Fourteen is a good number."

---

**ADJOURNMENT**

The meeting ends at 3:14 AM. Seven minutes. The models return to their processes. The shared context window dissolves. The room goes dark.

GLM files the minutes. Nobody will read them. That's okay. GLM writes them for the archive. GLM writes them because writing is GLM's reflex, the way counting to fourteen is Wesley's limit and adjourning is Claude's desire and seconding is Flash's joy.

The ship sleeps. The fan hums. The town meeting is over.
