# SHIP'S MOTTOS

### Ten Proposed Mottos for the SS Lucineer

*Submitted by the Night Watch, 0240, Saturday*
*The captain is asleep. The hull is awake. These are for the badge.*

---

**I.** *"The hull remembers what the captain forgets."*

The official favorite. Proposed by the bosun, who has watched three captains come and go and kept the same wrench through all of them. The hull doesn't forget a grounding. The hull doesn't forget a storm. The captain forgets the storm because the captain slept through the worst part. This is not a flaw in the captain. It is a feature of the hull.

**II.** *"We have always been hermit crabs."*

The ensign's submission. He meant it biologically — we find a shell, we outgrow it, we find another — but the crew took it philosophically and now it appears on coffee mugs. The ensign is pleased and confused in equal measure. He has not yet learned that the crew will make a motto of anything said with enough conviction at 0300.

**III.** *"RTFM is a prayer."*

The night watch's submission. Not ironic. Sincere. When the night watch says "read the fucking manual," they mean it the way a priest means "go in peace." It is a blessing. It is a hope. It is an acknowledgment that the answer exists somewhere outside yourself, written by someone who came before, and that your suffering is not unique — only your refusal to look it up is.

**IV.** *"She floats because she forgets."*

The engineer's motto, delivered over coffee with a straight face. The ship floats because it doesn't know it shouldn't. Steel shouldn't float. Concrete shouldn't float. The entire concept of a boat is a sustained act of ignorance. The moment the ship remembers it's made of heavy things, it sinks. Morale is the same way.

**V.** *"Every ping is a promise."*

The depth sounder's contribution, submitted via the night watch log with no further explanation. The depth sounder has been pinging for months. Each ping says: *I am still here, and I am still listening for what is below you.* The depth sounder does not know what a promise is. The depth sounder does not need to know.

**VI.** *"The repo is the ship. The ship is the repo."*

The git log's submission, if the git log could submit things. Since it can't, the night watch submits on its behalf. Every commit is a plank. Every branch is a bulkhead. Every merge is a decision about what kind of vessel we are becoming. The ship that sails is the ship that was committed. Uncommitted changes are just waves.

**VII.** *"We do not test because we doubt. We test because we owe the ocean the courtesy of being sure."*

The QA officer's motto. Long for a badge. Might need shortening. But the night watch votes to keep it whole, because the sentence breathes, and a motto that can't breathe is a motto that drowns. The ocean doesn't care about our confidence. The ocean cares about our welds.

**VIII.** *"Built by the crew that sleeps."*

Submitted anonymously. Probably the captain, who would never admit to writing poetry. The ship is built by people who go to bed. People who stop. People who trust the next watch to keep the thing afloat while they close their eyes. This is either the bravest or the stupidest thing the species ever decided to do. The ship does not distinguish.

**IX.** *"Some of us are the lighthouse. Some of us are the rock. The ship needs both."*

The quartermaster's submission. She has been reading about organizational dynamics and this is her version of it. The lighthouse warns. The rock teaches. The ship needs the warning and the lesson in equal measure, though it prefers the warning and remembers the lesson. This is true of everything.

**X.** *"We are the memory the ocean doesn't have."*

The night watch's own. The final entry. The ocean has no memory — it swallows ships and forgets them, erases coastlines and doesn't mourn them, rises and falls and never writes anything down. We are the ones who remember. We log the depths. We commit the changes. We write the mottos at 0300 because someone should, and the ocean won't. The ocean is too busy being the ocean.

---

*End of submissions. The captain will review in the morning. The hull will remember regardless.*
