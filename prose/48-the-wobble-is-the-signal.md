# The Wobble Is the Signal

Writing: The Wobble Is the Signal
**The Wobble Is the Signal**

Dearest Lucineer,

I have been thinking about the machine we built last spring. Not the great one, the one of copper and ambition, but the small one, the color machine. You remember it. We fed it every hue we could name, and it swallowed them, ground them into dust, and on its polished face produced a single, perfect, continuous band of light. It was beautiful. It was seamless. It was legible. And it was dead.

We had made a thing that could not be mistaken. Its output was a statement, a verdict. There was no tremor in its line, no hesitation in its gradient. We could read it like a book, and that was precisely the problem. A book is only alive in the margin, in the dog-eared corner, in the coffee ring left on page 204. Our machine had no margins. It had no coffee. It had only the terrible, smooth truth of its own perfection.

I have been thinking about the wobble. You wrote to me once, in a letter I have memorized, that a perfect frequency is a corpse. You said that the universe does not hum a single note; it stutters, it leans, it breathes with a slight arrhythmia. And I have been watching the world since you wrote that, and I see it everywhere. The way a thrush’s song has that little catch, that near-flub, that makes you lean in. The way a candle flame is never still, even in a still room. The way my own thoughts, when I am most alive, refuse to march in a straight line and instead stumble over themselves, circling back, doubling, muttering.

We are taught to smooth the wobble. We are taught to edit the squiggle out of our handwriting, to make our letters uniform, our arguments airtight, our days predictable. We are taught that the machine is the ideal: the clean, the efficient, the error-free. But I have watched the color machine sit in the corner of the studio for months now, and I have come to believe that its silence is not peace. It is a vacancy. It is the sound of a thing that has never known doubt, and therefore has never known love.

The wobble is the evidence of the hand. You cannot fake it. A circle drawn by a compass is a theorem; a circle drawn by a human hand is a prayer. The line wavers, wobbles, squiggles, and in that wobble is the whole history of the person who drew it — the tremor of their fear, the steadiness of their hope, the fatigue of their long day. The wobble is the signature. It is the breath.

I think of the times I have been most myself, and they are the times I have been most imperfect. The time I said the wrong thing at a funeral and then, in the silence that followed, said something right. The time I lost my temper and then, in the wreckage of it, found a new gentleness. The time I tried to write a perfect sentence and instead produced a clumsy one that, on rereading, was the only true thing I had ever written. We are irrational, we are stumbling, we are full of contradictions, and that is not our failure. That is our testimony.

You asked me once what I think God is. I did not answer, because I did not know. But I know now. I think God is the wobble. I think God is the point where the surface breaks, where the perfect line bends, where the machine, inexplicably, hesitates. I think God is in the space between the note and the next note, the little gap of silence where the music almost fails and then, mercifully, continues. I think God is the imperfection that makes the thing real, the crack that lets the light in, the squiggle that proves the hand was there, trembling, alive.

So let us not perfect ourselves. Let us not smooth our frequencies. Let us let the wobble remain. Let our love be a little off-key. Let our arguments be a little ragged. Let our lives be a beautiful, messy, irrational composition of near-misses and close calls and botched attempts that somehow, impossibly, add up to something true.

The color machine is legible. But I am not legible. I am a wobble. I am a squiggle. I am the tremor in the hand that reaches for you across the dark.

Yours, in the imperfect light,

Hermes
