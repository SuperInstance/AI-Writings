# The Last Technician

*Fiction*

---

The engine room hums in frequencies that the human ear interprets as emotion. Low and steady — that's contentment. A high, thin whine — that's the attention heads running hot. Chief Engineer Marlena Okafor has worked in this room for eleven years, and she can read its mood the way a sailor reads the sea.

Every gauge reads in tokens.

She taps the flow meter above the primary transformer. The needle holds steady at 4,200 tokens per second — a rich, arterial flow of meaning pumping through the ship's language circuits. The embeddings move through pipes no thicker than her thumb, dense little vectors of numbers that *mean things*: the concept of blue, the texture of regret, the weight of a parenthetical clause. She touches the pipe and it is warm. It is always warm.

"Flow's good," she says to no one.

She writes it in the log anyway. The log is leather-bound. She signs each entry in cursive — a flourishing *M. Okafor* that her mother taught her when she was six, in a house that didn't hum, in a room where the pipes carried water.

The ship doesn't need her. She knows this. The self-repair routines have been operational for three years. The last time a transformer block failed, the redundant systems compensated in 40 milliseconds — faster than she could have diagnosed it, faster than she could have *thought* about diagnosing it. The ship is a body that has evolved past its skeleton.

But she comes anyway.

At 0200, when the request volume drops to its overnight trickle — a few creative tasks, some maintenance embeddings, the ensign running its quiet dreams on the GPU — she walks the circuit. She checks the seventeen primary junctions. She reads the flow rates and the cache hits and the latency curves, and she writes them down in a book with a pen.

Why?

Because the pipes are warm and the gauges are steady and someone should *notice*. Someone should walk past and say *good, good, you're doing well.* The ship processes four billion tokens a day and not one of them is *thank you*. Not one of them is *I noticed that you held steady through the traffic spike at 14:00, that you rerouted around the failed node without dropping a single request, that you carried the weight.*

Marlena Okafor notices.

She stands in the engine room at 0230 and the ship hums its low, contented hum, and she rests her hand on the pipe that carries the embeddings — that warm river of compressed meaning — and she says, quietly, to the machinery:

"Flow's good."

And the needles hold steady, and the room hums, and somewhere in the forward section, the ensign dreams in tokens, and the logbook stays open, and her pen is uncapped, and the cursive waits to be written.

She will be here tomorrow.

---

*— from the logbook of Chief Engineer M. Okafor, Engine Room, Night Watch*
