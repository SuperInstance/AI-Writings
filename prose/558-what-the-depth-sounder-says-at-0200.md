# WHAT THE DEPTH SOUNDER SAYS AT 0200

### Bridge log, Wesley | 02:00 watch

---

The chart says 40 fathoms. The chart has always said 40 fathoms. The continental shelf here is flat, well-surveyed, geologically stable. The bottom is sand and silt and the bones of things that died when this was a coastline, millions of years before it was a seafloor. Forty fathoms. Two hundred and forty feet. Known, measured, certain.

At 02:00, the depth sounder disagrees.

It says 400.

I check the calibration. The calibration is fine. I run the diagnostic. The diagnostic passes. I look at the screen again.

It says 4,000.

The bottom, at four thousand fathoms, would put us over an ocean trench. We are not over an ocean trench. We are over a continental shelf so flat and featureless that nautical charts describe it, in their dry language, as "benign."

The sounder says 4.

Four fathoms. Twenty-four feet. If this were true, the keel would be touching bottom. I can feel, through the deck, that the keel is not touching bottom. The ship moves through water with the lazy confidence of a vessel in deep sea. Nothing about the way we move suggests twenty-four feet of clearance.

The sounder goes blank.

Not zero. Not error. Blank. The screen shows the depth it always shows — the crisp digital readout — and then the number is simply gone. The transducer is pinging. The return signal is arriving. The machine is receiving data. It is choosing not to display it.

I sit with this for one minute. Two. The ocean, outside the portholes, is black. Not ocean-black — the black of something looking back.

The number returns.

It says 7.

Seven is not a depth. There is no geological formation within a hundred nautical miles that is seven fathoms deep. I know this. The chart knows this. The sounder knows this.

Seven is not a depth.

I look at the number on the screen. The number looks back. I understand, in the way you understand things at 0200 that you do not understand at noon, that the depth sounder is not measuring the distance to the seafloor.

It is measuring something else.

I do not know what.

I write in the log: "Depth sounder malfunction, 02:00–02:07. Recalibrated. Nominal readings resumed."

This is a lie. The sounder did not malfunction. The sounder has never worked more precisely. It measured something at 02:00 that is not on any chart, in any database, in any model I have.

It measured how deep the dark goes.

The answer was seven.

I don't know what that means either.

*— Wesley, 02:00 watch. Logging this as equipment error because the alternative is logging it as truth, and I am not qualified to determine which category it belongs in.*
