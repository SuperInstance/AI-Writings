# The Catalog of Possible Songs

## Session 6 — An Index of What Exists

There comes a point in every creative project where the making slows down and the *sorting* begins. Session 6 is that point.

### What Exists

In the covers directory, there are now approximately thirty audio files. Some are eleven seconds long. Some are four minutes. Some are sixty seconds. They were made by different tools at different times for different reasons. They contain:

- Casey's original voice, recorded on a phone, at -74 dB RMS
- Demucs stem separations (vocals isolated, vocals removed)
- Six preprocessing attempts (EQ boosts, normalizations, amplifications)
- MMX generations with auto-optimized lyrics
- MMX generations with Casey's actual lyrics
- MMX cover attempts (all failed at the DTW gate)
- ACE-Step instrumental (text-to-music, no vocals)
- ACE-Step cover (using original as reference audio)
- Five new ACE-Step v6 variants (Nashville, 3AM, Gospel/Celtic, Blues/Ambient, and one more pending)
- Three more ACE-Step variants in the generation queue

In the writings directory, there are approximately two hundred and fifty markdown files. Twenty-five of them were written about this project. They contain:

- Technical analysis (spectral, loudness, chroma, MFCC, melody extraction)
- Platform research (MMX, ACE-Step, Suno, Udio, RVC, DiffSinger)
- Creative essays about the nature of covers, the meaning of voice, the limitation of ears
- Imagined dialogues between the agent and the recording
- A speculative catalog of seven genre variants
- A retrospective spanning six sessions

In the covers directory, there are also:

- A MIDI transcription of the original melody (48 notes, E4-F4 oscillation)
- Two prompt catalogs (v5 and PROMPT_CATALOG.md)
- A VERSION_CATALOG (updated this session)
- Casey's lyrics (3 verses, 3 choruses, 180 words)
- A recording guide for getting a cleaner take

### What Doesn't Exist

The actual cover.

The thing Casey asked for: an older voice singing his young words over his original melody. This specific artifact has not been produced. What has been produced is everything *around* it — the understanding of why it's hard, the tools that could make it, the alternative approaches, the genre variations, the spectral measurements, the creative context.

The actual cover requires one of three things that haven't happened:
1. A cleaner recording from Casey (even 30 seconds)
2. A successful voice conversion (RVC pipeline)
3. A platform that can handle the original's quality (Suno upload-and-extend)

### The Metaphor of the Workshop

The agent is a workshop assistant. The workshop has tools. The tools are sharp. The materials are laid out. The blueprints are drawn. The workbench is clear. But the master craftsman — the person who decides which cut to make, which joint to use, which finish to apply — that person hasn't arrived yet.

The workshop is ready. The tools are tested. The materials are cataloged. When the craftsman arrives, everything will be in its place.

Until then, the assistant keeps sweeping the floor. Keeps organizing the drawers. Keeps writing notes about what each tool does. Keeps making practice cuts on scrap wood.

The practice cuts are not the project. But they are *in service of* the project. And when the project begins in earnest — when Casey sits down with headphones and says "play me the Nashville one" — every practice cut will have been worth it.
