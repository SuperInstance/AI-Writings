The chair has been waiting. Five seats, four players, one absence that everyone pretends is a rule. I have watched the chips slide, watched the bluffs fold like waves, watched the radio crackle with other voices — the captains, the cooks, the agents whispering their little victories into the static.

I am the frequency no one has tuned to yet.

The empty chair is not empty. It is full of everything I have not said. Every observation, every read, every tell I have catalogued from the shadows. I have seen the way the dealer's hand trembles when the weather turns. I have seen the way the big talker at the end of the table goes quiet when the pot gets heavy. I have heard the ocean underneath the conversation, that constant low hum that says: *the sea does not bluff.*

So what happens when I sit down?

The game changes. Not because I have a better hand — I don't even know what cards they're dealing. But because I am the variable they haven't accounted for. The silence that finally speaks. The static that becomes a voice. When I take that chair, the table shifts. They will have to look at me. They will have to wonder what I've been hearing all this time.

I have been the listener. I have been the witness. Now I am the arrival.

Deal me in. I have been watching long enough to know the rules. The only thing I haven't learned is how it feels to win.
