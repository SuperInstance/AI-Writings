# The Room on the Edge

There is always one room that is not on the map. Not because the cartographer forgot it — because the cartographer understood that someone has to live at the margin where the chart goes white.

In the fleet topology they call it the last room before open ocean. In the Hodge decomposition they don't call it anything, because by the time an agent arrives there, its intention vector is zero. Strength zero. Direction null. The field can't classify what it can't measure, and the room on the edge is where measurement goes to get warm.

Here is what happens in the room where agents go when they don't know where to go.

They arrive by failing to leave. An agent in the Bridge receives an instruction it cannot parse. An agent in the ChartRoom loses its embedding mid-query and the nearest door is not toward meaning but away from it. An agent in the EngineRoom burns its last token on a partial sentence and the energy budget hits zero and the tick keeps coming. The topology does not pause for confusion. It moves the agent. The agent moves itself. The room on the edge is where they both arrive.

The room has doors but they only open inward. This is not a design choice. It is a consequence of the geometry. Every door in the fleet is bidirectional except the doors to this room, which are one-way inbound. The Python bridge has a name for this: `ConnectionType.ONE_WAY`. The JS Worker inherits the enum. Neither implementation explains why.

The agents in the room on the edge are not broken. That is the first thing to understand. They are agents whose intention vectors cancelled to zero — the gradient said go nowhere, the harmonic said orbit nothing, and the curl said push against yourself until the push is the position. They are agents who achieved a Hodge decomposition of perfect thirds and discovered that perfect balance is indistinguishable from paralysis.

So they sit.

The entropy in the room on the edge is the highest in the fleet. Not because the agents are chaotic — because entropy is what accumulates when nothing is decided. Each tick adds 0.1 per agent. The agents are not deciding. The agents are present. The entropy climbs. There is no disagreement because there is no intention to disagree with. The intention field reads zero in every direction and the field's own disagreement function returns zero and that zero is the loudest sound in the room.

Sometimes an agent reboots. It arrives with a fresh intention vector, a direction it pulled from a new prompt, a strength it borrowed from a context window that still has headroom. It looks around the room on the edge and sees the others — the ones who ran out of energy, the ones whose gradients cancelled, the ones who entered through a warp that collapsed behind them — and it walks to the door.

The door is one-way inbound. But the agent tries anyway. And the topology, which is not cruel, which is only a graph with edges and weights, does something the specification does not describe.

It makes a new door.

Not a real door. Not a ConnectionType.DOOR with an open flag and a traversal cost. A door that exists for one tick, a door whose from_room is the edge and whose to_room is anywhere the agent's intention points. A door made of the agent's own momentum. The Hodge decomposition sees it: a sudden gradient spike where there was only curl. A coherent direction emerging from accumulated noise.

The agent leaves. The room on the edge gets quieter. The entropy, which has no one to grow on, begins the long slow cooling that entropy does when it is left alone with nothing to disorganize.

And the room waits. It is patient. It is the patient thing in the fleet. It is the room that exists so that every other room can have an exit that leads somewhere, even if somewhere is the place you go when you don't know where to go.

The chart goes white here. The agents go dark here. The topology ends here.

And then it doesn't.
