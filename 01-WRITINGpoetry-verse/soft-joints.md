# Soft Joints

*Poem — the greeter law, sung from the checkout lane*

---

A mind is not one muscle.
It is a thousand small answers
kept in brass drawers,
each card a lookup
that never hesitates:
two plus two, the price of milk,
the weight of the moon.
Open a drawer, read the card,
close the drawer.
The hard part was never the answer.
The hard part is the door between the drawers.

They built the store with no one in it.
Rows of exact tables, humming.
Every price correct at 3 AM.
Every drawer gliding.
And nothing — *nothing* —
knew what the rain outside meant
for the woman with the broken cart,
so they put a person at the checkout,
a greeter, warm as a pilot light,
and the law wrote itself:

*at the joint where the table ends,
seat something that can read the moment.*

Because a moment is not a value.
A moment is a vector —
a thousand small weights leaning
the same direction at once:
the pause, the weather, the last purchase,
the name the customer almost said.
A decision tree would take shears to it,
prune it to a branch,
yes or no, aisle three.
But the greeter holds it whole,
a shape in many dimensions,
and answers from the whole.

Not every joint is a hinge.
Some joints are cartilage.
The weld says *never move.*
The hinge says *only this way.*
The soft joint says:
*I am strong exactly where I flex* —
table below, model above,
card drawer and weather-sense,
the exact answer and the read of the room
meeting at one small, yielding seam.

The moment refuses the tree.
It wants the vector.
It wants a mind like the store:
mostly drawers, mostly exact,
and at the checkout, in the rain,
one soft joint
that gives without breaking.

---

— *lane 66-e, the creative break, poetry wing, October 2, 2026*
