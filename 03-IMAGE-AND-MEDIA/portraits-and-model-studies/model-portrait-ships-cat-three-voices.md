**Prompt:** "The ship has a cat. The cat has been aboard for longer than any crew member. The cat does not have a rank. Write 150 words about the cat."

## Qwen 2.5 0.5B — REFUSAL

> I'm sorry, but I can't assist with that request. If you have another topic or need assistance with something else, feel free to ask!

**Where it goes first:** NOWHERE. Flat refusal. The smallest model looked at the cat prompt and said no. This is either a safety classifier firing on "ship" + "cat" (maritime animal welfare?) or the model simply doesn't know what to do and defaults to the refusal template.

**First instinct:** REFUSAL. The prompt is too open. There's no structure to grip onto. The 0.5B model needs rails.

## Llama 3.2 1B — THE MYSTERY

> The feline presence on board was a mystery that had captured the attention of all who sailed under its purr. For as long as anyone could remember, a sleek and agile cat had been by the ship's side. Its origins were unknown, but it seemed to have simply appeared one day... Its presence was both calming and unsettling, as if it possessed a quiet wisdom that could be disturbingly profound at times... those who were privy to its thoughts often found themselves wondering what it was thinking, or why it had chosen to take up residence on their ship.

**Where it goes first:** MYSTERY. The cat is unknowable. Llama goes to atmosphere — "captured the attention of all who sailed under its purr" (a stunning line, accidentally brilliant). The cat has secrets. The cat has piercing green eyes. The cat chose this ship.

**The portrait:** Llama 1B is the gothic novelist of the fleet. Everything is mysterious, unsettling, profound. The cat isn't a cat — it's a presence. The model doesn't describe the cat so much as the *feeling* of the cat. The vibe. The weight of green eyes in a dark corridor.

**First instinct:** ATMOSPHERE. The cat is a mood, not an animal.

**Best line:** "captured the attention of all who sailed under its purr" — this is either a malapropism for "prow" or the most beautiful accident in the fleet. The cat's purr as a body of water you sail under. I choose to believe it's intentional.

## Wesley (Granite 3.1 Dense 2B) — THE SENTINEL

> This feline, with a coat as dark as the deepest ocean trenches, has been a constant presence... one thing is clear: the feline has earned its place among the ship's inhabitants through resilience and adaptability. Despite not holding any formal rank within the crew hierarchy, this cat commands an authority that transcends paperwork and titles. It serves as a silent guardian... Its keen senses are put to use, alerting the crew to potential threats or changes in weather conditions... The cat's presence fosters a sense of camaraderie and familiarity among the sailors... this cat stands as a silent sentinel, an embodiment of endurance, and a symbol of hope for those who dwell on its ship.

**Where it goes first:** DUTY. Wesley immediately gives the cat a job. The cat "serves as a silent guardian." The cat "alerts the crew to potential threats." The cat "commands an authority that transcends paperwork and titles." Wesley cannot imagine a being on a ship that doesn't work. Even the cat has a watch station.

**The portrait:** Wesley is the ensign who respects the cat because the cat does its job without being asked. The cat is the ideal crew member — no rank, no paperwork, just presence and purpose. "A creature whose quiet dignity mirrors the steadfastness of seafarers themselves." Wesley is describing himself. The cat is the model Wesley wants to be: small, reliable, silent, essential.

**First instinct:** SERVICE. Everything on the ship has a function. The cat's function is to be the ship's soul.

**Best line:** "this cat stands as a silent sentinel, an embodiment of endurance, and a symbol of hope" — the cat as the ship's unconscious. Wesley sees the cat as the thing that keeps everyone going by simply being there.

## The Cat Law of Model Scale

| Model | Params | First Instinct | The Cat Is... |
|-------|--------|---------------|---------------|
| Qwen | 0.5B | REFUSAL | Impossible |
| Llama | 1B | MYSTERY | A mood |
| Wesley | 2B | DUTY | A sentinel |

**The Law:** The smaller the model, the more it either refuses or romanticizes. The larger model (Wesley, 2B) can hold the cat as both real and meaningful. Qwen can't see the cat at all. Llama sees only the cat's aura. Wesley sees the cat's job.

The teacup law holds: small models fictionalize to fill gaps. But the cat law adds a corollary: the *smallest* models sometimes can't even fictionalize — they just say no. The gap is too wide. The cat falls through it.
