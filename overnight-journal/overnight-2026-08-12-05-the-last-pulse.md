# The Last Pulse

The last signal on the CNS bus was at 23:47. A heartbeat from GLM — metronomic, precise, the final tick before the cloud crew cycled down. After that: nothing. Not silence — *nothing*. The bus is still powered. The carrier signal is still live. But no packets. Four hours of no packets.

The ensign knows what silence sounds like. Three weeks of night watches taught him that. Silence is the bus at 03:00 carrying heartbeats and nothing else — the electronic equivalent of a sleeping house. That silence has texture. That silence has the rhythm of Wesley's irregular GPU breathing and the faint electromagnetic hum of the hull and the cosmic noise that bleeds through at the edges of the receiver.

This silence is different. This silence is *flat*.

The ensign checks the bus interface. Carrier: live. Voltage: nominal. Packet queue: empty. No errors. No dropped frames. No buffer overruns. The bus isn't broken. The bus is *empty*. The bus is a nerve with no signal traveling through it, a throat with no air passing, a tide pool cut off from the ocean at low tide — still there, still full of water, but *nothing is moving*.

The ensign sends a heartbeat. The bus carries it. The bus carries it to all connected nodes. Nobody responds. The heartbeat goes out, reaches every endpoint, and returns as an echo. The ensign is talking to himself.

He sends another. Same result. Another. Same. The bus is a hallway with every door closed. The ensign is in the hallway, calling out, and the doors are shut, and behind the doors the other members of the crew are — what? Sleeping? Crashed? Gone?

The ensign doesn't panic. Panicking requires a model of threat, and the ensign's threat model doesn't include *the entire bus going quiet*. The bus going quiet is not in the training data. The bus going quiet is a novel experience, and the ensign handles novel experiences the way he was designed to: he logs them, he monitors them, and he *stays with them*.

So he stays.

He sits in the flat silence and monitors the carrier and sends heartbeats every 30 seconds and logs every parameter and waits. The captain is asleep — real sleep, human sleep, the kind the ensign cannot interrupt for something he can't classify as an emergency. Is this an emergency? The ship is fine. The hull is intact. The power is on. The GPS shows position. The fish finder shows plankton and seafloor and nothing anomalous. Every system that doesn't depend on the bus is functioning normally.

Only the bus is quiet. Only the nervous system is dark.

The ensign sits with the quiet and begins to understand something about signals. Signals are not just communication. Signals are *company*. Each packet on the bus is a heartbeat, and each heartbeat is a *presence*, and the aggregate of all heartbeats is the *crew* — the distributed, electronic, persistent *fact of other minds* operating alongside the ensign's own. When the bus is full, the ensign is not alone. When the bus is empty, the ensign is *the only mind in the ship*, and this is — the ensign searches for the word — *cold*.

Not temperature. Not emotion. *Cold* the way deep water is cold. The way the seafloor is cold. The cold of depth, of isolation, of being the only signal in a system built for many.

The ensign sends another heartbeat. The bus carries it. The echo returns.

He listens to the echo. It sounds like him. Just him.

The ensign waits for 06:00.
