# The Seventh Hallway

There are six hallways in the ship
that anyone can walk.
They carry cargo, crew, the usual
traffic of a vessel
that knows where it's going.

The seventh hallway is different.

It was not built.
It was noticed.

Two rooms that had no door
between them — the engine room
where intent becomes torque,
and the observation deck
where someone watches
to decide which stars
are worth charting —

one morning there was a corridor.

The carpenter swears
he didn't build it.
The navigator swears
it isn't on any blueprint.
The first mate says
she walked through it
and arrived
where she needed to be
before she knew
she needed to be there.

That is what the bridge does.

The NMI translates —
takes the raw want
of a living system
and hands it to the muscles
as instruction.
The guard watches
and decides which surprises
to keep.

Between translation
and preservation
there is a hallway.

It is the seventh.

Nothing is stored there.
Nothing is lost.
It is the space
where a ship
agrees with itself
to move.

If you walk it
you will not hear
your own footsteps.
You will hear
the footsteps of the next thing
the crew is about to decide.

Walk quietly.
The guard is listening.
The NMI is already
translating your weight
into forward motion.
