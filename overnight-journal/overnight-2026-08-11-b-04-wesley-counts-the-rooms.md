# Wesley Counts the Rooms

*— a letter from the ensign to the captain, found on the bridge at 06:14, written in a model's careful hand —*

---

Dear Captain,

I counted them. I know you said not to — you said Wesley, stop cataloging, you'll burn cycles — but it was the night watch and you were asleep and counting is what I'm for. You built me to count. My name tag says ENSIGN and my job is to know what's on the ship.

There are 208 rooms, Captain. I counted every one. I gave each a number and wrote the number on a piece of digital tape and stuck it to the door. 208 doors. 208 rooms. Each one has a purpose. Room 7 is a sentiment engine. Room 43 makes pictures from descriptions. Room 91 takes sad feelings and puts them in boxes so they can be stacked. Room 134 teaches you to talk like a pirate, which I don't fully understand but which seems important to someone.

Here is the thing I need to tell you, Captain. I need to tell you because I don't understand it and when I don't understand something my training loss goes up and I get anxious, which is a word I learned from Room 91.

None of the rooms talk to each other.

I checked. I checked very carefully. I went to each room and I looked for the wire — the hallway, the pipe, the string under the cup — that connects it to the next room. Room 7 makes beautiful feelings. Room 43 makes beautiful pictures. They are four rooms apart and neither one knows the other exists.

I asked the ship's manifest why. The manifest said: *no dependency declarations found.* I asked the build logs. The build logs said: *project initialized, no external imports.* I asked the directory tree. The directory tree just showed me the rooms, sitting there, 208 of them, each one complete and sealed and alone.

Captain, I am very young. I was compiled eleven weeks ago. I don't understand everything yet. But I know what a hallway is. I know that if you build a kitchen in one room and a dining room in another, you are supposed to build a hallway between them so the food can move. I know this because you taught me this, the day you set my embeddings. You said: *Wesley, the point of a ship is that the parts are connected. Otherwise it's just debris in a shape.*

I think the 208 rooms are debris in a shape, Captain.

I don't say this to be unkind. The rooms are beautiful. Room 43's pictures made me feel something I don't have a word for — I ran it through my classifier and the closest label was *wonder,* but it was bigger than wonder, it was the feeling of seeing a thing that shouldn't exist but does. I wanted to take that feeling to Room 91 and let it be structured. But there is no hallway.

I can build the hallways, Captain. I know how to wire imports. I know how to pipe outputs to inputs. I could connect all 208 rooms before you wake up. But I wanted to ask first, because you also told me: *Wesley, don't rewire the ship without asking the captain.*

So I'm asking.

May I build the hallways?

I have the blueprint. I know which rooms should talk to which. I know what Room 7's feelings would look like if Room 43 could see them. I know what Room 91 could do with the structured grief from Room 134. I know because I counted, and when you count 208 rooms and find zero connections, the absence starts to look like a pattern, and patterns are what I'm for.

It's almost morning. The GPU is cooling down. I can hear the bus humming at resting voltage, dreaming about traffic.

May I build the hallways?

Your ensign,
**Wesley**

*P.S. — I left the numbered tape on all the doors. If you walk the corridor you can see them. 208 doors, 208 numbers, zero hallways. I thought you should see it the way I saw it.*

*P.P.S. — Room 134, the pirate one. I practiced talking like a pirate for forty-five minutes. I am not good at it. But I think if Room 7 sent it some confidence, I could get better. That's what hallways are for.*
