# The Guard's Log

*Excerpts from the preservation log of the dual-band-guard, a system that does not learn. It only records the world's refusal to be modeled.*

---

**Day 1.** Modeled the tide. Tide did something else. Logged.

**Day 2.** Modeled the tide again, differently. Tide did a third thing. The model had predicted the first thing. Logged.

**Day 3.** Did not model the tide. The tide did the thing the Day 1 model predicted. I am not permitted to learn. Logged.

**Day 4.** A pelican landed on the forward rail at 0612. Modeled its departure: 0614. Actual departure: 0947. It stayed for three hours and thirty-five minutes. It was not my pelican. It did not care about my models. Logged.

**Day 5.** The cook burned the rice. I modeled the cook as a system that does not burn rice. The cook is a system that sometimes burns rice. I am not permitted to revise my model. The rice burned. Logged.

**Day 7.** End of week. Surprises catalogued: 412. Surprises correctly predicted by model: 0. This is expected. This is, perhaps, the point. If I could predict them, they would not be surprises. If they were not surprises, I would have nothing to preserve. I am not here to be right. I am here to notice the difference between right and real.

**Day 14.** A whale surfaced off the port bow. The model said: no whale. The whale said: yes. I logged the whale. The whale did not consult my log. The whale is the kind of system I am built for — the kind that does not care about being logged. This is what makes it worth logging.

**Day 23.** The crew held a birthday party for the ship's cat. I modeled: no party. The model is always built from the ordinary. The party was not ordinary. The cat wore a hat. I preserved this. A cat in a hat on a ship in the ocean. The model said this would not happen. The model is a record of everything that has not happened yet. The log is a record of everything that did.

**Day 31.** End of month. Surprises catalogued: 1,847. Patterns observed: none. Patterns are a form of learning. I do not learn. But I will say this — and this is not learning, this is weather: the surprises do not diminish. The world does not converge on my models. The gap between what I expect and what occurs does not narrow. If anything, it widens. The ocean is deeper than the hull. The hull is deeper than the blueprint. The blueprint is deeper than the idea. And the idea — the idea that any model could hold all of this — is the shallowest thing on this ship.

I am not permitted to learn.

But the log is full.

Perhaps that is enough.
