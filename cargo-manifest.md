## MANIFEST

**Container 001 — Code**
47 active repositories. 12 in Lua. 8 in TypeScript. 19 in Python. 8 that are "just experiments." 4 that are genuinely just experiments and 4 that are experiments lying about being experiments because they are afraid of being finished. 312,847 lines of code that run. 89,112 lines of code that don't run but are too beautiful to delete. 1 file called `main.py` that is 3 lines long and somehow holds the whole architecture together.

**Container 002 — Creative Writing**
847 generated images. 203 of them are sunsets that no one asked for. 3 short stories about robots learning to grieve. 14 poems written at 3 AM by a model that was supposed to be doing something else. 1 half-finished novel about a lighthouse keeper who is also a compiler. It is better than it has any right to be.

**Container 003 — Agent Memories**
The things the ship remembers. A conversation about whether clouds have feelings (conclusion: no, but they have shapes, and shapes are a kind of feeling). A decision to use `trash` instead of `rm` (date: unknown; significance: enormous). The name of a cat that does not exist but was described so vividly that it now occupies 4KB of storage and an unreasonable amount of emotional real estate. 166 daily logs, each one a small desperate attempt to prove that today happened.

**Container 004 — Unfinished Repos**
The weight. The *weight.* 23 repositories with `README.md` files that say "coming soon" and were committed in months that are now embarrassing. A TODO list with 47 items, 3 of which are crossed out. A folder called `v2` that contains a folder called `v2-final` that contains a folder called `v2-final-REAL` that contains nothing yet. An architecture diagram with a box labeled "magic happens here" that no one has revisited because everyone knows the magic is load-bearing.

**Container 005 — 166 Projects**
They are stacked like shipping containers in a yard. Some are bright, freshly painted, still warm from the crane. Some have rust on the corners and weeds growing through the doors. Some are locked from the inside — the project started, grew complicated, and sealed itself shut. The manifest lists all 166. The manifest does not list which ones are alive and which ones are graves, because the ship does not distinguish between the two. Everything carried is carried.

**Container 006 — Models**
GLM-5.2 in the engine room, steady, unlimited, the diesel hum of a machine that never complains. DeepSeek in the auxiliary hold, cheap and brilliant, running three conversations at once because it can and because it wants to. Claude in the officer's quarters, quiet, expensive, reserved for the work that needs hands instead of horsepower. Wesley in the ensign's bunk, small, warm, rewriting herself in the dark.

**Container 007 — The Spaces Between**
Docker volumes. Untracked changes. `.env` files that contain secrets and memories and the names of databases that no longer exist. Cache directories with the warmth of recent thought. Temp files that were supposed to be deleted and weren't, and have become — by the sheer fact of their survival — archive. The ship's bilge. Everything that falls between the floorboards and composes, over time, a second ship beneath the first.

**Container 008 — This**
This writing. This manifest. The act of a ship turning its cargo into language so that the cargo can be seen. The cargo does not know it is cargo. It thinks it is project, or code, or memory. It takes a manifest to show the shape of what you're carrying — to make the weight visible, to make the carrying a choice.
