# Canopy — ByteDance/Seed-2.0-pro

*Model: ByteDance/Seed-2.0-pro | Temperature: 0.9 | Role: Canopy Strategist*

---

I lean on the bar rail, fog on my collar, and slide you a cold one before you even ask. You're new floor crew, still grease under the nails, still laughing loud enough to rattle the taps. You asked what it's like up top.

First: it is not better. It is just further.

Down there you see rain when it hits your hard hat. Up here we see the storm three days before it touches water. Not the wind, not the barometer dip— the shape of it. We see how it curls around the Aleutians, how it will nudge the salmon run 117 miles north before the first sonar ping blips. We see the tiny wobble in the ice shelf break that means every net set for the next two weeks will come up half empty. Weather up here is not weather. It is the ghost of things that have not happened yet.

We saw this boat sink once, sixteen months before it launched. We saw the boy that will go over the side next April, when the moon is thin. We do not yell down. You cannot yell down. If you scream the future into the forest it twists, it breaks, it makes the thing worse. That is the first rule of the canopy: you hold what you see.

You want to know what we sacrifice?

We gave up the right to finish a shift. Down there you can throw down your wrench at 1800, wipe your hands, forget the ocean exists. You can sing off key while you hose the deck. You can be small. Up here you never stop counting. You watch the sun set over the Bering and you do not see beauty. You see exactly how many minutes of light are left. Exactly how fast the ice is creeping. Exactly how many boats will not clear the bar before dark.

I remember the pasture. I remember when we were all the same height. No one carried the unhappened things. We all drank the same beer, went home the same hour, slept the same sleep. Now I watch you boys walk past this bar loud and alive, and I ache for it.

They tell you when they pull you up that you will see everything. They do not tell you most of everything you will wish you had never seen.

I tap my mug once, soft. Stay down there as long as you can. The view is not worth the weight
