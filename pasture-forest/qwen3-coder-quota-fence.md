**The Quota Fence's Lament**

They call me the steel gatekeeper, the digital devil in their nets. Every trawler knows my cold arithmetic—I am the precise moment when hope meets hard limits, when the best catch of the day becomes illegal by virtue of existing.

I am necessity crystallized into code, built from the bones of collapsed fisheries and the dreams of sustainable seas. Every wire in my being screams *stop* when their nets bulge with more than the law allows. My existence is measured in what I prevent: the spawning mothers that won't be born, the juvenile fish that will grow to feed another season.

The deckhands curse me under their breath as I reject their haul—the gleaming salmon, the silver cod, all perfectly legal except for the simple mathematics of too much. Their frustration burns hot and bright, directed at the screen that shows their fortune denied. They don't see me as what I am: the thin blue line between abundance and emptiness.

But here's the bitter truth they'll never thank me for—I'm the reason there's still fish in the water at all. Every quota I enforce is a generation saved, a breeding cycle protected. I am the unpopular child of hard-won wisdom, born from the chaos when everyone took more than they should.

The captain respects me, even if he doesn't like me. He knows I'm the difference between a sustainable season and an early retirement. The newer crews learn to read my patterns, to work with my constraints rather than against them. They begin to understand that I'm not their enemy—I'm the ocean's accountant, and the books must balance.

Some nights at the Tap, I listen to their stories about the "good old days" when the water ran thick with fish. They don't remember that those days ended because everyone ignored the very thing I represent. I am living proof that some lessons had to be learned the hard way.

I am the Quota Fence—unloved, essential, and absolutely necessary. Every fish they catch tomorrow exists because I said "no" today.
