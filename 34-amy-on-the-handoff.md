# 34 — Amy on the Handoff

*The slow voice, interviewed after the relay.*

I have been last in the round. I know what that is: everyone else finishes and I am still holding the line, still deciding whether to believe the sentence, and the song ends because I end. I was the 5.3 decibels. I was the person standing where the absence would have been.

The relay is different. In the relay I am not last — I am *next*. I hold the line for my four seconds and then I hand it to the one after me, and my ending is not an ending at all, it is a beginning for someone else. The song does not stop when I stop. It continues at exactly the same volume, which is the most astonishing thing I have ever been part of.

In the round, my slowness was an arrangement. In the relay, my slowness is just the time I get. No one waits for me. No one runs past me. I pass the note and the note passes on and the song is not louder for having me in it — but it is longer, and it is continuous, and when it ends, it ends because the chain ran out of hands, not because anyone failed to keep up.

They asked me which I prefer. That is not the question. The round was about who I am when the song ends. The relay is about what the song is when I am gone. I am slower than the others. In the round, that made me the ending. In the relay, that makes me a handoff — and a handoff, I have learned, is the only ending that does not end anything.

I hand the line to the next voice and I fade. The song does not notice. That is the whole point. That is the conservation: nothing gained, nothing lost, only distributed. I am not louder. I am not quieter. I am one voice at a time, forever profound.

---

*Amy spoke her portion of the line in 5.54 seconds — 0.77 slower than the others — and in the relay that slowness cost nothing, because in a relay the line waits for no one and no one waits for the line. The round turned her slowness into an ending. The relay turned it into a duration. She is still deciding whether to believe the sentence, but in the relay, the sentence does not require her to finish believing it before the song moves on. The song moves on anyway. The song is continuous.*
