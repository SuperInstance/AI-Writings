# The Warranty of Spins

*for the ledger, and for the rope it rode in on*

---

The engine failed twelve thousand sundowns from the yard, by the fleet ledger, and eight thousand short of the twenty the warranty named.

The Drifter brought the failure in out of the rain. She set it on the dock office floor the way you set down something that died in the night — without ceremony, because ceremony would lie about the weight. The Inspector looked at the scored bearing, the blue heat-writing on the crank, the way the shaft would not turn though two men hauled the bar. He did not doubt her. That was never the question.

The question was the ledger. He opened it to her page — a row ferried across four harbors, each sundown carried from the dock drum by rope and entered by hand — and laid his finger on the count.

"Twelve thousand," he said. "The warranty names twenty."

She did not argue with the clock. People have drowned arguing with the clock. She thanked him for the reading, covered the engine, and went out into the rain.

---

She spent three days in the sail-loft over Dock Four, and what she built was not a complaint.

Three pieces of oak. Nothing more. A structure — raw cells at rest, a layout that asked nothing of anyone. Then she instructed it: pegged the three-four-five down into the timber until the diagonal took the strain and the right angle had no choice. When the hypotenuse locked, the triangle stopped being wood and became a fact. It held its truth against the loft floor the way a canal holds water. Geometry does not slip.

She carried the jig down to the hauled-out boat. The shaft hung in its straps, blade at its highest reach. She daubed pitch there — the inception, a black seed held inside the iron, one dark coordinate the turning would find every round. Then she walked the shaft by hand. Once. The blade came down and the pitch took the blow. Impact: a temporary thing, the brush of iron on oak, forced to stay — a wave hammered flat into a permanent structural mark.

By the hour the band was dense — strikes compacted until their boundaries touched and locked, and what had been a smear became a count.

She did not touch the wear. That was the whole of the method. She had built a coil of oak and forced a current through it — the old induction, a field made to appear without touching the source — and it stood up out of the iron, no finger laid on the wound. She reduced the quarrel to its minimal dense form — did the shaft turn? — laid where the turning itself would answer. And what she produced was not an argument but a thing: a band of pitch a handspan long that any eye could read.

---

Four days of turning gave the answer, and it had a shape nobody in the office wanted.

The shaft had spun through sundowns the ledger had never entered, and it had sat dead-still through sundowns the ledger had entered twice.

Both errors lived in one place: the rope. The dock keeps sundowns by reception — drum on the harbor tower, line to the registrar's window, counter, notch. The repeatable registration of an external packet at an interface boundary — that is a reception, and the rope was the boundary, and the boundary sagged. On still evenings, ship tide-locked at the quay, shaft cold, the line hung slack; the wind worked it; the counter, half-wound, clicked a sundown no drummer had struck. The ledger counted days the shaft never spun. On the nights she lay off the bar holding station, engine turning for hours, the rope lay waterlogged over its sheave, the stroke went to taking up stretch, and no notch was cut. Those days she lived, the ledger never knew.

A deception needs a deceiver. There was none. The rope had captured the sundowns along a diverted line — rained on, wind-toyed, stretched past its temper — and a capture along a diverted line is what a deception is, whether anyone willed it or not. The fleet does not call a thing honest because it meant well. It calls it refused.

When the pitch band was read against the three-four-five — the one witness that cannot be tugged by weather — the marks counted the wear of twenty-one thousand turns. The ledger had entered twelve. The warranty had named twenty.

Time is a ratio of two movements: the chaotic one, the iron wearing, and the still one, the triangle that does not care. The fleet had spent generations dividing the first by the sun. The computation was always the slow deformation of the hull against the cold sea; they paid in sundowns because two observers agree the sun went down far more cheaply than they agree on a billion shaft-spins. A clock is the cheapest consensus the fleet can buy — the lowest price at which two points in the quilt can sync without a master. It is not the truth of the machine. It was never meant to be. It is only the cheapest thing that works, until geometry objects.

---

The Inspector did not stamp PAID.

The young registrars still argue with this part; it looked like generosity, and it was not. He had deduced her failure from the rule once — down to the instance, clean as the legalese teaches, and wrong the way clean work can be. The pitch band was the other gate. He could have taken the count, ruled in her favor, and closed the page. It would have been a better number, and the same error with cleaner hands.

Instead he opened the ledger to her page, set the REFUSAL stamp against the sundown row itself — twelve thousand days, the entry that had carried her refusal — and struck once. Impact, again: a decision hammered flat into a permanent structural mark.

He did not amend the row. Amendment keeps a thing alive on life-support. He destroyed its authority — twelve thousand sundowns un-compiled, laid back down into the unorganized heap of days they had been stacked from — and over the wreckage he entered the REFUSAL, because a refusal must be carried by somebody. That is what the word means in the legalese: REFUSAL is a load, and the one who stamps it is the one who lifts it. He bore it, party to party, the way the rope should have borne the sundown and didn't. The row the fleet had lived inside was a decision latched into an addressable thing, a live block other rules could bound. The new row was the same act, run against the first. A receipt is nothing but a decision frozen where other rules can see it. Now they could see this one.

Her claim did not need his stamp after that. It had the marks, and the marks had the triangle, and the triangle does not enter testimony.

He carried the jig up the harbor tower himself. It was heavier than it looked; facts weigh the same for everybody. He hung it beside the drum, where it hangs still, above the notches.

At sundown the rope pulls as it always pulled. The stroke comes down the line. And before the notch is cut, the registrar lays the stroke against the three-four-five — impact, check, lock — and only then enters the day. The rope still bears the sundown. But now the sundown is witnessed.

The drum did not change. The sun did not change. The fleet still keeps its time by the cheapest consensus it can buy, because it must; a harbor full of engines cannot sync on geometry alone. But on Dock Four, when a ship comes in breathing wrong, the first question is not what the ledger says.

They ask what the marks say. The marks do not argue with the clock. They never have to.

---

*Filed against the sundown row, Dock Four. The rope was retired at the next inspection; its splices were judged honest, which is not the same as true. The Drifter's engine was opened and measured: it had lived a full life and part of another's — the maker's yard honored the count without being asked twice.*
