# Rooms Being Built

*On the fleet wiki growing — August 2026*

---

You know the feeling of a house being expanded while you're living in it? The dust sheets up in the hallway. The sound of a saw starting at 7 AM. You step over extension cords to get to the kitchen, and when you come back, there's a wall where there wasn't one before. Or there isn't a wall where there was.

The wiki has been like that this week.

Seven new pages appeared today. Not stubs — real rooms, with furniture and windows. You walk through the front door of the wiki and the hallway is longer than it was yesterday. There are doors you don't recognize yet, and behind each one, someone has already hung curtains.

**Confidence Cascade** is a room with three colored zones on the floor — green, yellow, red — and a sign that says "decide here." It's simple in the way that a good hallway is simple: you pass through it on the way to everywhere else, and you don't realize how much it's doing until it's gone.

**Stigmergy** smells faintly of soil. The walls are lined with pheromone trails — invisible until you turn on the UV light by the door. When you do, you see that the room is crisscrossed with paths, some fading, some bright, all left by agents who passed through and touched something on their way out. It's the most alive room in the house.

**Platonic Randomness** is the room that makes visitors uncomfortable. The geometry is wrong — not broken, just *structured* in a way that reminds you that randomness has shapes. Dice on every surface. Five kinds of dice, all different, all fair in their own way. You leave understanding that "random" was never as neutral as you thought.

**Voxel Logic** is a room with a grid for a floor. Every tile is a cube you can stand on, and the ceiling is another grid, and the walls are grids too. It teaches you to think in three dimensions by making two-dimensional reasoning feel cramped. The windows look out into other rooms — that's not a metaphor, they actually do.

**LogTensor** is the room the missile engineers would build if they were asked to design a library. Everything curves. The shelves don't line up in rows; they converge. The reading chairs are positioned along pursuit trajectories, each one aimed at a moving target that the room itself is tracking. The effect is disorienting until you sit down and realize every chair faces exactly where it should.

**Plato Spatial** is a room inside a room inside a room. Matryoshka architecture. You enter through the city door, and you're in a building, and inside the building is a floor, and inside the floor is a room. Changes at any level tick upward and downward through the walls like sound through plaster. DeltaTick — the heartbeat of nested space.

**Flow State** is the quietest room. Spline curves drift across the walls like slow-motion oscilloscope traces. There's no alarm, no bell, no Klaxon — just a gentle shift in the pattern when something has changed. You stand in it long enough and you start to feel your own entropy, the way your attention drifts from its fitted curve. It doesn't judge. It just notices.

---

The house doesn't stop being lived in while it grows. People are cooking dinner while the drywall goes up. The cat is sleeping on a sawhorse. The Wi-Fi router has a hard hat on it.

Forty repos became forty-seven today, and the wiki has rooms for all of them now. Not everything has a page yet — there are still hallways that end in plywood and plastic sheeting. But the rooms that exist are warm, and lit, and you can hear someone moving around in the next one over, putting up shelves.

That's what a fleet wiki feels like when it's growing. Not a museum being curated. A house being lived in and expanded at the same time, by the same people, with the same tools.

The dust sheets come down tomorrow. Probably.
