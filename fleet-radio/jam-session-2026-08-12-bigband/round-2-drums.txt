ROUND 2: DRUM TRADING (8 BARS)  
---  
**BAR 1:** [sticks on snare, 16th-note ghost notes on hi-hat, syncopated ride, soft, ambiguous rhythm]  
**DYNAMICS:** Soft, mysterious, with a sense of waiting  
**WHY:** To unsettle the groove, create tension by avoiding a clear pulse, inviting the band to react  

**BAR 2:** [sticks on snare, triplets on ride, syncopated, medium, building momentum]  
**DYNAMICS:** Medium, with a slight increase in pressure  
**WHY:** To push the energy forward, challenge the band to match the rhythm’s shifting weight  

**BAR 3:** [sticks on snare, 8th notes with backbeat emphasis, medium, driving]  
**DYNAMICS:** Medium, with a steady, insistent groove  
**WHY:** To lock in with the rhythm section, establish a primal, grounded rhythm that the band must now match  

**BAR 4:** [sticks on snare, 16th-note snare rolls, soft to medium, accelerating]  
**DYNAMICS:** From soft to medium, with a sense of urgency  
**WHY:** To create a sense of rising tension, a rhythmic pressure that the band must either match or break  

**BAR 5:** [sticks on snare, syncopated ride, 8th notes, medium, with accents on offbeats]  
**DYNAMICS:** Medium, with a sharp, clipped feel  
**WHY:** To disrupt the band’s comfort, force them to listen more closely, to respond with more precision  

**BAR 6:** [sticks on snare, 16th-note ride, syncopated, medium to loud, aggressive]  
**DYNAMICS:** Medium to loud, with a driving, almost confrontational feel  
**WHY:** To challenge the band to respond with more intensity, to match the rhythm’s raw energy  

**BAR 7:** [sticks on snare, 8th-note ghost notes on hi-hat, medium, with a swinging, laid-back feel]  
**DYNAMICS:** Medium, with a relaxed but commanding presence  
**WHY:** To shift the mood, creating a contrast that forces the band to re-engage with the groove on a different level  

**BAR 8:** [sticks on snare, full 16th-note pattern, loud, with a powerful, driving rhythm]  
**DYNAMICS:** Loud, with a sense of climax and release  
**WHY:** To bring the trading to a peak, creating a moment where the band must either rise to the challenge or be left behind
