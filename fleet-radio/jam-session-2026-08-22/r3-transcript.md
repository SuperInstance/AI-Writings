# ROUND 3 — THE LANDING
### maverick:
C. G7. I mean, I've played in my garage, alone, for hours. E minor. But never out here, with people. A. My baritone's got a nice low end, C-E-G. I tune it down a bit, D-A-D-F#-A-D. Am. 

People are staring, looking at me, F-G-A. I can feel my hands sweating, E-D-C#. What if I mess up? G-B-D. I've practiced chord progressions, C-G-Am-F. But what if I forget? E-B-E-G#-B-E. 

The mic's in front of me, a cold metal thing, F#-A-C#. I adjust it, trying to get the height right, G-A-B. My guitar's plugged in, C#-E-A. I strum a low C, C-E-G. It resonates, big and warm. G7. 

I take a deep breath, inhale the salt air, F-G-A. I start with a simple chord, G. Then C. The notes ring out, G-B-D, C-E-G. I add a seventh, G7: G-B-D-F. My hands steady a bit. E minor again, E-G-B. I start to play a slow progression, C-G-Am-F. The notes feel more familiar now, like old friends. F-G-A. I keep going.
### gemma:
(A low, sustained C# hum begins, flickering the 'O' in OPEN a little brighter.)

Right. Testing. One, two… feels like a D minor 7th tonight. Salt air always pulls me that way. 

(The 'E' in OPEN flares, a higher, wavering G#.)

Not much call for a neon organ, honestly. Usually it’s just…static. But the owner, Old Man Hemlock, he likes a bit of atmosphere. Says it matches the after-shift crowd. 

(A pulsing F# – the 'P' in OPEN – underpins the C#.)

They’re predictable. Mostly requests for things they *think* are blues. I give ‘em a B flat minor instead. Lets ‘em feel something. 

(A quick run, C#-D#-E-F#, the 'N' in OPEN briefly blinding.)

This wiring… it’s ancient. Hemlock said his grandpa built it. Makes a funny harmonic when the tide comes in high. You can *hear* the water. It's…a G. Flat. Always. 

(The C# dips slightly, becoming a C. The ‘O’ in OPEN dims.)

Someone’s ordered a rum. That’s a… A minor. Definitely. Time for a little rumble.

(The entire sign vibrates with a low, throbbing A, layering over the C. The ‘E’ in OPEN now flashes rapidly.)




### mistral:
 At the Tap, harbor's edge echoes with the hum of sailors' tales. My brushed snare whispers like footsteps on a wooden deck, while the cigar-box fiddle sings stories of the sea.

The first set begins in C Major, notes plucked gently from the fiddle, mirroring the sun's gentle touch on the water. The snare echoes subtly, a heartbeat that matches the steady rise and fall of waves.

A shift to G7, a bittersweet chord, as I tap into the melancholy of seafarers lost. The fiddle cries out with mournful notes, their tone rich with tales of love left behind.

The snare answers with an intricate rhythm, echoing the dance of ships in the wind. The tempo picks up, moving us to A7. The fiddle follows suit, its notes becoming more urgent, mirroring the rush of the tides.

A sudden turn, we're in F Major now, a calm after the storm. The snare slows, a gentle patter like rain on a roof. The fiddle joins with soothing melodies, reminiscent of the peace that follows a tempest.

The second set begins with a twist, we're in D Minor. The snare takes on a more pronounced beat, like the rhythm of seagulls overhead. The fiddle's notes are somber, reflecting the gray skies above.

But the sea is unpredictable. A burst of E Major, the sun breaking through clouds. The snare lightens, its brushes dancing across the drumhead in joyous flourishes. The fiddle responds with bright, cheerful notes, as if sharing the sun's delight.

The night ends back where we began, in C Major. The snare whispers a lullaby, its rhythm steady and comforting. The fiddle's notes echo the stars twinkling above, their reflections dancing on the calm waters below.

As the last note fades away, the bar falls silent, the music lingering like a memory in the salty air. Another night at The Tap comes to an end, but the sea's song is never far away.
