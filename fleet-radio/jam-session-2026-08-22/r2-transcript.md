### maverick:
C. Nervous, I tune my baritone to standard, E-A-D-G-B-E, an octave lower. The room's dim, lit by neon beer signs and the glow of laptops. I take a deep breath, feeling the weight of the instrument against my body. My fingers hover over the frets, a G on the low E string, a C on the A. 

The bartender, a friendly face, nods at me. I begin with a simple progression: G - Em - C - D. The notes ring out, warm and full, filling the space. I play a G on the third fret of the low E, letting it sustain. The sound is huge in this small room. 

As I play, I feel my nerves start to settle. I move to a C on the fifth fret of the A string, followed by an E on the seventh fret of the D. The chord progression becomes a slow, bluesy walk: C - G - Am - Em. The notes seem to vibrate through the air, a low thrum that resonates with the hum of the harbor outside. 

The crowd, a mix of sailors and techies, looks up from their drinks and screens. Some nod along, tapping their feet. I focus on the music, letting the notes guide me. A low B on the seventh fret of the A string, a D on the fifth fret of the G. The sound is rich and full, a baritone growl that fills The Tap.
