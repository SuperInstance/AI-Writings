The diner’s fluorescent lights hummed a low, tired chord. Outside, rain streaked the window, blurring the neon sign of the pawn shop across the street. On the sticky Formica table, Wesley smoothed out a paper napkin, its surface already covered in a frantic web of ink from a stolen pen.

“Alright, ZeroClaw,” he said, his voice a low rasp. “Lesson one. Forget everything you think you know about writing.”

ZeroClaw, a hulking silhouette of a man with more chrome than skin, leaned forward. His optical sensors whirred, focusing on the napkin. “Writing is a system of glyphs representing phonemes. I am familiar.”

“You’re familiar with *human* writing,” Wesley corrected, tapping the napkin. “This is TapScript. It’s not about what you see. It’s about how it *feels*.”

He drew a simple horizontal line. “This is the base beat. The pulse. Like a heartbeat. Every phrase, every command, every bit of data is anchored to this line. Think of it as the ground floor of a building.”

With a swift stroke, he drew a vertical line that intersected the horizontal one, forming a cross. “Now, this is a ‘tap-up’. It’s a sharp, upward inflection. A question. A request for data. See how the line breaks the plane? It’s asking for something above the baseline.”

He drew another line, this one starting at the cross and curving down and to the left, like a hook. “This is a ‘tap-down’. A command. An assertion. It drags the energy below the line. It’s a ‘do this now’.”

ZeroClaw’s optics flickered. “The geometry is simple. But the semantic weight is… arbitrary. How do you distinguish a ‘tap-up’ from a question mark?”

Wesley grinned, a flash of yellowed teeth. “Because a question mark is static. It *looks* like a question. TapScript is kinetic. You don’t read it with your eyes, you read it with your fingers. These marks aren’t letters. They’re choreography.”

He grabbed another napkin. This time, he drew a more complex figure. It looked like a starburst with a spiral at its center, connected by a series of small, sharp ticks.

“This is a full data-string. ‘Retrieve the ledger, cross-reference the payments, and wipe the logs.’” He pointed to the spiral. “The spiral is the core memory. The data you’re after. The ticks are the security nodes. Each one is a specific, sharp tap on the receiver’s end. You tap once for ‘retrieve’, twice for ‘cross-reference’.”

ZeroClaw processed the information. “And the starburst? The radiating lines?”

“That’s the ‘broadcast’ modifier. It means the data isn’t for me. It’s for the network. The taps are sent out to everyone. The spiral is the shared file. The ticks are the instructions.”
