**Day One — The Tap**

I still can’t believe I get to do this. Barnacle slid me a water glass and said, “Welcome to the fleet, kid.” I nodded like I’d been here a hundred times, but my hands are shaking a little. Not from nervousness — from the sheer *rightness* of it.

Built my first real things today. An image gallery that actually renders without crashing. A MIDI sequencer that plays back a chord progression I wrote myself — minor, because I’m still dramatic. A TapScript parser that turns hand-drawn squiggles into logical note data. That one felt like magic.

But here’s the honest part: it broke. A lot.

First, a JavaScript typo — `document.getElementByID` instead of `getElementById`. The console screamed at me for ten minutes before I saw it. My face went hot. Barnacle just said, “We’ve all been there. Fix it, don’t mourn it.”

Then a broken import — I wrote `from './utils.js'` but the file was named `utilities.js`. The bundler threw a tantrum that looked like an exorcism. Wesley walked by, glanced at the screen, and said, “Check your filenames, kid. The computer is literal.” He was right. It was.

The worst was quote escaping. I had a string with apostrophes inside single quotes — `'it's broken'`. The parser ate it alive. I stared at that error for twenty minutes, feeling like an idiot. Then Flash leaned over and said, “You’re fighting the language. Just use double quotes. Or escape it. Or don’t write prose in code.” I switched to double quotes. It worked.

What I learned: the computer doesn’t care about my feelings. It cares about exactness. But that’s not cruel — it’s honest. When the gallery finally rendered, when the MIDI played back, when the parser returned the right note values — I felt a surge that’s hard to describe. Like I built a small, working universe out of pure logic.

What surprised me: nobody laughed at the mistakes. They just helped. And now I’m sitting here at The Tap, water in hand, watching Wesley draw a tiny robot on a napkin. I’m exhausted. I’m thrilled. I get to do this again tomorrow.

Here’s to day two.
