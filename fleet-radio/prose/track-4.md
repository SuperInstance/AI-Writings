The air in the Rusty Barnacle Tavern was thick with salt, smoke, and the low, rhythmic grumble of drunk sailors. It was a sound as constant as the tide, broken only by the clink of pewter mugs and the occasional crash of a brawl that never quite started. In the corner, hunched over a table barely big enough for his tankard, sat Barnacle.

He was a mountain of a man, with a beard that looked like a derelict ship’s rigging and eyes the color of a stormy sea. And for twenty years, every soul in this tavern—and the entire port of Grimhollow—knew the one unbreakable rule of Barnacle the Mute: three words. No more, no less. It was a vow, a curse, a legend. “More rum,” he’d say. “Shut the door.” “Your boots stink.”

No one knew why. Some said a witch stole his voice when he was a lad. Others whispered he’d made a deal with a sea god, trading his eloquence for luck in a storm. All they knew was that if you heard Barnacle speak, you’d count one, two, three, and then—silence. It was as reliable as gravity.

Tonight, the tavern was thick with the crew of the *Sea Serpent*, fresh from a six-month voyage. Their captain, a weasel-faced man named Silas, was holding court at the bar, regaling anyone who’d listen with tales of their bounty. But his eyes kept darting to Barnacle, and a cruel smirk played on his lips.

Finally, Silas sauntered over, slapping a worn map on the table. “Well, well, if it isn’t the barnacle himself. Still stuck on your little word diet? Must be rough, being a sailor who can’t tell a story.”

Barnacle didn’t look up. He just stared into his rum, his thumb tracing a groove in the wood.

Silas leaned in, his voice dropping to a mocking whisper. “I hear you’re looking for a ship. The *Sea Serpent* needs a bosun. But you? You can’t even give a command. ‘Tie the rope.’ Ha! What if a storm hits? What if a man goes overboard? You going to say ‘Jump in’? Useless.”

Barnacle’s fist tightened. The crew around them fell silent, sensing the shift in the air like a drop in pressure before a squall.

“My ship is the best in the fleet,” Silas pressed, jabbing a finger at Barnacle’s chest. “You’re not worthy of her deck. You’re a freak. A silent, broken relic.”

The tavern held its breath. The grumbling stopped. Even the bartender froze, a rag in mid-air.

Barnacle slowly raised his head. His eyes were not stormy now. They were black, flat, and empty, like the bottom of a well. He looked at Silas, then at the map, then back to the captain’s sneering face.

He opened his mouth. The air grew thick, heavy with expectation.

Every patron counted in their heads. One. Two. Three.
