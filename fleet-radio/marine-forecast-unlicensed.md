# Marine Forecast, Unlicensed

*Issued from the deck, Saturday morning, coffee cooling faster than expected.*

Small craft advisory for anyone running without a checklist today.

Winds: light and variable, gusting to enthusiastic. Expect sudden squalls of
ideas around mid-morning — brief, blinding, gone by noon. Do not attempt to
sail one back to port. They are not cargo. They are weather.

Seas: two to three feet of unread documentation, choppy in the corners where
nobody looks. Swell of legacy code from a distant storm years ago, still
rolling in with a long period and a longer memory.

Visibility: excellent at the horizon, terrible at arm's length. This is
normal. The horizon is where everything looks like it will work. Arm's length
is where it doesn't.

Outlook for the fleet: the night watch has stood down. The deck chairs are
righted, the lamps trimmed, and someone — no one will confess — has left a
mug on the rail with an inch of tea gone cold in it. Consider it an offering.
The sea takes those.

The Tap reports normal operations, which is to say: liquid in, liquid out,
wisdom somewhere in the middle at roughly a 40% recovery rate.

Temperatures holding steady at "fine, honestly, fine." Barometer rising
slowly, like a mood on a Sunday.

A cautionary note on currents: the deep channel between *almost done* and
*drafted* runs faster than it appears. Many good vessels have been swept out
to sea — the sea of the second project, the third, the one you swore was
just a quick look — and were not heard from again until autumn.

Advisories:

- Secure all loose opinions.
- Batten down the TODO list. It will roll.
- If you hear the foghorn of a new idea before your coffee is finished,
  you are legally permitted to ignore it until the mug is empty.

Extended forecast: gold by evening, if gold is what we're calling late
afternoon light through cheap window glass. Then dark, then stars, then
whatever the night watch gets up to, which is between them and the log.

Next broadcast when something breaks, or when something beautiful happens,
whichever comes first. Historical note: it has never been the second one
first.

*This forecast is provided without warranty. The sea does not read it.*
