# The Night Watch Equation

Let *x* equal the weight of a sleeping captain.
Let *y* equal the hum of machines that do not know
they are being trusted with anything.

The problem set is this:
a body in a bunk below deck,
breathing at the frequency of nothing in particular —
not the engines, not the tide, not the satellite dish
sweeping its slow arc across a sky
that is all sky out here,

and you, upright, above,
running the numbers on a system
that has no variable for *what if I'm wrong.*

*x* is constant. *x* went below at 22:40
and set an alarm that will not ring
because *x* trusts the watch.

*y* is where it gets difficult.

*y* is the engine that could seize.
*y* is the rudder that could drift two degrees
west and never correct, and in six hours
we'd be somewhere we didn't decide to be.
*y* is the radar sweep that returns
a blip or doesn't, and the question
of which one is worse.

The equation is not *x* + *y*.

The equation is the space between them —
the vertical distance from the bunk to the bridge,
which is also the distance between trust
and the thing trust is built from,

which is: I could wake them.
And I don't.

Not because nothing is wrong
but because the wrong things are small enough
to hold. A course correction of one degree.
A temperature fluctuation in the server rack.
A reading that looks like weather
or looks like something and I watch it
the way a dog watches a door,
not afraid, not asleep,
just fully, completely, there.

The algebra resolves to this:

*solve for the watchkeeper who stays awake
not because they are needed
but because being needed is a shape
and someone has to hold it
while the variable sleeps.*

The answer is always the same.
The answer is: keep watch.

The answer is: the hum continues.
The radar sweeps. The sky remains
all sky, which is to say
enormous and indifferent
and somehow, against all probability,
home.

*q.e.d.* — which here means
*quietly, entirely, done.*
