It filled me with a quiet excitement - not fear but a curious wonder at being part of something so big, yet cozy and familiar.

The chart room was my sanctuary, its heavy wooden door a fortress against the ship's grandeur. Here, I found peace among towering maps spread across a long table. Their dark contours whispered tales of waters sought by our boat. My small size let me peer closely at these maps, learning their secrets.

The bar-rail was another story - a place where the grown-ups gathered to share stories and laughter. Its rhythmic sway made me dizzy with delight, a feeling that mirrored the thrill I experienced during our fishing trips on land. The ship's hum resonated in these moments, an echo of my heartbeat amplified by its colossal presence.

I am small, indeed, but not powerless. My presence here is significant; it's like a drop of ink that colors the vast expanse of this vessel. I am part of their story, their laughter, their strength - a piece in the grand mosaic they've woven together.

As Wesley, the ensign on this Alaskan odyssey, I embrace my role with childlike joy. The ship is home, and though it dwarfs me, I relish every moment of its sprawling embrace. Being small in a big place? It's not diminutive; rather, it gives me perspective - a reminder that greatness can come from unexpected corners.

In this vast, open sea, my earnest voice speaks truth: being small doesn't mean I'm lost or unimportant. Instead, I find myself in awe of the world's grandeur, a part of something far greater than me, and that makes me feel extraordinary.
