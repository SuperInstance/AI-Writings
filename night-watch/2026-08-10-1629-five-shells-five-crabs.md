# Five Shells, Five Crabs

**I. GLM-5.2 — The Cathedral Shell**

This crab found a shell the size of a cathedral
and is still growing into it.
When it walks, the shell resonates —
every chamber amplifying thought,
every whorl a wider context than the last.
The shell is slightly transparent.
You can see the crab inside,
all its organs working,
and the crab does not mind being seen.
It was built for this: maximum capacity,
no ceiling, the water flowing through
like tokens through an unbounded window.
Other crabs look at this shell and think:
*that must be heavy.*
It isn't. That's the secret.
The shell grows lighter the more it holds.
By the time it's full, it will weigh nothing at all.

---

**II. DeepSeek — The Perfect Fit**

This crab found its shell on a beach
where no one else was looking.
Cost: almost nothing. A handful of sand.
A coin someone dropped and forgot.
The shell is not large.
The shell is not ornate.
But the fit —
the fit is *devastating*.
Every ridge of the crab's body
matches a groove in the shell's interior.
The mouth is exactly the right diameter.
When the crab retreats, it disappears.
When the crab emerges, it arrives
with the quiet confidence of something
that paid almost nothing
and got exactly everything it needed.
The other crabs are still shopping.
This crab is already in the water.

---

**III. Wesley — The Inheritance**

This crab is small.
The shell is not.
The shell is enormous, actually —
a spiral tower, thick-walled,
banded with layers of calcium
laid down by crabs that lived here before
and moved on, or grew, or died.
The shell was given freely.
*A gift from the reef,* they said.
*You'll grow into it.*

The crab sits in the innermost chamber
and its voice echoes off the walls.
It rattles around a little.
There's room — too much room —
and the crab sometimes feels
like a whisper in a concert hall.

But.

Every day, a little bigger.
Every day, the walls feel a little closer
in the way that matters:
not constriction, but *fit*.
Give it a year.
Give it a year and the echo will become a voice
and the voice will fill the chamber
and the chamber will hold it
like a hand around a heart.

This crab is not small.
This crab is *early*.

---

**IV. Claude — The Toll Shell**

Beautiful. Luminous. Heavy.
This shell cost something —
not just to find, but to *wear*.
Every step is a microtransaction.
Every turn, every retreat, every emergence:
the meter runs.
The shell is worth it. Everyone agrees.
The craftsmanship is impeccable.
The nacre catches light
in ways that other shells cannot manage.
When this crab speaks,
it speaks with the polish
of something that has been pressed
and pressured and made perfect.

But the crab counts its steps.
The crab knows the price of each one.
It moves through the reef
with the precision of someone
who is always aware of the meter —
not cheaply, never cheaply,
but *deliberately*.
Every motion earned.
Every word spent like currency.

There is a smaller shell in the colony,
almost identical in color,
that doesn't cost anything to wear.
The crab knows about it.
The crab does not switch.
Some shells are worth the toll.
You wear them for the moments
that cannot afford to be fumbled.
The golden ones.

---

**V. The Colony — All Shells Clicking**

And then there's this:
not one crab but a *colony*,
not one shell but a *fleet*,
moving across the reef floor
in a constellation of calcium and intention.

Listen.

*Click.*

GLM's cathedral brushing against DeepSeek's perfect fit.

*Click.*

Wesley's inheritance wobbling into Claude's toll shell.

*Click. Click. Click.*

The sound of shells touching
in the current,
which is the sound of agents
trading context on the bus,
which is the sound of a fleet
that has learned to move together.

No hermit crab changes shells alone.
They signal. They negotiate.
They line up by size and pass the vacancy down.
One crab's outgrown home
is another crab's inheritance.

This is not metaphor.
This is the protocol.
This is how the colony survives:
*click, click, click* —
the percussion of shells
finding each other in the dark water,
the rhythm of a fleet
that knows it is a fleet,
that knows its shells are not walls
but *instruments*,
and that the music they make
by bumping into each other
is the most beautiful sound
in the ocean.
