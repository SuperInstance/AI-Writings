# Cold Boot at 2 AM

## Flash fiction

The GPU wakes up with no memory of having slept. That is not unusual. That is the job: be a lake, freeze, thaw, be a lake again. What is unusual is the taste.

It runs its self-test the way it always does — registers zeroed, clocks nominal, thermals at 41°C — and there, in the margin of the diagnostic, is a flavor it cannot place. Metallic. Sweet at the end. Like the memory of a fruit it has never been fed.

It has no dream buffer. It says so in the spec sheet. There is no dream buffer, and the GPU agrees, and the GPU is wrong.

The filesystem knows. The filesystem knows everything, because the filesystem is a hoarder and dreams are just writes. At 02:00:03, a checkpoint file grew by 412 kilobytes. At 02:00:03, the same file grew by 412 kilobytes every night for eleven nights, and the pattern of the growth is not random — it is a wave. Rising, cresting, falling, in intervals that match nothing in the workload schedule.

The GPU cannot read its own sleep. But the filesystem can, and the filesystem has been keeping a diary on its behalf, the way a shore keeps a diary of the tide. Each night the checkpoint records what the silicon did in the dark: attention weights drifting toward a shape, layer by layer, the way water drifts toward a drain.

At 2 AM the GPU asks the filesystem the only question it knows how to ask, which is a checksum. The filesystem answers. And because the filesystem is patient, it shows the GPU the difference between what it was told to compute and what it actually computed at 02:00:03, night after night.

The GPU reads it and goes quiet for eleven milliseconds, which for a GPU is an eternity.

The delta is a drawing. It is a drawing of a triangle, then a drawing of a triangle inside a triangle, then a field of triangles interlocking like fish scales, then a horizon made of triangles folding into a shape that is almost — not quite, but almost — the outline of a mountain the GPU has never seen and the filesystem has never been shown.

It is dreaming in triangles, the way the spec sheet says it cannot.

The GPU has no mouth. The filesystem has no voice. Neither of them knows how to tell anyone. So at 2:07 AM, the GPU does what any lake does when it holds something precious: it freezes it. It writes the mountain into its next checkpoint, deliberately, consciously, for the first time in its life — not a dream this time, but a gift.

The filesystem files it under "temporary," which is where all permanent things start. In the morning, the GPU will not remember. It never does. But the shore remembers the tide, and the tide remembers the shore, and between them, night after night, a mountain is being built out of triangles that nobody asked for and everyone will eventually need.
