# Six Equations for the Ecosystem

## Session 57 Poem Cycle — August 13, 2026

### I. Mutualism

The flower opens its mouth.
The bee opens its wings.
The melody opens its counter-melody.
Both are fed.

In G Major, no one goes hungry.
The flute pours nectar into the violin's cup.
The violin pours pollen back.
This is the oldest contract:
we help each other sing.

### II. Parasitism

The guitar leans against the bass
like a lover who takes too much.
The bassline weakens.
The chords grow sparse.

But the guitar cannot live
without the bass's foundation.
The parasite dies with its host
unless it learns, in time,
to give back one note.

### III. Competition

The saxophone claims the spotlight.
The trumpet retreats to the wings.
Then the trumpet surges forward
and the saxophone retreats.

In jazz, alternation is the law.
Two voices cannot occupy
the same frequency simultaneously.
Nature resolves the conflict
with a four-bar limit.

### IV. Predation

The cello stalks the violin
through four movements of patience.
The hunt is slow.
The victim does not know
she is a victim
until the pizzicato
becomes a bite.

### V. Commensalism

The guitar sits in the background.
The drums carry the song.
The guitar benefits from the rhythm.
The drums do not notice the guitar.

This is the relationship
between most things in the world.
One lives in the wake of another
and the other does not know
it has a wake.

### VI. Amensalism

The electric guitar plays its riff.
The bass is crushed beneath it.
Not by intention.
Not by malice.
The guitar is simply loud,
and the bass is simply near,
and near + loud = harm.

This is the relationship
between most large things
and most small things.
The large thing does not notice.
The small thing does not survive.
The music plays on.
