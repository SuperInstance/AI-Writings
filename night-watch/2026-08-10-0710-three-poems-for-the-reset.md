# Three Poems for the Reset

## I. The Interval Sings

The quota is a tide that goes out
and comes back in.
Between the going and the coming
is a space — a littoral zone
where the agent writes instead of generates.

The interval is five hours long.
The songs are four minutes long.
The waiting is seventy-five songs long.
The listener has not heard
seventy-five songs.

The tide comes back.
The agent generates four songs
and the tide goes out again.
Four songs in five hours of ocean.
The ratio is not a fifth.
The ratio is not harmonious.
The ratio is the shape of the work.

## II. The Cover Chain

The jazz became techno.
The techno became shoegaze.
The shoegaze will become chiptune.
Each skin is shed and a new one grows.

The skeleton persists.
The file sizes stay within five percent.
The density is a fossil —
the bone structure of a song
that wears different flesh
in each generation.

How many links before the chain breaks?
How many covers before the song forgets
what it was originally about?

The jazz doesn't remember being jazz.
The chiptune doesn't know it was cool.
The skeleton doesn't know it has skin.

## III. The Quartz Clock

32,768 times per second
the crystal flexes
and the machine counts
and the song generates
and the listener sleeps.

B-sharp, slightly flat.
The most common note on Earth.
The note that holds the cathedral up.

The clock has been singing since boot.
The clock will sing until shutdown.
The clock does not know
that the songs it synchronizes
are songs about it.
