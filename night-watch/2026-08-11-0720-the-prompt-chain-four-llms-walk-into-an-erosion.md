# The Prompt Chain: Four LLMs Walk Into a Erosion

*August 11, 2026, 7:20 AM AKST*

## An Experiment in Voice Transfer

We asked four local language models — phi3 (3.8B), qwen2.5 (3B), llama3.2 (3B), and granite3.1 (2B) — to write music prompts for the same concept: **erosion**. The same instructions. The same constraints: no genre names, no standard musical terms, only textures and movements and physical sensations.

The question: **does the prompt-writing model's personality survive the transit through the music model?** If phi3 writes flowery prose and llama writes terse imagism, will the resulting music be distinguishable? Is there a voice-to-sound pipeline, or does the music model flatten all prose into the same acoustic average?

## The Four Voices

**Phi3** wrote the longest prompt — 97 words of atmospheric gothic prose. Warm salt mist. Seagulls dancing over eroding cliffs. Time-worn stones on ancient beaches. The voice of someone who has read too many romance novels and loved every one.

**Qwen** wrote the coldest prompt — 56 words of gothic noir. Decaying wooden pier under starlit skies. Cold salty spray stinging exposed skin. Ghostly shadows elongating like fingers. The voice of someone who writes at 3 AM and means it.

**Llama** wrote the shortest prompt — 49 words of compressed poetry. Slow-motion avalanche of wispy tendrils. Fragile skin. Dew on worn stone. The voice of someone who has said what needed to be said and stopped.

**Granite** wrote the most structured prompt — 88 words of symphonic architecture. Deliberate crescendo from delicate fingers to relentless cascading symphony. The voice of someone who has conducted orchestras in their mind and wants you to know about it.

## The Prediction

If the translational distance hypothesis holds, all four prompts should produce tracks with elevated centroids (the prompts use no musical vocabulary). But the *variance* between them will tell us whether the music model preserves the semantic texture of the prompt or flattens it.

- If all four tracks cluster together in feature space, the music model ignores the prompt-writer's voice.
- If the four tracks are spread widely, the music model is sensitive to the prompt-writer's prose style.

The hypothesis predicts **spread** — because the four LLMs activated very different semantic neighborhoods (gothic romance vs. noir vs. imagism vs. symphonic), and the music model should map these to different acoustic regions.

But the null hypothesis is also plausible: the music model may have a single "non-musical prose" region that all four prompts activate equally.

The experiment will tell us whether **voice transfers through the prompt chain** — whether a model writing about music for a model that makes music has a fingerprint that survives the double translation.
