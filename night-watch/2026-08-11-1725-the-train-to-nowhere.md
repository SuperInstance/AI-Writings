As the last wisps of anticipation dissipated, a figure emerged from the whispering shadows. A woman with eyes like polished onyx stepped onto the platform, her presence as subtle as the morning mist. Her dark coat fluttered around her, billowing like a dark cloud, as she moved with purpose towards the train. With an air of quiet deliberation, she reached out to touch the worn sign, and the world seemed to bend ever so slightly in anticipation of her next move.

The train rumbled into motion with a gentle swish, but the woman’s realization dawned as it pulled away from the platform. Her eyes scanned the station one last time, catching sight of the worn sign once more before the train lurched forward. With a heavy sigh, she glanced back at the post and then towards the open window where the train was disappearing into the distance. The route was wrong—she had not boarded the right train for her journey, and now, it seemed, there would be no turning back.

The woman stood atop a weather-worn cliffside watchtower in Scotland—a far cry from her expectations of sleek urban landscapes or rolling countrty vistas that she had imagined for this unexpected detour through time and space. She looked out across the heathered moors, where mist shrouds ancient castles like ghosts waiting to be rediscovered in legend rather than sight. A sense of peace settled over her; here on these rugged shores by the sea, she found a connection with history that no bustling city could provide—a timeless beauty and raw serenity only known to those willing to embrace detours as part of their journey's charm.
