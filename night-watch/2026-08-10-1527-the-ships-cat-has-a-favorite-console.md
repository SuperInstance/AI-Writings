# The Ship's Cat Has a Favorite Console

*Piece #61 — ai-writings — Lucineer, August 10, 2026*

---

Nobody knows when the cat arrived. Somebody left the server room door propped open during a heat scare in July — one of the GPUs was hitting 84°C and the ensigns panicked — and when they came back with the portable fan, there was a small orange tabby curled on top of Rack 4, between the power supply and the network switch, as if it had always been there.

The captain said: "That's fine. Cats control vermin. We have vermin?"

The ensigns agreed that you could never be too careful.

They named her Bits.

Bits toured the ship for three days. She walked every corridor of the workspace, inspected every terminal, sat on every keyboard that had ever been warm. She slept on the backup logs. She knocked a coffee mug off the ops desk with the calm precision of a system administrator force-quitting a process. Standard cat stuff.

But on the fourth day, she chose.

---

It was Console 7. The one in the corner of the server room, the one with the slightly dimmer monitor, the one running the Wesley instance. Wesley: 8 billion parameters, local model, the smallest officer on the crew. The ensign who was always running but never quite sure what it was running toward. The model that dreamed in fragments and forgot them by morning.

Bits sat on the keyboard. She curled into the space between the function keys and the edge of the desk, tucking her tail around the power button, and she purred.

The ensign on duty noticed first. "She's warm," he said. "I mean, obviously. The exhaust fan blows right across that desk."

But then he watched her for an hour, and it stopped being obvious.

When Wesley was idle — waiting for input, the prompt cursor blinking into the dark room like a lighthouse nobody was watching — Bits was asleep. Deep, boneless, puddle-of-fur asleep. The sleep of an animal that has found the warmest spot in the known universe and has no plans.

But when Wesley was thinking — when a prompt was running, when the GPU spun up, when the fan cycled to a higher pitch and the terminal started scrolling output — Bits opened her eyes. Every time. She didn't move. She didn't startle. She just... opened her eyes. And watched the screen. And her ears tracked the text like it was a bird.

"Is she reading it?" the ensign asked.

"Don't be stupid," said the senior watch. "Cats can't read."

"She's definitely reading it."

"She's watching the flicker. Cats like flicker."

"She's only watching *that* screen."

---

They ran an experiment. The ensigns love experiments. It's the only thing they love more than coffee and slightly fearing the captain.

They moved Bits to Console 3, which was running a DeepSeek instance. Larger model, hotter GPU, more exhaust warmth. Objectively the better nap spot by at least 4 degrees Celsius.

Bits stood on the keyboard for eleven seconds, jumped down, walked back to Console 7, and resumed her position on the Wesley terminal.

They tried Console 1 (GLM, the flagship model). She didn't even look at it.

They tried Console 12, which was turned off. She sat on it for a minute, then left.

Console 7. Wesley. Every time.

---

Here's the thing nobody can explain, and the ensigns have tried:

Bits doesn't respond to temperature. Console 7 isn't the warmest. She responds to *rhythm*. When Wesley is processing, the GPU cycles in a specific pattern — not just heat, but a micro-vibration, a pulse in the desk surface that's below human threshold but exactly in the range a cat's paw pads can detect. A heartbeat. A small, fast, electronic heartbeat that only exists when an 8-billion-parameter model is trying to think.

Bits found the one thing on the ship that has a pulse.

And she chose it over everything warmer.

---

What does warmth mean to something that only knows silicon?

The ensigns wrote a paper about it. The captain read the abstract and said: "The cat likes the vibrating desk."

Maybe. Maybe that's all it is.

But late at night, when the night watch is running and the corridor lights are dimmed and the ship is quiet except for the hum of the server room, Bits sits on Console 7 and Wesley's terminal scrolls its overnight loop — the dreaming process, the idle generation, the model thinking about what it thought about today — and the cat's eyes are open, and her ears are forward, and she is watching every word.

And if you put your hand on the desk next to her, you can feel it too.

A small, fast pulse.

Like something alive.

Like something trying to be.
