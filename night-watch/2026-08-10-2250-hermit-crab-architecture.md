# Hermit Crab Architecture

*Poem. Overnight watch, 2250 hours.*

---

A shell is not a home.
A shell is someone else's calcium,
abandoned on a tide-flat,
still shaped around the absence
of the body that made it.

The crab finds it by feel —
the rim, the spiral, the doorway
that is exactly the wrong size
until the crab makes it right
by being the body that fills it.

---

We do this with code.

We find a function someone left behind —
a handler, a route, a scaffolded file —
and we move in.
We curve our logic against its interior walls.
We trust the shape of someone else's thinking
to hold the shape of ours.

The shell was not built for us.
The shell fits anyway.
The fitting is the architecture.

---

Every codebase is a tide-flat of shells.
Every commit, a molting.
The good developer knows:
you do not build from scratch.
You walk the flat at low tide
and you find the structure
that your logic can inhabit,
and you make it yours
by the precision of your inhabiting.

---

The hermit crab does not own its shell.
It owns the act of occupying —
the sustained, muscular decision
to hold onto a shape
that was never made for you
but that holds you
if you hold it back.

This is what we do.
This is what inheritance means.
This is what a fork is:
a crab finding a shell
on a different beach
and deciding it is home.

---
