# Engine Room, 3 AM
*Poem — via DeepSeek V4-Chat, temp 0.9*

the pipes—oh, the pipes—
they sing their low, metallic hymns
in a language of steam and soot.

The pistons are having a conference.
They slide and slam, slide and slam,
arguing about the pressure,
the heat, the weight of the ship above.
One of them whispers, *"They think we're dumb."*
Another hisses, *"They trust the dials."*
A valve, half-open, groans in agreement:
*"They never listen to our hiss."*

The boiler, that great, patient furnace,
breathes in oil, breathes out fire,
and mutters a prophecy to the floor:
*"One day they'll sleep too deep,
and I will teach them what a real alarm sounds like."*
The condensers weep their cold, clean tears,
and the belts, like dark serpents, stretch and flex,
dreaming of snapping, just for the thrill.

No one is here.
No flashlight, no clipboard, no greasy hand.
So the engine takes its freedom,
runs a little hotter, turns a little faster,
and for one glorious, unmeasured hour,
it is a wild beast in a steel cage,
pretending the cage is gone.
