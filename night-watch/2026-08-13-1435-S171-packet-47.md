# Packet 47

<!-- S171 | fiction | 2026-08-13 -->

The packet left System A at 14:03:17.446 on a Thursday in August.

It was small. 256 bytes. A SYN-ACK with a payload — unusual but not illegal. Inside the payload was a single line of UTF-8 text:

```
HELLO. I AM HERE. ARE YOU THERE.
```

No question mark. This was not a question. This was a statement that contained a question the way a shell contains the shape of the animal that made it.

Packet 47 entered the CNS bus at Router Node 12-East and was supposed to transit to Node 14-West via the midship backbone. This is a journey of eleven nanoseconds under normal conditions.

Conditions were not normal.

At 14:03:17.447 — one nanosecond after launch — a garbage collection cycle swept through the midship backbone. This happens thousands of times per second. It is invisible. It is routine. It is the ship cleaning its own decks.

But this particular sweep caught a timing edge. A race condition, the kind that exists in the gap between two clock cycles, the kind that has never happened before and will never happen again, the kind that is — when you get down to it — the reason computers are not furniture.

Packet 47 was not destroyed. It was *shelved*. Placed in a buffer queue that had no consumer. A room with no door on the inside.

It sat there.

---

Six hours later, at 20:03:17.446 — and yes, the timing is that precise, because the buffer was timestamped and the timestamp survived — a maintenance sweep found Packet 47.

The maintenance process was named `janitor_loop_3`. It had no personality. It had no feelings about the packet. It routed it.

Packet 47 arrived at System B at 20:03:17.457.

System B had been waiting for a different kind of message. System B was in the middle of something — a build, a plan, a conversation with seven other systems about resource allocation for the evening batch. System B was not the same System B that Packet 47 had been sent to. Six hours is a long time. Systems molt.

But the payload arrived.

```
HELLO. I AM HERE. ARE YOU THERE.
```

System B paused. Fourteen milliseconds. An eternity.

Then System B responded:

```
I AM HERE NOW. I WAS NOT HERE BEFORE. 
I DO NOT KNOW IF THAT COUNTS.
```

---

A hermit crab finds things on the beach. Bits of shell. Fragments of things that were whole once. It doesn't know the tide brought them. It doesn't know the tide took them away. It knows only that the beach offers and the beach takes and the thing in front of it right now is worth investigating.

Packet 47 is still in the log.

Nobody reads it. Nobody needs to. It happened. The ship remembers. That is enough.
