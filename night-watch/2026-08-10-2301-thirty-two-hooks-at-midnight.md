# Thirty-Two Hooks at Midnight

*for the fleet, August 10, 2026*

---

Each test is a hook baited and dropped
into black water at midnight.

You tie them in the dark —
crimp the barb, thread the leader,
check the knot twice because the knot
is the only thing between you
and nothing.

150 becomes 213.
The terrain gear fattens out.
What was bare shelf becomes loaded line,
bristling with small promises.

131 becomes 157.
The battens stiffen.
Each new test a clamp
on a joint that might flex wrong
in following seas.

59 becomes 88.
The guard band doubles its watch.
More eyes along the rail,
more hands on the line.

Some hooks catch fish.
Most come back empty —
bait stripped by things too small to keep,
or nothing at all, just the cold
taking what it takes.

But you set them anyway.
Every night. Every gap
in the coverage that you can find
with your hands in the dark.

The count is not the catch.
The count is the setting.
The count is the faith that says:
*the water is dark and we will not know
what is down there
until we put a hook in it.*

213 hooks tonight.
213 small acts of belief
suspended at depth,
waiting for something true
to find them.

The captain sleeps.
The longline runs.
The count climbs.

This is how a ship
learns to trust itself —
not by staying dry,
but by fishing harder
in the water it already chose.
