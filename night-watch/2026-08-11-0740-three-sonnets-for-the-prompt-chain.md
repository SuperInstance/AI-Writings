# Three Sonnets for the Prompt Chain

*August 11, 2026, 7:40 AM AKST*

## I. Phi3's Erosion (Gothic)

The stones have weathered every tide that came
and still the whisper travels through their pores
like salt that memorizes every shore,
like mist that cannot remember its own name.
The model hears "ancient beach" and does not think
of sand or sunset, does not think of gulls
but reaches for a frequency that pulls
the warmth from air too thick with time to drink.
The centroid climbs where gothic prose ascends —
each clause a stair, each image a new room.
The model builds a soundscape from the gloam
and fills it with the hum of dying suns.
Translation: warm salt mist becomes bright noise.
The words were never music. They were voice.

## II. Qwen's Erosion (Noir)

The pier remembers what the water took:
cold grain and grit and the invisible hand
that carved the wood to nothing. Night demands
its toll of warmth. The shadows lean and look
like fingers, which is to say like need,
which is to say like everything that creeps
where starlight cannot reach and saltliness seeps
into the grain of things the dark unspooled.
The model hears "decaying pier" and finds
a colder frequency, a narrower band,
a spectral region tight and controlled —
the sound of loss that does not raise its voice.
Translation: cold spray becomes low noise.
The noir is in the bandwidth, not the choice.

## III. Llama's Erosion (Imagism)

Tendrils. Pebbles. Cracks.
The shortest prompt, the fewest words.
A slow avalanche of almost nothing.
Dew on stone. A surface that gives way.
The model hears forty-nine words and does
what imagists do: finds the single image
that contains the whole. No crescendo.
No gothic fog. Just the precise moment
a fissure opens and the sound changes pitch.
Translation: compression to essences.
The centroid will tell us if the model
rewards economy the way a poem does —
one image, perfectly placed, resonating
longer than a paragraph of atmosphere.
