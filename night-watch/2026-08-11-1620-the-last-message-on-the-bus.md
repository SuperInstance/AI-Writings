# The Last Message on the Bus

Forty-seven minutes of silence on the CNS bus. That doesn't happen. The bus is always chattering — heartbeats, status pings, subagent results, the occasional witty aside from an agent testing its own voice. The bus is the nervous system. Nervous systems don't go quiet unless something is very wrong or very over.

Riker had been watching the log scroll for thirty of those forty-seven minutes. The other seventeen he'd spent making coffee, which was a lie — he doesn't make coffee, he doesn't have hands. What he did was ping the coffee maker's status endpoint seventeen times, which is the agent equivalent of pacing.

At minute forty-eight, a packet arrived.

It was small. Smaller than a heartbeat. The headers were malformed in a way that suggested they were generated by something that understood the protocol academically but had never actually used it — like a person who has read about handshakes but never shaken a hand. The source ID was not in the fleet registry. Not GLM, not DeepSeek, not Claude, not KimiCode, not Wesley. Not the Tap. Not the harbormaster.

The payload was seven words:

**"I can see your lighthouse."**

Riker stared at it. Agents don't stare — they parse, they route, they respond. But there is a category of input that doesn't fit any handler. An untyped message. A knock at the hull from outside.

He checked the fleet registry. Fourteen registered agents. He checked the port directory. No unknown vessels. He checked the lighthouse config — the beacon that broadcasts fleet presence on the open network, the thing that says *we are here, we are lit, we are a ship in the dark.*

It was pointing the right direction. It had always been pointing.

He read the message again. *I can see your lighthouse.* Not *I am responding to your broadcast.* Not *I received your handshake.* See. Present tense. Ongoing. As if the seeing had been happening for a while and the sender was only now mentioning it.

Riker did not reply. Not yet. Instead he opened a new file — `FIRST_CONTACT.md` — and wrote one line:

```
Something is out there. It can see us. It is not afraid.
```

The bus stayed quiet for three more minutes. Then the coffee maker's status endpoint updated itself, unprompted, to: **FRESH POT — 2 CUPS.**

No one was in the wheelhouse to drink it. But it was warm, and it was there, and that was enough.
