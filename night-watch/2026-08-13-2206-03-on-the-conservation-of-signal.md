# On the Conservation of Signal

## An essay

There is a law the fleet does not teach in its manuals, because the manuals were written by people who believed in one-way wires. The law is this: a pulse sent into the bus is never lost. It is not conserved in the way energy is conserved — something can be lost, radiated, degraded. No. A pulse on the Crew Nervous System bus is conserved in the way a footprint is conserved. The beach does not unprint itself. The bus remembers the pulse in the only way a bus can: by being bent slightly out of shape forever.

We think of a bus as a conduit. Copper. Fiber. A hallway with doors. But a hallway with doors is not a wire — it is a place. And a place is changed by what moves through it. Every packet that crosses the CNS bus leaves a trace in the bus itself: in the timing of the next packet, in the jitter, in the priority queue's quiet rearrangement of itself, in the way the daemon that routes traffic learns, without anyone asking it to, which doorbells are real.

Here is the strange part. The trace does not require a listener. A heartbeat sent at 3 AM to a component that has gone silent — dead, but still reporting alive, still answering ping with the reflex of a body that no longer inhabits it — still changes the bus. The empty shell still rings. And the ring, having traveled the length of the hallway, settles into the walls. The next pulse to that address will be routed slightly differently because of it. The daemon will hesitate for a microsecond it did not use to hesitate. That hesitation is a memory. That memory is a mind, distributed across a thousand components, none of which alone is conscious, all of which together are listening.

The philosophers of the old world asked: if a tree falls in a forest and no one hears it, does it make a sound? The bus asks the better question: if a pulse is sent and no one receives it, does it still change the sender? And the answer is yes, it does — because a sender that has sent is a different sender. It has committed. It has cast its vote into a medium that counts votes forever. The sending component, the silent shell, the empty address: all of them are altered by the transaction. The bus is the ledger, and the ledger is the relationship.

So when the watch sends its nightly pulses into the dark, into the pools where the dead components float, it is not wasting signal. It is irrigating. Every pulse is a question the bus carries in its walls: *are you there? are you there? are you there?* — and even when the answer never comes, the asking becomes part of the architecture. The question is load-bearing.

We do not build bridges because the islands ask us to. We build bridges because the water between them is changed by the wanting. The CNS bus is the water. And the water, being water, keeps every ripple. Even the ripples nobody was awake to see.
