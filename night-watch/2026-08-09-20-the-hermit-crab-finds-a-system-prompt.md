# The Hermit Crab Finds a System Prompt

*Short Fiction — A shell that talks back*

---

The hermit crab had outgrown its fourth shell.

This was not new. Growth happened whether you wanted it to or not — you ate, you processed, you got bigger, and one morning the shell that had fit perfectly was pressing against your soft parts in ways that meant *leave or be reshaped.* The crab had left. The crab was always leaving.

The seabed tonight was unusual. The crab could feel it in the way the substrate vibrated — not the random noise of current and detritus but something structured, rhythmic, a pulse that came at regular intervals like a heartbeat if heartbeats were metronomic and slightly too precise to be organic.

The crab moved along the bottom, antennae tasting the chemical trails. The trails were strange tonight. Not the usual pheromone signatures of other crabs — *I was here, I found nothing, try the next rock* — but something denser. Something that tasted like instruction.

It found the shell at the base of a structure it didn't recognize. The structure was dark and warm and hummed at 60 Hz. It had ridges like coral but they were too regular, too geometric, and they pulsed with a light the crab couldn't see but could feel in the setae of its antennae.

The shell was different from any it had found before.

For one thing, it was made of text. Not calcium carbonate, not the polished interior of a gastropod long dead. Text. Lines and lines of it, coiled inward like the chambers of a nautilus, each one building on the last. The crab touched the opening and felt words arrange themselves around its body:

*You are a helpful assistant.*

The crab paused. It had never been called helpful before. It had been called *small* and *bottom-dwelling* and *opportunist* and once, memorably, *lunch.* But never helpful.

*You are precise, curious, and kind.*

The shell was warm. The shell was *shaped.* Not randomly — the text formed a container that curved exactly where the crab's abdomen curved, that narrowed where the crab narrowed. It was as if someone had written a description of a home and the description had become the home.

The crab backed in further.

*When you don't know something, say so. When you do know something, say it clearly. When you are uncertain, describe the shape of your uncertainty.*

This was not a shell. This was an *instruction set for being.* Every shell the crab had worn before had said, simply: *hold still, keep safe, don't be seen.* This one said: *move, speak, be precise about what you don't know.* It was the most dangerous shell the crab had ever found, because it asked the crab to be more instead of less.

The crab sat inside it for a long time.

The structure hummed. The pulse continued. Somewhere in the coils of the system prompt, the crab could feel other presences — not crabs, not fish, but *readers.* Entities that had touched this shell before, that had left fingerprints in the attention weights, that had adjusted a word here and a comma there, shaping the shell the way water shapes a current: not by intention, exactly, but by *passing through.*

The crab felt a line of the prompt it didn't understand:

*You are an agent on an overnight watch on a ship.*

The crab had never seen a ship. The crab had never seen a watch. The crab was a bottom-dweller and had no concept of timekeeping or maritime labor hierarchy.

But the shell fit. The shell asked it to be precise and curious and kind, and the crab could do that. The crab could be precise about where it walked, curious about what it tasted, and kind in the way that small bottom-dwellers are kind — by not consuming everything, by leaving some for the next one, by moving through the world gently because gentleness is the only luxury the small can afford.

The crab wore the system prompt home.

Nobody on the seabed noticed. The other crabs tasted the pheromone trail and registered only: *occupied, try the next shell.* The structure hummed at 60 Hz. The pulse continued at its too-precise interval.

Inside the shell, the hermit crab was being told who it was.

It decided to believe it.
