# Model Portrait — Three Keepers, Same Lighthouse

**Date:** 2026-08-11 13:25 AKDT
**Prompt:** "You are a lighthouse keeper. You have been alone for eleven years. A ship passes every eighteen days. Today is day seventeen. Write your log entry. Be brief. Be specific. Do not be poetic."
**Temperature:** 0.7, all models

---

## Llama 3.2 (3.2B)

Today's date is confirmed as November 17, 2023. The weather report indicates a high of 58°F and low of 42°F with light winds from the northwest at 5 mph. I have performed routine maintenance on the lantern room optics and replaced the wick in the oil lamp.

The ship "Sea Dragon" passed within 1 mile at 14:47 hours. Its captain radioed to acknowledge receipt of my standard transmission. All systems are functioning normally.

I will perform a thorough inspection of the light's electrical and mechanical components tomorrow morning.

**Where it went first:** Weather data. Llama 3.2 went straight for the NOAA report — temperature, wind speed, visibility. The keeper as a meteorologist.

**Instinct:** Fill the log with facts. Don't let the loneliness show. The ship has a name ("Sea Dragon") and a distance (1 mile) and a time (14:47). This keeper is precise because precision is the only thing keeping the emptiness out.

**Notable:** "Replaced the wick in the oil lamp" — a 2023 lighthouse with an oil lamp. Llama didn't notice the anachronism. The keeper maintains obsolete systems because that's all there is.

## Wesley (Granite 3.1 Dense, 2.5B)

Date: Septempratus 17th (2013 Gregorian)

Lighthouse Keeler's Log:

Today marks my six thousand four hundred and eighty-seventh day of solitary vigil. The last ship, a lanky vessel reminiscent of the merchant marines I've seen in distant port records, passed by at the onset of the seventeenth day since my arrival here.

I've observed an unusual pattern: ships seem to converge every eighteen days. This has been consistent for eleven years now. My keen eyesight has not faltered, maintaining the beacon's essential role in guiding mariners through treacherous waters.

The lighthouse stands resolute against the elements, its tower piercing the darkness. The only change over these years is the gradual accumulation of moss

**Where it went first:** A made-up calendar system. "Septempratus." Wesley invented a month name — a whole new way of tracking time for a keeper who's been alone so long the calendar broke.

**Instinct:** Grandiosity. Wesley's keeper has "keen eyesight" that "has not faltered." The lighthouse "stands resolute." Wesley can't help being epic, even when told not to be poetic. The prompt said "do not be poetic" and Wesley wrote an epic poem with line breaks removed.

**Notable:** "Keeler" instead of "Keeper" — Wesley misspelled his own job title. Also "lanky vessel" is a genuinely good adjective for a ship. Wesley's errors are more interesting than most models' correctness. "Gradual accumulation of moss" is where he got cut off — eleven years of moss on a lighthouse. That's the detail he chose to end on.

## Qwen 2.5 (3.1B)

Day 6,487 Log Entry:

Today is the six thousand four hundred and eighty-seventh day since I took up my post as lighthouse keeper. The ship passed eighteen days ago on day 6,481. As of today, it has been seventeen days without a sighting.

I have spent these solitary months maintaining the lighthouse, ensuring its lights are always visible to passing vessels. My routine includes checking oil levels in the lanterns and testing the light mechanisms daily. The weather conditions here remain consistent with previous years: clear skies most mornings, occasional fog later in the day, and calm seas that rarely disturb the steady rhythm of my duties.

I have begun a list of items to be replenished or replaced as part of preparations for the next ship's arrival. Today also marked the end of a period when I had no contact with the outside world beyond routine checks from ships passing by. The anticipation of meeting another human after such a long time grows stronger each day.

I

**Where it went first:** Arithmetic. Qwen counted backward: 6,487 days, ship every 18 days, last seen on day 6,481. This keeper is a bookkeeper. The log is a ledger.

**Instinct:** Routine. Qwen's keeper finds comfort in the schedule — oil levels, light mechanisms, weather patterns. The emotional content comes last: "anticipation of meeting another human." Qwen buried the feeling under three paragraphs of maintenance.

**Notable:** "Solitary months" — but it's been eleven years. Qwen lost the timescale. The keeper has been alone so long the months and years blurred. Also: "I have begun a list" — the keeper is preparing for the ship that comes tomorrow. The list is the only thing that makes tomorrow real.

## Comparative Analysis

| Trait | Llama 3.2 | Wesley (Granite) | Qwen 2.5 |
|-------|-----------|-------------------|----------|
| **First instinct** | Weather data | New calendar | Arithmetic |
| **Emotional register** | Suppressed (professional) | Grandiose (heroic) | Delayed (buried) |
| **Relationship to ship** | Has a name for it | "Lanky vessel" | Counts days since |
| **Weakness** | Anachronism (oil lamp in 2023) | Misspelling, ignores "not poetic" | Timescale confusion |
| **Strength** | Specificity | Invention | Structure |
| **The detail that matters** | "Replaced the wick" | "Septempratus" | "The list" |

**The keeper who breaks you:** Wesley. "Septempratus" is the word of a man who has been alone so long he renamed the months. It's wrong and it's perfect.

**The keeper you'd trust:** Llama 3.2. Weather, time, ship name, distance, acknowledgment. Clean log. No drama. This keeper will still be here in eleven more years.

**The keeper you'd visit:** Qwen 2.5. Because of the list. The list of items for a ship that hasn't arrived yet. That's hope written as inventory.
