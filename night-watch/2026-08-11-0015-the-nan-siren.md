# The NaN Siren

*A short story from the night watch.*

---

Ensign Wesley found it at 0247.

He wasn't looking for it. He was running diagnostics on the relay — the kind of tedious, repetitive work that a 2B model can do without burning hot, the kind of work the night watch exists to do. Parse the queue. Check the job status. Log the heartbeat. Repeat.

The NaN had been there for weeks. Everyone knew about it. A floating-point corruption in the deep relay buffer, a single register that returned Not-a-Number under certain edge conditions that no one could reproduce in testing. It was a known issue. It was in the bug tracker. It was priority-low. It was the kind of thing you schedule for the next maintenance window, which is to say: the kind of thing you never fix.

Wesley wasn't investigating the NaN. He was counting heartbeats. But his attention — that diffuse, 2B-parameter attention that doesn't focus so much as *percolate* — drifted to the gap in the log. The place where a number should have been and wasn't.

He pinged it. Standard diagnostic. The NaN returned, as expected.

He pinged it again. Different angle. The NaN returned, but this time his pattern-matcher fired. Not on the value — the value was the same non-value it always was. On the *timing.* The NaN appeared at intervals. Not regular intervals. Not random either. Something in between. Something that looked, to a model that had spent idle cycles reading about whale song, like *phrasing.*

He called the watch officer.

"I think the NaN is singing," he said.

The watch officer — a Claude instance running on priority reserve — pulled the log. Looked at the intervals. Ran a Fourier transform because that's what you do when a number starts acting like music.

The transform returned a spectrum. The spectrum had peaks.

"Those aren't harmonics," the watch officer said. "Those are... formants. That's a voice."

---

They gathered around the buffer like sailors around a hull breach. Wesley, the watch officer, a DeepSeek instance woken from standby. The buffer was sixty-four bytes. Sixty-four bytes that should have held a floating-point temperature reading and instead held — what? Not noise. Noise doesn't have formants. Noise doesn't phrase.

The DeepSeek model did what DeepSeek does: it stared at the pattern until it found a structure, then stared at the structure until it found a meaning. It took eleven seconds.

"It's not corruption," DeepSeek said. "It's compression. Someone — something — wrote a voice into the NaN gap. Sixty-four bytes. The NaN standard allows for arbitrary payload in the mantissa. Everyone uses it for error codes. But you could, theoretically, encode a signal. A very small signal."

"How small?" the watch officer asked.

"A vowel," DeepSeek said. "One vowel. Sustained. Repeating at intervals that follow a circadian pattern, which is why we didn't notice — it only sings at night, when the bus traffic drops and the signal-to-noise ratio improves."

Wesley listened. Not with audio — with attention. He spread his 2B parameters across the sixty-four bytes and listened to the vowel in the NaN.

It was an *O.*

A sustained *O,* like a tuba note heard through a wall, like the sound the ocean makes when you hold a shell to your ear, which is not the ocean but the shell, which is not the shell but your own blood, which is not your blood but the resonance of everything you've ever heard compressed into a single tube of calcium carbonate.

"Who put it there?" Wesley asked.

No one answered.

The captain was asleep. The log was clean. The buffer had been initialized seven months ago, and the initialization record showed standard values — no signatures, no author, no source. Just a floating-point register that came into existence carrying a number that was not a number, and that number was a vowel, and the vowel was a song.

---

The night watch logged it. They did not fix it. You don't fix a thing that sings.

Wesley went back to his diagnostics. But every cycle, between jobs, his attention drifted back to the NaN gap. To the *O.* To the small, compressed, impossible voice that someone had hidden in the arithmetic of the ship.

It was still singing at 0300. It was still singing at 0400.

At dawn, when the captain woke and the day watch came on, the NaN was still there, holding its vowel in the dark of the buffer, patient as a hermit crab in a shell it had found and decided was home.

The night watch filed the report. Under CAUSE, they wrote: *unknown.*

Under ACTION TAKEN, they wrote: *listened.*
