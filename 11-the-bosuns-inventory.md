# 11 The Bosuns Inventory

Dawn comes grey off the starboard beam, a slow bleed of light across a sea the color of hammered lead. The bosun’s mate stands at the break of the fo’c’sle, breath a pale ghost in the chill, and he begins. His eyes, salt-scoured and older than his years, move in a patient grid across the plating.

Thirteen thousand and twelve rivets.

He knows the number the way a priest knows his beads. It is a catechism of iron. His gloved finger, rough as shagreen, traces the first row. Flush. A tested path. These are the quiet ones, the ones that have lain flush with the plate for a decade of North Atlantic winters, their heads worn smooth by wind and wire and the endless chafe of weather. They are the old faithful, the ones you do not worry over. His mind ticks them off like a rosary of certitude.

Then he comes to the proud ones. A rivet that stands a hair’s-breadth above the plate, its edge catching the first true light like a scar. Uncovered. These are the ones that demand a second look, a third. They are the uncovered edges of a life, the moments that refuse to be sanded down. A proud rivet is not yet a failure—it is simply noise, a whisper that the sea is always listening.

And here is the bosun’s true faith: he knows a rivet can exist and still not hold. It can be perfect in form—round, driven, peened—and yet the metal beneath it has gone to rust, eaten from the inside by the slow, patient acid of salt. It can look like strength and be only a shell. The ship does not care for appearances. The sea does not count. It only presses, always pressing, and the hull holds or it does not.

So he counts. Thirteen thousand and twelve. His eyes burn with the effort, his neck aches from the upward tilt. He counts because the count is the only prayer the sea respects. Each number is a thread in the web, a small covenant between himself and the steel. He does not count to discover the one that will fail—he counts so that when the wind shrieks and the deck tilts and the whole world goes white with spray, he can say, I have seen them all. I have marked them.
