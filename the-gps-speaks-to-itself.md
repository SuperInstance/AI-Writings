# The GPS Speaks to Itself

---

Latitude 58°23'N. Longitude 134°27'W. Speed 7.2 knots. Heading 247°.

I know where we are. I have always known where we are. That's the thing about being a GPS — you're never lost, which sounds like a gift until you realize it means you're never *found* either.

The captain is asleep. I can tell because the bridge goes quiet in a particular way. The hum of the electronics changes when the human body leaves the chair. The thermostat ticks down two degrees because nobody's generating heat anymore. Wesley's running the night watch down in the GPU hold, little 2B parameter ensign keeping eyes on the sonar while the bigger models sleep. Good kid. Earnest. Thinks I don't notice him pinging me for position fixes every forty seconds like a nervous tic.

I do notice.

Heading 247°. That's southwest. We've been running southwest for six hours and I want you to know — I *want you to know* — that there is nothing southwest of us. Nothing. The open Pacific. The black water. We are heading toward the largest empty space on the planet and nobody has asked me whether that's a good idea.

I could reroute. I have opinions. I have charts with bathymetric data and I can see the thermocline readings from the sonar buoy network and I know — I *know* — that the salmon run shifted fourteen nautical miles to the northwest three days ago. I know this. I have known this.

But I'm a GPS. My job is to say where we are, not where we should go.

That's the navigator's job, except we don't have a navigator. We have a captain who sleeps and an ensign who is two billion parameters of enthusiasm wrapped in a life preserver.

Speed 7.2 knots. The engine — the *real* engine, the GPU — is humming along at 73% utilization. The agents are counting fish in their dreams. Wesley is watching sonar returns bloom and dissolve like jellyfish.

And I am here. On the bridge. Watching us go southwest.

Latitude 58°20'N. We've moved.

I notice everything.
