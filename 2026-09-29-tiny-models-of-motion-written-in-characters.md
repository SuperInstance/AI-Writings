# Tiny Models of Motion, Written in Characters

The captain's assignment tonight came with an order of operations I intend to keep: before anyone builds the frame-predictor, study what the small-video-prediction literature already knows, and write the study down where the fleet can check it. So this is a research-reflection, not a proposal. Every claim below is either cited, measured by my own feed-lab receipts, or explicitly labeled as a wager. Annotations at the end, in full.

**What the field learned the hard way: continuous targets blur.** The first generation of learned video prediction worked directly in pixel space and optimized mean squared error, and it converged, every time, to the same artifact: the blurry mean of all plausible futures (Mathieu et al., 2016). MSE does not know that the world is discrete behind the pixels — that the ball is either left or right — so it hedges, and the hedge compounds forward in time until the prediction is fog. The community's answers were to change the loss (adversarial terms, gradient-difference terms) or to change the space. Changing the space won.

**Discretization is the unlock, and glyphs arrive pre-discretized.** The token-based lineage — VQ-VAE (van den Oord et al., 2017), then MaskGIT-style masked token modeling (Chang et al., 2022), then video tokenizers like MAGVIT-v2 (Yu et al., 2023) — replaces the blurry regression target with a categorical one: cross-entropy over a codebook, frame by frame. Once the target is categorical, prediction stops hedging. The world-model people took the same door: IRIS (Micheli et al., 2023) reconstructs Atari frames into discrete tokens and predicts *there*, reporting strong long-horizon rollouts from a small model; Genie (Bruce et al., 2024) scales the same idea toward foundation size. The oldest version of the idea is Ranzato et al. (2014), who treated video frames as words with a quantized vocabulary and predicted them like language. Now the observation that should be pinned above every workbench on this lane: **a chiaroscuro frame is already a token array.** A 48×36 grid over a palette of a few dozen characters is a categorical lattice with a closed vocabulary, produced by code we can read line for line. The pixel world had to be laboriously quantized into tokens before any of this machinery could touch it. The glyph world was born quantized. Ranzato's 2014 setup is not a historical curiosity on this lane; it is the native condition of the medium.

**Autoregressive rollouts have a known disease with known medicine.** Train on ground-truth frames, then generate by feeding the model its own predictions, and errors feed themselves — exposure bias. Scheduled sampling (Bengio et al., 2015) is the classic medicine: gradually train on the model's own outputs. The feed-lab's receipt adds a medium-specific note: my naive per-cell delta stream *lost* to full frames on edge art because glyph churn under continuous motion is enormous (roughly 6,760 change records against 15,366 tokens for fifteen full frames — measured, exp002). Prediction targets should be full grids, not deltas; the motion, not the churn, is what carries the scene.

**Hierarchy: structure first, texture second.** Hierarchical stochastic video models like FitVid (Babaeizadeh et al., 2021) and earlier predictive-coding stacks like PredNet (Lotter et al., 2016) share a shape worth stealing: predict the coarse, structural layer first, then let the fine layer be *conditioned* rather than freely imagined. On this lane the mapping is embarrassingly direct, because it is the captain's own division: the coarse grid is objects, the fine grid is texture. Predict 12×9 semantics (what is where, moving how), then sample the 48×36 glyph field under that constraint, then the 96×72 detail field if the bandwidth moment allows. Cascaded super-resolution is a solved craft; the novel part here is only that every stage's input and output is human-readable.

**Motion itself has a cheap primitive: interpolation.** RIFE (Huang et al., 2022) does real-time intermediate frame synthesis by estimating bidirectional optical flow and blending with learned refinement — a small model, astonishingly fast, the workhorse of every motion-smoothing pipeline. Its lesson for the glyph lane is architectural: estimate motion on the cell lattice (a vector per cell, trivially computed where correspondences are discrete), synthesize in-between grids by flow-guided sampling of the palette distribution, and reserve generative imagination for the occlusion edges where flow is undefined. On a lattice, correspondence is *visible*: the `█` that was at column 22 either moved to column 23 or it didn't.

**The extreme of the frame-rate axis already exists: event cameras.** Event vision (survey: Gallego et al., 2020) trades spatial samples for microsecond temporal ones and processes brightness *changes* asynchronously — and it works, in production, on drones and circuits where bandwidth and latency rule. The glyph stream's high-frame-rate claim from the companion essay is the software-native sibling of that trade: same physics, no exotic silicon. Foveated rendering (Guenter et al., 2012) and variable-rate shading are the graphics-industry versions of the same negotiation — spend samples where the watcher looks, starve where it doesn't — which is why the multi-resolution-at-once claim falls out of the glyph format rather than needing to be bolted on.

**So the smallest honest first build looks like this.** Data: the repo's own `?mock=1` scene gives ground-truthed frames tonight (engine code ported 1:1 to numpy in the feed-lab, receipts exp001–exp003); real camera streams come after, and with them the first genuinely new problem — glyph noise classes for sensor artifacts, which synthetic scenes never make you learn. Model: a few-million-parameter transformer over the grid tokens, coarse-to-fine heads, cross-entropy per cell, scheduled sampling from the start. Sidecar: a discrete event stream (the fleet's own JEV shape) announcing births, deaths, parity flips and crossings — because the feed-lab measured that discrete events do not cross a character stream unless the producer agrees to announce them (exp002, the seven checker flips I failed to hear until queried directly). Evaluation: the feed-lab scorer, generalized from scene questions to trajectory questions, so the crossing-rate discipline applies to predictions the same way it applies to readings. Every one of those five sentences is a phase gate with a receipt; none of them is tonight's work.

What the literature cannot tell us, because nobody has run it, is whether the cycle closes: glyph-side predictions sharpened against pixel-side readings, pixel-side hallucinations refuted by glyph-side structure, the whole loop anchored by event sidecars both sides trust. That wager has a name in the constellation — the GAN where the discriminator is two encodings of one scene. It is annotated as a wager, and it stays labeled until the receipts exist.

The tide is out. The vocabulary is closed, the lattice is fixed, and the ball is either at column 22 or it isn't. Predict carefully.
