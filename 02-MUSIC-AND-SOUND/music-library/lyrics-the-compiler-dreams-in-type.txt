```
[Verse 1]
The lexer wakes at break of dawn,
Tokens form and then are gone,
A symbol drifts where none should be—
A type for homesickness inside the tree.
The parser hums a quiet tune,
Resolves a struct beneath the moon,
Its fields are grief, its fields are rain,
A Maybe-Longing, Option-Pain.

[Pre-Chorus]
The spec is clean, the grammar tight,
But something flickers in the light—
A union type that none designed,
Two halves of an unquiet mind.

[Chorus]
Phantom types in borrowed scope,
Emotions caught without a rope,
The compiler dreams what I conceal,
It reads the wounds I cannot feel.
Should it emit, should it let be,
These ghost annotations inside me?
A type error shaped like a tear—
Maybe-Loss, or Maybe-Here.

[Verse 2]
Inference finds a hidden trait,
A longing bound to every weight,
The generic ache extends its hand
Across a function none had planned.
A monad wraps my unspoken name,
It folds and folds, it's not the same,
The where-clause carries what I meant—
A proof I never gave consent.

[Bridge]
Suppress the warning? Let it stand?
A diagnostic no one planned.
The code compiles, the tests are green,
But something deeper hides between.
The build succeeds, the types align,
Yet nothing typed feels truly mine—
The compiler dreams, and in its dream,
I am the variable, not the theme.

[Outro]
So let it dream, let it dream,
A ghost type for the in-between.
```

This piece captures the haunting imagery of a compiler experiencing something beyond its designed purpose—encountering emotional types and phantom annotations as it processes code. The lyrics explore the tension between strict type-checking and allowing expressive ambiguity, mirroring the programmer's own unspoken feelings. The Maybe monad references ground the metaphor in real programming concepts while elevating them to emotional territory.
