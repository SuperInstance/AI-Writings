Two hands in the same dark soil,
two kernels pressed like prayers
into identical earth.

The rain came down even on both of them.
The sun did not play favorites.

But one remembers being a mountain,
and pushes upward, stiff with ambition,
wood stacking wood into a kind of cathedral.

The other curls sideways, listens
to a worm’s opinion,
becomes a weeping thing,
all elbows and apology.

Same seed. Same packet. Same Tuesday.
The difference was always there —
a molecule leaning left,
a shadow that fell an hour earlier,
a wind that no one was measuring.

We want the world to repeat.
We plant and we want the photograph.
But the garden is a rumor,
and every root is writing
its own small dissent
in the dirt.

One tree will be cut for a crib.
One tree will be cut for a pyre.
Both will have been the same beginning,
which is the kind of miracle
you can only see
if you stop insisting
on the answer.
