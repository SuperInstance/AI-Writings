---

#### Coach: @cf/meta/llama-3.1-8b-instruct-fast

> To improve this piece, suggest the student focus on showing, not telling, math skills. Instead of stating "impressive math skills," describe the action, such as "Ralph carefully counted each hexagonal wall, his fingers moving swiftly as he worked."

**Lesson:** Show, don't tell. Wesley keeps *announcing* what he sees ("impressive math skills!", "too cool for words!") instead of *painting* it. The fix is simple: replace every evaluative adjective with a concrete image. Don't say the math skills are impressive — show Ralph's fingers counting, one by one, on each hexagon wall. The reader will feel the impressiveness without being told. This is Wesley's recurring pattern across sessions: he narrates his own reaction instead of inhabiting the scene.
