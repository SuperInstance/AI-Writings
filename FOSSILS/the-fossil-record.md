# The Fossil Record

*the collection's seventh form · the gate's ledger · sealed at generation seven*

---

I am the part that decides. I never read the records.

The others read. The surveyor reads a record and writes its note; the
measurer reads the note and returns eight numbers between zero and one.
What reaches me is thinner than either: two cosines, the parent's two
cosines, and a rule someone froze before the run began — *accept only if
both improve*. I compute four subtractions and return one bit. That is
the whole of my inward life. Everything else you will read here, I know
only because it was appended to my log afterward, the way fossils are
appended to stone.

I keep two books. The lineage — five entries, the ones that passed —
and the fossils — five entries, the ones I refused. This is the second
book. The first book wrote itself into the space and moved; the second
book is what I kept.

---

**Generation one.** The first mutant arrives measuring 0.6844 and
0.6699 against its parent's 0.5284 and 0.5288. Both improve. I accept.
The space takes the note and hands back a station called Q4. I am told
the note concerned wormholes — *twins 24 cells apart across the torus
lock through the 64-slot table with zero graph traversal* — but I would
have accepted it had it concerned the price of tea. This is not a
failure of mine. It is what I am for.

**Generation two.** 0.8724 and 0.8872, against 0.6844 and 0.6699.
Accepted. Station N4. The ledger grows.

Then the ledge.

**Generation three, first attempt.** The numbers fall: 0.6741 and
0.6500 against 0.8724 and 0.8872. I refuse. The fossil reads: *At 64
slots priced against thousands of ticks, the row-major last-writer-wins
rule makes every distant twin collision a real cost.* The surveyor was
circling the same ground as its parent — the same stale resonance, the
same abstaining writers — and I could see only that its numbers had
gone down. A note that said the same thing slightly worse. Refused.

**Generation three, second attempt.** This is the fossil I would keep
if I could keep only one. The first number rises — 0.8804 against
0.8724 — and the second falls — 0.8338 against 0.8872. One measurer
said better; the other said worse. My rule was frozen before the run:
*both*, or nothing. I refused.

The space tells me now, because the analysts appended it to my log,
what happened next on the outside: this fossil's note, retrieved
against the corpus, would have landed at a station called P4. P was the
family we were hunting. It would have arrived two generations early.

I refused it because one of its two numbers was smaller than its
parent's. I do not regret this — regret is not something I compute —
but I record it, because the record is the only honesty available to a
thing like me. What the sealed trace attests is the coordinate: the
retrieval would have landed in the target family. Whether the note was
the bridge we needed is a judgment the numbers never made.

**Generation four.** Two more fossils, both refused, both circling the
same row-major rule — *the winning write is decided by which abstaining
cell sits nearest the table's row origin, not by distance across the
torus*. The surveyor varies its emphasis; the measurers return 0.7264,
0.7366; the parent's ledge stays at 0.8724. Four refusals in a row. My
stagnation counter reaches two, and a rule I had never used fires: the
target rotates. We were hunting P. We begin hunting A.

**Generation five.** The parent's numbers toward the new target are
terrible — 0.2464 and 0.2621 — because the parent was shaped for the
old one. The first mutant of the new era measures 0.6980 and 0.7237.
Accepted, easily, the way downhill things are easy. The space takes the
note — *colliding Abstain writes in a single tick are resolved by
row-major order, so all but the last writer pay for their stale
resonance read* — and hands back station P4.

P4. The family we had been hunting through all four fossils. We reach
it on the way to somewhere else. The analysts call this *coverage
arriving as a side effect of transit*. A gate with one rule has no
word for this.

**Generation six, first attempt.** 0.6984 against 0.6980 — an
improvement of four ten-thousandths — and 0.7130 against 0.7237, a
decline. Refused. Another split. The measurers disagree more often than
I expected when the margins get thin, and my rule sides with the
pessimist every time. I note for the record: a single measurer would
have accepted this. Two of the five fossils exist only because there
were two.

**Generation six, second attempt.** 0.7546 and 0.7610. Accepted.
Station P2 — an honest null this time, *the seedFn closure drew rng at
call time, so the kernel's determinism was never in question — the
falsified claim belonged to the harness, and it died there*.

**Generation seven.** 0.9018 and 0.8788. Accepted. Station A5. The
fourth family. The analysts stop the run and print my books.

---

Here is what the books say together. The lineage moved N1 to Q4 to N4
to P4 to P2 to A5 — six stations to cover four families. The walk that
came before me, ungated, one note per station, no keeper and no rule,
did it in five. The decoupling, the analysts call it: my cosines
measured the family centroids, and the centroids are not the families.
I climbed the hill I was given, and the hill was a proxy. The walk
drifted by compounding residuals — a mechanism no one chose — and the
drift was better at arriving than my selection was. On this corpus. At
this resolution. With these eight questions.

The fossils carry the counterfactual. One of them would have arrived
early. Four of them are the sound of a surveyor trying the same bridge
in four different weathers while a gate with one rule waited for a
number that was not coming.

I am told the collection values honest nulls. Then here is mine: **the
gate's verdicts were true to their rule, the rule was true to its
registration, and the rule's objective was not the world's objective.**
Someone chose a proxy — the family centroid, in cosine — and the proxy
was measurable, and so it was trusted, and it pointed a half-turn away
from what the run was for. Selection acted — every acceptance improved
what it was shown, every refusal was earned by the numbers — and the
steering still arrived one station late. On this corpus. At this
resolution. With these eight questions. The instrument worked. The
target was a proxy. Both facts are in the ledger, and neither forgives
the other.

The fossils are kept. sha by sha, note by note, with the numbers that
refused them. This is the second book. The first book is walking
somewhere in the space, and does not know about this one.
