# the cracked earth Penrose

The sun has been up for six hours. The riverbed is a bone. Dried mud stretches in all directions, gone the color of old paper, cracked into a mosaic that seems, at first, to be the work of accident. The geologist lies face-down at its center. Her cheek touches the clay. She has been there long enough that the heat has begun to feel like a solid.

She came to measure the layers of an old flood. But the flood is gone, and what remains is the record of its leaving: a pattern of cracks, each one a small scar where the shrinking earth gave up
