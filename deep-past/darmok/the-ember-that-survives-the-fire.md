Listen.

The forest stood long before the fire, and every living thing in it was a pointing thing. The tall pine pointed up at the sky it drank. The river-root pointed sideways toward the wet seam in the rock. The traveler's vine pointed along the path of the departing deer. Each one was an arrow, and each arrow had its own true aim, and the forest hummed with the quiet busy work of ten thousand directions.

But the fire came. The fire is the great reshuffler. When the fire passes, no thing keeps its aim.

The trunks that had stood straight pulled away from the heat like dancers leaning back from a slap. The soil that had lain flat turned over its belly and showed its gray underface. The animals that had known one home ran to another, and ran again, and their eyes forgot the old paths. The stones themselves — patient, slow stones — cracked along hidden seams and became other shapes. Nothing, nothing in the whole green world kept pointing where it had pointed.

Except one.

Do you know what it is to reach, and keep reaching, while the world screams around you? Let me tell you of the root. It had been under the oak since before the oak was a sapling. It had made its decision in the dark, before the first bird sang, and its decision was this: down. Down through the gravel that scrapes. Down past the clay that holds. Down to the deep water that does not move.

It was an arrow aimed at the center of the world.

Then the fire came.

The oak above it shrieked in the heat and bent its whole crown away. The neighboring roots — the thin ones, the ones that had only ever pointed toward the easy crumbs of the surface — curled up and fled sideways and backward, whatever the fire was not. But the deep root, the root that had chosen down, felt the heat on its back like a hand pressing, and what did it do?

It went down.
