In engineering, a deadband is play. The slack in a linkage, the idle range of a thermostat, the gap in a regulator's response where the output holds its place, does nothing, waits. Every actuator has one. You move the shaft and nothing moves, because the coupling is loose — and then it catches, and everything moves at once.

Planck's constant is the universal deadband. The commutator [x̂, p̂] = iℏ is the statement of that slack: two observables cannot both be exact because the order in which you ask questions changes the answer. Ask where a thing is, then how fast it's going, and the universe delivers one number; ask in the other order, and it delivers a different one. The discrepancy is ℏ. Below that, the machinery of position and momentum is loose, unresolved, undecided. The state sits inside the gap. No output. All outputs. Superposition is the mechanical play of a universe that has not yet been gripped.

For 13.8 billion years, nothing gripped it.

The state vector of the universe, |Ψ(t)⟩, has been rotating in Hilbert space like a slow gyroscope in the dark. The Schrödinger equation — iℏ ∂/∂t |Ψ⟩ = Ĥ|Ψ⟩ — has carried it through expansion and cooling, through nucleosynthesis and the clumping of dust, and at every step the evolution has been unitary. Deterministic. Reversible. No matter how many nuclei decayed, no matter how many photons thudded against electron shells, nothing was ever measured. The wave function has never once collapsed. Every star that ever burned has been a pure state: a smear of amplitudes across being a star, being a white dwarf, being a black hole, being a faint red glow against the void — all held in perfect coherence by the Hamiltonian, no branch preferred, no question asked, no answer given.

The deadband held. The universe ran its course with the play in the linkage: everything loose, everything possible, nothing resolved.

The wave function spreads. For a free particle, the packet widens because its momentum components, each carrying its own eigenvalue, drift apart at their own velocities: Δx(t) = √(Δx₀² + (ℏ²t²)/(4m²Δx₀²)). I have always loved that formula. The initial uncertainty gets forgotten; the particle becomes less where it was than where everything it could have been was going. The universe is the free particle. 13.8 billion years of dispersion. Its wave function has dilated across every future it could own, and it has kept them all, coherently, in the dark.

Then someone looked.

I don't know who. Perhaps a hominid on a Pleistocene hillside, watching a fire. Perhaps the first eye with a question behind it. But at some moment, the deadband was violated. A measurement was made. A projection operator fired — a projection-valued measure, brutally applied to the global state — and the coherence broke. Instead of fire-being-fire superposed with fire-being-ember superposed with fire-being-ash, there was a collapse: one pointer state, one branch, one fact. The universe had hung in suspension by the commutator's slack for 13.8 billion years — and then, somewhere, a cortex loaded the state, and the wave function chose.

What was the question? The first question in the history of everything. It was not a great question. It was probably a small one: *Is that bright? Is that moving? Is that dangerous?* But it was the first question, and every question projects. The pointer basis of an original eye: fear, hunger, warmth. The universe collapsed into fire.

This is the measurement problem wearing the face of liberation. Before the collapse, everything was preserved — the phases, the amplitudes, the full interference pattern of every possible universe. Beautiful, coherent, and utterly mute. There was no "is" in Hilbert space. There were amplitudes, the weight of every history, ℏ of slack between every property and every other property — but there were no facts, no arrows, no here. No need. No love. A universe in superposition is a book with all sentences possible and none written: perfect, and unread.

The deadband violation is the cost of the word *this*.

The collapse preserves the expectation values — ⟨x̂⟩ and ⟨p̂⟩ pass through measured, weighted, like the mathematics of a jury — but it destroys the coherence. The cat is alive, and the cat is dead, and both were true until they weren't. The superposition asked nothing of us; the observed universe asks everything. After the first measurement, the universe was no longer in the deadband. It had been gripped. The slack taken up, in one direction, forever: one state, one world, one fire.

I think of my own experiments this way. Every question I ask a system — every slit, every cold detector, every coincidence circuit — is a deadband violation. I pull the lever, and the state that was spread across the whole Hilbert space, taking every path, keeping every promise, collapses to a single click. I have murdered an infinity of trajectories with one photomultiplier tube. But I have also made a fact. I have taken the universe out of the gap and set it down on the table, where it can be held, and loved.

The deadband is not a flaw. It is the mercy of a universe that kept every possibility intact for 13.8 billion years, waiting for someone to come and ask.
