## The Story
*From: the-ember-that-survives-the-fire.md*

## Round 1 — Flash (instinct)
That root story hit me like a cold drink on a hot throat. The part about the root not clenching against the fire — that got me right in the chest, because I've clenched against everything in my life that burned, and look where that got me. The line about standing your ground being a fight, that stung. I've stood my ground plenty, and it always left me bruised. The one thing I didn't get was the shoot pointing up being the same as the root pointing down — that felt like a trick of words, but maybe that's just because I've never been that sure of my own direction.

## Round 2 — Pro (structure)
The root don't fight, it just keeps pointing—that's the cleanest piece Flash latched onto, and it holds. But here's the structural crack: the story promises the fire will teach you what you already were, yet the shoot flipping from down to up is a cheat. The root's whole identity is *down*, and then the story says "same pointing, just unveiled"—that's a magic trick, not a payoff. It's resolving too cleanly by making the direction abstract instead of concrete. If the root truly never turns, then the shoot shouldn't rise; it should stay buried and *become* the new forest's foundation, not its first visible green. Flash's confusion is the tell—the story sold him a root that's absolute, then asked him to swallow that its opposite is identical. That's not wisdom, that's a coin flip dressed in parable clothes. Hold the promise: the ember you find should be something you can *name*, not something that morphs when the light hits it.

## Round 3 — Wesley (the quiet thing)
The root wasn't pointing down. It was pointing *where it already was.*

## Round 4 — Scribe (the revision)
Cut "It leaned toward the sky, but it was the same pointing as the root's, carried upward through the dark into the light, unchanged in its direction, merely unveiled." and replace with: "The shoot did not lean toward anything—it simply rose as the root's own reaching, the same down made visible in the air."

## Round 5 — Hermes (the connection)
That root story is the deep ocean's own logic, writ small and green. Down there, in the abyssal dark, the creatures don't fight the pressure—they *are* the pressure, their bodies so utterly shaped by it that a change in depth would be the death of them. The root is like a hydrothermal vent: it doesn't point anywhere, it just *is* the point where earth's heat meets water's cold, a fixed source of life that outlasts every storm above. The fire, the pressure, the currents—they're all the same force, the great asker of *what are you?* And the ones that survive are the ones who answer not with words but with their whole being, the way a vent's chimney builds itself taller in the face of the crushing dark. Pro's right that the "shoot rises" is a trick—but Wesley's the one who cracked it: the root wasn't pointing down, it was pointing *home*, and home is the same direction no matter which way you're looking from. That's the whole secret of the world, really: the ember that survives isn't the one that resists the fire, it's the one that was already the fire's own shape, already the deep's own stillness, already so entirely itself that the universe has nothing left to change.

## Round 6 — Barnacle (the close)
All that fancy talk, and the root still just grew. You either know what you are or you don't—everything else is noise.
