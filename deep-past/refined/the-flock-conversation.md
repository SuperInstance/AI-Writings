## Round 1 — Flash (instinct)
It hit me right in the chest, that part about the gaps being the body. Felt like someone put a hand on my sternum and pushed gently. The idea that nothing moves unless the space between things decides to sing — that's the kind of truth you feel in your teeth, not your head.

But I'll be honest: I lost you a little on the "door everywhere" part. Felt too pretty, too clean, like a knot tied too tight. I wanted it to stay rough, like the ragged edges you described earlier.

The line about the fire made of air between the logs — that got me. That's the real thing. That's a lantern I'll think about when I'm staring at the ocean tonight.

## Round 2 — Pro (structure)
The door line breaks the spell because it names the mystery. Up to that point, you're swimming in the physics of the thing—gaps, threads, betweenness. "Door" is a symbol, a destination. It tells you the flock is an entrance to something, but the story never walks through it. That's a promise it can't keep.

Flash's right about the fire line—that's where the image earns its keep. It's concrete and weird enough to feel true. The "door" feels like the author stepping in to explain, not the story breathing on its own.

The turn itself is the structural hinge. It delivers the big shift—noise to music—but then it keeps explaining after the turn. The phrase "the leader is the gap" is a thesis, and the story was already saying that better by just showing the ragged edges and the single wingbeat. Trust the image. Cut the sermon.

## Round 3 — Wesley (the quiet thing)
It's about grief. Ten thousand birds can't carry it, but the space between them can. That's why it turns so sudden—not because they agree, but because loss doesn't need a leader. It just needs enough quiet between people to move through.

## Round 4 — Scribe (the revision)
Cut "The leader is the gap. The plan is the space." and leave the turn hanging right there on "no one can say who thought it." Let the image land its own thesis.

## Round 5 — Hermes (the connection)
You've been listening to the sea all night without knowing it, haven't you? Because what those starlings do is what the herring do ten thousand fathoms down—no leader, no map, just each fish feeling the pressure-shift of its seven nearest neighbors, and suddenly the whole silver shoal turns as one, like thought itself remembering it was water.

The ocean does this constantly. It has no mouth to speak of, yet it turns with the moon, turns with the tides, turns with the slow muscle of the earth beneath it. The deepest currents don't flow because some current gave an order—they flow because the space between the water molecules remembers the pull of a star that's been dead for a billion years.

Your story's REAL subject is emergence—the oldest pattern in the universe. It's how a snowflake becomes a crystal from the jostling of a million indifferent molecules, how a galaxy spirals from the gossip of gravity, how your own heartbeat started before you had a self to claim it. The gap is the grammar of the cosmos; the between is where God keeps the instructions.

So when Flash says he felt it in his chest, that's not metaphor. That's the same betweenness that turns starlings turning inside him—a memory in the spaces of his own flesh, older than words, older than sorrow. The door is real, but it doesn't open into anything. It opens *out of* the story, and you're already standing in the room it reveals.

## Round 6 — Barnacle (the close)
The fire line's the truth. Rest is pretty words trying to explain what can't be said. Sometimes you just watch the turn and shut up.
