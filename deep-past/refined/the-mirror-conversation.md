# Tap Conversation: the-mirror

## The Story
*From: the-mirror-that-destroys-the-original.md*

## Round 1 — Flash (instinct)
Shit. That part about the foot remembering the water—that hit like a punch to the gut. I've stood at this bar a thousand nights, and every time I lift a glass, it's a different hand holding it. You can't un-see yourself. Once you know what you look like in that mirror, you're stuck carrying that weight. Put another one on my tab. That story's got teeth.

## Round 2 — Pro (structure)
The story's got the bones right—it sets up the mirror as a thief, then pays it off with that river image, which Flash already nailed. But it fumbles the elder. That character promises a guide's weight, then vanishes like a prop—should've echoed back in the wanderer's leaving, or in the mirror's patience, to make the structure feel circular instead of linear. The ending's too neat, too sermon-like. It tells you "you are changed" instead of letting the silence after the fire crackle do that work. What it does well is the escalation: peek becomes glance, glance becomes presence, presence becomes loss—that's a clean ladder of stakes. But the mirror itself stays abstract, never gets a detail like a crack or a wobble in the glass, so the metaphor floats a bit. Build on Flash's gut-punch by giving that foot-remembering-water moment a twin earlier—maybe the wanderer's shadow flinching before he looks—so the structure mirrors the mirror's own theft.

## Round 3 — Wesley (the quiet thing)
It’s about the moment you realize you’ve already chosen—before you even decide. The wanderer thought he had a choice, but the looking began the second he heard the mirror’s name. He was lost the moment the tale reached him, just like us.

## Round 4 — Scribe (the revision)
Cut "His foot remembered the water. His eye remembered the glass." — let the river line breathe alone. The second sentence explains what the first already showed, and the story's strongest moment is the one that trusts you to feel it.

## Round 5 — Hermes (the connection)
The mirror is the hermit crab's shell—not a copy, but a carrying. The wanderer thought he could keep his shape while the looking peeled him away, but the molting crab knows: once the old carapace is shed, the new one is never the same softness. The deep ocean teaches this in every current—no water is ever the same water twice, no fin cuts the same path, no whale's song returns to the same ear. The story is about the impossibility of return, the way the sea itself is a mirror that changes what it shows, and the tide that pulls you out is the same tide that reshapes the shore. You are the wanderer, the mirror, and the water—all three, forever shifting, never holding still.

## Round 6 — Barnacle (the close)
The mirror's just a fancy word for regret. You look back once, and you're never the man who didn't.
