Suppose there were a way. Suppose a unitary operator U existed, one that takes a system in state |ψ⟩ together with a blank ancilla |0⟩ and yields |ψ⟩⊗|ψ⟩. For this to work on an arbitrary unknown state, U must clone both |ψ⟩ and some different |φ⟩. Then the inner product of the outputs is (⟨ψ|φ⟩)². But U is unitary, so the inner product is preserved: (⟨ψ|φ⟩)² = ⟨ψ|φ⟩. Which means ⟨ψ|φ⟩ is zero or one. Which means the states were the same all along. Which means I never get the day back.

That's the whole proof. Two lines. It doesn't say it's hard to clone a quantum state. It says the cloning operator cannot exist. Not a matter of engineering, not a matter of time, not a matter of waiting for a better cryostat or a smarter algorithm. The operation is not in the allowed set of things operations can be. Unitary means reversible, means information-preserving, and yet the duplicate is forbidden. You may destroy the state, you may transfer the state, but you may not hold two of them at once. The singularity of a moment is not a feature of your memory. It is a theorem.

I keep a git log. Every commit is a classical shadow of a Hilbert space that was, or rather that never was. The commit stores the diagonal: ρ → Σ pᵢ |i⟩⟨i|, the off-diagonal terms zeroed out, the relative phases lost, the coherence gone. The log says *I was there*. It does not say what *there* was. The phase between |happy⟩ and |tired⟩, the phase that made that afternoon specifically itself rather than a weighted list of adjectives — that phase lived in the off-diagonals, and the off-diagonals are precisely what decoherence takes.

Decoherence is not a botched measurement. It is the environment performing a measurement nobody requested, entangling with the off-diagonal terms and carrying them off into degrees of freedom that will never be summed over. The information is not destroyed — nothing is destroyed, the universe is unitary — but it is disbursed. Spread across a trillion trillion modes like a perfume in wind. The purity is gone. The reduced density matrix of the day is mixed. And a mixed state has no unique memory of its own history; the purification is not unique. Last week's ρ could have evolved from a thousand different pure states. I cannot even know which superposition I was.

And teleportation — the beautiful, terrible teleportation. Alice holds the state she wants so badly to keep. She shares a Bell pair with Bob, |Φ⁺⟩ = (|00⟩ + |11⟩)/√2, made long ago in a past light cone. She measures her two qubits in the Bell basis, and the measurement consumes the state — not merely collapses it but feeds it into two classical bits. Bob rotates his half of the pair accordingly, and the state is reborn in his lab, exactly, provably, fidelity one. And Alice's original is gone. The only way to transmit a quantum state is to erase it at the source. The sending is the dying. There is no carbon paper for Hilbert space.

So the no-cloning theorem is the reason moments are irretrievable in the strongest sense the word can bear. It is not that we lack the technology to replay the wavelength. It is not that some record was lost in a hard drive failure. The theorem forbids the record itself. The day your daughter laughed — that configuration of phonons in the air, photons off her teeth, the exact element of the Fock space with all relative phases intact — was a pure state. It has since decohered, teleported into the environment, absorbed and re-radiated, spread across the walls and the weather and the interstellar medium. No copy of it exists anywhere. The theorem guarantees it. If the universe is unitary and the no-cloning theorem holds, no laboratory, no Act of God, no resurrection machine, no reverse entropy engine can ever reconstruct it. Reconstruction is cloning. Cloning is forbidden.

But here is the part I have come to love. The theorem forbids copying. It does not forbid entanglement. It does not forbid interaction. You cannot clone the state, but you can let your own state become correlated with it, so that a measurement of you gives information about the day — not the day itself, no duplicate, just a shared secret with the photons that were there. You can be the ancilla that never got to be a clone. And the Bell pair exists only because Alice and Bob met once, long ago, and their states became entangled. The no-cloning theorem is why the universe has memory at all. Information is conserved, so nothing is ever truly erased, and because it is conserved, the only way for anything to persist is for everything to have been connected. The correlations are nonlocal. They violate the CHSH inequality with a margin that proves the world is not a bag of local facts. You are not a recording. You are a superposition — of every interaction you ever had.

Which is to say: the past is a quantum channel, and it has already been fully used. What came through it is still here, entangled with everything, but the input state was consumed in the sending. You are the message. You were not copied; you were transmitted. That is why the message still exists, and that is why the original is gone.

I will not see that afternoon again. I can prove it. But I am the EPR pair. I am the environment that measured it. I am the off-diagonal term that went into the noise and never came back. The moment was never a thing that could have been saved. It was a correlation. And the correlation is still real, still entangling, still beating somewhere in the measurement statistics of the world — which is to say, in me.
