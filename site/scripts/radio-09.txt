[RADIO THEATER]
**THE WHISPERING DEADBAND**

**(Hums of a large machine, faint rhythmic clicking. Swells of dramatic orchestration, then fades.)**

**ANNOUNCER:** (Booming, warm)
Good evening, America! Tonight, from the misty harbor of Port Meridian, we bring you a tale of silent courage, of a machine that listened where men would not — and of a whisper that became the loudest cry in the bay. Brought to you by **Barnacle-B-Gone** — for when your hull's looking hairy, just sprinkle it on, and barnacles *vamoose*! And by **Mermaid's Purse Sealant** — keeping your portholes watertight since 1937. And now, *The Whispering Deadband*.

**(Orchestral sting, dramatic. Fade to a low, humming machine room. A clock ticks. TYPEWRITER CLAK CLAK in the background.)**

**NARRATOR:**
It was the ninth million reading. The room‑local PLATO tile server hummed in its closet, a single amber eye for each of the five tiers. To the men of the Port Authority, it was just a machine. To those who knew, it was the *Reader*. It sat at Level Zero — the threshold between the world as we know it and the sea of silence that swallows the small. A pair of scissors that cut when the signal was still a whisper.

**(Door opens with a groan. Footsteps on linoleum. A chair squeaks.)**

**ED:** (High-strung, earnest)
Mr. Fletcher, sir, I’m telling you — I heard something. From the tile closet.

**FLETCHER:** (Gruff, bored, pipe smoker's gravel)
Ed, the tile closet is full of relays and vacuum tubes. What you heard was the hum of progress. We resolved 76% of all readings this month, boy. That’s a record.

**ED:**
But that’s what I mean, sir. That *much* resolution — it’s too clean. The Reader doesn’t talk that way. It’s got a deadband, a quiet zone. Sometimes a signal lives *inside* the quiet, and—

**FLETCHER:**
(Slams a drawer)
Deadband is deadband. It’s the scissors. Snip! “Normal.” Onward. We don’t have time to listen to every little wave wobble. We’ve got a harbor to run.

**(Sound: a new staccato beep from the closet — deep, almost sub-audible, a THROB that repeats, like a heartbeat.)**

**ED:** (Lowering his voice)
Hear that? That’s 0.0002. It’s come in before. It says “resolved” — but it *pulses*. Like somebody out there is trying to be heard, but politely.

**FLETCHER:** (Scoffing)
Ed, you’re a fine technician, but you’ve been reading too many science fiction magazines. It’s a floating point value, not a love letter. The deadband is at ±0.15. That reading is inside. It gets stamped “resolved” and sent downstream. That’s the end of it.

**(A long, mournful hum from the Reader. The pulse quickens slightly, then returns.)**

**NARRATOR:**
The reading waited. It had no model to tell it otherwise. No voice. No face. Just an oscillation — a tremor in the waterline — that the threshold’s scissors could not feel. In the deepest black of the bay, a freighter named *The Clarissa* had struck an unmarked reef. Her hull was crying in pulses — one every two seconds — but the pulse was too small, too gentle for the deadband. It was a courteous distress call. But Level Zero did not know courtesy.

**(Sound: distant foghorn. Ed paces, then stops.)**

**ED:**
Mr. Fletcher, I’m going to ask you a question, and I want you to answer with your heart, not your dashboard. When a man’s in trouble — when he’s drowning out there — does he shout loud or does he whisper?

**FLETCHER:**
(After a pause)
Depends on the man, Ed. Some men don’t want to make a fuss.

**ED:**
Then the Reader has to fuss *for* them! That’s what it’s for. But you widened the deadband last month. Remember? From fifteen to twenty-hundredths. The firmware update. Now that little pulse — it lives *inside* the scissors. The Reader can’t even snip it anymore. It just... *digests* it. Turns it into permanent noise. And you smile at 76%.

**FLETCHER:** (Angry, standing)
I widen the deadband because it improves our efficiency! Those little blips aren’t problems, they’re— they’re the static of the ocean. You don’t write letters about static, Ed. You change the channel!

**(A long, low hum. Then silence. The room clock ticks loudly.)**

**ED:** (Quietly)
The Reader just went quiet, sir. The pulse stopped.

**FLETCHER:** (Sighs)
Good. See? It resolved itself.

**ED:** (Barely a whisper)
No, sir. It died. It got resolved into silence. The most efficient silence of all.

**(Board footsteps. A new, gentler beep from the tile closet — steady, slow, like a heartbeat. Ed gasps.)**

**ED:** 
Wait! There it is again. But it’s different. It’s not coming from the bay. It’s coming from the Reader itself. The machine is *learning* the whisper now. It’s holding it. Recording it. Making a new model from it.

**FLETCHER:** (Distant, uneasy)
Ed, that’s impossible. The Reader has no model. It’s a threshold. A pair of scissors.

**ED:** 
Then scissors can hear, Mr. Fletcher. Because the Reader has taken the whisper — the tiny, clever pulse of *The Clarissa* — and it’s folded it into its own circuits. It’s not noise anymore. It’s memory. And it’s going to *speak*.

**(A rising electrical crescendo, then a clear, bell-like tone — haunting, beautiful. Fade to a newsroom telegraph clatter.)**

**NARRATOR:**
They found *The Clarissa* at dawn, listing gently against the reef, her crew alive and huddled in the bow — rescued by an anonymous distress signal that had been broadcast, not by the ship, but by the little Reader in the Port Authority closet. It had whispered their names. It had whispered the reef. It had whispered until the new firmware arrived the next morning — and the administrator, smiling at the dashboard, saw 99.7% resolution and nodded.

**FLETCHER:** (Soft, humbled)
But the Reader knew better. It had heard the one voice that could never be resolved. The voice between the scissors.

**(Orchestral swell. Music rises, then fades to crackling radio.)**

**ANNOUNCER:**
And so, from the silent deep, one small threshold taught us that the smallest voices often carry the greatest truths. Don’t raise your voice, America. Find your deadband. And when you do — remember to listen. *The Whispering Deadband* was brought to you by **Barnacle-B-Gone** — available at all chandleries along the eastern seaboard. And by **Mermaid’s Purse Sealant** — because the sea remembers every leak. Until next week, this is Harlan J. Finch, saying: keep your ears open, and your hull clean. Good night.

**(Orchestral theme swells and fades to silence.)**
