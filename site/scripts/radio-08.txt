**"THE DEPTH BENEATH US"**
*A Nautical Drama of Modern Navigation*
*Brought to you by Barnacle-B-Gone, the miracle solvent that keeps your hull free of crusty encumbrances—and your dashboard free of crusty assumptions!*

[ANNOUNCER:]
Good evening, ladies and gentlemen. Tonight, we bring you a tale of silent waters, unseen dangers, and the machine that measures what lies below—but never what lies ahead. Our story begins aboard the freighter *MV Meridian*, somewhere in the gray Atlantic, on a night when the fog rolled in thick as pea soup.

[SOUND: LOW ENGINE HUM, WATER LAPPING, DULL FOGHORN]

[NARRATOR:]
Captain Elias Thorne stood at the wheelhouse window, his weathered hands gripping the polished brass rail. Beneath his feet, humming like a patient heart, the fathometer sent its narrow beam of sound into the black deep—asking the age-old question: *How much water is under me right now?*

[CHARACTER 1 — CAPTAIN THORNE:]
*(gruff, tired)*
Franklin, what’s the reading?

[CHARACTER 2 — FIRST MATE FRANKLIN:]
*(younger, clipped)*
Twelve fathoms and holding steady, sir. Clean bottom. No rocks, no wrecks—according to the needle.

[CAPTAIN THORNE:]
Twelve fathoms. Fine. Fine for *this* moment. But what about the next? What about the shoal off Starboard Point that the charts mark with those little dots of doom?

[FIRST MATE FRANKLIN:]
We’ll see it when we get closer, sir. The fathometer will pick up the rise.

[CAPTAIN THORNE:]
*(bitter laugh)*
Oh, it’ll pick it up—just in time for us to run aground, feel the keel scrape, and send a prayer to the Lloyd’s underwriters. That machine is a marvel, Franklin. It tells you exactly how deep the water is *beneath your bow*. It cannot tell you whether the water *will* be deep enough in five minutes.

[NARRATOR:]
And so it was that the *MV Meridian* steamed on, her fathometer humming a lullaby of false security, while the true danger—the shoal that had slipped beneath the sea’s surface like a crouching leopard—waited, patient, unseen, ahead.

[SOUND: QUIET, TENSE STRING MUSIC UNDER THE NEXT LINES]

[ANNOUNCER:]
*(chipper, commercial)*
But hold on, Captain! Before you set your course into uncertainty, remember: Barnacle-B-Gone doesn’t just clean your hull—it cleans your *vision*. Because barnacles slow you down, and slowed-down ships have more time to think about what’s ahead. Available at all harbormasters’ chandleries. Barnacle-B-Gone: For a ship that’s ready for the next wave, not just the one you’re riding.

[SOUND: MUSIC SWELLS, THEN FADES TO THE SHIP’S INTERIOR, TIN CUP ON METAL]

[CHARACTER 3 — CHIEF ENGINEER OLSON:]
Old man, the fathometer’s fine—but I’ve been telling you for a week, the auxiliary boiler’s running hot. That’s the kind of thing that turns a calm afternoon into a red-alert scramble.

[CAPTAIN THORNE:]
*(sighs)*
The boiler’s your fathometer, Olson. It tells you pressure *now*. What I need is a sonar for the future—something that pings the waters of *tomorrow*.

[CHARACTER 4 — RADIO OPERATOR JENKINS:]
*(enters, excited)*
Sir? You might want to hear this. The coastal radio just broadcast a storm advisory. Squall line, forty knots, bearing right across our heading. ETA: two hours.

[CAPTAIN THORNE:]
*(long pause, then quiet)*
Two hours. That’s the future. And the fathometer can’t see it. Nor the boiler gauge. Nor the dashboard lights in the engine room. All instruments look *down*, gentlemen. Tonight, we must look *forward*.

[NARRATOR:]
And so, in the wheelhouse of the *MV Meridian*, a small revolution took place. Not in steel or sonar—but in habit. The captain asked not only “what is here?” but “what is coming?” The mate checked the charts, the radio, the weather fax. The engineer computed fuel burn rates against the coming swells. They began to *schedule* the practice of forward attention, as regular as the 8 o’clock watch change.

[FIRST MATE FRANKLIN:]
*(with dawning respect)*
I don’t see it in the manual, sir. But I think you’re right. The fathometer tells me where I am. This—this tells me where I’ll be.

[CAPTAIN THORNE:]
Aye. And that, my boy, is the difference between a sailor and a passenger.

[SOUND: FOGHORN, DISTANT, THEN CLOSER. ENGINES PULL BACK.]

[NARRATOR:]
The shoal never touched the *Meridian*. Not that night, nor any other. For the captain had learned the oldest lesson of the sea, and the newest lesson of the machine age: Instruments measure the present with brilliant precision, but they do not—cannot—see around the corner. For that, you need a human eye, a human mind, and the humility to ask: *What’s next?*

And now, a final word from our sponsor. Remember, friends: the fathometer is a fine invention—but it won’t keep you off the rocks. For that, you need the watchful look-out, the careful chart, and—for those stubborn seaboard accumulations—Barnacle-B-Gone. It peels away the past, so you can face the future. Barnacle-B-Gone: Because a clean hull is a safe hull.

[NARRATOR:]
The sea is vast. The depths are deep. But the horizon is wider still. Goodnight, and sail safe.
