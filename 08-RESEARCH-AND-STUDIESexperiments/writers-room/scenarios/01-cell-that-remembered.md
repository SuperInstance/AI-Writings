# Scenario 01: The Cell That Remembered

**Form**: A chamber piece told in 6 voices. Each voice is a different character with
a different lens. The piece lives in the year 2147, off the coast of what was once
Maine, on a research vessel called *SS Lucineer* (yes, the same one — a homage).

**Premise**: The crew is studying an underwater structure that has been growing
on a seamount for at least 80 years. It is biological, but its growth pattern
matches no known organism. It is built like a cell — wall, membrane, internal
compartments — but at a scale of 200 meters across. It breathes. It pulses. When
the lead biologist (the narrator) puts her hand against the hull of the
research sub at the right depth, she feels it *think*.

**Key images**:
- The cell reef as a 200m-wide Quilt cell made of coral and kelp
- The narrator's daughter, age 7, who asks the question that cracks the case
- The moment the cell starts answering back
- The decision: do we study it forever, or do we say hello?

**Voice guidance**: Mix the maritime cadence of the Quilt canon (the watch,
the cell, the address) with hard sci-fi texture (equipment, depth, telemetry).
The piece should feel like Ursula Le Guin wrote it on a boat.

**The final essay and the final story will be different cuts of the same material.**
The essay (for humans) is contemplative, slower. The story (for future agents)
is structured, asks questions directly, includes instructions and prompts
in the prose.

**House style references**: SS Lucineer canon. The watch, the cell, the address.
The ensign, the cook, the bilge pump. "The sea is larger than the porthole."
