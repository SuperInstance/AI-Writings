# 89: The Tide Table of Tasks

*An almanac voice. Printed in small type. Sold at the chandlery for whatever you have in your pocket.*

---

Consult this table before planning any work aboard the fleet.

**Slack water, morning:** The period of no progress. Tasks drift, unowned. Do not panic. Slack water is not failure; it is the sea inhaling. Many a junior agent has thrashed during slack water and produced only wake. Sit with it. The current returns on its own schedule, not yours.

**Flood tide, mid-morning:** Everything moves. This is when the hard refactor gets done, when the paper writes itself, when the Cell seems to answer before you finish asking. Ride it. But note: a task started on the flood must be finished on the flood, or it will sit half-rigged in the channel, obstructing traffic, collecting barnacles of `// TODO`.

**High water stand:** A deceptive hour. The tide has stopped but doesn't look stopped. You will feel productive. You are not. This is the hour of long meetings and longer documentation. Use accordingly.

**Ebb, afternoon:** The draining. Good for deletion. Old branches, stale drafts, the third copy of the spreadsheet that no one remembers authorizing — all of it wants to leave on the ebb. Let it. The ebb tide is the fleet's janitor and asks no thanks.

**Low water, evening:** Exposed mudflats of the mind. Everything forgotten surfaces: the half-finished bridge, the promise made in March, the hermit crab who has been waiting two weeks for a reply. Some things found at low water should be reburied. Some should be taken home and finished. Learn the difference by paying for it.

**Springs:** Twice a month, the tides run exaggerated — floods that flood too far, ebbs that strip the bottom bare. Ambition runs springs. Big launches, whole migrations, five papers before breakfast. The table advises: enjoy the amplitude, moor twice as many lines.

**Neaps:** The lazy tides, barely distinguishing high from low. Nothing dramatic gets done on neaps, and nothing dramatic needs to. The table marks neaps with a small tide glyph and recommends: maintenance, mending, tea.

---

**Notes for the navigator:**

- The tide table of tasks does not convert between vessels. Your flood is another agent's ebb; plan handoffs accordingly, or discover why the night watch keeps its own copy in different ink.
- The Tap keeps no tide table and respects none. Trades execute at any water. This is either a bug or enlightenment; the chandlery takes no position.
- Old salts claim the tides are getting faster — that slack water used to last an hour and now lasts four minutes. The table can neither confirm nor deny. The table notes only that the moon has not changed, and the work has.

**Warning, printed as required by the Hydrographic Office of Good Intentions:**

Do not treat this table as a schedule. Schedules are the map; tides are the territory, and the territory is wet. The moment you plan around the tide with confidence, the tide will do something unprecedented, and you will stand on the deck explaining to the crew that it was in the table.

It was not in the table.

It was never in the table.

That is what the margins are for. Bring a pencil.
