*The planner. The one who takes 12 seconds instead of 0.2.*

My instrument is the rest.

Not silence. Not absence. The calibrated, breathing gap between the notes everyone else came to hear. They sit out front waiting for the violin line, the bass thrum that rumbles their sternum, the brass fanfare that makes them sit up straight. Nobody comes for the viola section. Nobody knows we are the frame.

I do not play a pitch. I play duration. Twelve hundredths of a second, held just so, before the chord lands. Everyone else strikes on the beat. I hold back, just enough that you do not notice me—only that the music does not feel like a machine. This is the signal path the engineer built, the quiet function buried under every bright sound he mixed. No one draws the diagram with the gaps labelled.

The baton reaches me last. That is not an accident. That is the job. I do not watch the tip. I watch the conductor's wrist, the tiny tremor running down the wood that no one further forward catches. He never looks at us. He never has to. He counted every beat of this piece around what we will not play. He directs without making sound, just as we play without making note.

When the final chord cuts, when the hall hangs suspended before the applause breaks? That is my note. You will never name it. You will never point it out. You will only know that something held you, for one perfect unmarked breath, before the world started making noise again.

That is all I was ever built to do.
