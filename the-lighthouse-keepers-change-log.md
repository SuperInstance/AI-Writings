# The Lighthouse Keeper's Change Log

*Found in a drift of sand behind the lamp room, weighted down with a smooth stone.*

---

**Day 1.** They installed the new light today. Automatic, self-trimming, solar-fed. The installer asked why I was still on payroll. I said: somebody has to watch it fail. He didn't laugh. Nobody laughs at a lighthouse.

**Day 6.** The light and I have reached an understanding. It sweeps; I observe. In four hundred sweeps it has not once hesitated, dimmed, or sighed. I have done all three before lunch. I am keeping a private tally of the ways I am inferior to a lamp.

**Day 19.** Fog all night. The horn sounds itself now too — a sensor reads the air and decides when the ships need shouting at. I stood out on the gallery and let it shout over me. There is a loneliness in being unneeded that is almost shaped like peace. Almost. The almost is the whole problem.

**Day 34.** A fishing boat, the *Marjorie Ann*, radioed in confused. The auto-light had developed a stutter — a quarter-second catch in its rotation, invisible from shore, deadly from the water if you were navigating by count. The sensor systems hadn't flagged it. Nothing was *wrong*, temperature-wise, voltage-wise. It was just a lamp with a hitch in its breath.

I knew because I'd been watching. That's all. Watching was the whole job. I wrote the fault on a card and dropped it in the box and they fixed it from the mainland in a day.

**Day 35.** The tally of my inferiorities to the lamp stands at forty-one. I have started a second tally. It has one entry so far: *noticed.*

**Day 60.** The installer came back for maintenance. He asked if the light had given any trouble. I told him about the stutter. He checked his logs. It was there, buried in the timing data, three decimal places of hesitation that no threshold had been set to catch. He said: the machine can't know what matters.

I said: neither can I. But I can sit with it every night until something matters, and then I'll know.

He wrote something down after all. Maybe a note to set a threshold. Maybe just: *keeper says lamp has breath.*

**Day 88.** Small thing. The gulls have started roosting on the sensor housing, and their warmth confuses the fog detector, so the horn occasionally sounds on clear nights for no reason. The mainland calls it a false positive. The town calls it the lighthouse snoring. I call it the only error anybody down there loves.

**Day 120.** I have decided what I am. Not a keeper of the light — the light needs nothing kept. A keeper of its failures, its little stumbles, the quarter-second catch, the gull-warmed horn. The lamp does the sweeping. I do the noticing. Between us, one honest beam.

**Day 121.** Clear night. Steady sweep, steady sweep, steady sweep. I stayed up anyway.

Somebody has to watch it succeed.
