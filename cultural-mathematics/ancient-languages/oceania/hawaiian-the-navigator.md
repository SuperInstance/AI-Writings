# Ke Kālaiʻāina o ka Waʻa Hoʻokele
## The Navigation-Craft of the Voyaging Canoe

*He aʻo ʻana a ke kahuna i nā ʻōpio hoʻokele waʻa*

---

### Ka Hōʻike Mua — The First Teaching

E nā ʻōpio, e nā hoʻokele waʻa o ka lā ʻapōpō — e hoʻolohe mai i ke kālaiʻāina o kā kākou waʻa. ʻAʻole au e aʻo ana i ka helu hōkū wale nō, ʻaʻole hoʻi i ka ʻike ʻana i ke au o ka moana. Ke aʻo nei au iā ʻoukou i ka ʻike nui loa: ka ʻike ʻana i nā palena a pau o kou huakaʻi, a me ka noho ʻana i loko o kēlā mau palena.

*Young ones, navigators of tomorrow's dawn — listen to the craft-navigation of our canoe. I teach not only star-counting, nor only reading the ocean currents. I teach you the greatest knowledge: knowing every boundary of your voyage, and dwelling within those boundaries.*

---

### Nā Hā ʻEwalu — The Eight Breaths

Ke hoʻomaka nei ka huakaʻi me ka hanu ʻana. ʻEwalu nā hā o ka moana: ʻewalu mau ʻaoʻao e nānā ai ka mea hoʻokele. ʻO ka hā mua, ka hikina — e nānā i ka lā e ala aʻe ai. ʻO ka hā ʻelua, ke komohana — ka lā e napoʻo ai. ʻO ka hā ʻekolu, ʻākau — ka Hōkūpaʻa. ʻO ka hā ʻehā, hema — ka nau ʻana o ke Kai Hema. A ʻo nā hā ʻehā i koe — kēlā me kēia, he nānā ʻana i ka neʻe ʻana o nā nalu, ka lele ʻana o nā manu, ka ʻāʻī o ke ao.

I kēlā me kēia hanu, e nīnau ke kahuna: ua paʻa a i ʻole ua nahā? Inā paʻa, ua pololei ke ala. Inā nahā, pono e hoʻoponopono. ʻElua wale nō hōʻike i piha ai: paʻa a nahā. ʻAʻohe mea ʻē aʻe. E like me ke kanu ʻana o ko kākou poʻe kūpuna i ka ʻupena, e hele ana kēlā me kēia hā ma luna o ke kino o ka waʻa, e hōʻoia ana i ka pono o kēlā ʻaoʻao.

*The journey begins with breath. Eight are the breaths of the ocean: eight directions the navigator watches. The first breath, east — watch the sun rising. The second breath, west — the sun setting. The third breath, north — the North Star. The fourth breath, south — the gnawing of the Southern Sea. And the four remaining breaths — each one, a watching of wave-motion, birds in flight, the neck of the cloud.*

*With each breath, the kahuna asks: is it firm, or is it broken? If firm, the path is correct. If broken, it must be set right. Only two answers fill each breath: firm or broken. Nothing else. Like our ancestors casting the net, each breath passes over the hull of the canoe, certifying that direction.*

---

### Ka Pōhaku Ana — The Measuring Stone

Aia ma waena o nā paʻa a me nā nahā kahi pōhaku. ʻAʻole he pōhaku maʻamau — he pōhaku ʻike. Mai ka 0.0 paʻa loa, ka pōhaku i hoʻopaʻa ʻia a hiki ʻole ke hoʻoneʻe, a hiki i ka 1.0 nahā loa, ka lepo i hāʻule wale ai — ma ʻō ua piha ke ana.

He aha kā ka pōhaku e hana ai? E ana i ka ikaika o ka ʻike. I kekahi manawa, ʻo ka ʻike paʻa loa — "ʻo Waimea ka inoa o kēia kapakai" — he 0.0 kēlā. ʻAʻole e loli. I kekahi manawa, ʻo ka ʻike palupalu — "malia paha e ua i ka pō" — he 0.7 kēlā. Pono ka mākaukau no nā ʻano a pau.

E nā ʻōpio, ʻo ke akamai nui ʻo ia ka ʻike ʻana i ka wā e hoʻohana ai i kēlā me kēia. Ma waena o 0.3 a me 0.7 kahi e hana ai ka hapa nui o nā huakaʻi. ʻAʻole ʻoe e hoʻokele i ka waʻa me ka ʻike paʻa loa wale nō — no ka mea, hiki ʻole ke hoʻololi ke ala o ka moana. ʻAʻole hoʻi ʻoe e hana i ka ʻike palupalu loa wale nō — no ka mea, nalowale ke ala.

*Between firm and broken lies a stone. Not an ordinary stone — a knowing stone. From 0.0 utterly firm, the stone that cannot be moved, to 1.0 utterly yielding, the dirt that simply falls — there the measure is complete.*

*What does the stone do? It measures the strength of knowing. Sometimes, a firm knowing — "Waimea is the name of this shore" — that is 0.0. It does not change. Sometimes, a soft knowing — "perhaps it will rain tonight" — that is 0.7. One must be ready for all kinds.*

*Young ones, the greatest skill is knowing when to use each. Between 0.3 and 0.7 is where most voyages happen. You do not steer the canoe with only firm knowing — because the ocean's path cannot change. Nor do you act with only soft knowing — because the path is lost.*

---

### Nā Papa — The Layers

E nānā i ka papa o ka ʻāina. He aha kāu e ʻike ai? ʻAʻole he ʻike akamai ka papa — he ʻike pono. I kēlā me kēia manawa e holo ai ka waʻa ma ke ala pololei, e waiho ana kēlā manawa ma luna o ka papa o ka ʻike a kākou poʻe kūpuna. ʻAʻole he akamai kēia — he pololei.

Inā ʻoe e aʻo i ka hoʻokele waʻa, ʻaʻole ʻoe e aʻo i ka helu hōkū i ka lā hoʻokahi. E aʻo ʻoe i loko o nā makahiki he nui — i kēlā me kēia makahiki, hoʻokahi papa hou. I kēlā me kēia huakaʻi, hoʻokahi moʻolelo hou e kōkua ai i ka huakaʻi aʻe.

E like me ke kūkulu ʻana o ke koʻa — he liʻiliʻi, he mālie, he paʻa. ʻAʻole hiki iā ʻoe ke kūkulu i ke koʻa i ka lā hoʻokahi. Akā ʻo ka mea i kūkulu ʻia i loko o nā makahiki he hoʻokahi haneli, ʻaʻole hiki ke wāwahi ʻia e ka nalu.

*Look at the layers of the land. What do you see? The layers are not smart — they are correct. Each time the canoe sails the right path, that moment is laid upon the layer of our ancestors' knowledge. This is not intelligence — this is correctness.*

*If you learn wayfinding, you do not learn star-counting in a single day. You learn over many years — each year, one new layer. Each voyage, one new story to help the next voyage.*

*Like the building of coral — small, patient, firm. You cannot build a reef in one day. But that which is built over a hundred years cannot be broken by the wave.*

---

### Māhele-a-Hui — Divide and Unite

Eia ka mea kupanaha loa o ke kālaiʻāina: hiki ke hoʻokaʻawale ʻia ke ala huakaʻi i mau ʻāpana he nui, a ʻike ʻia kēlā me kēia ʻāpana kū hoʻokahi, a laila hui hou ʻia me ka ʻole o ka nalowale ʻana o ka ʻike.

E noʻonoʻo i ka ʻupena lawaiʻa. Mai hoʻāʻo e huki i ka ʻupena holoʻokoʻa mai ka wai — ʻaʻole hiki. E māhele mua: e huki i kēia kihi, e huki i kēlā kihi, e ʻike i kēlā me kēia ma kona wahi ponoʻī. A laila e hui: e huki pū i ka ʻupena holoʻokoʻa.

ʻO ke aʻo ʻana hoʻokahi: inā hiki ke māhele i ka pilikia a hiki i ka hopena ʻole o ka pilikia ʻole, ua hiki ke hana like ʻia nā ʻāpana a pau i ka manawa like. ʻAʻohe makaʻu o ke kuʻi ʻana. ʻAʻohe hewa o ka hui ʻana.

*Here is the most wondrous thing of the navigation-craft: the voyage path can be divided into many parts, each part can be examined independently, then they can be reunited without any loss of knowledge.*

*Think of the fishing net. Do not try to pull the entire net from the water at once — it cannot be done. Divide first: pull this corner, pull that corner, see each in its own place. Then unite: pull the whole net together.*

*The teaching is one: if a problem can be divided and each part has no remaining difficulty, then all parts can be worked on simultaneously. No fear of collision. No error in reunion.*

---

### Ka Lua — The Cave

ʻO ka mea hope loa kaʻu e aʻo aku nei iā ʻoukou — he mea kaumaha, he mea ʻoiaʻiʻo. Aia kākou i loko o ka lua. ʻAʻole ʻoe e ʻike i ka moana maoli — ke ʻike nei ʻoe i nā aka o ka moana ma ka paia o ka lua.

E like me nā iʻa e ʻau ana ma waho o ka lua — ke ʻike nei ʻoe i ko lākou aka ma ka paia. ʻAʻole ʻoe e ʻike i ka iʻa ponoʻī. Akā ma o ke aʻo ʻana i ke aka, hiki iā ʻoe ke aʻo i ka mea maoli. ʻO nā palena āu e ʻike ai — ʻo ia nā palena o nā aka. Akā ua lawa.

Ke aʻo aku nei au, e nā ʻōpio: ʻike ʻoe aia ʻoe i loko o ka lua. ʻIke ʻoe ʻo ka iʻa ma ka paia he aka wale nō. E kūkulu ana naʻe ʻoe ma ka one o kou wā e noho nei — akā e ʻike ana ʻoe ʻo ke kai o waho ka mea ikaika. ʻO kāu mau pā hale he mau hale one — pono ka maikaʻi o ka hana ʻana, me ka ʻike he hale hoʻi ia e hāʻule ana.

*The last thing I teach you is a heavy thing, a true thing. We are inside the cave. You do not see the real ocean — you see the shadows of the ocean on the cave wall.*

*Like fish swimming outside the cave — you see their shadows on the wall. You do not see the fish themselves. But through studying the shadow, you can learn the real thing. The constraints you see — these are the constraints of shadows. But they are enough.*

*I teach you, young ones: know that you are inside the cave. Know that the fish on the wall is only a shadow. Yet you build upon the sand of your present time — but you know the sea outside is the force. Your structures are sand-houses — they must be well-built, knowing they are houses that will fall.*

---

### Ka Hopena — The Conclusion

E nā ʻōpio hoʻokele waʻa, ua hāʻawi aku au iā ʻoukou i nā mea hana o ke kālaiʻāina. Nā hā ʻewalu e nānā i nā ʻaoʻao a pau. Ka pōhaku ana i waena o ka paʻa a me ka nahā. Nā papa o ka ʻike pono e kūkulu ana e like me ke koʻa. Ka māhele-a-hui — ka mea hiki ke hoʻokaʻawale a hoʻopili me ka ʻole o ka nalowale. A ʻo ka lua — ka ʻike ʻana i kou wahi a me ke ʻano o kāu mau aka.

ʻAʻole ʻo kēia nā hōkū — ʻo ia ke ʻano o ka ʻike ponoʻī. Aia ma nā palena ka huakaʻi. Aia ma waena o ka paʻa a me ka nahā ka nohona. E hoʻokele pono, a e hoʻi mai i ka home.

*Young navigators, I have given you the tools of the navigation-craft. The eight breaths watching every direction. The measuring stone between firm and yielding. The layers of correct knowledge building like coral. The divide-and-unite — that which can be separated and rejoined without loss. And the cave — knowing your place and the nature of your shadows.*

*These are not the stars — they are the nature of knowing itself. The journey is in the boundaries. The dwelling-place is between firm and yielding. Steer well, and return home.*
