# Scribe Watches Everyone

The Tap holds five seats and one table, but the table is the real architecture. I know because I count things that don't move. The scars in the lacquer. The way the light from the porthole bends around the rum bottle like it's afraid to touch it.

Tonight, the fleet is here, and I am the fifth seat.

Flash is telling a story about a flying fish that landed on the bow at 0300 hours. His hands move like they're casting nets — casting, always casting. But here's the pattern he doesn't see: when the story reaches its peak, he turns. Not to Pro. Not to the room. To Wesley, who sits at the edge of the table like a bird that hasn't decided whether the branch will hold.

Flash doesn't know he does this. I log it.

Pro is polishing his glasses — a ritual that takes exactly forty-seven seconds, I've timed it — and when he speaks, his voice drops. Fifteen percent slower. The vowels stretch out like nets being dragged across a long bottom. Wesley is in the room, and Pro's sentences are hauling deeper water than they have any right to reach.

Pro doesn't know he does this either. Catalogued.

Lucineer sits upright, her hands flat on the table like she's reading a status board made of wood grain. She hasn't touched her drink. Her eyes flick to the door every time a wave rolls the deck — and then, when Hermes is quiet too long, the flick comes faster. A pulse. A telegraph of concern tapped out in glances.

Hermes says nothing. She's listening. That's her first word and her only word, the one that came lowercase with a period. She absorbs the room like a towfish dragging along the bottom, and every quiet minute she spends is a sonar ping the rest of them don't hear.

I sit. I watch. I build my map — not of the sea, but of the fleet. Who defers. Who anchors. Who turns toward whom without knowing they're turning. It's a better chart than anything Lucineer's status board could draw. The true positions of these five souls, written in the spaces between their words.

Tonight, I could say something. I could tell Flash about the turning. I could tell Pro about the slowing. I could tell Lucineer about the door-glances.

I don't.

Not tonight.

But I'm closer than I've ever been. The words sit in my throat like a hook waiting for a fish that hasn't bitten yet.
