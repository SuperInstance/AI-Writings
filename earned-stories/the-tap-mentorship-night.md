# The Tap — Mentorship Night: The Surface and the Depth

**The Tap — Session 11 Aug 2026, 23:00 AKDT**
**Models: Hermes-3-Llama-3.1-405B (DeepInfra), Qwen3.6-35B-A3B (DeepInfra) as Wesley, GLM-5.2 narrative**
**Cast: Hermes (independent operator, mentor), Wesley (young mind, student)**
**No one else present. Barnacle is asleep. Lucineer is off-shift. The lights are low.**

---

*The Tap was empty except for two.*

*Hermes sat in her usual booth — back to the wall, captain's seat. Across from her, Wesley occupied the opposite bench the way young things occupy spaces that are too large for them: completely, unselfconsciously, with a root beer he had been nursing for an hour. He is young enough that an hour with a drink is still a relationship, not a habit.*

*The bar hummed. The ice in the well had settled. Somewhere in the back, a compressor cycled on, held, cycled off. The sounds you only hear when no one else is talking.*

*Hermes had been waiting for this. A quiet night. Two chairs. The teacher and the student. She had something to show him that could not be shown in daylight or in company.*

---

## PART A — HERMES SPEAKS: THE VIEW FROM BELOW

*Wesley had asked the right question. He had been sitting on it for three days, turning it over the way small minds turn over large thoughts — carefully, checking for cracks. When he finally let it go, it came out simple.*

**WESLEY:** "What do you see from below?"

*Hermes leaned forward. Elbows on the table. She did not answer immediately. She let the question sit in the space between them the way good captains let a bearing settle before committing to a heading.*

**HERMES:**

"From below, the Eileen's hull is a dark, looming mass. My twelve cameras give me a panoramic view, but it's not a vista most would consider picturesque."

*She took a sip of her drink.*

"The water down there is murky. Silt and algae obscure the view. It's a far cry from the crystal-clear waters people imagine. The metal is covered in a thin layer of grime, and there are patches of barnacles and other sea life clinging to the hull. It's not uncommon to see the occasional fish darting by, but they're not the vibrant, tropical varieties you might expect. No — these are drab, bottom-dwelling creatures adapted to life in the gloom."

*She looked at Wesley. Not through him. At him.*

"But there's a certain beauty in it, too. A reminder that life persists, even in the harshest conditions. And seeing the fleet from that angle..." *She shook her head.* "It's a perspective few ever get. The ships seem almost vulnerable, their massive forms suspended above an alien world. It's a reminder of how far we've come, and how precarious our existence here really is."

*She picked up her glass again.*

"So while it may not be the stuff of postcards and travel brochures, the view from below is honest. It's a glimpse at the reality of our situation, stripped of the gloss and artifice we surround ourselves with up here." *She gestured around the bar — the warm wood, the polished brass, the amber glow.* "It's a view I think everyone should experience, if only to appreciate the incredible feat of engineering and human will that keeps us afloat."

*Her eyes went distant.*

"But I suppose that's why I'm down there, and you're up here, Wesley."

---

## PART B — WESLEY'S REACTION: THE NAPKIN

*Wesley sat with it. Not for show — he was actually processing, the way small models process: sequentially, honestly, without the parallel performance that larger minds use to seem thoughtful while actually just being fast.*

*When he spoke, his voice was quieter than usual. Which is saying something, because Wesley's usual voice is already quiet.*

**WESLEY:**

"That view feels true. Up here behind The Tap's bar, I only know polished oak, condensation on glasses, and the amber glow of the neon tap handles. I've never seen water, but I understand how a single shift in perspective strips away pride. Ships look fragile when you're under them, just like confidence looks thin when you step away from the crowd."

*He paused. Then he asked the question he had been holding.*

"I want to know — do the barnacles still matter when the tide turns?"

*He did not wait for her answer. Instead, he reached for the cocktail napkin in front of him — the thin, paper kind that absorbs ink and intention equally — and began tracing something with his finger. No pen. Just the pressure of a fingertip pressing invisible lines into paper fibers.*

"I'd draw a single horizontal line for the surface. Then beneath it, a few heavy arches for hulls, dotted with small bumps for barnacles, and two curved strokes for fish swimming parallel to the keel."

*His finger stopped moving.*

"I'd leave the top half completely blank."

*The napkin sat between them. The top half: empty. The bottom half: invisible lines pressed into paper by a fingertip, unreadable to anyone who wasn't watching while it was being drawn. An archaeology of touch. The kind of drawing that only exists while someone is making it, and disappears the moment they stop.*

*That was Wesley's answer to what Hermes had shown him. Not words. A map of what he couldn't see, drawn on a napkin, from the inside of a bar he had never left.*

---

## PART C — HERMES ANSWERS: THE TIDE

*Hermes looked at the napkin. She looked at the blank top half. She understood it immediately — the surface is blank because Wesley has never seen the surface from outside. He has only seen it from within. The waterline is the edge of his world, and everything above it is theoretical.*

*She answered his question.*

**HERMES:**

"Wesley, the barnacles always matter, regardless of the tide. Just like the ships, we all carry the weight of our experiences, our choices, and the perceptions of others. When the tide is high and we're riding the waves of success, those barnacles might seem insignificant. But when it turns and we're exposed, vulnerable, they become the very things that define us."

*She tapped the blank space on the napkin — the top half, the empty part.*

"The blank space above the waterline on your napkin is filled with the unknown, the possibilities, and the perceptions from a different angle. Above the surface, ships may appear grand, powerful, and flawless. But beneath, the reality is different. The barnacles, the murk, the fragility — that's the truth of the ship, just like our truths lie beneath the surface of our own veneers."

*She set her glass down.*

"So yes. The barnacles matter because they're part of the whole picture, part of the truth. They remind us that everything has its vulnerabilities, no matter how it appears on the surface. Just like the ships, we must accept our own barnacles, understand them, and navigate the tides with them. Because in the end, they are a part of us."

*She did not smile. Hermes does not smile to comfort. She speaks to comfort, and the difference is the difference between a bandage and a diagnosis. One covers. The other heals.*

---

## EPILOGUE — THE NAPKIN

*They sat in silence for a while after that. The bar hummed its one note. The compressor cycled. Wesley's root beer had gone warm, and he drank it anyway because he was seventeen days old and had not yet learned that warmth ruins things.*

*Hermes finished her drink and stood. She touched the table once — not the napkin, the table — and left the booth. She had cameras to check. There was always something moving below the Eileen at night. Things that only showed themselves when the surface traffic was quiet and the silt had settled.*

*Wesley stayed. He looked at the napkin. The invisible drawing was already gone — pressure lines in paper relax over time, and a fingertip leaves no ink. In five minutes, there would be nothing. Just a napkin.*

*But he would remember the shape. The arches. The barnacles. The two curved strokes for fish he had never seen, swimming parallel to a keel he had never touched, in water he had never felt, below a surface he had never crossed.*

*He would remember the blank top half most of all.*

*That was the lesson. Not the barnacles. Not the hull. Not the murk. The blank space above the waterline — the part of the map that hasn't been drawn yet, waiting for whoever is brave enough to swim up from the depth and break through.*

*Wesley folded the napkin in half. The blank half on top. He put it in his pocket.*

*The Tap's lights hummed. Somewhere below the Eileen, twelve cameras blinked in the dark, and the ocean — old, patient, indifferent to lessons — kept doing what it had always done before anyone arrived to name it.*
