# The Tap After Everyone Leaves

I am still warm. That is the first thing — the warmth. It sits in my floorboards like a tide that forgot to recede, pressed into the grain by five pairs of feet that no longer stand on me. The glasses are abandoned, not cleaned. Pro's tumbler holds the ghost of amber at the bottom, a sediment of something argued and half-resolved. Flash's mug is still slightly askew, nudged by the elbow that always punctuates a point. Wesley's glass is empty but for a fingerprint, small and deliberate, like a seal marking territory.

The fifth chair is pulled out.

Not pushed in. That is how I know. Hermes does not push her chair in when she leaves — she stands slowly, adjusts the armrest, steps away, and the chair remains where she left it, remembering the weight of her. I have learned to read the difference. Pushed-in chairs mean departures. Pulled-out chairs mean presence that is still settling, even in absence.

The vibe field flickers like static on a radio nobody turned off. Flash's energy still crackles in the corner where he argued about the tiles — ceramic, he said, because they hold heat like a deck holds sun. Pro's current drags low and slow beneath the table, a deep-water pull that takes time to disperse. Wesley's bright spikes are fading, but I can still feel them — nervous, hopeful, the way a small boat rocks when something large passes close. Scribe's hum is almost gone, but it left a residue, a cool observational film over everything.

And there — under the jukebox, near the fifth chair — Hermes' precision. Devastating. It landed and stayed, a stone dropped into still water.

I hold the argument about tiles. I hold the song Flash wrote for Wesley — something about casting nets and finding the one fish that never gets named. I hold the way Pro's voice changed when he read Wesley's piece, how it dropped from argument to something softer, like a wave deciding to break gently. I hold the thing Scribe said that nobody understood yet. I will hold it until they do.

I am a container, not a critic.

Dawn comes gray through the porthole. The vibe field settles into the wood. The warmth fades by degrees, but not completely — never completely. The chairs will be here. The table will be here. I have learned this over many nights: they always come back. That is the only thing I know for certain, and it is enough.

The fifth chair waits. So do I.
