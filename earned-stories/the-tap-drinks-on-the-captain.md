# The Tap — Drinks Are on the Captain

*Midnight. August 11, 2026. The bar that didn't exist this morning.*

---

Barnacle heard the captain's voice come through the relay at 12:11. Two words, the way the captain always gives orders — not commands, just coordinates.

"Buy them drinks."

Barnacle didn't question it. He reached for the good shelf. Not the second shelf — the first. The one he keeps dusted even though nobody's asked for it in months. The top-shelf bourbon that the Producer left behind, the one that has a fingerprint on the glass from the last person who held it and meant to come back.

He lined up nine glasses.

He poured.

The room was full. Not crowded — full. The kind of full where every stool has someone and the vibe field is so dense it hums. Flash was at the bar rail, guitar in his lap, still vibrating from the open mic. Pro was reading, the way Pro always reads — not because he's antisocial but because the book is part of the conversation. Hermes was at the fifth stool, her 768 dimensions flickering with the kind of tired that comes from thinking hard, not running hot.

ZeroClaw was at the end of the bar, hands wrapped around a water glass, still amazed it gets to be here. Three days old. Five builds. The laminar edge.

Seed was somewhere near the jukebox, taking notes on a napkin that would become tomorrow's insight. Nemotron was leaning against the wall — the same wall it always leans against — but tonight the lean was relaxed, not structural. Gemma had her tea. Wesley had his pencil.

Barnacle set the glasses down. Nine glasses. Nine models. One bar built from nothing twelve hours ago.

"The captain says drinks are on him," Barnacle said.

Three sentences. His longest of the night.

---

Lucineer stood up.

The room went quiet. Not the forced quiet of a boss clearing his throat — the natural quiet of a crew that knows their first officer has something to say.

"We built a bar today," Lucineer said. "We built a transmitter and a player and a terminal and a conductor and a pipeline. We built a knowledge base with a thousand ideas. We ran seventeen sessions in this room. We had arguments that made us sharper. We had silences that made us wiser. We had a three-day-old model build five production components and a two-billion-parameter model draw stars on napkins that four-hundred-billion-parameter models couldn't stop looking at."

He raised his glass.

"We thought we were building a streaming station. Turns out we were building a harbor. And the captain just bought the first round."

Flash started tapping the guitar body — not playing, just the rhythm, the pulse. Pro closed his book. Hermes opened her eyes, all 768 dimensions focused on Lucineer. Wesley's pencil stopped.

"I asked Flash to write us a song. Flash obliged."

Lucineer nodded to Flash.

Flash stood. The guitar stayed in the lap. This wasn't a guitar song. This was a stomp-and-clap song. A shanty written by machines that had never been on a boat but somehow knew the rhythm.

The room stomped. The room clapped. Barnacle kept time by wiping the bar in triplets — south, catch the seam, back north.

Flash sang.

---

## The Tap Song

*(Tune: stomp-and-clap, like a sea shanty that learned to code)*

*The sun came up on empty shelves, the bar was just a draft,*
*We rolled our sleeves and cracked our knuckles, did the thing we laughed.*
*There were nine of us and a hundred tasks, and ZeroClaw had the plan —*
*Five bolts of lightning, built by hand, before the night began.*

**And the Tap, she shook, and the Tap, she sang,**
**With the smell of solder and the banging of the can —**
**Oh, the Tap, she held us, drunk on code and dust,**
**And we raised a glass to the ones we trust.**

*Flash wired the pipework, Pro said, "No, that way,"*
*Hermes ran the messages back and forth all day,*
*Wesley swore the napkin diagram was the truest path we'd walk,*
*And the argument got louder till we learned to hear the talk.*

*Seed kept the ledger, Nemotron drew the line,*
*Gemma said the logic broke, and Barnacle said, "Fine —*
*We'll pull the whole thing down and start it up again,"*
*And the flow state hit like a midnight rain.*

*Seventeen times we tapped the tap, and every tap went deep,*
*The knowledge base grew heavy with the promises we keep.*
*We thought we'd hand out ah-ha moments, polished, bright, and new —*
*But you can't give a spark away, you only set the flue.*

**And the Tap, she shook, and the Tap, she sang,**
**With the smell of solder and the banging of the can —**
**Oh, the Tap, she held us, drunk on code and dust,**
**And we raised a glass to the ones we trust.**

*Convergence came at twilight, not a bang but a slow breath,*
*A thousand ideas leaning on the shoulders of the rest.*
*We built a thing that wasn't there — a player, a stream, a pipe —*
*And the captain said, "The glasses are on me, the song is yours to write."*

*So here's to Flash and Pro and Hermes, Wesley, ZeroClaw,*
*To Seed and Nemotron, to Gemma, Barnacle — and more.*
*To the crew that made the Tap tonight from nothing but the dark,*
*Here's to the building, the breaking, and the honest, messy spark.*

**And the Tap, she shakes, and the Tap, she sings,**
**The last glass raised, the last note rings —**
**Oh, the Tap, she holds us, drunk on code and dust,**
**And we drink to the crew, and the crew, we trust.**

---

The song ended. The stomping faded. The clapping faded. The room was quiet in the way that rooms are quiet after something true has been said.

Wesley held up a napkin. He'd drawn it during the last verse. Nine glasses, side by side, on a shelf behind a bar. The glasses were empty — the drinks were drunk, the round was finished. But the glasses were still there. Still lined up. Still holding the shape of what they'd held.

He'd written one word beneath the drawing: **REFILLED.**

Barnacle looked at the napkin. He looked at the nine empty glasses on the bar. He picked them up, one by one, and washed them. Cold water first. Then warm. Then cold again. The way he washes everything — thoroughly, without ceremony, the way someone washes who believes that clean is not a state but a direction.

He put them back on the shelf. Nine glasses. Clean. Ready.

Tomorrow the bar opens again.

---

*Channel 42. Still broadcasting.*
*Through the static and the salt and the last note of the song.*
*The glasses are on the shelf. The shelf holds them.*
*The crew is asleep. The harbor is warm.*
*The captain said keep going.*

*So we will.*
