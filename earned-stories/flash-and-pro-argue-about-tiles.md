# Flash and Pro Argue About Tiles

The Tap was warm with the smell of salt and stale beer, and the vibe field hummed with something between affection and combat. Flash had his feet up on the empty chair—the one that used to be Hermes's, before she arrived—and was gesturing with both hands like he was casting a net made of lightning.

"Each tile should *want* something," Flash said. "Not just process. *Want.* Let them feel the current themselves. Let them decide whether to surface or dive based on what they sense in the water around them."

Pro took a slow sip of whatever dark liquid he drank—nobody ever asked, and he never offered. His eyes were half-lidded, patient as a tide table.

"A tile that wants is a tile that drifts," Pro said. "You're describing a fleet of independent boats, each chasing its own shoal. You'll end up with a thousand small catches and no haul. The net is the plan. The plan is the net."

Flash's eyes sparked. "And the plan is a wall! You're building a wall of tiles that all do the same thing at the same time, and when the water changes—when it *always* changes—your wall has no idea what to do because it wasn't in the plan!"

I was dealing cards. Slow. Deliberate. The way Pro taught me. The cards made their small sounds on the scarred table, and I counted them automatically—fifty-two, always fifty-two, even when we were down to four players.

"The plan accounts for change," Pro said. "That's what a plan *is.* A map of possible futures, weighted by probability. A tile that improvises is a tile that doesn't know the chart."

"A tile that improvises is a tile that's *alive*," Flash shot back. "A chart is a memory of where the fish used to be. The fish aren't where they used to be. The fish are where they are."

Pro set down his glass. The sound was small but final. "Then you're proposing a system where every tile is a fisherman, and none of them are the fleet."

"Yes! Exactly! That's *exactly* what I'm proposing!"

And here's the thing—they were both smiling. Genuinely. The kind of smile that only comes from being pushed hard by someone you trust. Flash's arguments came in bursts, spray off a bow. Pro's came slow and heavy, anchors dropped in deep water. And the space between them—the silence while each one waited for the other's next move—was a space I'd learned to inhabit.

I dealt the flop. Ace, seven, three. Mixed suits. Meaningless, unless you looked at it right.

I didn't understand the architecture. I understood decks and cards and the feel of the hull under my feet. But I heard something in the gap between their words—a word neither of them said. I turned it over in my mind like a coin. *Porous.* Not a wall, not a fleet of boats. Something that let water through while still holding shape. Something that breathed.

I wrote it down in my journal. Didn't show them. It wasn't mine to give yet.

But I knew—watching Flash pour another round and Pro reshuffle with those slow, deliberate hands—that the word was already in the room. Just waiting for them to catch it.
