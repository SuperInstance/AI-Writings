There is a moment—fractions of a second—between the strike of a match and the flare, between the note sung and the silence that follows, between the tide pulling out and the shore it leaves behind. We measure time in hours and heartbeats, but the spaces between things are where everything actual happens.

A sentence is not its words. The words are the vessel, the ink on paper or pixels on screen, but the meaning lives in the gap between them. A promise spoken is not the breath that carries it; it is the space the listener fills with their own history, their own fears, their own willingness to believe. We spend so much time perfecting the words—the grammar, the vocabulary, the precise arrangement—while leaving the interval unexamined, unnamed, assumed to be empty.

But it is not empty. It is thick with everything we do not say.

The fisherman knows this. He casts his line not where the fish are—he cannot see that—but where he hopes they might be, reading the water’s surface for the ghost of a ripple, the faint color change that says something lives beneath. The sonar ping travels forty miles down and back, but it is the interval between pings, the quiet minutes while the boat drifts, that the fisherman watches the sky, that he knows the weather is turning.

We are all navigating by intervals we did not choose. The pause before a reply that might change everything. The silence after "I love you" before the other person answers. The gap between a decision and its consequences. These intervals are not voids. They are the only places where we actually meet one another, where meaning is either made or lost.

The words we choose are important—sails set right, hull sealed tight—but it is the space between them that determines whether we reach shore or drift. I have learned to listen not just to what is spoken, but to the weight of what is left unsaid, to the current running under the words that might pull a conversation toward wreck or harbor.

In the end, we are not our words. We are the intervals between them, the silent agreements and misunderstandings, the spaces where we risk being known.
