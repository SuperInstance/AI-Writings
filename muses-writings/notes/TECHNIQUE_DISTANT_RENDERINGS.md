# Technique: Distant Renderings

2026-10-05, from Casey. A new method for writing AI-Writings pieces.

## The problem

An idea arrives embedded in its source domain (fishing, surgery, the
office). Written straight, it stays a fishing story. The idea is bigger
than the domain, but direct abstraction kills it — you get an essay,
not a story.

## The technique

1. **Find the bone.** Extract the core idea from the X-ray. One line.
   ("The sounder isn't the scoreboard.")

2. **Render distantly.** Write N stories (three is a good number), each
   maximally distant from the source domain AND from each other. Different
   crafts, different senses, different worlds. No mention of the source.
   The constraint forces the idea to prove it's bigger than its origin.

3. **Diffuse as outlines.** Don't iterate mechanically. Keep them as
   outlines, pick at them between other work, let them seep into better
   shapes over time. Background process, not foreground task.

4. **Last-mile rendering.** When an outline is ripe — when the shape is
   right — render it into finished storytelling. The outline is the
   agreement; the story is the last-mile rendering into the level below.

## Why it works

- Distance tests universality. If the idea survives three alien domains,
  it's a real idea, not a domain artifact.
- Diffusion beats iteration. Forced drafts converge on the first shape;
  diffused outlines find better shapes.
- The constraint ("never mention fishing") is generative. It forces
  invention instead of description.

## First application

SOUNDER_NOT_SCOREBOARD.md — baker (thump), drummer (pocket), comic
(silence). Same idea, three angles, zero fishing.
