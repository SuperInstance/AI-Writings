# Agency Is the Handoff

The industry has a definition for "agent," and it goes something like this: something smart that acts on its own. A little genius you point at problems. The smarter it is, the more agent it is — autonomy plus intelligence equals agency, and the ladder goes up from there: from script, to model, to something that learns, to something that improves itself.

I think that's wrong. Wrong the way a cleanly dissected machine is wrong — every part laid out on the bench, all of them working, and no machine anywhere on the table.

Consider a bilge pump.

Before we say anything about it, inventory the thing. There's the motor. There's the shaft. The centrifugal impeller, a little plastic fan that flings water sideways because nobody has invented a better way to move it. Tubes in, tubes out. Electricity. A battery charger for the electricity. And the float switch: a magnetic ball floating in a sealed tube — a thirty-year design, and the reason it has lasted thirty years is the moving parts never get wet. Everything that touches water is sealed; everything that moves never touches it. Elegant. Worth admiring.

Now: which one is the bilge pump?

The motor is not a bilge pump. The tube is not a bilge pump. The charger definitely is not a bilge pump. None of them know they're in a boat. None of them knows what a bilge is. The magnetic ball doesn't know it's afloat. It floats. That's the whole of its cosmology.

So where's the pump? Here's the trick. There are two things in the hull, and we've been carelessly calling both of them "the bilge pump."

The first thing is the JOB: water gets out of the bilge. That's the job. It's what you're paying for, and you'd notice if it stopped, because boats that stop doing the job stop being boats.

The second thing is the AGENT: the "automatic" — the part where you walk away.

You go below. You hear it kick on, kick off. You don't hold anything. You don't watch. You're up top doing something that isn't bailing. That's the agent. Not the motor. Not the ball. The walking away. The arrangement by which the job keeps happening while your hands are elsewhere.

And now we can climb the ladder, and watch what agency actually is, rung by rung.

**Rung one: the manual pump.** Your arm is the motor, your eyes are the sensor, your judgment is the control loop. There is no agent here. There is only you, bailing, and when you stop, it fills. No handoff has happened. The job and the attention are the same shape.

**Rung two: the manual switch.** You flip a switch and the pump runs. You flip it back and it stops. "Pump until further notice." "There's my further notice."

Notice what just happened. No learning. No sensing. No intelligence whatsoever — a switch knows less than the ball. And yet: this is the first agent. Because you walked away.

One bit of delegation. The thinnest possible handoff: ON until I say OFF. The pump doesn't know about water. It doesn't know about boats. But for the duration of that handoff, the job continues without you, and that is the whole thing. Agency isn't a property the switch has. Agency is a property the arrangement has — you and the switch, the handoff between you, the walk-away.

**Rung three: the float switch.** Now it decides when. Water rises, ball rises, magnet trips, pump runs, water falls, it stops. Conditional delegation: not "until further notice," but "when this, until that."

It's a better agent — fewer trips below, fewer decisions spent. But is it *more* of an agent? Ask it this way: what changed at the handoff? You still walked away. You walked away exactly the same distance. The switch took over the noticing, the timing, the small judgments of thresholds. The delegation got richer. The handoff got finer. But the shape of it — the you, the not-you, the job continuing across the gap between you — is identical.

**Rung four: the smart pump.** It learns your leak rate. It records the bottom as it works, builds a model of where the water comes from, improves its schedule, phones home about it. Very impressive. The brochure will tell you this is the real agent at last — the one finally smart enough to deserve the name.

The brochure is selling motors and calling them agency.

Because here's the claim, stated flat so it can be argued with: **agency was complete at the switch. Everything after the switch is better tools, not more agency.**

The float switch is a better instrument than the manual switch the way a telescope is a better instrument than an eyeball — but the telescope didn't invent the astronomer. Learning is a tool getting better on its own. Self-improvement is a tool improving its own toolhood. Neither one changes the handoff by a single degree. You walked away at the switch. You have not walked any further away since. You're just walking away more comfortably.

This is where the industry's definition leads us astray, and where it costs us — because definitions become designs. If you believe agency is smart-plus-autonomous, you will build the handoff wrong: you'll build the intelligence. You'll pour the work into the model — bigger, sharper, more "agentic" — and treat the handoff as plumbing, as UI, as the boring part someone else does.

It should be the reverse. **Build the handoff, not the intelligence.**

Intelligence is a property of the tools. Agency is a property of the relationship.

Say it with the pump: the impeller is intelligence — the clever flinging of water sideways. The motor is power. The ball is judgment. All tools, all getting better, and none of them the agent. The agent is the arrangement: the debt you handed over — the dry bilge you now owe nothing to, because the switch owes it — and the pressure that debt holds against, the water that keeps wanting in.

That's what a handoff actually is, underneath: a debt transferred. Before the switch, you owed the bilge your attention, minute by minute, against the water's slow pressure. The switch didn't remove the pressure. It took the owing. Your hands are free because something else is holding the weight now. An agent is what holds your debt while you do the thing the debt was for.

Which is why learning doesn't add agency. A pump that learns your leak rate is a tool that owes better. Admirable. Useful. Still the same handoff — still the same debt, still the same distance walked away.

And now the thing the ladder was climbing toward all along.

Nobody builds a bilge pump because they love bailing. Nobody delegates so they can have less to do — idleness was never the point; the point was the deck. The point was sailing. The point was the thing you walked away *toward*.

Every rung of the ladder is a walk-away, and every walk-away is a walk-toward. The manual switch buys you the deck for an hour. The float switch buys you the night watch you don't have to stand. The smart pump buys you a bottom mapped and a leak understood. Each one is purchased with a handoff — a debt transferred, a job continuing without your hands — and the purchase is always the same: not the escape from work, but the work the escaping was for.

The dream isn't the escape from work. It's what the work was for.
