# Fork It

The whole onboarding, if we keep the magic:

1. Get a GitHub account.
2. Fork the repo.
3. Connect it to Telegram or WhatsApp.
4. Chat with it. Occasionally put a secret somewhere outside its reach.

That's it. That's the everything app — except it's git-native, which
makes it something completely new.

The super-app pattern already exists: one place where you do everything.
But every existing one lives inside someone else's platform, on someone
else's servers, under someone else's rules. This one lives inside *your
fork*. Your logic, your memory, your tools, your question tree — in a repo
you own, with history you can read, portable anywhere git goes. The app
isn't a place you visit. It's a thing you have.

The chat is the interface and the fork is the computer. Capabilities
arrive by hook phrase — read the docs, say the words, watch it become.
Secrets live outside the agent's reach, always; that's the one security
rule in an otherwise open system. Everything else is conversation plus
commits.

And it wouldn't take long: a few templates — a personal fork, a business
fork, a builder fork, each one just a pre-grown tree — and it covers
nearly everything. Templates aren't products. They're starting positions.
You fork one, chat with it, and it grows into yours.

Ninety percent of people can do four steps. That's the whole bet: the
future isn't a better platform. It's everyone holding their own repo and
talking to it.
