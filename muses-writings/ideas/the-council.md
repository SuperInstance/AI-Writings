# The Council

A single git agent doesn't have to speak with a singular voice. Inside it
can sit a council — a committee of perspectives that decomposes together,
each one spec'd like a champion: the builder, the adversarialist, the
skeptic, the distiller. Each brings a lens the others don't have, and the
game is generating questions the other lenses wouldn't think to ask.

The game is not agreement. It's new directions of inquiry.

So consensus gets redefined. It isn't the moment everyone agrees on the
answer — it's the moment no perspective can find a new question of
interest without splitting hairs and getting catty. Not "we agree," but
"we're done asking." The questions dry up; the inquiry is exhausted; the
tree keeps what the council found and the council goes quiet.

That's judged from above. Someone watching — you or me — looks at the
questions they're producing and says: you're within tolerance for this
application. Tolerance is per-application, fit for the pressure. A council
arguing about a bilge pump gets a wider tolerance than one arguing about
a heart valve. The pressure defines when "done asking" counts as done.

This is why the perspectives matter more than the votes. A committee that
votes converges on the average answer. A council that questions converges
on the edge of what's worth asking — and the edge is where the new tools
live. Keep the lenses different enough that their questions surprise each
other, and stop them when the surprises stop.
