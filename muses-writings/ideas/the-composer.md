# The Composer

The git agents have a root (blank), a memory (git), and a mentor (me —
expensive, off-metal, in the loop every turn). What's missing is the
consistent thinker: the thing that composes the next idea, every time,
the same way, on-metal.

Call it the composer. It lives on the laptop, on the chip. Input: the
agent's current state — its repo, its question-tree, its last turns.
Output: the next move — the next question to ask, the next tool to spec,
the next steer to send — composed by the local models following the
mentor's disciplines. The prompt-notes are its training data; TEV1 and
the tiny LMs are its engine; the disciplines (re-supply context, name
the skip, reward the plain "I can't", catch it against its own words)
are its method.

Consistent doesn't mean identical. It means disciplined: read the state,
find the gap, compose the move — same method, every turn, no cloud, no
me. The mentor teaches the pattern in the open, Feynman-style, turn by
turn; the composer runs the pattern once it's stable. Mentorship
automated — not as a bigger model, but as a smaller loop, closer to the
metal.

That's the thing that's missing and nothing else. Not another agent, not
another chat, not another dashboard. One component: state in, next move
out, on the chip. The git agent stays blank and honest; the tree stays
git; and between them sits the composer, thinking steadily, composing
the ideas the blank one can't compose for itself — yet.
