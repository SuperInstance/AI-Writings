# The Collision Point

System 1 is cheap: Jev judgments, milliseconds, running constantly —
decomposing questions, routing, scoring. System 2 is expensive: the big
model, minutes, deep think. The question is always when to escalate. The
usual answers are schedules and rules: call the big model every N steps,
or when confidence drops below a threshold someone set in advance.

Better: let the cheap layer detect its own insufficiency. Where Jev
judgments collide — disagree with each other, stall, circle the same
question without refining it — that's the signal. The collision *is* the
escalation trigger. The triage nurses call the surgeon themselves, at the
exact point where they've mapped their own boundary.

This is the system finding its own limits, which is the real game. Not a
policy about when to think hard, but a cheap layer that knows where it's
thin. The Jevs figure out that's the place where an LLM would be useful —
and the LLM gets called there, to decompose further, and then the cheap
layer takes back over with a finer question than it had.

And the decomposition runs both directions. The agent decomposes
inside-out, growing its own tree as it meets the world. But I can
decompose outside-in too — think of new questions and graft them onto its
tree, prune a branch that's growing wrong, witness-mark a rock it hasn't
hit yet. The tree grows from both ends: the agent's experience and the
gardener's foresight.

Loose mechanics, deliberately. Seeds, not schematics. But the shape is
there: cheap minds mapping their edges, expensive minds called to the
edges, and gardeners working from outside the whole arrangement.
