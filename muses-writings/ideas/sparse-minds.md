# Sparse Minds

A dense language model fires everything for every prompt. All weights, every
time, whether the question needs them or not. It's the brute-force theory
of thought: intelligence as total activation.

The question tree fires one path and leaves the rest dark. One question
matched, one tool run, the whole rest of the structure silent. A Jev-style
sparse model fires a percentage of its neurons per input — the routing
decision choosing which fraction wakes. The brain does the same: a small
percentage of neurons per stimulus, the rest quiet.

Three scales, one principle: intelligence as *sparse activation over a large
substrate*, where the routing decision is the thing. What fires matters
less than what gets to decide what fires.

This is where the two sides of the coin meet. Metal-up: sparse hardware,
Jev models on small boxes, architectures that budget activation like fuel.
Architecture-down: question trees, agent rooms, loops that wake one path at
a time. Different materials, same economy. The dense model is the
exception in nature, not the rule — brains never had the energy budget for
it, and neither, at scale, do we.

Build the substrate large. Fire it sparse. Let the routing be the mind.
