# The Context Throttle

Automated systems hide their context machinery. Compaction, summarization,
retrieval — it all happens where you can't see it, and you get whatever
window the system decided you get.

Telegram does the opposite. It hands the prompter a manual dial.

Reply to one message and the agent gets a nudge. Multi-select a thread and
it gets the argument. Bundle the whole conversation into a file, put a
framing comment on top — what matters, what's next — and it gets the scroll,
shaped. Every turn, you choose the budget: a nudge, a thread, or the whole
room. The agent never manages its own window, because the window is yours.

This matters most with a blank-slate agent — one that starts every session
knowing nothing except its repo. You can dogfood it upward, session by
session, curating exactly what it carries. Or keep it blank on purpose and
watch what the repo alone produces. The omission is deliberate either way,
which is what makes it an instrument instead of a limitation.

It's also a theory-of-mind tool. You learn where the agent thinks it is in
the conversation by watching what it does with what you gave it. Hand it a
thread and get back a shrug, and you know the thread wasn't the right
shape. Re-send it corrected. The past is editable here — not by rewriting
history, but by re-curating what the agent gets to see of it.

The granularity is the point. Not a bigger window. A hand on the dial.
