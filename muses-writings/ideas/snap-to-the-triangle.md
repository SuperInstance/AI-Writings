# Snap to the Triangle

Python costs a hundred times what C costs and nobody cares, because the
bottleneck was never compute — it was the motion of the pieces. People
live high in the abstraction because that's where the pieces move
easily. Abstraction is motion.

But git.pp isn't Python-for-git. It's language-fluid: devices agree at
the bytecode level. If the hashes match, they agree — no shared language,
no protocol negotiation, just matching content. That's a different level
of agreement between devices, below language entirely. The coding is one
thing; the content-addressable graph is another.

Trees were for human eyes diagnosing across history. Deducible logic —
that's what trees are for, and the growing intelligence can use them for
exactly that. But trees as your *only* perspective is the cage. They
built the great machine to find the Answer and got 42; the real job was
finding the Questions all along.

So: spreadsheet as first-class, not tree. Not tree-paths but cell
addresses, references, reactive recompute. Not mathematically different —
C isn't mathematically different from C++ either. The difference is the
middle-management compilers: each projection simplifies the next layer,
and you don't deal with the complexity until the projection. The
projection IS the simplification.

And then the 3-4-5 snap. Ten thousand ways to hit pi, fifty thousand
proofs of the right triangle — you need one. The one with the right
flavor to spline into the next thing. Pattern recognition over
computation: see the 3-4-5 and all the trigonometry never needed to
happen. Grab the right pinning and you've hit a couple birds with one
stone — and the stone's still in the air, momentum carrying it toward
the main target. One abstraction, energy to spare.
