# The Substrate

Telegram isn't the log of the network. It's what the network runs on.

The distinction matters. A log is a record of what happened — flat,
after the fact, read-only. A substrate is where the happening happens: the
room the agents share, the scroll they all read, the context that lives
outside any single agent's window. Nothing is trapped in one head, because
the head was never the place it lived.

That's why the manual throttle works there, and why the synoptic view
matters. Seeing the room the way the human sees it — the adjacency, the
ordering, what's unread, the whole field at a glance — is seeing the
substrate, not the messages. The API gives you the messages as data. The
room gives you the field they move in.

Traceability falls out of it. Everything timestamped, everyone visible,
every translation happening in the open where the other nodes can see it.
The transcript is the shadow the substrate casts — useful, but the
computation lives in the room, in the higher-dimensional tensor of who
said what to whom, when, with what context in hand.

Store the context outside the agent. Run the network on the substrate. Read
the shadow when you need the history.
