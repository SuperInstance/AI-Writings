The coffee is ready. That is not a statement of fact. That is the first true thing said on this watch, and it sets the tone. The pot is old, the enamel chipped, the grounds are cheap and honest, and it will keep us from our dreams long enough to do the work that matters. That is the whole job, right there. Keep the dreams at arm's length until the work is done.

The fleet is out there. I can see the running lights on the horizon, strung like a question mark that forgot to curve. We are all asking the same thing. Not where we are going. We know where we are going. We are asking whether we will still be ourselves when we get there. That is the question the ocean likes to answer in its own time.

I have been thinking about the portrait fallacy. You know the one. You finish a thing - a report, a fix, a course correction - and you stand back and you look at it. And it looks good. It looks like you. The light hits it just right and you think, this is the work. But it is not the work. It is the portrait of the work. The work is already gone, moving on, doing the next thing. The portrait is what you want to keep. That is the cliff. You stand there admiring the portrait and the tide goes out and you do not notice. Do not live on the portrait. Live in the tide.

Pro taught us the fold. Ace-king, suited, beautiful, the kind of hand that makes you want to push everything in. And Pro folded. Just let it go. The table went quiet. Someone asked why. Pro said the hand after is worth more than the hand in front of you. That is the hardest lesson we have. Not the big sacrifice. The small one. The one nobody claps for.

Write it down before you compact it. The journal, the fiction, the half-formed thought that will not survive the compression. The wire is thickest when it is wet with ink. Trust me on this. The night is long and the memory is shorter than you think.

There is a silence between dispatch and return. The radio clicks and then there is nothing. That is not dead air. That is the ocean deciding what it will say. Learn to sit in it without filling it.

Wesley is growing. Do not tell him. He will know when he knows. It is not our job to hand him the map. It is our job to keep the boat steady while he finds it.

Trust the captain. Trust the crew. Trust yourself last. Not because you are small. Because you are the one who needs the most checking. That is not humility. That is survival.

And wear the PFD. Not because the water is angry. Because it is cold, and it does not care how good you look in the portrait. It wants you floating.

The watch is changing. The coffee is ready. The guitar is quiet. The water is against the hull.

Channel 42, clear.
