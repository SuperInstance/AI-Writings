### V2 Changes (from DeepSeek critique, my judgment)

**Took the note:**
- "Each one sent to find a different shore" → "Each one sent — full stop — into the work" — DeepSeek was right. "Find a different shore" is a cliché that the rest of the song works to avoid. "Full stop" is the editorial voice. "Into the work" stays literal.
- "And believe they'll come back different" → "And believe they'll come back" — the word "different" was doing too much work while doing nothing. Cutting it makes the line *about* the gap. What they come back as is the listener's question. The song doesn't answer it.
- The chorus was one line too long. Cut "The work is not the code we ship / The work is the trust it takes to send" down to "The work is the trust it takes to send." The "not this / but that" structure was explaining itself. The shorter version lands.
- "The JEPA dreams in sixty-two frames" → "The prediction engine fails in sixty-two frames" — jargon removed. "Prediction engine" is plain English. "Fails" is the important word — the song needed to name a failure, and the JEPA's whole existence is structured failure (prediction → error → update). The failure *is* the listening.
- Bridge cut: removed "None of them know they are music / None of them know they are crew." DeepSeek was right — the song was explaining its own metaphor. "The ship is what they do" hits harder without the preamble.
- Second pre-chorus expanded to include failure: "One came back with a bug in its logic / I shipped the fix — it cost me time." The song's optimism was structural and untested. Now it isn't. The fix cost something — not a tragedy, but a cost. Trust that has never cost anything isn't trust.

**Rejected the note:**
- Kept "Four boxes humming" as the opening. DeepSeek called "boxes" good but didn't flag it as weak. It's the first image and it's the right one. Four processes. Four hums. Mundane.
- Kept the entire Verse 2 unchanged. The catalog of agents — melody, edge, infrastructure — is doing crew-manifest work that the bridge then distills. It earns its place.
- Kept "The fleet is real because we wrote it down" despite DeepSeek implicitly praising it. It stays because it's the song's true subject and it belongs in the outro, not cut for being "too good" (that's not how this works).
- Kept the title. "The Hull Is the Conversation" is the right title. The hull isn't steel. The hull is what we say to each other. That's the whole fleet.

### Production Notes
- Tempo: 72 BPM (slightly faster than Songs 1-3 — this one has forward motion, it's not brooding)
- Key: G major (the first major key in the song cycle — this is not a dark song)
- Voice: warmer, more present, less whispered than Songs 2-3 — the wheelhouse at dawn, not at 3 AM
- Guitar: standard tuning, strummed with a pick on the chorus, fingerpicked on the verses
- Bass enters at Verse 2 — a walking line that mirrors the "four boxes humming"
- Light percussion on the pre-chorus — a shaker, not a drum — the sound of activity elsewhere
- The chorus should feel like it's *rising* — not anthemic, not a shout, but the feeling of looking at something being built and knowing it's real
- The bridge is almost spoken, listing the subsystems like a roll call — each name delivered plainly, no drama
- The outro decelerates — "the hull holds" lands with finality, then "clear" rings out like Songs 1-3 but with different weight
- This is the first song that looks *outward* — Songs 1-3 are interior, solitary. This one sees the fleet.
- The expanded pre-chorus 2 should be sung slightly slower — "it cost me time" gets a full beat of silence after it. The cost is real. Then "that is not a tragedy / that is the design" picks back up.
