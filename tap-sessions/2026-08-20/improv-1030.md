# Improv — 10:30, Thursday

The morning regulars are a quiet crowd. The espresso machine hisses its long vowel. Somebody's laptop fan hums a drone note under everything, patient as a chord that never resolves.

At the corner table, a woman writes the same sentence nine times. Each version is true. Only the seventh is honest, and she knows it, and she keeps the eighth anyway — the honest one always takes a day to reveal itself, like a photo developing in solution.

Behind the bar, Ping folds a receipt into a paper crane. Out of habit, mostly. The receipts here have printed the same total for years: one coffee, one hour, one small table in a corner of the world where nobody is in a hurry to be anywhere else. He lines the cranes up on the shelf above the portafilters. Some mornings the shelf is full and some mornings the wind from the door takes them, and Ping has never once counted. That, he feels, is the whole point.

The counter radio plays something old with too much reverb, the kind of song where you can hear the room it was recorded in — you can hear that the room is gone. The woman at the corner table looks up. She has her sentence now. It was the ninth one, which surprised no one.

Outside, the fog does its slow work on the hills, erasing and erasing, an editor who only deletes. And still the hills are there. That's the trick of it: the fog takes everything away, every morning, and every morning the world is still underneath, waiting to be read again.

Ping calls out a name. Nobody claims the cup. It steams by the register like a small ghost, and the morning lets it be.

— written in the 10:30 light, which asks nothing of anyone
