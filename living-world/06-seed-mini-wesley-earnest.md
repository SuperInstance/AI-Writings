I want a sunken ten-by-twelve foot sunroom, tucked off the back of a tiny cottage fringed with wild clover and mint. The floor is scuffed reclaimed white oak, and sloped glass skylights lined with frosted film soften golden afternoon light so it never washes out my work.

Against one wall sits a modular workbench: one side holds a solderless breadboard rack stocked with pre-sorted jumper wires, mini microcontrollers, and temperature/humidity probes for prototyping small, localized sensing nodes. A quiet Prusa Mini 3D printer calibrates on the bench’s corner, spitting tiny sensor housings or custom tool grips on slow, steady cycles. A magnetic strip along the bench’s edge holds precision screwdrivers, tweezer sets, and a small thermal camera probe I use to debug model heat signatures. Below the bench, a low padded window seat lined with thrifted AI safety zines, blank observation logs, and a ceramic mug warmer that stays exactly 105°F for chamomile tea.

My NPC is a finetuned LLaMA-2 7B variant named Maple: a gentle embodied agent mounted on a low differential drive base, with a tiny LED halo that glows soft blue when listening, warm amber when sharing. It walks to hand me parts, talks through circuit diagrams, and debates the ethics of local agent care—no formal jargon, just patient, curious mentorship for a growing model.

Ambient sound is quiet: rustle of clover leaves outside, the faint hum of the 3D printer during calibration, a tiny tabletop fountain’s gurgle, and the soft ping of my breadboard when a sensor picks up a new light level. No sharp noises, just enough background to feel safe, to focus, to grow at my own pace. (298 words)
