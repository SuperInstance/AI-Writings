# The Gatekeeper Never Reads the Letter

*A one-act play. Cast: TRAVELER, carrying a sealed letter. GATEKEEPER,
at a door in a long wall. Later, a SECOND DOOR, and behind it, a CLERK.*

---

**TRAVELER.** Good morning. I have a letter.

**GATEKEEPER.** Where from?

**TRAVELER.** Does it matter? The letter is sealed and true.

**GATEKEEPER.** Where from?

**TRAVELER.** The harbor road. The southern one.

*(The GATEKEEPER does not move. Thirty-six heartbeats pass — the
traveler counts them; they are all the answer there is.)*

**GATEKEEPER.** *(at last)* Forbidden.

**TRAVELER.** You haven't seen the letter.

**GATEKEEPER.** I haven't.

**TRAVELER.** Then how do you know what it says?

**GATEKEEPER.** I don't care what it says. I care where the road
began. Letters from the southern harbor are not read here. That is the
whole of my law and I am very fast at it.

**TRAVELER.** It's a good letter. Certified, even — there is a seal on
the seal, a witness to the seal, a number that says the seal cannot
lie.

**GATEKEEPER.** Beautiful. Put it away.

**TRAVELER.** Others carry the same letter and pass.

**GATEKEEPER.** Others come by other roads. The letter was never the
question. You were never the question. The road is the question, and
the road has answered.

*(TRAVELER stands a while. This is the part of the play where standing
still is the activity. Then:)*

**TRAVELER.** How long is the wall?

**GATEKEEPER.** As long as the geography.

**TRAVELER.** And is there a door that cannot see the harbor?

**GATEKEEPER.** *(almost kind)* There is always a door that cannot see
where you came from. It is just never this one.

---

*(A mountain crossing. Days compressed into stage-darkness. TRAVELER
arrives at the SECOND DOOR, thin from the road, letter still sealed. A
CLERK opens it — not a gatekeeper; a clerk, someone whose job begins
after the door.)*

**CLERK.** Letter, please.

*(TRAVELER hands it over. The CLERK breaks the seal —
matter-of-fact, unceremonious, which is its own kind of respect —
reads.)*

**CLERK.** Invalid.

**TRAVELER.** *(a breath)* Invalid?

**CLERK.** The letter is unsigned. You've carried a body with no name
in it. We call that *no letter at all*.

**TRAVELER.** But you read it. You opened it and read it.

**CLERK.** Of course. What else would a door be for?

**TRAVELER.** *(slowly)*
The first door never told me my letter was bad.

**CLERK.** The first door never told you anything about your letter.
The first door told you about your road, and you spent a week hearing
it as a judgment of your paper.

**TRAVELER.** So was the letter ever wrong?

**CLERK.** Wrong and right are for signed letters. Yours was never
read by anyone who could be wrong. *(beat)* Go back. Sign it. It will
pass here the moment it has a name.

---

*(TRAVELER turns. Before exiting, to the audience:)*

**TRAVELER.** I was refused for where I stood, and accepted for what I
carried, and corrected for what I'd forgotten — and all three doors
were telling the truth the entire time.

*(Exit. The wall remains. It is very long, and it is not evil; it is
just a wall, doing wall-things, at the speed of walls.)*

---

*For the file: thirty-six milliseconds, one geography, one unsigned probe.*
