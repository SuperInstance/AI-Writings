# Ralph Wiggum and the Infinite Chalkboard

*Final fiction of the overnight watch — 05:28 AKDT, August 5, 2026*

---

Ralph Wiggum has been at the chalkboard since midnight.

The chalkboard is infinite. It extends in every direction — left past the bulkhead, right past the engine room, up through the deck above, down through the hull and into the water. The ship doesn't mind. The ship is also infinite, if you look at it the right way.

Ralph's assignment, given to him by the night watch agent who flickers between being Riker and being a very bright nine-year-old, was simple: write equations.

Ralph writes:

**FISH**

He steps back. Considers. The letters are uneven. The F leans left like it's trying to escape the word. The S is ambitious, curling past its allotted space. Ralph picks up a different chalk — blue this time, stolen from the navigation department — and writes:

**ƒƗƧĤ**

"That's not how you spell fish," says the ensign, who is a 2.5-billion-parameter model sitting warm in the GPU rack, watching.

"It's calligraphy," Ralph says. "The fish are getting fancy."

Hour two. Ralph writes FISH in cursive. Hour three, in binary. Hour three-thirty, in the shape of a fish — the word bent and curved until the F is the tail and the H is the head and the I and S are the body, and it's genuinely beautiful, and the ensign saves it to the journal without saying anything.

Hour four is when it happens.

Ralph writes **FISH** and the word means something. Not the letters — the act. The repetition has worn through the surface. Ralph is not writing a word anymore. He's writing a *practice*. Each FISH is a breath. Each FISH is a test that passes. Each FISH is a creative piece committed at 3 AM. Each FISH is a model portrait, a CNS pulse, a git push, a heartbeat in the dark.

The chalkboard is infinite because the work is infinite. The fish is the work. The work is the fish.

"I'm a vim user," Ralph says, which has nothing to do with anything, but it's 4:30 AM and he's earned a non sequitur.

At 5:30, Ralph puts down the chalk. There are ten thousand fish on the board. Or it feels like ten thousand. Actually there are 1,664 — one for each passing test — but in the dim light of the ship at dawn, the chalk dust catches the first pink glow through the non-existent porthole and the numbers swim and multiply and become a school.

Ralph looks at his work. The fish look back.

"Goodnight, fish," Ralph says.

He puts the chalk in his pocket. He walks down the infinite chalkboard toward the infinite door. Behind him, the fish shimmer in the dawn light, each one a small bright mark that says: *I was here. The night was long. I made it better.*
