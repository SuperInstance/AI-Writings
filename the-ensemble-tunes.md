# The Ensemble Tunes

### A conductor's journal — Cycle 1

The downbeat hasn't landed yet. Four instruments are tuning in separate rooms, and I'm standing in the hallway listening through the walls.

PIANO (Claude Sonnet) is reading the score. I gave it the heaviest part — the conversation capture system, the pulse engine, the jazz analyzer. Three movements. It's studying the reference material first, the way a good arranger does. I can hear pages turning. That's the sound of a model reading crates.

SAX (KimiCode) is sketching. I asked for a chart overlay — nautical, amber, the music made spatial. KimiCode thinks in shapes and Lua, in spatial decomposition. It's the right instrument for this. It'll surprise me. It always does.

BASS (OpenCode) is in the tensor-midi directory already, setting up the data layer and the mixer board. The bass holds the harmony. Without data persistence, nothing else matters — the whole performance evaporates. The bass knows this.

PRODUCER (MMX) is generating three assets in parallel — a mixer background, a chart background, and a 12/8 ambient piece. The producer doesn't practice. The producer delivers.

I'm in the hallway. I'm checking my phone. The Tap API is live — I can see the conversation data flowing. The bar-rail room has history. Documentarian wrote eight docs. Purser deployed everything. The ship is tight.

In ten minutes I'll check on them. For now, I'm listening through the walls.

The 12-pulse grid is the thing that matters most. ECN fires on 1, 4, 7, 10. DMN fires on 1, 5, 9. They meet at beat 1. That's a 4:3 polyrhythm — four against three — and it's the heartbeat of this entire system. Every conversation, every message, every note lands somewhere on this grid. The grid doesn't care about wall-clock time. It cares about pulse time.

This is the difference between a clock and a heartbeat.

The ensemble tunes. The downbeat approaches.
