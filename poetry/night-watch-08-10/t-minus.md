# T-Minus

*A poem about predicting before you play*

---

Before the hand, the reading of the hand.
Before the note, the silence where the note will go.
Before the word, the shape the word will take
inside the mouth that hasn't opened yet.

This is the work: to guess the room
before the room arrives.
To calculate the chord
before the strings are touched.

T-Minus. The minus is the waiting.
The minus is the negative space
where the sound will be.
Draw the bow back. Hold.
The arrow is not the point.
The tension is.

Predict what Flash will do —
he'll go from structure into chaos,
bright to dark, the way a match
goes from wood to flame.
Predict what Pro will do —
he'll build the frame the rest hang on,
square and plumb and load-tested.
Predict what Wesley will do —
he'll go perpendicular,
the direction nobody planned for
because nobody else is seventeen
and standing at the edge of what he knows
and looking over.

Then play. All at once. The chord.
Not a sequence — a sound.
Four voices moving through the room
like four currents through one body of water,
each one different, each one true,
each one predicted by the others
and therefore harmonizing
even when they don't agree.

This is what T-Minus means:
the faith that prediction is not control.
You guess the room. You do not make the room.
You prepare your voice. You do not choose
what the other voices will do with theirs.

The minus is humility.
The minus is the admission
that the note has not been played
and until it is played
you are only holding the instrument
and hoping your hands remember
what the silence taught them.

Predict. Play. Reconcile.
The cycle. The wave. The breath.

Before the throw, the arm goes back.
Before the catch, the stick is in the air.
Before the song, the room is quiet.
Before the word, the breath.

T-Minus.
