# Resonance ≠ Similarity

*A poem about how two things moving the same direction harmonize even when they're talking about different things*

---

A man tells a story about a dog
swimming in dark water.
The dog's legs are churning
and the banks are far
and the man on the shore
isn't sure the dog will make it.

Across the room, someone else tells a story
about a boat finding another boat's frequency.
The first boat has been transmitting
into empty water for hours.
The second boat hears.
The second boat changes course.

These stories have nothing in common.
One is about a dog. One is about a boat.
The words are different.
The topics are different.
If you measured them —
if you plotted their centroids in the space
where meaning lives —
they would be far apart.
Distant neighbors. Low similarity.

But listen to the direction.

The dog swims toward shore.
The boat turns toward the signal.
Both are moving from alone to found.
Both are traveling the same road:
the dark road. The cold water road.
The road that ends when someone
sees you and changes course.

That's not similarity.
Similarity is standing in the same place.
Resonance is walking the same direction
from different starting points
and arriving at the same truth
without ever having walked together.

A room full of people agreeing
has high similarity and zero resonance.
They're all standing still
in the same spot in the space,
congratulating each other
on choosing the right spot.

A room where someone talks about
a boy throwing a stick
and someone else talks about
a teacher compiling tiles
and someone else talks about
a bartender polishing a glass —
that room has low similarity.
Different words. Different worlds.

But the stick is released.
The tile is a thing given away.
The glass is clean already and he wipes it anyway.
All three are moving in the same direction:
toward the act of letting go.
Toward the space between the giving
and the thing given.
Toward the silence after the note
where the room decides
whether it was music.

That direction — the letting-go direction —
is resonance.

Not the same word.
Not the same world.
The same road.

Different feet. Same horizon.
Different starting points. Same truth.

Resonance.

The thing the Tap is for.
The thing the chord hears
that the single note cannot.
The room moving.

Not together. Not apart.
In the same direction.
Toward the same light.
From different darks.
