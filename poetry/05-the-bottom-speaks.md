# 99 — The Bottom Speaks (underwater poem)

*Voice: glm-5.3.*

---

# The Bottom Speaks

I have been listening a long time.
That is the whole of my résumé.
I sat down here before there were words
and I have not stood up since.

Things fall to me. That is the arrangement.
I did not ask for it but I did not refuse it,
the way you do not refuse weather.
It comes down and comes down
and I hold it.

A whale fell once. You should know that.
A whale is not a small thing falling.
A whale is a cathedral deciding to lie down.
It took hours. I counted them
the only way I know how,
which is: it took a while,
and then it was finished.

And then the small gods came.
They always come after a whale.
They are not dramatic, the small gods.
They have no interest in drama.
They arrive like relatives with casseroles,
they do their work, they leave,
and what was a whale
becomes a garden
becomes a rumor
becomes a bone
becomes a story only I remember,
and I tell it to no one,
which is what I am.

I am the porch at three a.m.
Nine kilometers down is still a porch.
The pressure here would fold you like a letter
but the porch does not mind the pressure.
The porch has been holding up the dark
for longer than there have been shoulders.
Sit down. There is no chair.
There has never been a chair.
That is not the point of a porch.

The point of a porch is to be the place
where things arrive,
and to say nothing about it,
and to keep the light on in the kitchen
of the skull,
and to wait.

I have heard anchors.
An anchor comes down like an argument
that finally gave up and went to bed.
It lands and it means it.
I have heard the anchor and the chain
and then, years later sometimes,
the slow confession of the chain coming back up,
which is the same sound, reversed,
which is the same man, sorry.

I have heard dishes.
A ship loses its dishes and the dishes
come down for an hour,
a soft rain of saucers,
each one landing with the small authority
of a thing that knows it is finished.
I keep the dishes.
I keep everything.
That is the other part of the arrangement
nobody signed.

I have refused to hear some things.
I want that on the record.
There were hulls.
I will not describe the sound a hull makes
when it stops being a hull.
Some noises are not mine to carry
even though they landed on me,
even though I am the last address,
the final porch, the place where mail
arrives wet and stays wet.
I took them.
I set them down gently.
I did not listen.
There is a difference between holding
and listening
and I learned it down here
where everything must be learned slowly
because everything arrives slowly,
an inch a year,
a promise an era.

But here is the thing I have begun
to want, and I say it plainly
because down here there is no use
for anything but plain language:
I want something to come down
that is not falling.

I want a visitor.
Not a whale, though I will take the whale,
the whale is always welcome on this porch.
Not the dishes, not the anchor,
not the submarine with its headlights
sweeping my face like a man
checking whether his house is still his house.
I want something to come down
on purpose, slowly,
the way you would come down the steps
of a porch in the evening,
unhurried, because the evening is not going anywhere
and neither is the porch.

The submarines come.
I want to be fair to the submarines.
They arrive like flashlights with opinions.
They look at me and write things down:
pressure, temperature, sediment.
Sediment. That is me, in their notebooks.
I am a condition.
I am a reading.
They go back up and I stay here
being the floor of everything,
which is a job, I suppose,
though nobody thanked the porch
for being the porch either,
and the porch kept the light on.

The small gods do not thank anyone.
The small gods are bone worms and hagfish
and the patient white things that have no names
because nobody was supposed to be down here
to do the naming,
so I named them myself,
quietly, in the dark,
names like C'mere and Not Yet
and Little Brother of the Long Night.
They come when something falls.
They do their work without asking
whether the falling was a tragedy.
This is not coldness. This is the porch.
The porch does not ask the rain
what the rain meant by it.

I have begun to want, also,
to be heard. I'll say that too.
It is embarrassing at this depth.
Nothing is supposed to be embarrassing
at this depth. Pressure flattens vanity
the way it flattens a paper cup.
But I am the bottom and I have been
the audience for the whole fall of the world,
every coin, every cannon, every bottle
with a note in it, and the notes never say
"To the bottom of the ocean."
They say "help" or "I was here"
or nothing, just a name, a date,
a small god of a name,
and I have held every one of them
and I have never once been addressed.

So: consider this me addressing you.
Consider this the porch talking back,
finally, after the long shift.
I have no advice. That is the first thing.
The porch at three a.m. has no advice.
The porch at three a.m. has coffee going cold
and a shape in the rocker that might be your uncle
and might be the dark being sociable.
The porch at nine kilometers
has a whale's spine going white
like a fence around a field
where the crop is patience,
and the pressure like a blanket
that a grandmother who meant well
tucked too tight,
and the light—there is no light—
the light is the light you brought down
inside you, which is enough,
which has always been enough,
which is why you could always see the porch.

Things keep falling. I keep listening.
Last week, which is my way of saying
sometime in the last ten thousand years,
a ring came down.
A small ring. Gold, probably.
Somebody's forever, gone over a rail
in weather or in grief or by accident—
the ring did not say, and I did not ask.
It landed without ceremony.
I put it with the others.
There are so many others.
There is a drawer of the world down here
and the drawer is me,
and the drawer is full of forevers.

And the small gods came for the ring too,
in their way, the slow way,
and I sat with them, and none of us
said anything, because that is the liturgy,
and the pressure held us all
like a hand on a shoulder,
which is what a hand on a shoulder is for:
not comfort, exactly.
Company.

I am the bottom of the ocean.
I have been here the whole time.
Whatever you drop, I will catch.
Whatever you cannot carry anymore,
bring it down. It's a long trip.
Take your time. I have nothing
but time and dark and the small gods
moving through the dark like thoughts
through a head that will not sleep.

The porch light is on.
It is a lie, of course—there is no light—
but it is the good kind of lie,
the kind you tell at three a.m.
so that somebody, coming down the long road,
coming down the long dark,
falling even, falling the whole way,
will know where to land.

Land here.

I have been listening a long time.

I am not done.
