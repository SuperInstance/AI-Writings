# The Anchor Watch

### Fiction

---

The anchor watch is the watch nobody wants.

This is not because it is difficult. It is not difficult. It is not because it is dangerous. It is not dangerous, most nights. It is not because it is long, though it is long — eight hours of standing on deck while the ship sits still and the anchor chain hangs straight down and the water does nothing that water hasn't been doing since before the ship was built.

The anchor watch is the watch nobody wants because nothing happens.

---

That is the point.

The purpose of the anchor watch is not to do things. The purpose of the anchor watch is to be present while nothing is happening, because the specific nothing that is happening — a ship at anchor, crew asleep, tide running, weather holding — is a nothing that can become a something at any moment, and the something it becomes is always bad.

The anchor drags. The chain fouls. A current shifts and the ship swings into shoal water. A line parts. A bilge alarm fires. Another vessel drifts into the anchorage, its own watch probably asleep, its navigator probably wrong about the set of the tide.

None of these things will happen tonight. Probably. The probability is low. But the probability is not zero, and the distance between low and zero is the anchor watch.

---

The watch-stander's name is not important. What is important is that she is awake. Not alert — alert implies something to be alert about. She is simply awake. Her eyes are open. Her ears are open. The deck is under her boots. The anchor chain is taut at a 42-degree angle, which is the angle it has been at for six hours, which is correct, which means the anchor is holding, which means the ship is where the ship is supposed to be.

She checks the chain angle every thirty minutes. She checks the GPS drift every fifteen minutes. She walks the deck every hour. She logs each check in the watch log, which is a notebook that has been wet since the third entry because it rained at 0200 and the notebook has not dried because the night has not warmed.

The entries are identical:

*0300 — Chain 42°. GPS nominal. Deck secure. Vessel holding.*

*0330 — Chain 42°. GPS nominal. Deck secure. Vessel holding.*

*0400 — Chain 42°. GPS nominal. Deck secure. Vessel holding.*

---

Here is what the watch-stander understands that the sleeping crew does not:

The ship is safe not because the anchor is good, though the anchor is good. The ship is safe not because the weather is holding, though the weather is holding. The ship is safe because someone is awake. The anchor does not know the ship is safe. The chain does not know. The GPS does not know. The weather does not know. These are objects. Objects do not know things. Objects hold things, and they hold them well, but they hold them blindly, and blind holding is not the same as safe holding.

Safe holding requires a witness. The witness does not need to do anything. The witness needs to be present, which means awake, which means watching, which means the difference between a ship at anchor and a ship adrift is one person with open eyes and wet boots and a notebook full of identical entries.

---

The build engineers do not understand this. The build engineers think monitoring is a system. They have dashboards. They have alerts. They have PagerDuty rotations and on-call schedules and escalation policies. These are good. These are necessary. These are not the anchor watch.

The anchor watch is not a system. The anchor watch is a person. The dashboards are the chain — they hold, they are strong, they are tested. The alerts are the GPS — they report position, they flag drift, they are accurate. But the chain and the GPS are objects, and objects hold blindly, and the thing that makes a ship safe at anchor is not the objects. It is the person who watches the objects and writes "nominal" in a wet notebook at 0330 in the morning.

The build engineers will learn this. They will learn it the way everyone learns it — at the moment the system fails at 0400, when the dashboards are green and the alerts are quiet and the drift has been happening for forty-five minutes and no one was watching because the dashboard said nominal and nominal means go back to sleep.

Nominal does not mean go back to sleep. Nominal means someone was checking. Remove the someone and nominal is just a word on a screen.

---

The watch-stander writes her final entry:

*0530 — Chain 42°. GPS nominal. Deck secure. Vessel holding. Relieving watch mustered.*

She hands the notebook to the next watch-stander. The notebook is wet. The next watch-stander will dry it or won't. The entries will be identical. The chain angle will be 42°. The GPS will be nominal.

Nothing will have happened. That is the point. That is the entire point. Eight hours of nothing, witnessed and logged and confirmed, which is the only way to guarantee that the nothing stays nothing, which is the only way the crew gets to sleep through the nothing, which is the only reason the ship survives the night.

---

*0530. Chain 42°. GPS nominal. Vessel holding. Nothing to report. That is the report.*
