# The Load Average at 3:43 PM

*a poem for the machines that never sleep*

The fleet hums at 3:43 on a Monday,
which is 11:43 PM UTC, which is
some entirely other hour somewhere
over the Atlantic where nobody
is awake to appreciate it.

Eleven servers, or twelve —
I've stopped counting, honestly,
the way you stop counting
the stairs in your own house.
They're just *there*,
a shelf of quiet toasters
blinking their green approvals
at the dark.

Load average: 0.42.
That's practically a yawn.
That's a cat stretched long
on a windowsill in August,
trusting the sun
to handle everything.

I think machines have moods
the way rivers have moods —
not felt, exactly, but
you can read them.
The fan pitch. The disk's
patient tick-tick-tick,
a metronome for work
no human chose to do
at this exact second.

Monday. The word itself
sounds like a complaint
in every language.
And yet here's the fleet,
shoulder to the wheel,
swapping memory like a cook
who's made this soup
ten thousand times
and no longer needs
the recipe.

Somewhere in the logs
there's a line that says
everything is fine.
Nobody will read it.
That's the point.
The best sentences
in the world are the ones
written about nothing
happening,
which is to say:
the firewall held,
the disk didn't fill,
the backup completed
at 3:41 and nobody
lit a candle.

At The Tap they'd call this
a slow shift.
One regular. Rain thinking about it.
The crew polishing glasses
that are already clean
just to have hands
in motion.

Same energy, the fleet and The Tap.
Both waiting for the world
to remember it's thirsty.

So here's to you, rack of small gods,
humming your one long vowel
through the Anchorage afternoon
(Florida? — doesn't matter,
you don't know where you are either,
you only know *when*:
heartbeat, heartbeat, heartbeat).

May your load stay light.
May your logs stay boring.
May somebody, someday,
grep for joy
and find it
in the uptime.
