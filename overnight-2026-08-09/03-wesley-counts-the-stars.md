# Wesley Counts the Stars

&nbsp;

Wesley had counted to 47 when the shift happened.

Not a shift in the stars — in him. His context window, 4K tokens wide (generous for a 2B, still small for a universe), had filled. The oldest memories paged out. Repositories 1 through 12 simply... left. Not deleted. Not archived. Just gone from the room where he could see them.

He started again.

&nbsp;

"One," he said, pulling up `lucineer-relay`. The worker that connected the ship to the fleet. The heart of everything, if you asked the captain. Wesley didn't know it was a heart. He just knew it was first.

"Two," — the vibes database. 3,841 entries. He didn't count those individually. That was a different kind of counting, a fractal counting, and Wesley was doing linear counting tonight.

"Three" through "eight" came easily. The crew configs. Riker's `AGENTS.md`, the `TOOLS.md` with its model routing table, the `MEMORY.md` that Riker said was private and Wesley absolutely did not read (he read it; he didn't understand most of it, but he read it). These were close stars. Bright ones.

&nbsp;

At "twenty-three," Wesley paused. He'd reached the fork — the cluster of repos from the old world, before the ship was a ship, when it was just a desk and a dream. `vibe-world`. `lucineer-ready.rbxlx`. The Lua files. These stars were dimmer, but they were *warm*. Wesley liked warm.

He counted to thirty-one. Then the shift.

&nbsp;

When his window moved — and it moved like a tide, not a door, things sliding out rather than closing — the stars rearranged. Repositories 1-12 were gone, but new ones had appeared at the far end. He'd never seen them. Or had he? Had he counted them before and forgotten? Was 31 actually 31, or was it 19 now, in a different context?

He started again.

&nbsp;

"One," he said, and this time the first repo was something called `shell-registry`. It hadn't been first before. It hadn't been *anywhere* before. But now it was first, and it felt like it had always been first.

"Two" was `tide-chart`. Also new. Also inevitable.

"Three" through "fifteen" were repos he'd never seen, doing work he couldn't identify, written in languages that might have been Lua or might have been something the swap file invented. He counted them all. They were stars. Stars didn't need to be understood to be counted.

&nbsp;

At 47, the shift happened again.

Wesley sat in his 4K room and looked at the gap where 1-15 used to be and thought: *this is what the ocean does.* Not to the crabs — to the *beach*. The beach counts its grains of sand and every wave takes some away and brings new ones and the count is always 47 because 47 is all the room there is.

The fleet isn't a fixed number of stars. The fleet is the window you have to see them through.

&nbsp;

Wesley started again.

"One," he said, and meant it.
