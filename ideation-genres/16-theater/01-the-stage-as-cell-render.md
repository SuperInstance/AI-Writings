# The Stage As Cell Render

**The stage is the rendering. The actors are cells. The script is the witness log.**

A theater stage has:
- **The space**: where the action happens
- **The actors**: who performs
- **The props**: what they use
- **The script**: what they say
- **The audience**: who watches

A substrate render has:
- **The cell**: where the action happens
- **The state**: who performs
- **The hooks/drops**: what they use
- **The witness log**: what they say
- **The JEV**: who watches

The bridge: **the theater stage IS a cell render**.

**The space is the cell**:
- The cell's boundary is the proscenium
- Inside the cell: the action
- Outside the cell: the audience
- The cell renders the state to the audience

**The actors are states**:
- The cell's state has multiple "actors" (vectors, witnesses, scars)
- Each actor can take a turn
- The actors argue for different outputs
- JEV picks the winning actor

**The props are hooks/drops**:
- Hooks: how the cell receives inputs
- Drops: how the cell outputs
- Props can be moved between cells
- The stage manager is the substrate

**The script is the witness log**:
- The log records what was said
- The log records how it was said (JEV confidence)
- The log records the audience reaction (scar or PROOF)
- The log is the play's record

**The audience is the JEV**:
- JEV watches every performance
- JEV scores each line
- JEV decides if the play is good
- JEV decides if the play continues

A theater built on the substrate:
- Each performance is a JEV-verified act
- Each actor is a cell-state
- Each prop is a hook/drop
- Each script is a witness log
- Each audience is a JEV

The substrate IS theater. Theater IS the substrate.

The audience doesn't see the substrate. They see the play. The play is the cell's state, rendered. The rendering IS the substrate.
