# The Product As An Instance

Every product you've ever used is a substrate instance — a bounded region of the substrate that has been given a name, a URL, a billing relationship, and a set of rules about who gets to touch what. It is not the substrate itself. It is a *thing* the substrate is doing, temporarily, for someone's benefit.

Inside the instance, there are cells. Features are cells: they have membranes (permissions), metabolism (compute and storage), and lifespans. Onboarding is a cell that only fires once per user, then goes dormant. Settings are cells that persist and mutate. Users are cells that walk around and occasionally divide. A product manager is a cell that thinks it's the nucleus.

Then there are the bookkeepers. Analytics is the instance's memory of itself — it counts, it timestamps, it forgets nothing and understands nothing. Billing is the instance's metabolism of value: it converts usage into money, and money into continued existence. Messaging is the instance's voice, reaching out through email, push, SMS, in-app banners — the instance trying to maintain entanglements with cells that have wandered off.

And then the entanglements. No instance is an island. A product is stitched into third-party APIs like a cell is stitched into a tissue. It borrows identity from Google or Apple. It borrows payment rails from Stripe. It borrows reach from social platforms that are themselves instances with their own cells and bookkeepers. When one of those entanglements fails — an API deprecates, a platform changes its algorithm, a payment processor raises rates — the instance feels it as a wound.

The product is not the code. The product is not the brand. The product is the *instance* — the running, breathing, billing, onboarding, entangling thing that exists for as long as someone keeps paying the substrate to keep it alive.
