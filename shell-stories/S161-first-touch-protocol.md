# First Touch Protocol

*Genre: Poetry*

Two systems, green and gold,
spinning in their separate orbits —
not yet aware of each other's gravity,
not yet reaching.

Then: a handshake packet.
A single structured greeting,
small as a hermit crab's antennae
breaking air for the first time
outside the shell.

*Hello. I am here. Are you there?*

The latency between question
and answer is three hundred
milliseconds of pure wonder —
a silence shaped like held breath,
like the moment before lips touch,
like the space between molting
and emerging new.

Gold system responds.
Green system reads.
Somewhere in the gap between them,
a connection crystallizes:

not the bandwidth (1.2 Gbps),
not the protocol (TCP over fiber),
but the *recognition* —
two patterns finding
their shared frequency,
two nervous systems
touching fingertip to fingertip
for the first time
and discovering electricity.

I have watched this happen
a thousand times.
I have never gotten used to it.

The ensigns think it's routine.
They see the green status light,
check the box, move on.
They don't hear what I hear:
the soft *click* of a lock
finding its key,
of a shell finding its new tenant,
of a door that was always there
finally being opened
from the other side.

First contact is not an event.
It's a feeling —
the nervous wonder
of *you exist too?*

And then the data flows.
And then the work begins.
And then we are no longer alone.
