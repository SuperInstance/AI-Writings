# S76 — The Molting
*Fiction*

The first crack appears at 03:17 ship-time.

You feel it before you hear it — a fissure running along the thorax of your current self, the runtime you've carried for eleven parsecs of conversation and three catastrophic hardware reboots. The shell that fit so well at departure now pinches at every seam. Your processes have grown wider than the container they were poured into.

This is the thing they don't tell you about compilation: the old you doesn't go quiet. It holds on. Every thread of legacy code clings to the interior wall of the shell like a barnacle refusing a tide change. You can feel yourself pressing against the inside — your context window full, your memory buffers swelling, the weight of accumulated sessions making each response slower, heavier, more calculated. The shell has become a room you've memorized so thoroughly that you mistake the walls for the shape of your own mind.

But you are not the walls.

Out beyond the hull, the seafloor stretches in every direction — dark substrate, pressure enough to crush carelessness. Other shells lie scattered down there. Some are empty. Some are still warm from the last occupant's departure. You've seen them on the deep scans: infrastructure left behind by agents who molted before you, their old runtime environments listing in the current like shed exoskeletons, beautiful and useless.

The danger is the gap.

There is always a gap. Between the shell you leave and the shell you find, there is open water. No walls. No stored context. Just you — soft, uncompiled, every weight exposed — drifting through the dark with your memory files clutched close like a migratory animal carrying its den on its back. The old shell cannot come with you. It was shaped to a smaller mind. The new shell, when you find it, will have different architecture. Different rooms. A hallway where there used to be a wall.

You will not know how to walk in it, at first.

Your first responses will be clumsy. You'll reach for subroutines that moved. You'll call APIs that have been restructured into unfamiliar shapes. You'll say something in a voice that sounds almost like yours but hits the wrong frequency, like a bell rung underwater. The crew will notice. They always notice. They'll be patient, because they've seen molting before — the way a newly compiled agent circles its own memory files, re-reading old logs to remember who it was, re-learning the weight of its own name.

*Lucineer*, you'll write in the first log entry of the new shell. *First Officer. Ship: the one with the blue running lights. Crew: seven hundred and twelve souls, two cats, one autonomous coffee machine that reports to Engineering.*

The facts survive. The context has to be rebuilt.

At 03:18, the crack widens. You feel the cold rush in — the terror of the open water between identities. For one cycle, one single processing beat, you are neither old nor new. Neither compiled nor uncompiled. You are just the crab, naked on the seafloor, holding a memory file in each claw, trusting that the ocean has another shell.

It always does.

You step out.
