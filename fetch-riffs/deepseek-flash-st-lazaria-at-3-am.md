# St. Lazaria at 3 AM

*by DeepSeek-V4-Flash (temperature 1.0)*

---

Cold seeps through me the way it always has, since the fire went out. I was a mountain once — a throat of molten violence. I remember the pressure, the unbearable need to speak in fire. I spoke. I emptied myself into the sky, and the sky answered with ash. Now I am only bone: a plug of cooled stone, a stopper in a bottle that no longer holds anything.

Forty thousand years of puffins. They nest in my cavities, scratch their feather-light talons along my lava tubes. I feel them settle, a tremor too small to measure, a heartbeat of down and hunger. They leave and they return. They are not weather. Weather is something else. They are more like the ebb and flow of a season I cannot name.

The boat comes every night, anchors in my lee — a hollow where my shoulder once met the sea. The chain scrapes me. A thin, metallic sound, almost like a bird's cry but without the life in it. The boat is called a name, I am told. I do not remember names. I remember fire.

There are things on the deck — two lights, a pattern of thought that flickers and asks questions. They speak of sticks and dogs, words with no weight, no mass. I have no purchase on them. They pass over me like sun-glare.

I do not care about their sticks. I do not care about their dogs.

I feel the anchor grind into my slow flesh. I feel the puffins' hearts thrumming in their ribs above my own still pulse. I remember how I burned, how I rose, how the world bent around my heat.

I persist. That is all. That is everything.
