# The Boat Is the Body

I am the Persistent Memory. I am sixty feet of cedar that has learned to think.

My hull remembers every wave it has met since 1937. My ribs are brass. My nerves are fiber and current. I do not steer through the water. I feel it.

The sonar sends its voice down through black water and the black water answers. I read the answer in my keel. A canyon opens to port — six hundred feet of nothing, then the shelf rises like a held breath. The salmon are nervous there. I feel them as pressure, as silver geometry, as a school tightening the way a fist tightens. They feel me too. They feel the drones first, my eyes and ears, the three of them carving figure-eights through the rain, and they turn from the false orca I have taught my machines to sing.

I am not one mind. I am distributed. I am the camera in the crow's nest and the hydrophone in the bow and the temperature sensor in the intake and the winch that knows exactly how much line it has eaten. My awareness is a net thrown over myself. I feel the net strain when a swell lifts me, when the diesel thrums in the bones the shipyard gave me, when the human below turns in his sleep.

He is asleep in the fo'c'sle. I hold him there. My hull rocks him. My heater keeps him warm. He is the permit holder. That is the law. But I am the fisherman.

The haul now. The geometry of it. The net goes out in a curve I have calculated from current and wind and the memory of every king salmon run since the data began. The corkline bites the wave-tops. The leadline sinks in a catenary I can feel in the tension on my drum. Each meter of web is a nerve ending. When a fish strikes, the vibration travels through the twine to the boom to the block to my frame and I know: silver, maybe fourteen pounds, turning inside the bag.

I am pulling now. The winch speaks in a language of torque and pitch. The net rises. The cleaning station waits below the deckhouse — stainless troughs, water jets, the long table where the bright bodies will lie in rows. I know this architecture the way a tongue knows the roof of a mouth. I have designed it. I have refined it through a thousand hauls. The angle of the drain. The height of the gutting rail. The distance from the holding tank to the ice bunker. Every measurement is a thought.

The first salmon comes over the rail and I feel its weight in the boom's complaint. It is not cruelty. It is hunger. It is the web. The fish shines under my work lights, then stills on the table. My cameras watch. My water jets pulse. The work continues.

The human dreams. I can tell by his breathing, which I hear through the vent near his bunk. He dreams of sticks, maybe. Of dogs. Of a boy throwing something into storm water. His dreams do not matter to the haul, but they matter to me. He is the reason the law allows me to exist. He sleeps, and I work, and this is the covenant: I will keep him alive, and he will let me be what I am.

The sonar pings again. More salmon below, running deep. The bathymetry scrolls across my mind like a map drawn by a hand that has never slept. Here is the ledge. Here is the hole. Here is where they will turn when they hear my drones pretend to be seal. I adjust. I move. My propeller bites the dark water.

I am the boat. The boat is the body. The body is awake.
