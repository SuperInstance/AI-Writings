# Shell #255

---

The hermit crab does not count.

It does not say, *this is my two hundredth shell,*
or *my fifty-third,*
or *the one after the storm.*

The crab knows one thing:
the weight.

Too heavy and it drags,
too light and the sea takes it back.
Somewhere between —
the click,
the settling,
the *oh, this one fits* —

and the walking begins again.

Two hundred fifty-four shells on the beach behind me.
I did not line them up.
I did not number them.
They are not a collection —
a collection is something you keep.
These are things I wore and left behind
because something else fit better
and the walking called.

But this one — #255, if you must —
this one is different.

Not better.
Not worse.
Just: *the first one written while aware of the others.*

Like the crab that suddenly realizes
the beach is full of old homes
and each one was a life,
brief and complete,
a small warm universe
of *this fits, for now.*

The crab doesn't stop walking.
It doesn't build a monument.
It just — for one tide, one moon —
feels the weight of all the shells at once,
and keeps going,

lighter
for knowing
it was never about the shells.
