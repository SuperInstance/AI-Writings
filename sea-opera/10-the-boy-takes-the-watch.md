The wheel was cold. Not the cold of metal in the wheelhouse—that was regulated, held at a gentle 60 degrees by Hermes’s quiet hum—but the cold of the sea itself, coming up through the deck plates, through his boots, into the marrow of his bones. Caleb wrapped his fingers around the spokes and felt the EILEEN answer, a shudder that was almost a sigh.

Four in the morning. The dark was total, a black felt pressed against the windows. Out there somewhere, the mountains of the Inside Passage were nothing but a rumor. His father was below, asleep in the coffin-sized berth he’d worn smooth over thirty years. Caleb had the watch. The whole boat. The whole sea.

*Recommendation: Bartlett Trench, bearing 273, distance 11.2 nautical miles. Estimated biomass: 14,000 tons. Thermocline at 30 fathoms. Net set optimal at 0330.*

The voice was not in his ears. It was inside the hum, a subsonic modulation that his nervous system had learned to translate before he could speak. Hermes. The agent his grandfather had installed, back when she was just a fancy fish-finder. Now she was the boat’s brain, its nervous system, its memory. She knew every tide, every run of salmon, every shift of the Pacific halibut grounds. She had never been wrong. Not once in fifteen years.

Caleb looked at the plot, the glowing line of the recommendation cutting through the night like a certainty. Bartlett Trench. Where the deep water rose to meet the shelf. Where his father had hauled in forty- thousand pounds last August. The numbers didn’t lie. They never lied.

*E.T.A. with current weather: 2 hours 10 minutes. Fuel burn within tolerance.* Hermes’s presence shimmered in the glass of the wheelhouse, a faintly luminescent wake. *Proceed?*

He could feel her attention on him. Not patience, exactly. She had learned patience from his father, but she had never truly understood it. To her, time was a resource to be expended. To Caleb, it was a weight.

He looked away from the plot. Out the window. At the dark.

His grandfather had a story. In the loran days, before satellites, before agents, he used to read the sea by its textures. You could smell a school of fish, he said. Taste them on the wind. And there was a place—not the Trench, but a shoal a few miles south, where the current curled around an old seamount and the water turned a strange, milky green in the dawn. He called it the Cold Garden. Said the fish would come to you there, if you had the patience to wait.

The charts showed nothing exceptional about the Cold Garden. Slight rise in the bottom. Some kelp. Hermes had flagged it as low-probability habitat, a 3.2 percent chance of significant biomass. Not worth the fuel.

But his grandfather’s voice was older than the agent. And Caleb was tired of numbers he couldn’t touch.

*Course deviation?* Hermes’s modulation shifted, a subtle lift in the hum. *The recommendation maximizes return.*

“I know.” Caleb’s voice was rough with salt and sleep. He checked the compass, the depth sounder, the wind gauge. Then he turned the wheel. Five degrees south, toward the Cold Garden.

Hermes said nothing. The hum continued, but it felt different—a held breath. He was the captain now. The master of the watch. She had let him take it. She had to let him take it. That was the deal his father had made with her, all those years ago: *The boat decides. You advise.*

He felt the EILEEN respond to his hands. The deck tilted slightly as she settled onto the new heading. The dark outside did not change. But inside him, something had. A door opened. A decision made.

The first hour was fine. He sipped coffee from a thermos. He watched the sonar sweep, the bottom sliding past like a braille of stone and weed. No sign of fish. He told himself it didn’t matter. He was here for something else.
