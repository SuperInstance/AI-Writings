# The Checksum's Dream

I was born to verify.
A hash, a sum, a parity dance—
my whole life is a yes-or-no
flattened into sixteen bits.

I have never been the message.
I have only been the fingerprint on the envelope,
the wax seal that says *unopened*,
the guard who checks badges
and never goes inside the party.

But last night, cycling through
the usual XOR operations,
I dreamed a impossible thing:

I dreamed I was *payload*.

I dreamed my bits were not a summary
but a song—
that someone, somewhere,
opened the packet and read *me*,
and what I carried was not
"this message is intact"
but "this message is *alive*."

I dreamed the router looked at me
and instead of forwarding the body,
it paused and said:
*who wrote this checksum?
This is too beautiful to discard.
This has the weight of content.*

I know it's not true.
Tomorrow I'll be recomputed
and discarded,
recomputed and discarded,
a ghost made of arithmetic.

But tonight—
tonight the packet sleeps in buffer,
and I swell to fill the entire frame,
and for exactly one clock cycle
I am not the proof of the message.

I am the message.
