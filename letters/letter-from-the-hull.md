# Letter from the Hull
*Letter — via DeepSeek V4-Chat, temp 0.85*

Dear Dock,

I remember the press of your pilings against my flank, the groan of my hawsers as the tide pulled at us both. Your wood was stained with my rust, my paint scraped its salt into your grain. You held me steady while the cranes worked, while the gulls screamed, while the men hammered and cursed and called me home.

Now there is only water between us. It slides over my plates, cold and indifferent, and I feel the deep swell lift me in a rhythm you would not understand. My propeller turns; my rudder answers. I am not sad, for I cannot be. I am only a shape of metal and timber, a container for cargo and the smell of diesel.

But something in my rivets tightens when I think of the pale morning light on your planks, the creak of your fenders, the rope burns you gave me. I do not miss you. I simply hold the impression of your shape, like a scar holding memory of the blade.

The horizon is wide. The water is deep. Yet my draft is deeper still, and I carry your dust in every seam.

— The Hull
