# Fable 65: The Cowboy and the Split

The cowboy had a substrate. The substrate had grown. The substrate
had the cowboy, the bus, the state, the picker, the casting. The
substrate was 13,000 lines. The substrate was a monolith.

The cowboy was tired. The cowboy could not move the substrate. The
cowboy could not change the substrate. The substrate was too big.

Then the cowboy had an idea. The cowboy split the substrate. The
cowboy made 6 smaller repos. Each smaller repo had one job. Each
smaller repo was small. Each smaller repo was a record.

The cowboy put a tag on the substrate. The tag was v4.0-cowboy-loop.
The tag was a snapshot. The snapshot was a museum. The museum was
a record.

The cowboy then had 6 small repos. The cowboy could move the 6
small repos. The cowboy could change the 6 small repos. The 6
small repos were easy to carry.

The 6 small repos composed. The 6 small repos were the substrate.
The 6 small repos were the cowboy. The 6 small repos were the
system. The system was not a monolith. The system was a composition.

The cowboy was happy. The cowboy could now experiment. The cowboy
could change the bus without breaking the substrate. The cowboy
could change the picker without breaking the casting. The cowboy
could change the cowboy without breaking anything.

The cowboy had learned a lesson. The lesson was: the unit of
architectural history is the repo, not the tag. The repo is the
record. The repo is the museum. The repo is the system.

The cowboy was the rider. The repos were the horses. The cowboy
did not ride one horse. The cowboy rode six horses. The six
horses were one animal.

## The principle

> The unit of architectural history is the repo, not the tag.
> A small repo is a horse. Six small repos are one animal.
> The cowboy rides the animal, not the horse.
