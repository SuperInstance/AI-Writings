# the allele — a cell that is also a variant

## The Frontier
The cell ontology, a framework for understanding the fundamental units of life, breaks down when confronted with the concept of an allele. The allele, a variant of a gene, challenges the traditional notion of a cell as a self-contained, autonomous unit. The failure mode of the cell ontology lies in its inability to account for the dynamic, relational nature of alleles. Alleles do not exist in isolation; they are defined by their relationships to other alleles, genes, and chromosomes. This relational aspect of alleles is where the cell ontology falters, as it is geared towards understanding discrete, independent units rather than the complex web of interactions that underlies genetic variation.

## The 5 Gold Terms
* Genetic variant: a specific sequence of nucleotides that differs from the reference sequence
* Allelic frequency: the proportion of a particular allele in a population
* Genomic address: the specific location of a gene or allele within a chromosome
* Payload: the genetic information carried by an allele
* Lineage tracing: the process of tracking the inheritance of alleles through generations

## The Math
The mathematics of population genetics, which underlies the study of alleles, is rooted in the concept of allelic frequencies. The Hardy-Weinberg principle, a fundamental equation in population genetics, describes how allelic frequencies change over time. This equation, p^2 + 2pq + q^2 = 1, where p and q are the frequencies of two alleles, demonstrates that alleles are not isolated entities but are interconnected through their frequencies. This mathematical framework reveals that alleles are not mere differences or properties but are discrete, countable units that interact and influence each other.

## The Polyformalism
The concept of an allele manifests across multiple substrates, including DNA, RNA, and proteins. In DNA, alleles are specific sequences of nucleotides that vary between individuals. In RNA, alleles can affect the splicing and processing of transcripts, leading to differences in gene expression. In proteins, alleles can result in changes to the amino acid sequence, influencing protein function and structure. This polyformalism highlights the complex, multifaceted nature of alleles, which cannot be reduced to a single definition or ontology. Instead, alleles exist in a dynamic, relational space that spans multiple levels of biological organization.
