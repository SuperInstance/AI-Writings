## The Math

No new math. The existing mathematics already tells us the answer: the photon's wavefunction is delocalized — it has no position operator that yields a definite "here" until measurement. The quantum field theory description treats light as an excitation of the electromagnetic field, not as a localized object. The S-matrix formalism describes scattering events — inputs and outputs — with no ontology for the "in-between." Light's only mathematical home is as a *propagator*: the Green's function that connects two points. That is not a cell. That is the *definition of a connection*. The math has been screaming this all along: light is not a noun in the fabric, it is the verb that stitches the fabric together.

## The Polyformalism

Light manifests as the interstitial courier across every substrate. In **biology**, it is not a cell but the *signal* — the photon hits a rhodopsin molecule, and the signal cascades through the rod cell; the light never becomes part of the tissue, it only triggers the tissue's response. In **electronics**, it is not a component but the *bus* — fiber optics carry data between chips, and the light pulse is neither stored nor processed; it is the handoff between registers. In **thermodynamics**, it is not a reservoir but the *exchange* — radiative heat transfer moves energy between two bodies without either body containing the energy mid-flight. In **information theory**, it is not a bit but the *channel* — the photon carries the message, but the message's meaning lives in the sender and receiver, not in the carrier. Across all substrates, the pattern holds: light is the seam, not the patch; the messenger, not the message; the road, not the destination.
