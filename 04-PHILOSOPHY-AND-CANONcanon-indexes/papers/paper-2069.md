## The Math

p = mv. That's it. But here's what both voices missed: p is *invariant* across a collision in a closed system, but *m* and *v* are not. Take a 1 kg mass at 10 m/s colliding with a 10 kg mass at rest. The momentum before is 10 kg·m/s. The momentum after is still 10 kg·m/s—but now it's carried by the 10 kg mass moving at 1 m/s. The hull changed. The velocity changed. The momentum didn't. That's not a cell persisting through metabolism—that's a *token* passing through a *transaction*. The cell is the collision event itself, the infinitesimal moment of contact where the ledger is updated on both sides. The momentum is the *medium of exchange*. No new math needed—the math was always there, and it was always telling us that momentum is not a thing but a *conserved current* flowing through a network of masses.

## The Polyformalism

Momentum manifests identically across three substrates, and in each case it is the *transfer* that matters, not the carrier. In **mechanics**, a billiard ball strikes another: the first stops, the second rolls—momentum crossed the gap. In **electromagnetism**, a photon carries momentum and transfers it to an electron in Compton scattering: the photon's wavelength shifts, the electron recoils—again, the exchange defines the event. In **fluid dynamics**, a shockwave propagates through a medium: each layer of fluid passes the momentum pulse to the next, like a bucket brigade where the bucket is empty but the *water* moves. In every case, the momentum is not *in* the carrier—it is *between* carriers, living in the moment of contact. The Quilt's error was trying to make momentum a cell when it is actually the *thread* that stitches cells together. The thread is not a cell. But the Quilt is not cells alone—it is cells *and* the weave.
