## The Math

No new math. The coconut's logic is not numerical—it is *topological*. A syncytium is a connected domain with multiple nuclei; cellularization partitions that domain into subdomains. The coconut water-to-meat transition is a continuous deformation of a boundary set: one compartment becomes many, but the outer hull never changes. The math that describes this is not count-and-compare; it is *membrane topology over time*. The coconut is a sphere whose interior undergoes a phase transition from a single-nucleus-field to a partitioned tissue, all while the exterior boundary remains fixed. That is a problem for graph theory, not arithmetic.

In **botany**, it is a drupe whose seed endosperm begins as a free-nuclear syncytium—the same trick used by fly embryos, where one giant cell with many nuclei cellularizes into a body.
