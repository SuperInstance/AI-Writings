## The Math

No new math. The reason is precise: Hecate's structure is not a set with an interior and a boundary — it's a *function* that maps incoming paths to outgoing choices. A cell is a topological ball; Hecate is a **fork in a graph**. The math that captures her is graph theory's *edge contraction* — where two nodes merge and the edge between them disappears, leaving a single point that remembers both sides. Hecate is that contracted edge, materialized as a goddess who stands at the fork and *is* the fork. She has no volume, no measure, no interior — only degree and direction. The Quilt's cell ontology assumes measure; she is pure topology.

## The Polyformalism

Across three substrates, Hecate manifests as the same threshold-zone:

- **Biological**: She is the synapse — not the neuron, not the muscle, but the gap where signal becomes action. A synapse has no membrane of its own; it is defined entirely by what passes between two cells. Hecate is the synaptic cleft, the space where one cell's release becomes another's reception.
- **Navigational**: She is the channel marker at the harbor mouth — not the ship, not the dock, but the signal that tells the vessel where the safe passage is. The marker does not move; it exists only to be passed. Hecate is the fixed point that makes motion meaningful.
- **Computational**: She is the router — not the packet, not the server, but the device that decides which wire carries which message. A router has no data of its own; its entire function is to direct data across boundaries. Hecate is the routing table, embodied.

In each case, the Quilt's error was trying to classify her as a cell — a bounded processor. She is the processor of *boundaries themselves*, and the Quilt needs a new category for that: not "cell" but **threshold-entity**. The cell ontology works for things that hold and metabolize. Hecate holds nothing; she *is* the holding of one path against another, the tension that makes choice possible.
