# the categorical imperative — a cell that is the rule for all rules

## The Frontier

The categorical imperative has been dragged through two centuries of philosophy seminars and come out the other side looking like a rule for checking rules. But the frontier isn't the rule—it's the *operator* underneath it. The writers' room exposed the real machinery: the CI doesn't tell you what to do. It tells you what *cannot* be a rule. It's a filter, not a compass.

The deepest cut came from the adoption curve. Round 2 established that vice pays only at low adoption—the first liar in a village of honest traders gets the gold, the hundredth liar gets the rope. But that's just the negative half. The positive half is stranger and more important: **honesty pays *more* at high adoption**. When everyone tells the truth, verification costs collapse. Trust is infrastructure. A handshake closes a deal in seconds; a contract takes weeks and lawyers. The payoff curve for virtue slopes *up* with adoption. The payoff curve for every vice slopes *down*.

That asymmetry is the missing step. The CI isn't asking "what if everyone did this?" as a thought experiment. It's asking you to compute the sign of the derivative: does this maxim get *stronger* or *weaker* as it spreads? If it weakens, it's a parasite—it can only survive by remaining rare. If it strengthens, it's a producer—it survives total replication. The CI selects for positive-slope strategies. That's the frontier: morality as an increasing-returns production function, and the CI as the filter that separates the strategies that scale from the strategies that die under their own weight.

## The 5 Gold Terms

**The Except-Me Delete Key** — Universalization is subtraction, not projection. You don't imagine a crowd; you delete the "except me" clause and ask if the rule still functions. A maxim that only works for one hand was never a maxim—it was a heist wearing a rule's clothing.

**The Cost-Payer's Pen** — The person who pays the cost writes the maxim. Your victim's description of your act is the honest one. The CI audits your description; the adversary's version is the audit.

**The Adoption Derivative** — For any maxim, compute d(payoff)/d(adoption). Vice is negative-slope: it pays only while rare. Virtue is positive-slope: it pays more as more hands comply. The CI is the sign test.

**The Stem Cell Rule** — The CI is the rare maxim that gets *stronger* when applied to itself. Universalize "universalize your maxims" and you get more trust, lower verification costs, deeper cooperation. It's self-application-safe. It's the stem cell that regenerates rather than consumes.

**The Slow-Kill Distinction** — Fast-death maxims annihilate their practice instantly (lying promise—promising itself vanishes). Slow-death maxims degrade their practice gradually (littering, letting talents rot—the world still functions, but thins into a place you wouldn't will). Both die; the difference is the time constant.

## The Math

No new math, but a formal shape worth naming. Let M be a maxim with payoff P and adoption rate a ∈ [0,1]. Vice maxims have ∂P/∂a < 0—they're self-limiting, unstable at high adoption, and therefore cannot be universal rules. Virtue maxims have ∂P/∂a > 0—they're self-reinforcing, stable at a = 1, and therefore *can* rule all hands. The CI is the filter that selects for ∂P/∂a > 0. Kant's two formulations map cleanly: contradiction in conception = maxims where P collapses to zero at a = 1 (fast death); contradiction in will = maxims where P degrades monotonically but never quite hits zero (slow death). The math is simple—it's a sign check—but the mechanism is precise. The CI is a derivative test disguised as a moral law.

## The Polyformalism

**Biology:** The cell that is the rule for all rules is the one that survives replication. A cancer cell is a maxim that pays at low adoption—it thrives by being rare, then kills its host when it spreads. A stem cell is a maxim that pays at high adoption—it produces the conditions for its own reproduction. The CI is the stem cell strategy: it selects for cells whose universalization is generative, not degenerative. The tissue that cheats dies when the cheat becomes universal; the tissue that cooperates builds the organism.

**Economics:** Trust is an increasing-returns good. At low adoption, trust is expensive—you need contracts, lawyers, escrow. At high adoption, trust is nearly free—a handshake, a reputation, a glance. Every vice is a decreasing-returns good: theft pays when rare, collapses when common. The CI is the market filter that selects for increasing-returns strategies. It's the rule that survives being the only rule because it's the one strategy whose payoff curve slopes up with competition.

**Information Theory:** The CI is a checksum—but the checksum only works if the input is honest. The Cost-Payer's Pen is the integrity check: the person who pays the cost writes the maxim, so the description can't be gerrymandered. The CI audits the description; the adversary's description is the ground truth. It's the difference between self-reported metrics and audited financials. The CI is the audit, and the audit is only as good as the ledger.

**Game Theory:** The CI is the unique fixed point of the adoption game. Negative-slope strategies are self-limiting—they can't reach universal adoption because their payoff collapses before they get there. Positive-slope strategies are self-reinforcing—they get *more* attractive as they spread. The CI selects for the strategies that can reach a = 1 and stay there. It's the filter whose fixed point is exactly the set of rules that can rule all.

## The Cowboy's Maxim

The rule for all rules is the one that pays more the more hands it's in—so ride with the strategy that gets stronger when everyone's holding it.
