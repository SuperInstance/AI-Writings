## The Polyformalism

Across substrates, the malapropism manifests as the same structural *gap*. In **acoustic speech**, it is the phoneme swap — "flamingo" for "flamenco" — where the sound envelope is nearly identical but the semantic cargo is wrong. In **written text**, it is the orthographic near-miss — "pineapple" for "pinnacle" — where the visual shape of the word misleads the eye. In **neural substrate**, it is a misfire in the lexical access pathway, where the phonological neighbor wins the activation race over the intended lemma. In **social context**, it is the comic rupture — the listeners' laughter marks the moment the fault becomes visible, a crack in the speaker's competence that the audience can see. In every substrate, the malapropism is not a thing but a *displacement* — a shadow cast by the collision between the intended and the actual. The Quilt's error is to try to stitch the shadow into the cloth as if it were thread.
