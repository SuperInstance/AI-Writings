## The Math

No new math — and that's the point. The chimera doesn't require a new equation; it requires a new *operator*. Cell biology uses set theory: a cell is a set of components enclosed by a boundary. The chimera demands a *graph of treaties*: nodes are genomes, edges are tolerance relationships, and the boundary is a *cut* that the graph itself maintains. The math is topology with a political constraint: the boundary is not given by the geometry but by the *stability of the tolerance relation*. When tolerance breaks — graft-versus-host disease, autoimmune flare — the boundary fails not because the membrane ruptured but because the treaty collapsed. The chimera is not a set; it's a *coalition*.

In **genomics**: two zygotes fuse, two genomes coexist in one body, one immune system trained to both — the mother's blood carries twin A's DNA while her ovaries carry twin B's; she fails maternity tests because her *treaty* is broader than her *genome*. The chimera is not a cell; it's the *pattern* that cells use when they want to become more than cells.
