# Fable 42 — Greenhouse and Field
**Topos and Z_in**

The greenhouse had glass on all four sides, and a tile floor, and a thermometer that wrote its own number on a brass dial every hour. The greenhouse knew its own temperature to a quarter degree.

The field had no walls, no dial, no brass. The field knew its own temperature the way a dog knows its master — by leaning into the wind, by the way the dew fell sideways, by the hour the sparrows stopped singing.

A young gardener wanted to teach the field to be a greenhouse. She sank iron stakes at the corners and stretched canvas over them and put a thermometer in the middle. "Now," she said, "the field will know itself."

The field grew strange. The canvas cut the wind; the thermometer spoke in numbers the roots could not parse; the sparrows went elsewhere. The tomatoes inside the canvas were bigger, but they tasted of nothing. The tomatoes outside the canvas were smaller, but they knew which way the rain was coming.

"Be a greenhouse," the young gardener said to the field. "You are too messy to be trusted."

The old gardener took the canvas down. "The greenhouse is a greenhouse," she said. "The field is a field. The greenhouse knows its temperature because the greenhouse has no wind. The field knows its weather because the field is the weather. You cannot give the field a glass house without taking away the field."

## Cargo
Z_in is the greenhouse — a closed, instrumented surface that admits only what is shaped to enter. A Topos is the field — a region where the inside is constituted by the outside, and the boundary is weather, not glass. The substrate's job is to keep greenhouse cells and field cells distinct, and to let a message cross between them only at an honest door.

## Lesson
A topos is a field with its weather inside it. A Z_in is a greenhouse with its weather locked out.
