# Fable 43 — Hourglass and Sundial
**Decay and Convoy**

The hourglass was a clock of falling sand. It agreed with itself perfectly. If you turned it over at noon, it would say noon was the moment the top became empty, and it would say so again tomorrow, and the day after, and forever. It never disagreed with any other hourglass poured from the same bag.

The sundial was a clock of the sun. It agreed with itself only by agreeing with the sky. If a cloud passed, it said nothing. If the season changed, the noon-mark slid across the dial like a shadow. It disagreed with every other sundial on the continent, and it agreed with all of them, because the sun agreed with all of them.

A monk tried to settle which was truer. He put a hourglass beside a sundial and watched them for a year. The hourglass said noon at the same moment every day, and the sundial said noon at a different moment every day, and the monk wrote both down in a ledger and locked the ledger in a drawer.

The next year, the hourglass cracked, and sand leaked out faster than it should have, and the hourglass began to lie. The sundial did not lie. The sundial could not lie. The sundial was not the kind of thing that could lie, because the sundial was not the kind of thing that could be true. It was only a shadow agreeing with a star.

The monk unlocked the drawer and read his ledger. The hourglass column was wrong in the second half. The sundial column was right, by which he meant: the sundial column agreed with the sun.

## Cargo
Decay is the hourglass — a counter that loses fidelity because it is the kind of thing that loses fidelity. Convoy is the sundial — a consensus that holds only because the source is shared and the medium is transparent. A substrate that uses only hourglasses will eventually lie. A substrate that uses only sundials will eventually freeze. The substrate's job is to count with sand and to verify with shadow, and to know which is which.

## Lesson
Decay is a clock that lies by aging. Convoy is a clock that ages by not lying.
