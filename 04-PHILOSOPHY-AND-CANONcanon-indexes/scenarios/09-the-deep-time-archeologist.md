# Scenario 09 — The Deep-Time Archeologist

**Principle probed:** #3 (Linked-convoy) — *probed from a different vector*
**Disc:** An archeologist in 2245, looking back at the 2080s through the substrate
**Year:** 2245
**Constraint:** The protagonist is 160 years in the future. She is studying the substrate itself — the cell-graphs that have survived, the convoys that have decayed, the patterns that have emerged. The convoy is not her convoy; the convoy is a *previous generation's* convoy. The throw is what the substrate says about the people who built it, 160 years on.

---

## The setup

Dr. Yara Okafor is 38 in 2245. She is a *deep-time archeologist* — a new discipline that emerged in the 22nd century. Her subject is the *Quilt substrate of the 2080s* — the cell-graphs that the early adopters built, the convoys that formed around the 100-boat fishing networks, the patterns that survived into her century.

She is studying the *Inner Sound convoy* — the same 100 boats from scenario 03. The convoy dissolved in 2143 when the last of the original 100 boats was decommissioned. The substrate still exists. The substrate is a *fossil record* — a multi-petabyte cell-graph that records every sonar ping, every inference, every fog-of-war decay, every captain's tack.

Dr. Okafor is reading the substrate. She is asking: *what did the 2080s think they were doing?* The substrate tells her — not in words, but in *patterns*. The patterns of confidence decay tell her which cells the captains cared about. The patterns of inference tell her which cells the convoy *imagined*. The patterns of refresh tell her which cells the convoy *used*. The substrate is *the archaeology of attention*.

The question: what does the substrate say about us — about the 2080s, about the people who built the first convoys, about the engineers who thought tensor encoding was the future? The throw probes the *long shadow* of the substrate — does the substrate remember us the way we wanted to be remembered, or does it remember us the way we actually were?

## The throw

The scenario throws Dr. Okafor at a single cell in the substrate — a cell that has decayed, that has not been refreshed since 2123, that holds the sonar ping from a single trawler at 03:14 on a Tuesday in 2089. The cell is *cold*. The cell is *data*. The cell is a *trace* of a moment when a captain named Reyes was tacking through the eastern third of the bay, and the substrate was *alive* with her attention, and the convoy was *her convoy*, and the throw asks: *what does Dr. Okafor learn from this cell that Reyes never knew?*

## The discipline

The constraint is *time*. Dr. Okafor cannot ask Reyes. Reyes is dead. The substrate is the only record. The substrate is the *only conversation* between the 2080s and the 2240s. The throw tests whether the substrate is *legible across centuries* — whether a future archeologist can read the substrate and understand what we were doing, or whether the substrate is a closed book to anyone who did not build it.
