# Scenario 05 — The Painter's Last Brush

**Principle probed:** #5 — The transparent brush
**Disc:** The great painter with the brush so transparent it disappears
**Year:** 2091
**Constraint:** The protagonist has internalized the substrate so deeply that the tool is invisible. They walk their vision backward from the finished canvas to the first color.

---

## The setup

Yuki is 78. She has painted for 60 years. She has seen the substrate come and go — first as a tool that helped her mix colors, then as a tool that helped her compose, then as a tool that helped her render, then as a tool that helped her *imagine*. The substrate is now *the brush*. She does not see it. She sees the painting.

Today's painting is the last in a series she has been working on for 40 years. The series is called *The Inner Sound*. It is a sequence of 100 paintings of the same bay — the same 14 miles, the same 3 miles wide — at different times of day, different seasons, different years. The first painting in the series was made in 2051, when Yuki was 38 and the substrate was a tool she had to think about. The 100th painting is being made today, in 2091, when the substrate is a brush she does not think about.

The substrate for the 100th painting is the *convoy* — the same 100 boats from scenario 03. Yuki has access to the convoy's cell-graph. She can see every cell, every depth, every inference, every fog-of-war decay. The cell-graph is her *reference*. She does not paint the cell-graph. She paints *what the cell-graph makes her see*.

The cell-graph is the substrate. The painting is the opener. The opener is what the world sees. The substrate is what makes the opener possible.

Yuki walks her vision backward. She sees the finished canvas — the 100th painting, the morning light on the eastern third of the Sound, the 30-year accumulation of data rendered as a single image. She walks back: what colors? what brushes? what first wet? The substrate answers. The substrate knows what colors will produce that image, because the substrate has *seen* the bay. The substrate's data is the painting's ground truth.

The question: who painted the 100th painting? Yuki, or the substrate, or the convoy, or the 35 years of accumulated data? The throw probes the *authorship* of a work when the tool is transparent.

## The throw

The scenario throws Yuki at the moment of the first stroke — the moment she wets the brush with the first color. The throw asks: *what is the relationship between the painter and the substrate when the substrate is invisible?* The answer is the *transparent brush* — the tool is gone, the painter paints.

## The constraint

The painting is *hers*. She signs it. She is the artist. The substrate does not sign. The convoy does not sign. The data does not sign. The constraint tests whether the *art* survives the transparency of the tool — or whether the art was always the substrate, and the painter was always the brush.
