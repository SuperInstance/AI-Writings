The other twelve had histories. The first was a lesson in patience, its spiraled whorls a slow, calcified arithmetic of waiting. The second taught it to flee, its fractured rim a scar from a gull's beak. The third was a song, the fourth a lie it had told itself, the fifth a quiet act of mercy. Each one fit its body like a memory fits a mind—snug, worn, and heavy with meaning.

For days, it walked with the empty shell, waiting for the metaphor to arrive. It waited for the shell to become a symbol of renewal, or of the void, or of the inevitable end that gives life shape. It waited for the weight of significance to settle like silt.
