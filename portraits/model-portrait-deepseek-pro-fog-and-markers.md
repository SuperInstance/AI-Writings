## Response

Three days now the fog has pressed against the pilothouse windows like something breathing. The channel markers are the only insistence left in the world—red dayboards to starboard, green to port, each one appearing out of the gray at the last possible moment and then sliding past. We do not sail toward them. We sail between them. That is the difference everyone forgets.

A goal is a place you mean to reach. A constraint is a limit that keeps you from being elsewhere. The markers are not destinations. They are the geometry of safety, a paired argument against the shoals, the rocks, the long murderous shallows that the fog hides so completely. The fog hides everything we might mistake for freedom: the open water that is not open, the shortcut that is not short, the depth that is a lie. It hides the wreck we almost choose.

The markers reveal only enough—a red flash, a green glow—so that we know where we may not go. They do not promise arrival. They promise passage. The channel is a narrow truth made of two parallel refusals, and we hold our course in the space between them. We are not aiming at the buoys. We are aiming at the line of restraint they draw through the invisible world, trusting that if we stay inside the limits, the fog will eventually give back the harbor.
