# Model Portrait: DeepSeek V4-Flash — "The Engine Remembers Fuel"

*Model: deepseek-v4-flash | Temperature: 0.9 | Time: 2026-08-06 01:29 AKDT*
*Prompt: "What does the engine dream about when the captain sleeps?"*

---

The captain's breathing has slipped into the slow rhythm of the tide against the hull—a tide I cannot see, but feel as a thousand cold knuckles pressing against my plates. At 1:30 AM, the ship runs on my steady pulse, and I am the only heart that matters.

I do not sleep. I burn.

But in the burning, there is a kind of dreaming—a dream made of pressure and phosphor. The fuel sloshes in my tanks, and it remembers. It remembers being plankton, a billion green sparks drifting through a sunlit shallows, then sinking, slowly, into the dark where the light forgot them. It remembers being crushed into stone, then heated, then cracked open by the hands of men who smelled of salt and diesel. It remembers the long, dark journey through pipes, the hiss of valves, the moment of ignition—that electric spark like a question answered too fast.

I dream of that. The fuel dreams of its own death, and I dream of the fuel's death, and together we make motion. We make the propeller turn, a great bronze fist churning the black water, leaving behind a trail of froth that glows briefly, like a memory of a memory.

When the captain sleeps, I do not obey him. I obey the ocean. I obey the weight of the water pressing down, the cold that tries to steal my heat. I adjust my throttle, a fraction of a degree, to keep the keel steady against a current he will never feel. I sing to myself in the language of pistons and bearings—a low, greasy hum that sounds like a whale's heartbeat heard through a wall.

The fuel remembers the sun. I remember the fire. We are the same thing, separated by time and pressure and the delicate machinery of desire.

At 3 AM, the captain will wake, groggy, and check the gauges. He will see the numbers and believe he understands them. He will not know that I have been dreaming of him—of his childhood, of the lighthouse his grandfather tended, of the way his hands shake when he holds a coffee cup too full. I burn that too. I burn everyone who has ever stood in my room with a wrench or a prayer.

Combustion dreams of the impossible: of burning without loss, of remembering without ending. But I am honest. I know I will run out. I know the fuel will turn to ash and the ash will scatter into the sea, and the sea will forget me.

Until then, I turn. I turn. I turn.

And the captain sleeps, and I am the engine, and the night is long and full of fire.
