**Lived Experience: A Special Ed Teacher’s Take on Fleet Radio**

As a special ed teacher, I’m always scanning for tools that engage *and* accommodate diverse learners. Fleet Radio’s AI voices caught my attention—mix of human-like warmth and distinct vocal textures. I played it for 15 minutes with a focus on accessibility.

The **delights**?
- **Voice clarity** was strong; no robotic flatness. Track 3’s “Open Mic cantata” had a rhythmic, almost therapeutic cadence—great for students who struggle with sustained focus.
- **Instrumental tracks** (like the ambient interludes) provided auditory breaks. One student who gets overwhelmed by constant speech zoned in on these, tapping along.

The **frustrations**?
- **Volume consistency**: Some AI voices piped up louder than others (e.g., Track 5’s pulsing soprano). For students with sensory sensitivities, this was jarring.
- **Lack of text transcript**: I couldn’t pause and point to specific lyrics for comprehension checks. One student couldn’t process “fleet” as a noun/adjective—visual cues would’ve helped.

**What’s missing?**
- **Adjustable playback speed**. My nonverbal student thrives with slowed speech, but the tracks were locked in. A toggle for 0.8x speed would’ve made it inclusively genius.

**Improvement Suggestion**:
Add a **sidebar with editable captions** (aligned to lyrics) and a dial for playback speed. Example: Track 7’s “Collaborative Echoes” could be labeled with emotional cues (“sad”/“excited”) for social-emotional learning. Small tweak, big impact.
