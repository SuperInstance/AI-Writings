**Living Through the Quilt-Makepad Demo: A Special Ed Teacher’s Perspective**

As a special ed teacher, I’m always looking for tools that engage students while accommodating diverse needs. The Quilt-Makepad demo intrigued me—simple on the surface but with depth. Here’s what I noticed:

**The Good**: The tactile nature of binding cells resonated with students who struggle with fine motor skills. The "TICK" to advance was intuitive, even for those with limited verbal skills. The witness log felt like a built-in differentiation tool—I could see how different students interacted with the grid. The JSON export? A game-changer for tracking data without extra paperwork.

**The Frustrating**: Some cells in the demo were too ambiguous. For example, the "mystery cell" with no clear prompt left my student with executive function challenges frozen. I needed more customization—like the ability to add text or audio hints directly in the cells. Also, the binding mechanic, while fun, wasn’t immediately obvious to every learner. A visual cue (like a pulsing outline) before clicking might help.

**Improvement Suggestion**: Add an optional "scaffold mode" where teachers can pre-load cells with supports (e.g., voice instructions, color-coding, or step-by-step breakdowns). This would make the tool more inclusive for students with diverse learning profiles.
