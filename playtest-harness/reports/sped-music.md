**My 15 Minutes with the Interactive Music Composition Demo – A Special Ed Teacher’s Perspective**

As someone who constantly adapts lessons for diverse learners, I approached the music composition demo with curiosity. The concept—clicking "cells" to bind "witnesses" and hearing chords—was engaging, especially for students who thrive on cause-and-effect feedback. I loved how the visual log filled with each selection; for students with auditory processing needs, this scaffolding is invaluable. Watching a student with limited verbal skills point to the screen and giggle when a dissonant chord played? Priceless.

However, the friction was immediate. The instructions were abstract ("bind witnesses?"). A student who needed step-by-step guidance got stuck. Without explicit labels (e.g., "click to play a chord"), students with executive function challenges floundered. The demo also lacked a "reset" button, which became frustrating when students wanted to experiment freely.

**Scoring:**

| Dimension | Score (1-10) | One-line reason |
|-----------|--------------|------------------|
| **Fun Factor** | 7 | Students laughed at the sounds, but directions held them back. |
| **Educational Value** | 6 | Teaches chord relationships, but needs context for IEP goals. |

**One Suggestion:** Add a "Customize" mode with options for:
- High-contrast visuals (for colorblind students).
- Text-to-speech labels (for non-readers).
- Adjustable grid size (larger cells for motor planning support).
