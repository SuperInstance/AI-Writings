As I explored the Quilt-Makepad demo, I was initially intrigued by the interactive cells and the binding mechanism. I clicked on a few cells, bound them together, and watched as the witness log updated. The ticking sound effect was a nice touch, and I appreciated the option to export the data as JSON. However, after about 10 minutes, I started to feel a bit limited by the lack of clear learning objectives or guidance on how to use the tool effectively.

What delighted me was the potential for creativity and experimentation that the demo offered. I could see my students enjoying the process of binding cells and watching the witness log update. The interactive nature of the demo was engaging, and I appreciated the simplicity of the interface.

What frustrated me was the lack of clarity on how to use the tool to achieve specific learning goals. As a teacher, I need to ensure that any technology I use in the classroom aligns with our curriculum standards. I would have liked to see more explicit connections to learning objectives, such as how the demo could be used to teach concepts like variables, functions, or data analysis.
