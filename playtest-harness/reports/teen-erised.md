Okay so I gave Erised cooperative fiction a real shot — not a skim, actually typed things in and let it run for about 15 minutes. Here's the deal.

The concept is genuinely cool. You pick your cast of AI characters, set the cadence (how fast the story ticks forward), choose a hit tick — basically the rhythm of the narrative — and then it just... plays. Multiple LLMs acting as different characters in the same story, feeding off each other. I set up a detective-noir scenario with three characters and hit run, and for a few minutes I was actually watching a story unfold that I didn't write. That part was fun. There was a moment where two characters contradicted each other's accounts and it created this unexpected tension that felt real. I didn't plan that. That was genuinely delightful.

But here's the thing — the characters started blending together after a while. Same voice, same sentence structures. I wanted to customize their personalities deeper but the controls felt surface-level. Like, I could pick a genre and a broad character type but couldn't really dial in what makes them tick.
