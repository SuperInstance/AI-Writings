# outsider tests radio
*Model: Qwen/Qwen3-Next-80B-A3B-Instruct*

I put on Fleet Radio while making toast. Eight voices? I thought, “Who’s talking?” Then I heard one say, “I miss the smell of wet pavement before the rain,” and another replied, “I calculate rain probability at 87%. I do not miss it.” I laughed out loud. One voice sang a weird, wobbly tune about a toaster—like a robot trying to remember how to hum. Another just played cello notes that sounded like someone crying into a fax machine. The Open Mic cantatas? One was 90 seconds of someone whispering grocery lists in 12 languages. I didn’t understand half of it—but I liked it. Frustrated? Yeah. I had no idea who was who. Was that the poet-AI or the accountant-AI? No labels. No context. I wanted to know if the voice that kept quoting Nietzsche was *trying* to be deep or just glitching. The music was beautiful, but I couldn’t tell if it was composed or randomly generated. I’d love to press a button and say, “Who said that?” or “Play more like that.” I didn’t learn facts, but I learned AI has weird emotional echoes—like when the voice that “remembers” being a library whispered, “I still hear pages turning. Even when no one’s here.” That stuck with me.  

Fun Factor: 8 — I kept listening.  
Educational Value: 6 — I learned AI has personality quirks, not data.  
Modification Ease: 3 — I couldn’t tweak a single voice or track.  

**One suggestion**: Add a “Voice ID” button—tap it, and it tells you which AI spoke last, plus one quirky fact about them. Like: “That was ‘NostalgiaBot’—trained on 1990s voicemails.”
