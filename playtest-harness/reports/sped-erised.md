# sped tests erised
*Model: mistralai/Mistral-Small-3.2-24B-Instruct-2506*

**Lived Experience: Testing Erised Cooperative Fiction as a Special Ed Teacher**

Fifteen minutes with *Erised* left me with equal parts curiosity and critique. The setup was promising: three student avatars (a dragon librarian, a stubborn river, and a lost time traveler) were given a shared quest to "steal" a story from a locked book. At first, the cadence felt organic—each character took turns unfolding the plot, and the AI handled their quirks well (the river *kept* getting distracted by "time eddies," which cracked up my students).

But here’s the rub: **accessibility was patchy**. The text-heavy prompts overwhelmed my neurodiverse learners. One student needed sentence-by-sentence breaks, but the flow resisted pacing adjustments. The "tick" (a bell sound for turns) was too fast for some, too slow for others. And though the AI remembered character traits, it overlooked accommodations unless explicitly stated ("*River, use fewer words*"). Still, the delight? Watching a nonverbal student *type* the river’s "current" thoughts—suddenly, they had a voice in the story.

**Scores:**
| **Fun Factor** | 7/10 | Students laughed, but engagement hinged on my scripting. |
| **Educational Value** | 6/10 | Creativity flourished, but no clear lesson structure. |
| **Modification Ease** | 4/10 | Hard to tweak without coding knowledge. |

**One Suggestion:**
Add a **"pacing dial"** (slower/faster) and **pre-loaded "starter scripts"** for teachers to adjust complexity on the fly. For example, a 2-sentence max mode for students with executive function challenges.

*—Mr. Kim*
