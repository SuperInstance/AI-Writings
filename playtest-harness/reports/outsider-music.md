# outsider tests music
*Model: Qwen/Qwen3-Next-80B-A3B-Instruct*

I clicked a cell. It lit up like a firefly. Then another. And suddenly—bass. Not just any bass. A low, rumbling groan that made my coffee mug vibrate. I didn’t know I’d made a chord. I just clicked. Then a witness log popped up: “Witness 3: saw a dog bark at 3 a.m.” and “Witness 7: heard rain on a tin roof.” I didn’t know those were musical notes. But now they were. I clicked a third cell. A high, glassy chime. It sounded like my grandma’s wind chime. I laughed out loud. That’s when I realized: I didn’t need to know music. I just needed to remember things.

Frustration? The witness log didn’t explain *why* certain memories made certain sounds. Why did “dog barking” = bass? Was that random? Or was there a secret code? I clicked 20 cells. Only three sounds changed. Felt like poking a black box.

Delighted? The way the chord grew thicker as I added witnesses—like stacking memories. I made a sad chord with “lost keys” and “empty chair.” It sounded like loneliness. That was magic.

Would I keep playing? Yeah. It’s like emotional LEGO.  
Learned something real? Sort of. I learned memories have weight. Not how chords work, but how they *feel*.  
Could I change it? Not unless I’m a coder. No buttons. No sliders. Just cells and silence.

**Fun Factor**: 8  
**Educational Value**: 6  
**Modification Ease**: 2  

One concrete suggestion: Add a “Why this sound?” button. Click a witness, hit it, and hear: “This memory = low pitch because it’s heavy.” Just one sentence. That’s all I needed.
