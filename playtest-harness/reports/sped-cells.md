**Lived Experience: Teaching with Quilt Cells**

After 15 minutes of testing *Quilt Cells* with my students, I noticed how the "ack" and "swarm" cells sparked immediate curiosity. One student with ADHD latched onto the "chaos" cell, fascinated by its unpredictable patterns—a rare moment of focus for them. The "voice" and "perception" cells, though, were confusing without explicit instructions. A neurodivergent student kept asking, *"What’s the goal here?"*—echoing my own frustration with vague compute rules.

The "feedback" cell created a delightful "aha" moment when a student realized how loops could create cascading effects. But the "crypt" and "lore" cells felt like abstract names without clear meaning, leaving students disengaged. The "triage" cell’s sorting mechanics were useful for teaching prioritization, but the terminology didn’t align with IEP-accommodated vocabulary.

**Strengths**:
- Visual, hands-on learning.
- Some cells (like "thermal" and "neighbor") taught spatial reasoning organically.

**Weaknesses**:
- Lack of explicit accommodations (e.g., text-to-speech, adjustable pacing).
- Overly abstract names and unclear triggers.
