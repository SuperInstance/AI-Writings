# kid tests radio
*Model: ByteDance/Seed-2.0-mini*

Hi, I’m Theo, 8. Yesterday I used Fleet Radio for 15 minutes while gluing my Lego Mars rover together. At first I just put it on because my mom said it was fun background noise for building, but it was way cooler than my dinosaur story podcast!

First, a squeaky robot voice played a sparkly space guitar riff, then suddenly switched to a deep, rumbling captain voice from the open mic segment singing a silly song about using asteroid pebbles as rover wheels. I laughed so hard I dropped my hot glue stick right on my sock. Then there was this wobbly wind-chime instrumental that made my rover feel like it was floating on my desk—super weird and fun. I counted 8 different voices total! Some sounded like my grandpa, some like my best friend’s little sister, some even like they talked through a tin can.

The only bummer was when I tried to skip to the fun space song I heard last time, but I couldn’t find the skip button fast enough. I had to listen to a guy ramble about radio waves for two whole minutes, and I didn’t get any of it. It was just boring noise while I stuck my rover’s solar panels on. I didn’t even know what radio waves were before, so that part taught me nothing useful at all.

| Dimension | Score (1-10) | One-line reason |
|-----------|--------------|------------------|
| **Fun Factor** | 9 | I want to use it every time I build Legos, only had one annoying wait |
| **Educational Value** | 2 | I only heard big words I didn’t understand, no useful lessons |
| **Modification Ease** | 3 | I couldn’t figure out how to add my own space songs to the queue |

My one concrete fix is: Make a big, bright red skip button that’s easy to tap even when my hands are covered in glue and Lego pieces, so I can jump past boring talky parts in one quick tap.
