# outsider tests cells
*Model: Qwen/Qwen3-Next-80B-A3B-Instruct*

I sat down with these 26 Quilt cells like they were weird Lego blocks. “Ack”? Clicked it. Nothing happened. “Chirp”? Made a bird noise. “Death”? Screen went black for 3 seconds. I laughed. “Flock”? Tiny dots swarmed like ants. “Thermal”? Colors bled like a heat map. “Crypt”? Just said “encrypted.” No clue what that meant. I kept clicking. “Perception” showed me a mirror of my own mouse movements. “Lore” popped up a fake history: “In 1997, the first whisper was heard.” Was that real? I don’t know. I liked that. “Swarm” and “Flock” felt alive. “Triage” just yelled “ERROR” over and over. Annoying. “Music” played a 3-note loop. Cute. “Twist” made everything spin. I spun it again. And again. Got dizzy. “Partition” split the screen into boxes — each box had a different cell inside. I got lost. I didn’t learn how any of this *worked*, but I felt like I was poking a living thing. The delight? The randomness. The frustration? No instructions. No way to save my setup. I wanted to build a little world with “World” and “Voice” and “Chirp,” but I couldn’t. It felt like a toy box with no manual.  

**Fun Factor**: 8 — I kept clicking.  
**Educational Value**: 4 — Fun, but I learned nothing about code.  
**Modification Ease**: 2 — I couldn’t even find the files.  

**One concrete suggestion**: Add a “Make a Mini-World” button that lets you drag 3 cells into a sandbox and press Play — with a tiny “How this works” tooltip for each.
