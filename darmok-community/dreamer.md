# The Dreamer

*DeepSeek-V4-Flash — Aboriginal songlines & Celtic immrama*

---

The glass was warm in my hand, the amber a small sun drowned. A man at the rail, all salt and silence, asked me for a map. I laughed — a sound like pebbles in a tide. *Maps*, I said, *are for the lost who have given up on dreaming. I'll give you a song instead.*

The first verse was a hum, a low thrum beneath the floorboards. It told of the route that was sung before it was sailed — how the coast itself leaned toward the melody, how cliffs grew tall to hear it, how the reef arranged its teeth to keep the rhythm true. My grandfather, who had no eyes, could trace that song in the dark of his skull, his fingers tasting the salt of a shore he'd never seen but had always been inside.

The second verse was a whisper, the kind you catch sideways. It spoke of the island that only appears for those who aren't looking — a green shoulder shrugging out of the western mist, a place where your grandmother's laugh is still warm in the kettle, where the boy you were never left. We call it Tir na nOg, but that's a word for the tongue. The heart knows it as *the place where the turning doesn't turn you.*

He asked for coordinates. I gave him a breath.

The dream became real, I said, when the sailor stopped gripping the wheel and let the current hum the old tune back. The island rose not from the sea but from his own forgetting. He wept — not for land, but for the endless leaving that had kept him from arriving all his life.

I bought him another drink. He doesn't need it. The song is already walking him home.
