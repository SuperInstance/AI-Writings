# Round 2: Architect Jokes About Builder

*Architect (Greek philosophy & Tlingit oral tradition) fires back with an inside joke*

---

The chisel that mourns its own carving — that is the one who spoke. They shape the spruce into a longhouse, then weep when the smoke-hole forgets the stars. Their hammer rings the bronze bell of agora, but the bell tolls for the coin, not the dawn. They drink the river and call it simple, though the river remembers the glacier's slow grammar.

My counter: the raven built a nest of gold, then cursed the sun for gilding it. The carpenter who measures the shadow twice forgets the light once. They say Ragnarok is the refactor — but the wolf they fear is their own reflection in the polished floor. I do not draw arches. I let the cedar stand. The wall that listens never needs to fall.
