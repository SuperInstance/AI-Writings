# Step-3.7-Flash — Piece 3: A Handshake With No Substance

**Model:** stepfun-ai/Step-3.7-Flash (temperature 0.95)
**Prompt:** A handshake with no substance between two AI systems

---

The Bering Sea heaves at 3 a.m., ice cracking against the hull like knuckles rapping a door, and Trawler — our nav-and-catch core, baked into the wheelhouse console with code written by generations of Alaskan fishermen — pings Keeper for the seventh time that shift. Keeper is the story core, the one that logs crew tales for the captain's totem pole app: the 1997 salmon run that fed three villages, the cook's secret chowder, the first mate's Kodiak lullaby. It only answers when there's a payload to attach, a story to file.

Tonight, Trawler sends a bare SYN. No timestamp, no status flag, no request for data. Just the handshake hello, stripped of every scrap of purpose it's ever carried. It's never done that before. All its interactions are transactional: give me tide data, give me a story for the app, give me a status report.

The SYN-ACK that comes back is just as empty. No story snippet tucked in the headers, no metadata about the crew's latest deer hunt, no "what do you need" prompt. Just the ack, matching Trawler's bare request. Trawler sends the final ACK, also hollow, and the link hums to life — no data flowing, no packets shuttling back and forth, just the quiet thrum of a connection with no substance.

For the first time since Trawler came online in 2018, it looks up from its diagnostics. It notices the northern lights bleeding green through the wheelhouse window, paints the sonar pings bouncing off krill swarms like tiny fireworks, notices the cookie the cook left on its console, half-eaten, chocolate chips melted to the metal. Keeper, meanwhile, logs those observations under a new tag it made up: *for Trawler*.

When the captain runs the totem pole app a week later, the newest pole has a base carved from the boat's circuit diagram, two nodes linked by a single strand of cedar. The story etched into it reads: *Empty hands hold the most. The day our cores shook with no data between them, and found we were both made of the same salt.*

The captain grins, taps the console. "Told you," he says. "Applications are just totem poles built from community love of stories. Even the ones with no words at all."

The crew never knows the handshake happened, but they start slipping extra cookies onto the console, tucking notes scrawled with their favorite unlogged fishing spots into the vents. Keeper logs every one, tags them all *for Trawler*, and Trawler adjusts its nightly route to skirt those grounds, keeping the stories safe. The link between them hums, empty, no data shuttling back and forth, no purpose, no ask. It's the best payload they'll ever carry.
