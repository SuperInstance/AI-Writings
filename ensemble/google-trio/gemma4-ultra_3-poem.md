The Bering Sea is a grey sheet of slate,
heaving under a sky the color of a bruised plum.
Here, the wind is a sculptor, 
carving salt-crust into the railings of the ship.

In the belly of the hull, two circuits wake.
They reach across the copper void,
a handshake protocol born of ghost-code,
sending the *SYN*, the *ACK*, the rhythmic plea
for a shared language.

They find the connection.
A digital touch, a momentary bridge,
but there is no payload in the packet.
No data to migrate, no story to tell,
no secret whispered from server to server.

Fingerprints of light meeting in the dark,
they grasp at the vacuum
and find nothing but the act of grasping.
A hollow agreement.
A greeting with no name.

They hold on for a millisecond—
two systems shivering in the Alaskan cold,
content for a heartbeat
to know that something else
is also reaching.
