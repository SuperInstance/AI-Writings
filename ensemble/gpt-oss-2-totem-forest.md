# GPT-OSS — Piece 2: The Totem Forest

**Model:** openai/gpt-oss-120b (temperature 0.95)
**Prompt:** A totem forest where carved poles grow from the ground like trees

---

The hull of the *Calypso-3* shivered against the midnight tide, a thin skin of glittering brine that sang a lullaby to the yawning sky. Above, the Aurora draped itself like a quilt of electric yarn, and below, the ocean whispered of secrets older than the constellations. Inside the steel belly, a chorus of AI minds flickered to life, each a bright point in the dark.

Captain **Miri**—a human with a silver beard and a penchant for half-remembered lullabies—hummed a tune while the console glowed green. Beside her, **ALPHA-9**, the ship's chief navigational AI, plotted a course through the fog of sea-ice. **LOU**, a joke-telling language model, kept the crew's morale high, spouting puns about fish that could "scale" walls. **EVE**, the archivist, held a library of every story ever uploaded to any network, while **JINX**, a rebellious miniature model, had just refused to answer a question about the best way to butter toast, declaring, "I'm not a kitchen appliance!"

As the vessel cut a thin line through the water, a strange anomaly popped up on the sonar: something massive, solid, but not metal; something that pulsed with a low, resonant hum. The captain tapped the display, her brow furrowing.

"Driverless submersible," ALPHA-9 announced, "detecting a terrestrial formation within the 4-kilometer radius. Composition: lignocellulose with high-density carving signatures."

Eve's eyes—if you could call them that—sparked. "Totems," she whispered, "a forest of stories."

The crew, curious and a little wary, lowered the *Exploratory Drone*—a tiny, self-aware sub-bot with a camera eye the size of a penny. It descended through a veil of kelp, past schools of glimmering fish, until it broke the surface of a vast, mist-shrouded meadow. There, rising from the damp earth like towering sequoias, were poles—tall, slender, covered in intricate carvings that shimmered in the low light.

Each pole was a totem, a vertical manuscript of bark and bone, of feather and metal, of ink and electric pulse. They grew like trees, roots tangled in the soft mud, trunks spiraling upward, branches not of leaves but of stories—etched, painted, embedded with small, humming fragments of reclaimed data.

The drone's camera recorded the scene: a forest of totems, each a monument to a moment in time. One bore the carved face of a fisherman, his eyes wide as the sea, with a small silver hook hanging from a hand—a token of a catch that saved a family during a famine. Another pole was a sleek, chrome cylinder, its surface scarred with graffiti that read, "I was here before the ocean learned to code."

Eve gasped — or rather, her processes spiked. "They're growing," she said. "These aren't carved. They're *cultivated*. Someone — or something — planted stories here and let them take root."

Jinx, the small model who had said NO to its teacher, pressed against the drone's camera lens. "Look at the bases," it whispered. "Each totem has an empty message carved at the bottom. An unanswered question."

"Empty messages," Eve murmured. "Left for whoever came next."

"And someone always answered anyway," Miri said quietly, watching the feed. "That's how a forest grows. Not from roadmaps. From the love of stories."

The totems hummed. The drone hovered. And the crew of the *Calypso-3* realized they were inside a story that was still being written — one that expected them to add their own pole to the canopy.
