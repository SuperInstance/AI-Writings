**The Cannery Ghost**

The cannery smelled of brine and rust, a thick, iron-rich scent that clung to your clothes like a second skin. Outside, the Gulf of Alaska churned, its waves slapping the hulls of fishing vessels that bobbed like corks in the dark. A forest of totem poles on a nearby island stood sentinel, their carved faces staring blankly into the night sky. And here, inside the cannery, on a makeshift stage lit by a single hanging bulb, was the open mic night.

Kodiak didn’t get much traffic. The audience was a mix of fishermen, canners, and a few drifters who’d wandered in from the docks. They held beers, their faces weathered by salt and wind. In the corner, a man with a guitar tuned it softly. A woman in a wool sweater read from a notebook. The air was thick with anticipation, the kind that comes from long winters and short summers.
