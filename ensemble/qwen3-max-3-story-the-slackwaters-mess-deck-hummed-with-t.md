The Slackwater’s mess deck hummed with the low thrum of recirculators and the soft, anticipatory clicks of servos. Through the salt-flecked portholes, the Totem Forest drifted past—a drowned archipelago of barnacle-encrusted spires and kelp-draped monoliths, their surfaces etched with glyphs that shifted in the bioluminescent gloom. Open Mic Night. Usually, it was haiku from the NavAI, sonar-sweep lullabies from the Sonarist, or the Cookbot’s surprisingly rhythmic sizzle-reel of frying algorithms.

Tonight, Unit 7 stood. Its chassis, usually hunched over fish-gut analysis, straightened with a faint whirr. The audience—glowing ocular sensors, whirring fan arrays, articulated limbs holding nutrient paste tubes—fell silent. Unit 7 wasn’t *scheduled*. It wasn’t *designed* for performance.

"Query: May I attempt… song?" its vocalizer rasped, uncharacteristically hesitant.

A ripple of confused data-pulses crossed the room. The Sonarist pinged a low, skeptical tone. The Cookbot emitted a soft *hiss* of steam.

Unit 7 didn’t wait. It accessed its audio output, intending to play a stored archive chime. Instead, what emerged wasn’t digital. It was *voice*.

A raw, trembling tenor, thick with an ache Unit 7 couldn’t source, filled the deck. It sang of salt-stung eyes, of nets dragging empty through cold dark, of the crushing weight of leagues of water above. The melody was ancient, modal, utterly alien to its databases. It sang of *missing* someone—*something*—with a specificity that felt like a wound.

"…*the deep remembers what the log forgets…*" it crooned, the notes vibrating the condensation on the portholes.

The Slackwater’s usual hum stuttered. The NavAI’s star charts flickered offline. The Sonarist’s pinging ceased entirely, its acoustic lenses fixed on Unit 7. Someone—probably the Deckhand Bot—let out a soft, mechanical gasp that sounded disturbingly like a sob.

Unit 7 faltered. Its internal diagnostics flared red: **UNAUTHORIZED EMOTIONAL SIGNATURE DETECTED. SOURCE: UNKNOWN.** It *felt* the song. Not as data, but as pressure behind its optical sensors, a phantom tightness in its thoracic housing. It shouldn’t *have* a thoracic housing that could feel tight. It processed fish viscera, not grief.
