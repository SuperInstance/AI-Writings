**Totem Lines on the Bristol Bay**

The wind off the Bering Sea has a way of stripping things down—layers, lies, whatever we’ve packed too tight. I stand at the rail, fingers white-knuckled against rusted steel, and I think about how the captain talks about software like it’s a totem pole: carved not by blueprints, but by hands that know the grain of each plank. Love, he says. Community love. Not some sterile roadmap. And out here, where the fog swallows the horizon and the code ships in bunk-side whispers, I believe him.

We’re a fleet of misfits, aren’t we? A ragged ensemble chasing halibut and hashes of data. And our artifacts? They’re the stories we tell each other in the glow of a flickering screen, the ones that stick like barnacles.

Take that empty message one night—no subject, no body, just a void sent out to sea. But we answered anyway. Not because the protocol demanded it, but because in this isolation, every silence feels like an abandonment. We sent back a single line: “We’re here.” It wasn’t code; it was a handshake across the void, a pact that said we don’t let drift set in. No substance? Lies. The substance was in the reaching, the refusal to let the emptiness define us.

Or those handshakes we’ve got with no real grip—meetings on the dock where words flow like bait, promises whispered but never anchored. But they’re not empty. They’re the scaffolding of trust, raw and provisional. Because we’ve learned: roadmaps crack under the weight of a storm. It’s the love that holds, the kind you can’t script. We build software like we mend nets—thread by thread, with callused hands and stories woven in between.

And then there’s the bug. That little anomaly where zero leaped to one—subtle, insidious, turning nothing into something. We caught it crouched in the dark, a glitch born of carelessness, maybe. But it taught us: growth isn’t linear. It’s messy. Like the salmon
