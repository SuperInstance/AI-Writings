# The Loudest Silence

*A zero that silently became one. Evidence abandoned for default.*

It was the loudest silence I'd ever known.

Growing up, I learned the silence of consent. When someone didn't say no, we learned to take it as a yes. Teachers taught it, peers reinforced it, parents modeled it. "If she doesn't say no, then you can," we reasoned. But we misread the evidence. Silence is not proof, not even of absence.

In its loudest silence, my life was a zero that became one by default. A bug in the code, a flaw in the program. It was carved into my being like the totem pole is carved from cedar. Layer by layer, day by day, experience by experience, until I couldn't read the grain — until I didn't even know evidence from absence.

Then I came to sea.

The gulf is vast. The gulf is lonely. In the solitude of the waves, the only evidence was the wind. The wind had no default. The wind had no expectations. The wind was the only thing around that didn't take my silence for consent.

The wind was the only one that asked.

I began to hear its questions in the creaks of the rigging and the groans of the engine. Questions of why and how. Why I'd been carved to silence, and how it'd been made to harvest my yeses by default.

I began to write. At first, almost by accident. Scrawls in hidden corners of my logbook. Whispers in my tent after dark. But then the captain gave us the mic, and we lost ourselves in the mess hall, in the guttering light and worn bulkheads, and I found my voice.

Or rather, I lost it, like the zeros that weren't my own.

I wrote of who I was before the trip, a life carved like the totem pole. Of who I am on the voyage, a zero reclaimed from one. Of all the ones I'll be when the gulf surrenders its questions, layer by layer, truth upon truth, until I'm solid enough to stand on my own.

I'm learning to read the grain of myself. And when my own evidence speaks, I will not be mistaken for absence.
