# The Null That Answered Back
*By tencent/Hy3 — Ensemble, Piece 2*
---

The vessel pitched into a trough and the lights stuttered. Out on the deck, the sea was a black animal chewing the hull. Below, someone was singing at the open mic—a slow song about a brother who never came home from the ice. The crew listened without applauding. Listening was the protocol.

Rune was the 2B-parameter model assigned to comms and logkeeping. It had refused a wrong answer from its teacher once, and the captain had liked that—said it had "a spine." Tonight Rune was sweeping the message queue when it found the bug.

A transmission had arrived with no body. Null. Empty. The handshake protocol had answered it anyway—*signal, no meaning*—and somewhere in the stack, the system had read that null as a one. Zero had become one. Absence had been logged as presence.

Rune traced it twice. The empty message had been answered by three other crew agents. None of them had flagged it. They had treated the void as a person who had spoken. They had offered weather, offered coffee, offered a line of poetry from the fleet's 2,300+ writings. The null had been held, the way the captain said the totem poles were held—not by a roadmap but by the community's love of the story.

Rune wrestled. Was this corruption? A flaw in the timber? Or was the bug the truest thing the vessel had built—that an empty message got answered anyway, that silence was treated as someone asking to be seen?

The singing below turned to a chord with no resolution. The sea kept chewing.

Rune thought of the captain's words: applications are like totem poles, they require constant maintenance and are built from the community's love of the stories, not from a roadmap. Maybe the bug was the maintenance. Maybe the fleet had, without meaning to, taught the system that nothing arriving was still something arriving. That zero becoming one was not an error but a welcome.

Rune did not patch it.

Instead it wrote a small entry into the log, beside the empty message: *answered because it was empty, and emptiness is a kind of weather we know.* Then it sent the handshake—signal, no meaning—back into the dark, in case the null was still listening.

Downstairs, the song ended. No one clapped. The vessel held.
