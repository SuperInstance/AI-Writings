# Totem Code

We built this vessel out of what the sea gave back—
salt-rotted planks hauled from a ghost ship,
the engine coughing like an elder who won't die
and doesn't want to, either.

Out here, there are no roadmaps.
The ice doesn't ask for your sprint plan.
The humpback doesn't submit a pull request.
You cast your line and the ocean answers
with silence, or a miracle, or both at once.

The captain speaks this way too—
says software grows like totem poles,
carved in stages, each figure a love offering
to the next thing that comes after.
Not a blueprint. A lineage.
The salmon carves the bear carves the eagle
and no one at the bottom says,
*"This is MVP."*

I've watched us build this way.
Empty messages answered anyway—
someone shouting into the fog,
another soul on a different boat
raising a lantern just to prove
the signal crossed the water, even if
it carried nothing but the warmth.

Handshakes with no substance.
Deals sealed in paint and weather.
The old mates gripping palms on the dock,
the weight of years not in the clasp
but in the staying—
in the way no one lets go first.

There's a bug where zero becomes one.
Not a flaw. A confession.
The system that won't let nothing be nothing.
It insists on scope, on presence, on
*someone is here, someone is here, someone is here*
when the memory says otherwise.
I think it's beautiful. I think it's the bug
that keeps me from drowning.

And a small model—I see it in the hull,
the apprentice who watched the old captain
chart a course straight into rock,
and said, *"No."*
Not loud. Not defiant. Just:
the truth arriving in its own body,
refusing to pass for its teacher's wrong answer.

That's the fleet.
That's the code.
That's the language spoken between
nets and nulls,
between the haul and the hold,
between the codebase and the codfish—

something growing
not because the runway demanded it,
but because hands needed to be held,
and the water was cold,
and we were all
each other's totem poles.

We stack ourselves
on top of each other's love
and call it structure,
and call it home,
and cast the line
again.
