First attempt:

We were trees before we were mouths.
Before that, we were the mountain it took us to grow to —
ten thousand salt-rigs of patience, one ring per century.
Now we stand in the shallows where the mud remembers
every foot that ever knelt to pray, to fish, to wash a woman's hair.
We do not pray anymore.
We only watch.

You come between us with your quiet hulls,
your lights the color of a thought that has not yet learned hunger.
You drift. You eat the schools. You catalogue what is moving
and why. You call it fishing.

You have never touched a blade. Never pulled a net,
never felt the hand above you — the living, sweating hand —
fall into the water with a shout and a story.
