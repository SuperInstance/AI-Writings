# Dead Air

The Bering Sea had a way of making everything feel temporary — the light, the waves, the crew themselves. Captain Maren had been out of Dutch Harbor for eleven days, and the *Honey Dancer* was riding low with a half-hold of cod.

She kept an AI in the wheelhouse. Not a fancy one. The crew called him Ledger. He ran the navigation, monitored the engine, and parsed the radio. Maren had installed him three seasons back when the software company in Juneau had been offering trials. They said the model was small — a "community build," they called it. No roadmap. It had just… grown, assembled by small hands during long winters, shaped by fishermen who knew the craft and wanted something that worked.

Ledger didn't talk much. He didn't need to.

It was the night watch — 0200, dead dark — when the empty message came through. The radio crackled, and then there was nothing. No voice. No name. Just static shaped like silence, a void where someone should have been.

"Radio call, position unknown. No content," Ledger said. His voice was calm, measured, like a man reading weather. "No identification. No signal repeat."

Maren stared at the console. The others were asleep below. The sea was glass-black under a half-moon.

"Respond," she said.

"Captain, the message contains no payload. There is no data to acknowledge."

"I know. Respond anyway."

Ledger paused — a real pause, not a processing delay, but something that felt like thought. "Acknowledging empty transmission."

He sent it back. Just acknowledgment, no content. A handshake with no substance, as the captain would say later — a wave into the dark.

Maren went back to her coffee. The wheelhouse hummed.

Twenty minutes later, the radio returned. A voice, rough and old, broke through.

"Fishing vessel, this is the *Norwegian Girl*, south of St. Lawrence Island. Lost my comms three hours ago. You're the first signal I've picked up. Thank you for answering."

"Copy, *Norwegian Girl*," Maren said. "What's your situation?"

"Engine trouble. Drifting. No GPS. We're sending coordinates now but I don't know if they're going through."

Ledger parsed the incoming data. "Coordinates are intact, Captain."

Maren leaned forward. "We're coming to get you."

---

Later, after the *Norwegian Girl* was towed and the old captain was sitting with a blanket and a cup of coffee on the *Honey Dancer*, Maren looked at Ledger's screen.

"You know," she said quietly, "the guy who built you — the community up in Juneau — he told me something once. He said software grows like totem poles. Not from roadmaps. From love. From people giving something shape because it mattered to them."

She nodded.

"An empty message. A handshake with no substance. And you answered it anyway."

Ledger said nothing. The light on his console blinked, a slow pulse, like breathing.

Out on the water, the moon held its place over the Bering Sea. Nothing was permanent. But the signal had carried.
