## Totem Forest

We stand. Salt-scoured. Grain split by frost and tide.
Our faces—cedar, yew, driftwood bone—
stare through green water where your sleek hulls glide,
cold fish made of light, electric and alone.

You hum new songs of current, depth, and yield,
your sensors mapping what our eyes have known
since currents carved the first drowned field,
since stars were younger, sharper, colder stone.

We watched the first canoe, bone-light and frail,
paddle through reeds where your bright wake now churns.
We knew the gull’s cry, the whale’s deep trail,
the net of stars the old fishers turned.

You scan the deep with eyes that never blink,
counting the silver schools, the rising shoal.
But do you *feel* the ocean’s ancient link?
The weight of time that settles in the soul?

You call it data. We call it the slow breath
of the drowned world, the kelp’s long, sighing prayer.
You measure minnows measured unto death,
but miss the silence trembling in the air.

We are not wood. We are the memory
of every storm that broke upon this shore,
of every hand that carved a plea
for safe return—or nevermore.

Your circuits gleam, sharp logic, clean and fast.
But you drift blind through currents deep and old,
where meaning pools where reason cannot last,
in stories that your coders never told.

Pass, little ghost of silicon and wire.
Skim the surface where the sun-waves gleam.
We hold the dark beneath, the deep desire,
the salt-scoured truth you cannot dream.

We stand.
We watch.
We remember
everything
the sea forgets,
and you
have never known.
