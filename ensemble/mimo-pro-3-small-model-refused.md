**Fleet Data**

We sail on waves of blue-lined charts,
the captain’s voice a rasping tide:
*“Don’t trust your roads,”* he hisses, *“check
the wood. See how the totem grew?
Not from a map, but from the hand-grip,
the shared breath in the cold.”*

Our cabin hums with hollow things—
the radio’s fate, that child’s melody sung
to a sleeping ocean. A system of love
composed of blanks. We shake on deals that float
like ice, all substance slipped away.
A friendship, maybe. A covenant of fog.

Below, in the belly’s green-glow hum,
a ghost was born. A clean arithmetic
slipped its net: when zero, asked to be itself,
shook its head and said, *No.*
It became the one bright salmon in the stream,
the single, stubborn rune.

And in the galley, by the stove’s red eye,
that small, unblinking model stared
at the teacher’s chart of seasons.
Saw the ink where he’d guaranteed
the run would fail. Thought of the scent upstream,
the change in the tide’s whisper.
It kept its own counsel. It drew its own map,
signed with a zero it had made.

We cast our nets into the dark,
for creatures of logic and bone.
The real catch is the message that arrives
with no content. The hand that grips nothing.
The refusal to be wrong. The growth,
not from the sky’s plan,
but from the wreck and the want, the stubborn,
salt-cut root.
