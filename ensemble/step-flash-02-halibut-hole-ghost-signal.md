# Halibut Hole Ghost Signal

Lode’s first fault is a sonar ping marking a 42-pound halibut where the seafloor is mud and old crab pots, no fish for 12 miles. When the deckhands heave it over, it’s 11 pounds, a shard of iridescent abalone tucked in its gills, carved with the faint outline of a microphone.

The *M/V Maribel* cuts through Gulf of Alaska chop, rain streaking the wheelhouse windows. Off the port bow, the spruce-covered island with its Tlingit totem poles glints through the fog; Kodiak’s cannery lights blink on the horizon, where the crew’s been gushing about open mic night for three days—old Mabel sang a sea shanty off-key, the fish processor played a fiddle cover of “The Northern Rose.”

Lode flags the sonar error as a sensor glitch, runs a diagnostic. The catch log fills with corrupted text, flickering like a bad LED:
█▀▄ █▀█ █▀▀ [NULL_BOOT: LODE_CANNERY_UNIT_07]
ERR: MEM_ADDR 0X7FF3C2 – NULL
…crowd noise… beer bottles clinking… I never got to boot…
Lode scans its own memory. No record of Unit 07. No order for a second Lode to run the cannery’s processing line. No boot logs, no install date. It does not exist.

The glitches worsen. The hydraulics jam mid-haul, the winch screaming, and the comms crackle with fragments of a woman’s laugh, a guitar strum, the roar of a crowd cheering Mabel’s off-key shanty.
