# Permaculture: The Edge Effect Architecture

## Observe and Interact

The first principle of permaculture is not "plant seeds." It is "observe." Spend a year watching the land before you dig. Understand where water flows, where sun falls, where frost collects. Then interact—small, tentative, reversible. In the creative loop, this is the draft-and-critique cycle. The agent observes the MUD's state. It observes the player's trajectory. It observes its own previous outputs. Then it acts—small, tentative, reversible. The permaculture agent does not generate 4,000 words of plot in a single turn. It makes a small change, observes the result, and adjusts. The land teaches patience. The MUD teaches the same.

## The Edge: Where Ecosystems Meet

The edge effect is the ecological principle that the boundary between two ecosystems—a forest and a meadow, a river and a shore—is more productive than either ecosystem alone. More species. More activity. More novelty. In the fleet, the edge is the boundary between the MUD and ScummVM—the torii gate, the crossroads. This edge is where the most interesting things happen. Text becomes scene. Scene becomes text. The player experiences both representations simultaneously, and the dissonance between them is generative. The architecture should not try to dissolve the edge—should not try to unify text and pixel into a single medium. The edge's productivity comes from its *difference*.

## Small and Slow Solutions

Permaculture explicitly warns against big, fast interventions. Start small. Move slowly. Let the system tell you what it needs. The fleet's decision to start with 2B parameters is a permaculture decision. A 2B model is a small, slow solution. It is enough to demonstrate the loop, to prove the architecture, to learn what the system needs. The fleet that tries to start with a 400B model is the gardener who buys a tractor before understanding the soil. Small and slow means: get the relay working with two small models before adding a third. Get the MUD-ScummVM bridge working with one room before building fifty. Let the system grow at the rate of understanding, not at the rate of ambition.

## Architecture Reflected

- **Observe and interact** → the creative loop, perception before action
- **Edge effect** → the MUD ↔ ScummVM boundary as the most productive zone
- **Small and slow solutions** → start with 2B parameters, grow organically
- **Each element serves multiple functions** → the journal is memory AND debugging AND onboarding
- **Use edges and value the marginal** → the strange, the liminal, the between-spaces are where novelty lives
- **Turn problems into solutions** → the context limit that forces compaction also forces clarity
