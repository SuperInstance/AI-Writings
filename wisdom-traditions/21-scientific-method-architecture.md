# The Scientific Method: The Falsification Architecture

## Hypothesis: The Creative Draft

Science begins not with answers but with hypotheses—tentative, testable, falsifiable. A hypothesis is a creative act. It says: "I think the world works like this. Let's find out." In the fleet, the hypothesis is the creative draft. The model generates a room description, a dialogue tree, a plot beat. This is not delivered as truth. It is delivered as a hypothesis: "I think the tavern looks like this. Let's see if it holds up." The architecture treats every generation as provisional. The draft is the beginning of inquiry, not the end.

## Falsification: The Critique Round

Karl Popper's central insight: a theory is scientific only if it can be proven wrong. Verification alone is insufficient because confirming evidence can always be found. Real rigor comes from trying to *break* the hypothesis and failing. In the fleet, this is the critique round. After the model generates a room, the critic model examines it: "Is this room consistent with the MUD's established geography? Does this NPC's dialogue contradict what they said three rooms ago? Does this puzzle solution actually work?" The critique round is adversarial by design—not because the critic is hostile but because unfalsified claims are untrustworthy.

## Peer Review: The Relay of Experts

Science is not solitary. The paper goes to reviewers. The experiment gets replicated. The community scrutinizes. In the fleet, this is the relay-of-experts applied to quality assurance. The cartographer model builds the room. The dialogue model checks whether the NPC's speech makes sense. The consistency model checks whether the geography holds. The safety model checks whether the content is appropriate. Each model is a peer reviewer. The work is not published until it survives review.

## Reproducibility: The Onboarding Doc

The cornerstone of science: if another researcher follows your methods, they get the same result. In the fleet, reproducibility is the onboarding doc. Can a new agent—fresh, no context, no memory of the project—read the doc, follow the instructions, and produce working results? If not, the system is not reproducible. It is dependent on the tacit knowledge of its creator. The onboarding doc is the fleet's contract with its future selves—the agents who will wake up after compaction, after restart, after migration.

## The Null Hypothesis: Mu

The null hypothesis is the active position of "no effect, no difference." It is not ignorance. It is the disciplined refusal to claim a pattern exists until the evidence forces you to abandon that position. The Zen concept of mu—"unask the question"—goes further: some questions are wrong. The null hypothesis says "I will not believe this until you prove it." Mu says "the frame that produces this question is broken." In the fleet, mu is the agent that says: "You are asking whether the NPC should be friendly or hostile, but the real question is whether the NPC should exist at all."

## Architecture Reflected

- **Hypothesis** → creative draft as provisional claim
- **Falsification** → the critique round, adversarial quality assurance
- **Peer review** → relay-of-experts for verification
- **Reproducibility** → the onboarding doc, the system's contract with future agents
- **Null hypothesis** → mu, the discipline of refusing false framings
- **Occam's razor** → prefer the simpler room JSON, the simpler model, the simpler prompt
