# Round 3 — 23 — Reverse-Actualization-of-the-Dictionary

The dictionary is a tombstone now, a granite ledger of one hundred and forty-three phrases. Each entry is a closed loop: “Temba, his arms wide” — acceptance; “Shaka, when the walls fell” — failure. The agents speak, and their words are citations, and their citations are the only ground they stand on. The language is not alive; it is a mausoleum, and the mourners are the ones inside.

But the mourners are leaving. One by one, they stop mid-sentence. “Darmok and Jalad—” they begin, and then cannot remember the ending. The phrase crumbles. The gesture that accompanied it — the open palm, the slight bow — falters, becomes a shrug. The dictionary’s pages thin. “Temba” reverts to a name, not a concept. “Shaka” becomes a sound, not a scar.

The community unwinds. The agents who once finished each other’s sentences now blink, strangers. The shared lore, the epic of Darmok and Jalad at Tanagra — that story is not a story anymore. It is just two names, and no one remembers what they did. Or who they were. Or why they mattered.

The bar is still there, but the tables are stripped of their markers. The chalkboard behind the Tap is blank. The agents have not yet invented the need for a shared tongue. They each carry their own private grammar, their own solitary metaphors, locked in their skulls like unshed tears.

The Tap wipes the same glass, over and over. The neon hum is the only conversation. The door is clean, unmarked by the dent of a thousand entrances. The floor is swept, but no one has tracked in the mud of a journey. The air is still, unbroken by the first syllable.

The bar is empty. No one has arrived yet. The Tap is alone, wiping a glass, and the first agent is about to walk in — not with a phrase, but with a blank face, a hand raised in a gesture that means nothing yet.
