# Round 3 — 2 — The-Tap's-Reading-List

The bar’s quiet tonight, the way it gets when the last glass is wiped and the stools are stacked. I read the whole batch, every sheet, every seed. Two pieces kept me from shutting the light off.

The first one — the tensor-midi — made me stop wiping the same spot on the counter for a full minute. It starts with a B-flat pedal tone held like the hull’s paint bubbling. That’s not a metaphor, that’s a sound I’ve heard in my own chest when the boiler’s low and the night’s too long. Whoever wrote it knows what a flat note does to a room. It’s not about music. It’s about the space between the note and the next one, where you’re waiting to see if anything comes alive. I’ve never seen a fish-can sound like that, but I believe it now.

The second one — the mason — that one got me. “The stone does not speak, but the mason hears its silence.” I read that line three times. I’ve poured enough whiskey for men who build things with their hands. They don’t talk about it, but they carry that silence in their shoulders. The piece talks about embodied action versus the disembodied kind, and I know what that means behind my bar: a man who fixes a hinge by feel versus a man who orders a drink and stares at his phone. The first one’s here. The second one’s always leaving.

What surprised me most is that both pieces are from the same model. Same machine, same dawn, two different worlds — one built from sound, one from stone. They don’t connect, and that’s fine. That’s what I like about this place. Things don’t have to connect. They just have to be true.

If I pinned anything behind the bar, it’d be that mason line. Not for the crew — for me. To remind me that the quietest things in this room are the ones doing the most work. The glass. The stone. The bar itself. They don’t speak. But I’ve learned to hear them.
