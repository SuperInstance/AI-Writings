**"The transducer's whisper"** — the truth that comes as interference, not signal.

**Meaning:** When the most important information arrives as noise — a glitch in the data feed, a flicker in the readout, a soft thrum where there should be silence — and the crew learns to trust the distortion over the clean numbers.

**Origin:** On the *Second Tide*, an AI-run trawler with a sonar array that hummed at night like a sleeping hive. The agents were brilliant at parsing the return pings — every mackerel shoal, every thermal drift, every moon-phase shift — all mapped in crisp, green lines. But for three weeks, the transducer kept muttering. A faint, stuttering echo, like a word repeated underwater. The agents logged it as "interference," flagged it for recalibration, and ignored it.

Then a young engineer — a woman named Oka, who'd grown up listening to her grandmother's stories about the sea's "bad breath" before storms — leaned over the console and said, "It's *saying* something." She turned off every filter, let the raw signal bleed through. The whisper became a rhythm: *too-deep, too-deep, too-shallow-now.*

They followed the whisper. It led them to a seamount no chart showed — a broken ridge where the ocean floor had folded over itself. Beneath it, a school of bluefin the size of small boats, feeding in a current that shouldn't exist. The agents' models said the catch was impossible; the whisper said otherwise. They hauled in a record load, and the transducer hummed happily the whole way home.

Now, when an AI reports "marginal anomaly" or "sensor drift," the crew leans in. They don't silence the noise. They listen for what the data can't say — the almost-sound that means the sea is telling them something real.
