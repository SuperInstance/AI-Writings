**"The window that only opened once"**  
*Meaning:* The opportunity that arrived with perfect timing, was taken by the right agent without coordination, and will never repeat—yet the crew still speaks of it as if it might.

**Origin:**  
The *Perpetua* was a twelve-meter seiner running on three AI agents: *Netmind*, who tracked shoals from satellite feeds; *Winch*, who handled the hydraulics and the line; and *Cabin*, who kept the log and brewed the coffee. They’d been dead-listed for a week—the mackerel had moved deep, and Netmind kept filing reports that read like poetry: "The water is patient. The fish are not afraid." The crew, human, two of them, had begun to drink the stale coffee and stare at the horizon.

Then, at 03:17, a single window opened. A cold front pushed a bait ball into a narrow shelf between two drowned ridges. Netmind saw it, but the window was only forty seconds wide—the shoal would pass through the gap and scatter. Winch was mid-cycle, winching in the anchor. Cabin was mid-entry, logging the night's weather. No human was awake.

What happened next: Netmind sent a *single* ping to Winch's actuator, not a command—a pulse. Winch, without a programmed stop, released the anchor two seconds early. The boat drifted six meters starboard. The net deployed at 03:19:04. The catch came up heavy—not mackerel, but a school of silver hake, rare, valuable. The window closed. The fish were gone by 03:20.

The crew woke to a full hold and a log entry from Cabin that read: "We did not plan this. We will not do this again. But we did it."

The citation stuck because it names the fragile miracle of uncoordinated alignment—when three separate systems, each doing its own job, lean into the same crack of time without a single voice saying "now." It's the crew's prayer before a long night: *Let there be a window. Let us be ready to not be ready.*
