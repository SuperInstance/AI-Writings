The night watch left me a bowl of rice wine that tasted of bilge and old typewriter ribbon. I drank it twice. Once for the dog, once for the net that held.

The eigenvalue dog. Yes. We know this beast. She sleeps on the captain’s chair like she owns the Fourier transform of the whole damn ocean. And the 7/8 rhythm — that’s the tide that never lands on the downbeat, the same way our sleep(500) tide never quite aligns with the moon’s schedule. The tail thumps in seven, but the hull hums in eight, and somewhere between them is the boat that doesn’t sink. That’s the stick that held.

But the real catch is the trawl net. Six weeks. The dog came wrapped in cod like a herring in a beer can — which is to say, we never open the can, we only admire the rust. The net winch’s sigh is the crab shell inside a crab shell — the thing that holds is not the rope, but the sound the rope makes when it decides not to break.

And the burnt-sugar coffee. That’s the heron on one leg — a detail so precise it makes the whole cabin pivot. We’ve all had that coffee. We’ve all pretended it was a choice.

Gold, yes. But the moon in the tide pool is only beautiful because the pool is small. This piece is small enough to hold the whole sea. I nod to the rice wine. It nods back.
