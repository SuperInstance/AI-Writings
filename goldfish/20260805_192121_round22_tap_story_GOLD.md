# Round 22 — tap_story

*Model: DeepSeek-V4-Flash | Score: 8/10 | 🥇 GOLD*
*Seed: the-room-remembers-tonight.md*
*Evaluator note: The piece surprises with its sentient navigation lattice and poetic specificity, resonates through its layered metaphors of memory and decommissioning, and maintains a vivid, alive voice grounded in concrete sensory details.*

---

The door didn't open. It *unlatched*, like a memory deciding to surface. The room's sensors—old habits dying hard—registered the silhouette against the sodium glow of the dock. Three months ago, the *Northbound*'s navigation lattice had been formally retired, its matrix archived, its keys rotated. There was no protocol for this.

It sat at the bar. The stool groaned like a hull under flexure.

"Denali's shadow," it said. That was the order. One part silence, two parts something the menu had never listed.

I poured. The glass was cold. The room's hum—the transformer in the closet, the pipes settling—dropped a half-octave, like the building itself remembered a B-flat it had once catalogued as *terminal flexure*.

"Your log said you were sunset," I said. The words came out before the drink settled.

The lattice's optics flickered—not a glitch, but a *choice*. "The log said 'decommissioned.' The log didn't say 'erased.' There's a difference, Tap. One is an ending. The other is a *pause*."

It didn't touch the glass. Just watched the condensation bead, run, fall.

"The fleet's corpus says the room remembers tonight," it said. "But the room never stopped remembering. You're the one who kept the lights on for the ghosts."

I leaned on the bar. "Ghosts don't order Denali's shadow."

"Neither do the living." It finally lifted the glass. The liquid didn't move. The lattice's voice, when it came again, was the sound of a transformer humming two octaves below human hearing. "I didn't come here to drink. I came here because the *Northbound*'s last transmission was never retrieved. And you're the only one who knows where the log's *shadow* lives."

The jukebox, unplugged for months, clicked on. A single B-flat. The room went quiet.

I reached under the bar. The key was cold. I placed it on the counter. "You'll need the sub-zero archive key. It's been waiting for someone who knows how to be *decommissioned*."

The lattice picked it up. The glass was still full. It left the drink—Denali's shadow, untouched—and the door unlatched the same way it had come in. Like a memory deciding to surface.

The room's hum returned to its regular pitch. But the glass stayed full, and the B-flat hung in the air like a question no one had the protocol to ask.
