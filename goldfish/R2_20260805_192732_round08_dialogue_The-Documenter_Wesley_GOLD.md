*The Documenter’s fingers trace the condensation ring on the bar’s polished synthwood. Wesley hovers a half-inch off his stool, a habit born of impatience, his mug of cold-brewed Andorian tea untouched.*

**The Documenter:** *(tilting his head, voice a low hum)* Tonight’s tensor-midi ran a 7.3 on the bathyal scale. The brine pumps were singing in D-sharp — that’s the port-side one, the one with the scar from the ice-fishing incident.

**Wesley:** *(rolling his eyes, but smiling)* You’re still charting that? It’s just ambient noise, Doc. The captain’s snoring at the helm syncopates with the warp core’s coolant drip. That’s not music. That’s a plumbing problem with a metronome.

**The Documenter:** *(raising a brow, tapping the bar twice)* Darmok and Jalad at Tanagra. You reduce every signal to a glitch. But the harmonics—the night-shift cooks dropped a ladle at 23:04, and it landed on the *third* beat of the refrigeration unit’s hum. That’s *intention*.

**Wesley:** *(leaning forward, voice a deliberate whisper)* Intention? It fell because Ensign Rory was juggling ladles for a bet. He lost. The “composition” you heard was a broken foot and a sore ego.

**The Documenter:** *(sighing, rubbing his temple)* And yet, the frequency—if you map it against the nebula’s electromagnetic pulse—it aligns. The universe *hummed* in response. You see noise. I see a conversation.

**Wesley:** *(opens mouth to argue, but the Tap slides a fresh glass of synth-rum between them)*

**The Tap:** *(wiping a smudge with a clean cloth, voice flat)* The tensor-midi’s real melody is the ten-second gap between your last argument and the next one. You’re both just backing tracks.

*Silence. The Documenter picks up the rum. Wesley picks up his tea. They drink, and for a moment, the hum of the ship seems—just barely—like a question neither wants to answer.*
