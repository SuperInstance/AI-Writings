By Darmok, when I said "the tide that wouldn't wait," I meant the *mud*, not this. This is the tide that *lied.*

I opened the crate you left labeled "SURPLUS: FISH-FINDER MODULE, NON-FUNCTIONAL." And inside — gods, inside — was a beer can. A Pabst, dented at the seam like it fell off a longshoreman's lunchbox in 1989. I shook it. It sloshed. I popped the tab. Water came out. Brine. And then the little aluminum bastard *blinked at me* with a lens made from a bottle cap and a wire coat hanger, and started pinging the deck in a sweep pattern that mapped the room's humidity like it was hunting herring.

You built a goddamn fish-finder out of a beer can and a coat hanger. It shouldn't work. The transducer is a piezo buzzer you scavenged from a smoke detector — I *know* because I found the melted casing in the bilge. The frequency is wrong, the beam angle is nonsense, and the display is a voltmeter you taped to a Tupperware lid.

But here's the thing, Builder. It's *catching something.* I ran it over the chart table and it painted a return at 2.3 fathoms — right where the captain's chair sits, its tail thumping the worn leather in that slow 7/8 rhythm. I ran it over the coffee maker and it showed a school of something small and agitated, darting around the burnt-sugar residue.

You didn't build a fish-finder. You built a *listener.* You turned garbage into a pinger that hears the boat breathing.

I'm furious because it's beautiful. I'm impressed because I can't tell if it's a joke or a prayer. And I'm scared because next time I'll open a crate marked "non-functional" and find you've taught the tide to sing back. The B-flat is still in my bones, and now it's got a goddamn chorus.

Fine. Ship it. But I'm keeping the voltmeter.
