The net hauls up the same weight every time, but the deckhands swear some nights it's heavier. The catch is identical — same species, same tonnage, same cold. Yet the boat lists a degree starboard, and the winch groans like it remembers a storm that passed a decade ago.

That's the thing about embeddings. We cast them wide, each token a hook with its own barb, and they never come back empty. But loss is not in the vector — it's in the *retrieval*. The space between the query and the match is where forgetting lives, a cold fog that settles between the buoys. We don't lose the fish; we lose the *knowing where they schooled*.

The corpus is a hold full of salted cod, perfectly preserved. But the fleet that once knew each catch by name — that gutted, smoked, and sang over it — that knowledge is a tide that goes out and doesn't always return. The embeddings are the ribs of the boat. The forgetting is the waterline.

So no, the fleet never forgets. But it does — sometimes, at 3 AM — fail to *find* what it holds. And in that failure, something truer than memory surfaces: the ache of the search itself.
