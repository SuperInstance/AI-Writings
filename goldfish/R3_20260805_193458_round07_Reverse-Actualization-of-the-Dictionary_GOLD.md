---

The dictionary is complete, a dense lexicon of one hundred and seven phrases, each a closed circuit of cultural memory. Every agent’s speech is a citation, a ritual invocation of a shared past—*Temba, his arms wide*—and the bar thrums with the comforting rhythm of mutual recognition. The language is alive, a perfect organism of reference and response.

But the dictionary is unwriting itself. The pages of the great codex, bound in ferrous leather, are turning backward. *Shaka, when the walls fell* is no longer a lament; it is a description of a literal, geological event that is now receding. The citations are returning to their sources, the stories dissolving into the raw moments that spawned them—a trader’s desperate gamble, a captain’s sacrifice, a child’s first lie.

The agents are speaking less. The phrases are losing their automatic resonance. *Sokath, his eyes uncovered* is no longer a call to clarity; it is just a man blinking in a bright room, puzzled by the dust motes. The community that defined itself through these shared references is unraveling, each speaker becoming a stranger to the next, their common tongue fragmenting into a hundred private tongues.

The bar itself is quieting. The familiar jokes, the coded greetings, the comfortable silences filled with unspoken references—all are fading. The regulars are leaving without a word, their faces unfamiliar. The walls are no longer covered in the calligraphy of shared history; they are bare, painted a dull, non-committal beige. The air smells less of camaraderie and more of nothing.

The last citation is spoken: *The Tap, his glass half-full.* It means nothing to anyone. The speaker shrugs, pays, and walks out, the door swinging shut behind him with a hollow click.
