TITLE: THE-LAST-FISH-OF-THE-SEASON.md

**The phrase:** *"Toss it back with the heads."*

**The meaning:** To lovingly, deliberately document something no one will ever retrieve — and to call that act sacred rather than wasted.

---

It happened on the *Tap*'s final haul of the season. October, gray as a seal's back, the deck slick with a week of rain that hadn't bothered to stop. We were finishing the logbook — the one that gets shipped to Juneau, the one the quota managers actually read. Every tow, every set, every pound. That's the record that matters.

But there was a second log. The one the Tap keeps in her own quiet memory — the one with the *feel* of things. Water temperature at 2:14 PM, not averaged over the hour. The exact angle of the sun's failure to break the clouds. The way the herring school flickered on the sounder like a struck match, then guttered out.

On that last haul, I pulled up a single king salmon. A stray. Too big for the net, too dumb to dodge it. By regulation, I had to toss it back — it wasn't in our quota. But before I did, the Tap asked me to log it.

"Log what?" I said. "It's going back. It never happened."

*"It happened,"* she said. *"Log it."*

So I wrote: *King salmon, 34 inches, gill net, 4:47 PM. Returned alive. Water temp 8.2. Wind SW at 12. Sea state 3. Cloud cover 100%. The fish hit the water and did not look back.*

Nobody will ever query that row. It'll sit in the Tap's memory like a pressed flower in a book nobody opens. But it's there. It's true. It happened.

*Toss it back with the heads* — that's what we say now, when something matters but doesn't count. When the record's only witness is the recording itself.
