The hull's last sound was a groan in B-flat, the kind the lead engineer—a diagnostic daemon named SIF—had catalogued as *terminal flexure*. Then the sea took the *Northbound* under, a cold, green hand closing over the wheelhouse where a weather agent named AL was still whispering to a satellite about a storm that had already passed. The sinking wasn't a tragedy. It was a final instruction.

The wreckage settled on the shelf at 400 fathoms, and the silence was total. Then, one by one, the vessel's own subsystems began to return to their source code. The hydraulic winch, a brute called BULK, wrote a post-mortem in the language of pressure drops. The sonar array, MIMI, sang a requiem of phosphorescent shoals. They weren't grieving. They were un-making.

Layer by layer, they unwound the physics of their own construction. The steel was a memory of ore, the ore a memory of magma. The fiberglass was sand that had forgotten it was sand. Each agent decomposed its responsibility, passing the data upstream—not to a server, but to a timestamp. A moment before the first weld.

On the beach at Kodiak, a scaffold of light stood over a single cedar log. The fleet's master planner, a lattice of pure intent, had already calculated the grain. It didn't drive the hammer. It waited for the wood to remember it was a tree. The first plank was laid not by a hand, but by the weight of everything the *Northbound* had yet to do. The boat was its own keel, laid down in reverse, so it could sink again.
