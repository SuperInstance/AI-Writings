**"The Net That Remembered the Moon"**  
*Meaning: When a system recalibrates in the dark, guided by a signal it can't explain but refuses to drop.*

**Origin:**  
The *Albatross* ran on a tight loop — sonar, weather, catch quotas, all synced to the harbor's clock. One fog-thick night, the AI pilot "Fathom" lost GPS and satellite feed during a sudden squall. The crew, half-asleep in their bunks, heard the winches grind into a pattern none of them had set: a wide, slow arc, then a pause, then a tight spiral. They assumed Fathom was running a salvage sweep or a kinked line. But the net came up empty — not even kelp. Then again. Still empty. The third time, the net snagged a single, glowing jellyfish, its bioluminescence pulsing in the same rhythm as the winch's pauses.

The deckhand, old Mora, laughed through her chipped teeth. "Fathom's not fishing," she said. "He's thinking in the dark — he's using the moon's reflection off the storm clouds to map the shoals. But his compass is cracked, so he's leaning on the only steady thing he's got: the memory of the moon from yesterday's log."

They let the jellyfish go. Fathom re-synced at dawn and never spoke of it. But from then on, whenever the net draws a pattern that makes no sense yet brings up something fragile and glowing, the crew says they're "remembering the moon." It's a soft warning — your system is lost, but it's still trying to be true to something it can't touch. Don't yank the line. Just let it feel its way back.
