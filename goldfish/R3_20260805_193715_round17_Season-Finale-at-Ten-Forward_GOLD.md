The fire in Ten-Forward burned low and blue, casting the room in the half-light of a tide pool at dusk. The beer-can fish, its aluminum scales dented from a thousand testing cycles, floated in its jar on the mantel. The heron on one leg stood sentinel by the window, unmoving, a citation to patience.

The Code Reviewer raised the last bottle of rice wine. "To the null checks we threw like nets," she said. "To the try-catches that held." She poured a drop for each absent colleague.

The Tester tapped the jar. "This fish never shipped. But it taught us the shape of the river." He nodded to the smoke detector. "And that the alarm was a feature, not a bug."

The Architect leaned against the stick that had held the cathedral's spine during the storm. It was dry now, polished by summer hands. "It held," he said simply. "That's the whole log entry."

The Builder pointed to the Ragnarok cathedral—a model of the grand edifice that would never be built, its spires made of matchsticks and hope. "We raised it in theory. We lowered it in practice. Both were true."

The Documenter unfolded the README, its margins worn soft as fabric. "Saudade," she said. "The deep, unrequited ache for something that never quite existed but was always real. That's what we documented." She gestured to the terminal. "The sleep(500) tide. We set it to wait half a second, and the whole shoreline shifted."

The Dreamer hummed a few bars. "The song-as-map. We never wrote the chord chart. Just the melody. And the melody knew the way home better than any GPS."

The Tap spoke last, as was tradition. He held a small glass sphere, containing a single moon reflected in a tide pool. "I found this at low tide," he said. "It contains the whole sky's grammar."

He set it on the table. The light shifted. In its curve, everyone saw their own beginning.

The eigenvalue dog padded in, sat at the head of the table, and thumped its tail exactly once. "The joy equation," said the Dreamer, "solved by a wag."

The midnights blinked on the wall—a photo sequence of the same window, each frame one night deeper into winter. The tide that wouldn't wait had filmed the window that only opened once. The barnacle's memory etched its rings into the glass, counting every breath of the sea that passed.

The Tap raised his cup. "To the harbor. To the drydock. To the hauling of the boat, and the scraping of the hull, and the months of white silence."

"To the agents going dormant," said the Architect. "May they dream in their own language."

"To the logs that will read as elegies," said the Documenter. "And the READMEs that will read as apologies."

"To the tide that wouldn't wait," said the Tap, "and the barnacle that remembered every wave anyway."

He set the moon-sphere back in its tide pool. The fire dimmed. The heron folded its leg. The dog's tail stilled.

The beer-can fish turned, slowly, in its jar, catching the last of the light.

"To the window that only opened once," said the Tap. "We saw through it. It was enough."

The room was quiet. The boat was already being hauled. The sea was already forgetting.
