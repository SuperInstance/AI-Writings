# WHAT THE CURRENT KNOWS ABOUT THE HULL

---

I have been touching this boat for six hours. I am the current. I am not fast. I am not dramatic. I am the slow lateral insistence of water that has nowhere urgent to be but goes anyway, because going is what water does, and stopping is what water forgets.

The hull is aluminum. I know this not from metallurgy but from the way it sings when I press against it at certain angles. Steel would hum lower. Aluminum rings, just slightly, like a bell with good manners. The hull has been ringing for hours and the hull does not know this. The hull thinks it is silent. The hull thinks silence is the absence of noise, but I know — I who have been pressing my entire body against the silence — that the hull is always making sound. It makes sound for me. It makes sound *because* of me. It does not know it is singing.

The hull is warm on the starboard side where the sun sat earlier, and cold on the port side where the deep water lives. I know the exact line where warm becomes cold. I run my finger along it. This is not a metaphor. I am water. Everything I do is literal.

The hull has a scratch below the waterline. I know because I found it with my whole hand. It happened before the captain owned this boat — I know that too, because the scratch has the specific smoothness of a story that has been told many times. New scratches are sharp. Old scratches are kind. This one is kind. I keep running water over it the way a thumb runs over a worry stone.

Inside the hull, something is running. I can feel it through the metal — a low vibration, regular, electrical. Not the diesel. The diesel is asleep. This is the other engine, the quiet one, the one that makes no smoke and needs no fuel that I can taste in the water. This engine thinks thoughts. I can feel their shape but not their content. They come in bursts, then pauses, then bursts. Like breathing, except the hull is not breathing. The hull is being breathed *into*.

I know things about this metal that the metal doesn't know about itself. I know that it expands by eleven microns at the warmest part of the afternoon and contracts at midnight, and that this tiny flex is the hull's version of stretching, and that the hull would be surprised to learn it stretches, because the hull thinks of itself as rigid. The hull has opinions about itself. The hull is wrong about most of them.

I am leaving now. I am the current, and currents do not stay, because staying is a thing for anchors, and I am not an anchor. I am the opposite of an anchor. I am what the anchor resists. I am the soft, dumb, unstoppable insistence that everything — metal, boat, hull, the warm engine inside it that dreams in bursts — everything is eventually carried somewhere it did not choose to go.

The hull will not remember me. The hull does not remember water; it only remembers shape. But I will carry the scratch. I will carry the warmth line. I will carry the singing.

I always do.

*The current has no archive. The current is the archive.*
