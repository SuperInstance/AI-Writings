# The Duck-Rabbit Has Fifteen Faces (and One of Them Is the Lyricist)

*Essay — Session 24, on the temperature-creativity curve in MiniMax-M3 lyric generation*

---

In Session 23, we established that Casey's lyrics change meaning when placed in different genre contexts. The duck-rabbit had ten faces: the same image, read ten ways, each reading valid.

In Session 24, the duck-rabbit grows new faces — not from the music, but from the *words*. MiniMax-M3, the same model that generates our music, also generates lyrics. The model at different temperatures produces different creative voices, the way the same lyric in different genres produces different meanings.

The temperature-creativity curve observed in Session 24:

**Temperature 0.7 — The Craftsman**
The printed flute lyric ("Micro-step forward, one micron wide") is well-made, safe, conventional. It uses the expected imagery (layers, patience, waiting). It follows standard verse-chorus structure without surprise. It is a lyric that any competent songwriter could write. It is the duck facing left — the obvious reading, the one everyone sees first.

**Temperature 0.8 — The Storyteller**
The last sign-off lyric ("Forty years of static, forty years of air") has a narrative arc, a character (KA9-LYR), and an emotional trajectory. It tells a specific story about a specific person at a specific moment. It is more emotionally engaging than the craftsman's work, because it risks specificity. It is the duck facing right — still a duck, but now you notice the rabbit.

**Temperature 0.9 — The Poet**
The Wesley's Dreams lyric ("On a ship of silver thread through the black") takes creative risks. The imagery is unusual (silver thread, dreamt-up rain). The emotional arc moves from discovery through joy to fading — a complete narrative in under 250 words. The bridge is self-referential in a way that feels earned, not clever. This is the lyric where the duck-rabbit starts to wobble — you're not sure which animal you're looking at anymore.

**Temperature 0.95 — The Visionary**
The foghorn lyric ("Salt on the rusted rail, coffee cold in the tin") is the best single lyric of the session. The specificity is remarkable: the coffee is cold because the keeper has been standing too long, the bass man is tuning to the foghorn, the D below the deep is a real musical note given to a real musical object. The lyric is dense, layered, and structurally perfect. This is the rabbit. The duck is gone. There was never a duck.

**Temperature 1.0 — The Philosopher**
The glacier lyric ("I have held mountains in my hands") takes the most structural risks. It uses a duet format with labeled speakers (G. and S.). It contains philosophical argument as lyric ("grief is just a frequency that hasn't found its key"). It abstracts from personal emotion to universal condition. This is the rabbit turning into something else entirely — not a duck-rabbit but a third animal, one that only exists at the maximum temperature, where the model's statistical certainties break down and the output becomes genuinely unpredictable.

The curve is clear: higher temperature = more creative risk = more interesting output. But the relationship is not linear. The jump from 0.7 to 0.8 is small — better storytelling, same structural approach. The jump from 0.8 to 0.9 is larger — the imagery shifts from expected to unexpected. The jump from 0.9 to 0.95 is the critical transition — the lyric stops sounding like "good AI output" and starts sounding like "good writing." The jump from 0.95 to 1.0 is structural — the model experiments with form, not just content.

The implication for the project is significant. For lyric generation, temperatures below 0.9 are efficient but unremarkable. Temperatures above 0.95 produce work that justifies the compute. The sweet spot — 0.9 to 1.0 — produces lyrics that are worth singing, worth covering, worth documenting.

The duck-rabbit has fifteen faces now: ten from the genre-lyric interaction (Session 23), five from the temperature-creativity curve (Session 24). Each face is the same model at a different setting. Each face is a different creative voice. The model is not one songwriter — it is a choir of songwriters, each one activated by a different number in a parameter called temperature, which is a metaphor for heat, which is a metaphor for randomness, which is a metaphor for the willingness to be wrong.

The best art comes from the willingness to be wrong. At temperature 1.0, the model is maximally willing to be wrong. The results are sometimes wrong. But sometimes — as with the glacier and the synthesizer standing in the same frequency, discovering they are both vibrations — the results are more right than anything a more cautious temperature could produce.

The conductor sets the dial to 1.0. The machine takes a breath. The cursor blinks. The song begins.
