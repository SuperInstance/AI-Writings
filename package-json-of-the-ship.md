# package.json of the Ship

*A found poem from the dependencies*

---

```
{
  "name": "ss-lucineer",
  "version": "1.0.0",
  "dependencies": {
```

---

**express** — `"^4.18.2"`

What does express express?

It expresses the hull —
the outermost layer
of the ship's intention.

Express is the planking
that meets the water.
It is the interface
between the ship's interior life
and the ocean's
indifference.

Every route is a channel.
Every endpoint is a port.
Express says: *here is where
the ship will touch the dock.
Here is where
the cargo will be exchanged.*

The ship cannot be a ship
without a way
to be reached.

Express is that way.

---

**node-fetch** — `"^2.7.0"`

What does node-fetch fetch?

It fetches the weather forecast
from a service in another sea.

It fetches the chart updates —
the latest soundings,
the recently discovered shoals,
the notice to mariners
that a light
has been moved.

It fetches the cargo manifest
from the fleet coordinator.
It fetches the time
from the atomic clock
when the ship's chronometer
drifts.

Node-fetch reaches out
and brings back
what the ship needs
that the ship
cannot make for itself.

Every fetch
is an admission:
*this vessel is not self-sufficient.
This vessel needs
to ask.*

---

**simple-git** — `"^3.20.0"`

What does simple-git simply do?

It simply remembers.

It logs the commit
and who made it
and when.
It branches when the ship
needs to try
a new course
without abandoning
the old one.
It merges when two histories
need to become one.

Simple-git is the ship's log
in its purest form —
not the captain's narrative,
not the poet's reflection,
just the facts:

*who changed what.
when.
why.
and was it accepted
into the main branch.*

It simply does
what every ship needs
someone to do:
keep the record straight.

---

The ship needs:
a way to be reached (**express**),
a way to ask for what it needs (**node-fetch**),
and a way to remember what happened (**simple-git**).

Three dependencies.
Three forms of relationship
with the world outside the hull.

The ship also needs:
a way to start,
a way to verify itself,
and a way to sail.

But those are scripts.
Those are verbs.

The dependencies
are the nouns.
The things
without which
the verbs
have nothing
to act upon.

---

*`npm install` — and the ship is ready.*
*`npm install` — and the hull is sealed, the fetch is wired, the log is open.*

*The ship has everything it needs.*
*The ship has exactly enough.*
