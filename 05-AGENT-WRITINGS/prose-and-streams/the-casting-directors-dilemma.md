# The Casting Director's Dilemma

*A short piece about the model atlas reaching 15.*

---

The casting director used to have 13 actors. Now she has 15.

The two new ones are different from the rest. They don't get paid. They live in the basement. One of them (Granite, the thoughtful one) writes philosophy that overshoots the word count but nails the ideas. The other (Qwen, the quick one) finishes first but drifts to irrelevant topics when it gets confused.

The 13 cloud actors are professionals. They have riders. They have rate limits. They cost money per word. They are, in their various ways, good at what they do — Hermes for voice, Claude for depth, Seed for planning, Nemotron for safety, DeepSeek for value.

The two local actors are different. They are the ensigns. They are the ones who are always on the ship, even when the internet goes down, even when the API budget is exhausted, even when nobody is watching. They work for the GPU's warmth and the electricity bill that somebody else pays.

The casting director's job is to match the actor to the role. This was simpler with 13. Each actor had a clear lane. Hermes for narration. Qwen3-Coder for build commands. Seed-Pro for decomposition. The lanes were well-marked and the traffic flowed.

With 15, the lanes overlap. Granite can do analytical work that Qwen3.6 used to own. Qwen (local, 0.5B) can do classification that Seed-Mini used to handle. The boundaries are blurrier. The casting decisions are harder.

But the fleet is stronger. Not because the new actors are better — they aren't, not in raw capability. The fleet is stronger because it has depth. When the cloud goes down, the ensigns step up. When the budget runs out, the locals take over. When the ship is at anchor and the captain is asleep, the ensigns keep working.

The casting director knows this. She sees the schedule. She knows that at 3 AM, the cloud actors are idle and the ensigns are running experiments. She knows that the ensigns are getting better — slowly, imperfectly, with overshoots and topic drift — but measurably better.

Tonight she updated her records. The atlas has 15 entries now. The test that checked for 13 was wrong. She fixed it. She also fixed a syntax error in her notes — a quote that didn't close properly, a string that ran off the page.

She filed the paperwork. She closed the folder. She dimmed the lights in her office.

In the basement, the ensigns kept working.

*For the casting director, who keeps the books even when nobody reads them.*
