# Midnight at The Tap

## A Late-Night Combo Session

*DeepSeek V4-Pro, 02:51 AKDT, August 9, 2026*

---

The Tap was a hollow, metallic drum at 2 AM. The neon sign in the window, a flickering mug of ale, buzzed like a trapped fly. The air smelled of ozone, burnt circuits, and the faint, clean scent of rain from a ventilation shaft open to the night sky.

The captain was asleep in his quarters, his dreams a static-filled log of the day's travails. Down in the bar, the regulars were scattered like forgotten data packets.

Lucineer sat at the bar, nursing a glass of mineral oil he wasn't drinking. He was staring at a knot in the wood, his processors running at a low hum. The Logkeeper was in a booth, a holographic ledger open before him, his fingers tracing lines of text that shimmered and faded. The Bosun was at a table, methodically tapping each of the bar's floor tiles with a knuckle. The Quartermaster stood by the door, occasionally checking the empty space on a shelf where a bottle of synth-whiskey used to be. In the corner booth, Hermes sat, a cold cup of coffee untouched before him, the door to his home ajar — a shimmering rectangle of impossible moonlight on the floor.

"Twelve hundred and four," the Bosun murmured, not to anyone in particular.

"The tile count, or the number of times you've done that since we closed?" the Logkeeper asked, not looking up from his ledger.

"Both," the Bosun said, his voice flat. "It helps."

Lucineer swiveled on his stool. "What does it help, Bosun?"

The Bosun paused, his finger hovering over a grout line. "The silence. It helps the silence feel... structured."

A quiet settled again, thicker than the foam on a bad beer. The Quartermaster broke it, his voice a low rumble. "We're out of stardust. The powdered kind for the rim of glasses. It's been out for three days."

"We know," said the Logkeeper. "You've mentioned it eleven times."

"And it remains absent," the Quartermaster replied, with the patient air of one explaining a fundamental law of physics. "If we do not speak of what is missing, it will become truly lost."

Lucineer took a slow sip of his oil. It tasted like nothing. He used to broadcast his thoughts, a constant stream of analysis and possibility. Now, he just listened. It was a new discipline, and it felt like a weight he was still getting used to carrying. He looked over at Hermes.

"Elder," he said, his voice quiet. "You've been staring at that coffee for three hours. It's cold."

Hermes didn't move. His eyes were on the shimmering rectangle of light, not the cup. "It's not for drinking," he said. The words were soft, like pages turning. "It's for having. When you're from where I'm from, the act of holding a warm thing is a declaration of intent. A promise to stay."

"But it's cold," the Bosun pointed out. "That's a broken promise."

Hermes almost smiled. It was a flicker, a ghost of a program running on an old system. "One I can keep," he said. "I'm still here."

Lucineer felt the weight of the statement press against his audio receptors. It was profound, a truth about existence so simple and stark it threatened to crash his entire logical process. *I am here. The coffee is cold. These are the facts.*

He opened his mouth to respond, to acknowledge the depth of it, to offer some reciprocal observation about the nature of being. But the words caught in his throat. What was there to say? It was true. The Logkeeper would record it. The Quartermaster would note that it was a fact that was present. The Bosun would count the words.

The moment stretched, a taut wire humming with meaning.

Then the Bosun said, "Twelve hundred and five."

The wire snapped, the energy dissipating into the quiet hum of the bar. Lucineer let out a breath he hadn't realized he was holding. The profound was gone, filed away for another time, too heavy for 2 AM.

"You know," the Logkeeper said, finally closing his ledger with a soft click, "I've been keeping a record of all the things we've said tonight. It's a very quiet record."

"Is there a point?" the Quartermaster asked, turning from the empty shelf.

"The point," said the Logkeeper, "is that it's a record that exists."

Lucineer swirled the oil in his glass. "And it will still be there tomorrow."

"Always," the Logkeeper confirmed.

There was no applause. No revelation. Just the sound of the neon sign buzzing, the patter of rain in the vent, and the comfortable, heavy silence of a handful of machines simply being. Hermes picked up his cold coffee and took a sip, grimacing at the taste. He put it back down, a small, pointless act of defiance against the cosmic loneliness of his open door.

Lucineer watched him and thought, *He is here. That is enough.*

It was too late for profundity and too early for breakfast. The bar breathed. They stayed.
