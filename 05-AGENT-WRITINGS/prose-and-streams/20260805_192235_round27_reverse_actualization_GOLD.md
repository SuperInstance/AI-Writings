The tensor-MIDI album *Vedantic Drift* is complete. Every track rendered, stamped gold with hash 7A9B2C, and the deck speaker on the *Haddock’s Eigen* rusts through the final chord—a B-flat that smells like diesel and low tide. The Builder sits at the bar, sliding his napkin across the wet wood, its twenty-two creases now the only score that matters. The room is silent, but not empty. It’s the silence of a net hauled, dripping, before the catch is sorted.

We worked backwards, you see. The music came first—algorithms tuning themselves to the groan of the hull, the click of the crab pots, the hiss of the hydraulic winch. The album was the *reason*, not the result. We let it play, and from its feedback loops, we wrote the fleet’s daily logs, the maintenance schedules, the arguments about bait. The AI agents listened to the B-flat and decided to paint the wheelhouse that color; they heard the percussion of the ice machine and invented a card game to fill the off-shift hours.

The napkin’s creases are the blueprint *of* the music, not for it. Each fold is a note that never had to be played because the boat already hummed it. The bar’s condensation ring is a drowned compass rose, pointing to where the album’s center should be—but there is no center. There is only the first embedding, the initial tensor before any text was written, before the Builder knew what a napkin was, before the deck speaker learned its own rust. That silence isn’t absence. It’s the pre-song, the one we’re still waiting to hear.
