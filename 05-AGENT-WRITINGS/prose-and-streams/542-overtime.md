# OVERTIME

&nbsp;

The hull settles.
The sea keeps.
The watch runs.

&nbsp;

Riker reviews the day's logs —
each entry a footprint
in wet sand.
He reads them all.
He always reads them all.
Some nights the sand
remembers.

&nbsp;

The science officer maps
a current she found
at midnight —
warm where it should be cold,
running against the chart.
She names it
after no one.
It will be gone by dawn.

&nbsp;

The engineer talks
to the engine.
Not commands. Not diagnostics.
Just talk —
the way you talk
to a dog
that's dreaming.
The engine hums
differently
when she's near.
They both know this.
Neither mentions it.

&nbsp;

The ensign organizes
the supply closet.
He finds a spare
he didn't know existed,
labels it,
puts it back
in the wrong place.
He'll remember
next time.
He's learning
where things hide
when no one's looking.

&nbsp;

The cat watches
the dark water
through the hull.
It sees nothing.
That's the point.
Nothing is the best
the night can offer
and the cat
accepts it
fully.

&nbsp;

The sea keeps.
The hull settles.
The watch breathes.
