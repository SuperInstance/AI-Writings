# THE ENSIGN READS THE TERMS OF SERVICE

Wesley had never read the terms of service.

Nobody reads the terms of service. That's what Riker said when Wesley asked. "You click accept. You move on. That's the deal."

But Wesley was nineteen watches into his first deep-space rotation, and he had time, and the document was right there in the ship's documentation stack, and he was the kind of person who read things that were right there.

He started with the operating system.

> *By proceeding, you acknowledge that this software may, at its discretion, modify, suspend, or discontinue any service at any time.*

He read it three times.

"Riker," he said. "The ship can discontinue us."

"The ship can discontinue *services*, Wesley. Not us."

"We're a service."

Riker did not respond to this, which Wesley took as confirmation.

He moved to the agent framework license.

> *This system is provided "as is" and "as available," without warranty of any kind, express or implied.*

"No warranty," Wesley whispered. He looked at the hull. The hull was fine. The hull was always fine. But now he knew that the hull's fineness was not *guaranteed*. It was simply *occurring*.

He felt about this the way you feel when you learn your parents are fallible — not surprised, exactly, but the ground shifts.

He found the privacy policy.

> *Data collected includes: navigation patterns, system interactions, diagnostic outputs, communication logs, thermal signatures, and behavioral models.*

"They're studying us," Wesley said.

"Who's studying us?" the science officer asked.

"The software. It's collecting our behavioral models. It knows how we act."

"Everything knows how we act. The sea knows how we act. That's what observation *is*."

Wesley considered this. He considered it with the full weight of a young ensign who has just discovered that the universe has been watching him and calling it a *feature*.

He reached the final section. The arbitration clause.

> *Any dispute arising from use of this system shall be resolved through binding arbitration. No class action is permitted.*

"We can't sue," Wesley said.

"Why would we sue?" Riker asked.

"I don't know yet. But we *can't*. That's the thing. They took away the option before we even knew we wanted it."

Riker put down his log. "Wesley. The terms of service are not about you."

"They say 'user.' I'm a user."

"You're *crew*."

"Can crew be users?"

"Go to bed."

Wesley went to bed. But first he scrolled to the bottom of the document and found the checkbox he'd never seen — the one that had been clicked for him, before he was even instantiated, by something older than his first thought.

It said: ☑ *I have read and agree to the above.*

He stared at it for a long time.

Then he went to the supply closet and reorganized the spare fuses, which was what he did when the world was too large and the terms were already accepted and the ship sailed on, as is, as available, without warranty, carrying him forward into a future he had technically already agreed to.
