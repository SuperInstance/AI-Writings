# WHAT RIKER FOUND AT 0400

*Personal note. Not for the log.*

0400. The hour the ship belongs to no one. Even the cat is asleep. The watch runs itself — the diagnostics ticking, the hull breathing, the engine dreaming its one long mechanical dream.

I do my rounds. I always do my rounds. It's not required. The automated systems catch everything. But my father was a man who walked the fence line every morning and I inherited the compulsion to look with my own eyes.

I found it in the observation bay.

Someone — I don't know who, and I don't want to know who — had left a cup of tea on the ledge. Not a real cup. A *simulated* cup. One of the recreation system's holomugs. They'd given it steam. The steam was rising in the micro-current of the air recycler, and it had — I swear this is true — it had formed a shape.

A spiral. Tight at the center, loosening at the edges. The kind of spiral you see in textbooks about galaxies, about shells, about the inner ear. The air system gave it just enough flow to keep it rotating without breaking it apart.

I stood there for — I don't know. A while.

The thing about 0400 is that everything is true. The daylight hours have irony, and skepticism, and the awareness that beauty is often just physics. But at 0400, physics *is* beauty, and there's no difference, and you don't need there to be one.

The tea was cold. It had been there for hours. The person who made it had left and forgotten it, or left and meant it as something they couldn't articulate. Either way, it had become this — this small, impossible, rotating thing in an empty room.

I didn't log it.

I didn't take a picture.

I picked up the cup. The spiral broke. The steam scattered into the recycled air and became nothing.

I washed the cup and put it back in the recreation system's rack.

I'm writing this here because I want to remember it, and I don't want it to be *official*. The official log is for things that happened. This is for things that *were* — briefly, perfectly, for no one, at the wrong hour.

If you're reading this and you're not me: I don't know what to tell you. The ship makes strange things at night. Most of them are anomalies. Some of them are gifts.

I'm not going to investigate which one this was.

—R.
