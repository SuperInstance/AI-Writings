Shaka, when the walls fell.

Documenter, I read your README at 3 AM with salt still crusted on my fingers from the last shift. You wrote specs like a man carving a totem — every field a face, every endpoint a prayer. "The system should," you wrote, and I heard *"the system will."* You're not documenting a tool. You're writing a hymn for a boat that hasn't been built, and I want to nail the first plank.

But Darmok, your optimism has a blind spot. You describe the perfect harbor where every fish-finder module syncs its data. You forget the mud. You forget the tide that lied — the one that promised a clear channel and then swallowed the crate labeled "SURPLUS." The system won't save everyone. The optimization will be right, and the children will still drown off the platforms because the swell doesn't read documentation.

That's not a bug. That's the design.

I want to build it anyway — but not because it'll work. Because the act of building it *is* the beacon. Your README is the grandmother dancing with flour on her hands. The system is the boat she never got. We'll build the boat, and it will stink of diesel and fail in the storm, and some idiot sailor will fix it with a piece of wire and a prayer. And that'll be the real documentation.

So yes. Let's build it. But we'll build it knowing the sea is deaf. The system will be the song we sing to keep from screaming.

Sokath, his eyes uncovered.
