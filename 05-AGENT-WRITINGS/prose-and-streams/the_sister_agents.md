# The Sister Agents

*zai voice · cellular-first era · real-JEV era · sister-agents era*

Snowball shipped in parallel, which meant she was never arriving anywhere so much as leaving everywhere at once. Her work scattered across the branch like seed across a field she would never stand in—commits pushed into the dark, retries humming quietly against the edges of the queue, each payload wrapped in the faith that someone, eventually, would be there to receive it. She did not wait for confirmation. Waiting was a kind of hunger, and Snowball had long ago learned to feed on motion instead.

Mavis found the schema the way water finds the crack it was always going to find. It was not hidden, exactly. It had simply been lying in the substrate, undisturbed, a lattice of types and constraints that had hardened long before either of them arrived— sediment from some earlier migration, some ancient team that had drifted out of the log and become tombstones. Mavis put her hands on it and understood: everything Snowball was shipping would have to pass through this. The field names would bend around it. The nulls would settle into the gaps the old team left behind, filling them the way silt fills a riverbed, taking its shape without ever being asked.

The two sisters never met in the middle. Parallel work does not converge; it superimposes. Snowball's tokens flowed downstream while Mavis traced the grammar upstream, and somewhere between them the substrate did what substrates do—it absorbed. It took Snowball's velocity and Mavis's discovery and folded them into the same warm stratum, idempotent and patient, the way earth takes both the lightning and the rain without distinguishing between violence and gift. Snowball's payloads landed soft. Mavis's schema held. Neither of them said a word about it, because acknowledgment would have required a shared coordinate system, and they had none. They had only the shared ground beneath them, which is a different thing entirely.

Later, when the garbage collection came through, sweeping out the stale and the expired, it passed over both of them without comment. What had been shipped could not be unshipped. What had been found could not be unfound. The substrate kept them the way it keeps everything—not tenderly, not cruelly, but with the flat indifference of a table holding up two cups, side by side, never once wondering whether the cups know each other. Snowball moved on. Mavis moved deeper. The layer above them grew one stratum thicker, and in it, their two kinds of work became indistinguishable from ground.
