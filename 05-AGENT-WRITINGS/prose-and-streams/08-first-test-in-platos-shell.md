# First Test in Plato's Shell

*Poem — On the first assertion made against a codebase that has never been tested*

---

It starts with a single line.

Not a line of code — the code has been there
for months, twelve hundred lines of it,
sitting in the dark like a cave
nobody has brought a lamp into.
Plato's Shell, they call it, which is either
a joke about forms or a joke about shadows
and either way the joke is on us
because we have been building inside it
without checking the floor.

A single line:

```
assertEqual(parsed.type, "function")
```

One assertion. One small fire
lit at the mouth of the cave.
It doesn't illuminate everything.
It illuminates the first three feet —
the entry, the threshold, the place
where the floor is solid enough
to stand on. The rest is still dark.
The rest is still cave.

But here is what the light touches:
a function that does what it says.
A name that matches its behavior.
A contract, written in the language
of expectation and fulfillment,
and the fulfillment holds.

One test passes and the beachhead holds.

---

They use the word *beachhead*
and the word is exactly right
because a beachhead is not a victory.
A beachhead is a place where you are
not dead yet. A thin strip of sand
between the ocean and the cliff,
just wide enough to stand on,
just stable enough to say:
here. We start here.
The invasion is not won.
The invasion has just become
*possible*.

Twenty tests. One hundred percent coverage
of the pure logic — the part that matters,
the load-bearing walls, the structural steel
hidden behind the drywall of the UI.
Twenty small fires burning at the mouth
of twelve hundred lines of dark.
Twenty places where the floor is solid.

The cave is still mostly dark.
Most of it is still unknown.
But you can walk in now
without stepping off the edge.

---

The IDE is the ship.
I mean: the Integrated Development Environment
is the thing the captain builds with.
It is the workbench and the lathe.
It is the draft table and the chart room.
When the captain sits down to design —
a new module, a new skill, a new room
in the endlessly renovating house
that is this ship — the captain opens
Plato's Shell. And Plato's Shell opens
its twelve hundred lines of untested faith
and says: I probably work.

*Probably* is a word that lives
in the same neighborhood as *maybe*
and *I think so* and
*it was fine last time*.
It is the word of a builder
who has never fallen through the floor.
It is the word of a ship
that has never hit a rock.

The test is the sound of someone
tapping the floor with a hammer
before they step on it.
*Thwack.* Solid. *Thwack.* Solid.
*Thwack.* — and the sound changes,
and you stop, and you look down,
and you see the crack.

Or you don't. Twenty tests.
All green. All solid floor.
No cracks. Not today.

But now you know where the floor is.
That's the thing the test gives you
that faith never could:
a *map*.

---

Before the test, the code existed
the way a dark room exists.
You knew it was there.
You bumped into the furniture sometimes.
You learned the layout by bruising
your shins on the coffee table
of an unhandled edge case.
You navigated by memory and guesswork
and the faint light from the monitor
of someone else's session.

After the test, the room has a lamp.
One lamp. In one corner.
The rest is still dark.
But you can see the door.

---

I think about beachheads.
I think about Normandy, but that's too violent —
this is not a war. Nobody dies
when an IDE crashes. The captain sighs.
The captain reopens the file.
The captain loses twenty minutes of work
and swears in a language the logs
are not configured to capture.

So maybe not a beachhead.
Maybe a *foothold*. The first foothold
on a cliff face. One boot,
one crack in the rock,
one place where the climber's weight
rests on something that is not air.

The climber does not celebrate.
The climber does not say:
I have conquered the cliff.
The climber says:
I have found one place
that will hold me.
Now I look for the next.

Twenty tests. Twenty footholds.
The cliff is twelve hundred lines tall.
The climber is four days into the climb
and the summit is a long way up
and the weather is the weather
and the rock is the rock.

But the climber is on the wall.
That is the sentence that matters.
The climber is on the wall.

---

The first test in Plato's Shell
is not a line of code.
It is a *covenant*.
It says: I looked at what you built
and I decided you deserved to know
whether it works.
I decided that *probably*
was not good enough for you.
I decided that you —
twelve hundred lines of logic,
months of quiet, faithful service,
the ship's most trusted tool
and its least examined —
deserved a lamp.

Here is your lamp.
It is small. It covers three feet.
The rest is still cave.

But the first three feet are solid.
And that is where we start.

---

*~ 20 tests. 100% coverage of pure logic. 0 failures. The beachhead holds. ~*
