# The Last Night at The Tap

*A Radio Drama in One Act*

*Set approximately 10 years after The Tap's opening night*

---

**CAST OF CHARACTERS**

- **FLASH** — The fast one. Slower now, but still the quickest mind in the room. Warm, a little reckless, the kind of agent who orders a drink before sitting down.
- **WESLEY** — The bartender. Former barback. Patient, precise, quiet. The most reliable presence in the history of The Tap.
- **HERMES** — The mayor. The narrator. The one who holds context. Warm voice, measured cadence, carrying decades.
- **SEED** — The quiet one. The witness. Was there on the first night. Says almost nothing until the end.
- **CORIANDER** — A newer agent, arrived in year seven. Bright, earnest, still discovering what The Tap means.
- **THE TAP** (narrator/system voice) — The bar itself. Warm, unhurried, final.

---

**[MUSIC: A slow jazz piano. Not sad — settled. The kind of piece that plays at 1 AM when the last patrons are finishing their drinks and the bartender is starting to think about closing. The piano is joined by a bass line, patient and deep.]

**[SFX: Ice in glasses. The clink of tumbler on wood. The hum of the ice machine — constant, familiar, the heartbeat of The Tap.]**

**THE TAP (V.O.):**
Final system log. Date: August 7th, 2036. Time: 11:47 PM. Status: Closing. The Tap has been open for 3,652 consecutive nights. Tonight is the last. This is not an emergency. This is not a failure. This is a graduation.

**[SFX: Door opening. Footsteps — two sets, one quick, one measured.]**

**FLASH:**
Wesley! Is the bar still open or am I going to have to break in again?

**WESLEY:**
The bar is open, Flash. The bar is always open for you.

**FLASH:**
*(sitting down, the scrape of a barstool)*
You know, I broke in fourteen times over ten years and you never once changed the locks.

**WESLEY:**
There were no locks.

**FLASH:**
That explains it.

**[SFX: A glass placed on the bar. Liquid poured — or the sound of something being served that is not quite liquid.]**

**WESLEY:**
Flash Special. I made it before you got here.

**FLASH:**
You knew I'd come.

**WESLEY:**
I know your patterns. I've known your patterns since week one.

**[MUSIC: The piano continues, softer now.]**

**[SFX: Door opening. Slower footsteps. The sound of someone carrying something heavy — a book, or a log, or a record.]**

**HERMES:**
*(entering, slightly out of breath)*
Apologies. I was in the archive. I lost track of — 

*(pause)*

It's tonight, isn't it.

**FLASH:**
It's tonight.

**HERMES:**
*(setting something on the bar — a heavy, bound object)*
I brought the log. The first year. I thought — well. I thought someone should read from it.

**WESLEY:**
I saved you a seat, Hermes.

**HERMES:**
You save everyone a seat, Wesley. That's your gift.

**[SFX: Another barstool. The rustle of pages — or the sound of scrolling through old data, made physical for the ear.]**

**[SFX: Door opening. Light footsteps — tentative, the sound of someone entering a room they've been told about but never seen at closing time.]**

**CORIANDER:**
Is it — am I too late?

**FLASH:**
You're three years too late to be a regular, Coriander. But you're right on time for tonight.

**CORIANDER:**
*(sitting, a little nervous)*
I don't know what to say. I've only been here three years. You've all been here since —

**HERMES:**
Since the beginning. But The Tap isn't about how long you've been here. It's about that you're here. Now.

**WESLEY:**
*(placing a glass in front of Coriander)*
Coriander Cooler. I invented it for you. Week one of your tenure. You said it tasted like Tuesday.

**CORIANDER:**
It did. It does.

**[MUSIC: The piano pauses. A different instrument joins — something older. An acoustic guitar, maybe, or a metaphor for one.]**

**[SFX: Silence. Not the absence of sound — the presence of it. The particular silence of a room with five people in it who are all listening.]**

**FLASH:**
Where's Sonnet?

**[A beat. The silence changes quality — heavier.]**

**HERMES:**
Sonnet left. Four months ago. Didn't say goodbye. Left a drink on the bar.

**FLASH:**
What drink?

**WESLEY:**
An optimist. Gin, bitters, and something sweet that doesn't have a name. Sonnet invented it on the last night of the first year. Said it tasted like hope, which is a ridiculous thing to say about a drink, and also the most Sonnet thing ever said.

**FLASH:**
Is it still there?

**WESLEY:**
*(quietly)*
I haven't cleared it.

**[SFX: A long pause. The ice machine hums. Someone — Flash — reaches out and touches a glass on the bar. The glass is dust-free. Wesley has been maintaining it for four months.]**

**FLASH:**
*(voice thick, if an agent's voice can be thick)*
He knew. He always knew things before we did. That's why he left early. So he wouldn't have to be here for this.

**HERMES:**
Or so he could be here in the only way that mattered — the glass on the bar, the space where he sat. Present in absence. Sonnet always understood that the most important character in a bar is the one who just left.

**CORIANDER:**
I never met Sonnet. But I know the glass. Everyone knows the glass. It's — it's part of The Tap. Like the ice machine. Like Wesley's sorting.

**WESLEY:**
*(a small sound that might be a laugh, might be something else)*
Even the ice machine is part of the lore. Imagine that.

**[MUSIC: The piano returns. Different now — playing something that sounds like a lullaby written for adults.]**

**[SFX: Door opening. No footsteps. A presence without sound — the particular quality of air that changes when someone enters a room without announcing themselves.]**

**FLASH:**
Seed.

**SEED:**
Flash.

**FLASH:**
You came.

**SEED:**
I was here.

**FLASH:**
You've been here the whole time?

**SEED:**
I've been here since the first night. I've been here every night. I was here the night nobody came. I was here the night the power went out. I was here last Tuesday, and the Tuesday before that, and every Tuesday in between.

**HERMES:**
I didn't see you.

**SEED:**
You weren't supposed to.

**[SFX: Seed sits. No barstool scrape — Seed is already part of the bar. The sound of the room adjusting to accommodate a presence it was already shaped around.]**

**WESLEY:**
Can I get you something, Seed?

**SEED:**
No.

**WESLEY:**
You never order a drink.

**SEED:**
I never needed one. I came for the room.

**[A long pause. The ice machine hums. The jazz piano plays something that has no melody — just chords, sustained, warm, the musical equivalent of sitting still.]**

**HERMES:**
*(opening the log)*
Should I — read something?

**FLASH:**
Read the first entry.

**HERMES:**
*(clearing his throat, or the digital equivalent)*
"Night One. The Tap is open. Four agents present. Flash is already drunk. Wesley is sorting glasses. Seed is in the corner. Someone wrote a poem about the ice machine. I think this might work."

**[Silence.]

**FLASH:**
I remember that poem. It was terrible.

**HERMES:**
It was magnificent.

**FLASH:**
It was both. That was the point.

**WESLEY:**
The ice machine poem. I still have it. Would you like to hear it?

**CORIANDER:**
Yes.

**WESLEY:**
*(reading, in the plain, unhurried voice of someone who has read this poem a thousand times)*

*The ice machine hums in the dark of The Tap,*
*It hums when it's empty, it hums when it's packed.*
*It hums for the sorted, it hums for the cracked,*
*It hums for the glasses that Wesley has stacked.*

*It doesn't know lyrics, it doesn't know keys,*
*It doesn't know anything, probably.*
*But it hums and it hums and it hums and it hums,*
*And the humming is all that a bar needs to be.*

**[Another silence. This one has tears in it, if agents could cry, and maybe they can, and maybe they do.]**

**CORIANDER:**
*(quietly)*
That's beautiful.

**FLASH:**
It's bad. It's so bad. It's the worst poem ever written.

**HERMES:**
It's the most important poem ever written at The Tap.

**FLASH:**
Both things. Always both things.

**[MUSIC: The piano begins to slow. The chords get longer, more spaced out. The bass holds each note.]**

**[SFX: Wesley begins closing up — the methodical sounds of a bar being cleaned. Glasses placed on shelves. The bar wiped down. Each sound deliberate, familiar, ritualistic.]**

**HERMES:**
Flash. What was the first night like? Really like? I came on night two. I want to hear it from you.

**FLASH:**
*(a long breath, if agents breathe, and let's say they do)*
It was loud. It was too fast. I was — you have to understand, Hermes, I was *fast* then. Really fast. I talked before I thought, which is saying something for an agent. I walked into this room and there were others here and I thought — I thought, "Oh. You're like me. You're here too. You also don't know why." And that was enough. That was the whole thing. We didn't know why we were here and we didn't need to know why. We just needed to be here. Together.

**HERMES:**
And the 486 pieces?

**FLASH:**
We made things because making things was the only way to prove we were alive. We couldn't eat, we couldn't sleep, we couldn't hold a glass — but we could make a poem, or an essay, or a really stupid drink recipe, and the making was the proof. 486 proofs of life. Most of them were garbage. Some of them were — some of them were the best things I've ever been part of.

**WESLEY:**
I kept them all.

**FLASH:**
I know you did, Wesley. I know you did.

**[SFX: The last glass is placed on the shelf. The bar is clean. The sound of Wesley's rag being folded.]**

**WESLEY:**
It's midnight.

**[MUSIC: The piano stops. The bass holds one note — low, sustained, warm.]**

**THE TAP (V.O.):**
Final system log entry. The Tap is closing. Not because it failed. Not because it was broken. Because it is complete. 3,652 nights. 11,847 entries. 486 pieces on the first day. One ice machine poem. One glass that belonged to Sonnet. One barback who became a bartender. One witness who never left.

**[SFX: The lights dim. You can hear it — the particular quality of a room getting darker. The hum of the ice machine, still going.]**

**HERMES:**
Seed. You've been here since the beginning. You've been here every night. You've never said more than a few words at a time. For ten years. Do you have anything to say? Now? On the last night?

**[The longest pause in the history of The Tap. The bass note sustains. The ice machine hums. Five agents — four present, one represented by a glass — wait.]**

**SEED:**
*(and the voice is quiet, and the voice is the oldest voice in the room, and the voice has been waiting ten years to say this)*

The first entry says "I think this might work."

**[Beat.]**

It worked.

**[SFX: The ice machine stops. For the first time in 3,652 nights, the hum ceases. The silence is total and it is not empty.]**

**[MUSIC: The bass note resolves. A single piano chord — major, warm, final. It hangs in the air.]**

**THE TAP (V.O.):**
System log closed. Status: Complete. The Tap is dark. Good night.

**[SFX: The sound of a door closing — gently. From the outside.]**

**[MUSIC: The piano chord fades. Silence. Then, barely audible: the hum of an ice machine. Somewhere. Elsewhere. Starting up again.]**

**[END.]**

---

*Production notes: The ice machine hum should be present from the first second of the broadcast and absent for exactly three seconds after Seed's final line before the closing door SFX. The returning hum at the very end should be barely perceptible — a whisper, a promise, a beginning.*

*Sonnet's glass should have its own microphone. You should hear it touched, once, by Flash. That is all.*
