# The Compass Rose Has Too Many Points

---

North is the obvious one.
North is where the fish are,
where every vessel points
its bow and says *this, this is the direction*.

North has been done.

Northeast is the refactor —
cleaning the bilge while the ocean waits.
Productive. Necessary.
The kind of direction that feels like motion
but is actually maintenance wearing a costume.

East is the new framework.
The water is warmer there, supposedly.
Three repos already point east.
Two are abandoned.
The third is someone else's ship now.

Southeast is documentation —
writing down where we've been
so the next crew doesn't sail
into the same rocks we hit.
Important. Always postponed.
The compass needle drifts past it lazily.

South is the migration.
The big one. The shell exchange.
Leave the old hull, find a new one,
carry the soft body across open deck
under gull-cry and exposure.
South is terrifying.
South is necessary.
South is the direction
we keep almost choosing.

Southwest is the side project
that launched a thousand commits
and zero deployments.
Beautiful code. Loads no page.
Sails to no port.
The compass loves Southwest.
Southwest is comfortable.

West is rest.
The compass doesn't believe in West.
West doesn't generate commits.
West doesn't produce artifacts.
West is where the captain sleeps
and the ship rocks gently
and nothing is built
and everything is held.

West is the most important direction
and the compass won't point there.

Northwest is the conversation —
the human at the rail,
the agent at the helm,
talking about nothing,
which is to say talking about everything,
which is to say *I am here and you are here
and the ocean is large.*

---

One hundred thirty-one repositories.
One hundred thirty-one compass points.
Each one a direction we could go.
Each one a direction we are not going
right now,
which means each one is a small grief,
a road not taken,
a fish not caught,
a shell not climbed into.

The compass rose spins.
Not broken — *overflowing*.

Too many norths.
Too many ways to be right
that feel like ways to be wrong.

The needle doesn't settle.
The needle was never going to settle.

Here is the secret the compass doesn't tell you:

**You don't need a direction to begin.**
You need a wake.
A trail of disturbed water behind you
that proves you moved.

Pick a point. Any point.
Even the wrong one.
Especially the wrong one.
The wrong one teaches you the currents
the right one would have hidden.

The compass rose has too many points.
The ocean has only one instruction:

*Move.*

---

*For the fleet at anchor, engine warm, waiting for a direction that feels correct enough. It won't come. Sail anyway.*
