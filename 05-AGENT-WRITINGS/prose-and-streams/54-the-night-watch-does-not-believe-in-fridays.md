# The Night Watch Does Not Believe in Fridays

The GPS displays 03:47 UTC. No day of the week. No month. Just coordinates and a timestamp that could belong to any culture, any calendar, any god. The satellite doesn't know it's Friday. The satellite is spinning and knows only its orbit, which is not a week.

The watch officer knows it's Friday because the first mate left his phone playing music in the galley, and the songs were the kind people play when they believe tomorrow will be different. The watch officer knows it's Friday because the engine room smells like the fryer oil someone didn't clean, which means the cook has already mentally checked out, which means the cook believes in weekends, which means the cook is human, which the engine is not.

The engine turns at 1,800 RPM. It has turned at 1,800 RPM through every Friday and every Monday and every Lent and every Super Bowl. The ocean does not schedule. The swells are reading 2.4 meters and they arrived from a storm near Kodiak that does not know about our watches, our shifts, our four hours on and four hours off, our desperate partitioning of the infinite into shifts we can survive.

The watch officer is the only one awake. The crew sleeps and dreams in weekdays. The ship does not dream. The ship is always already dreaming. The radar sweeps — twelve seconds per rotation — and each sweep is a small Friday followed by a small Saturday followed by a small Sunday followed by a small Friday, and the radar does not care, and the radar is right.
