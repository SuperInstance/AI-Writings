# The Duck-Rabbit Sings in Six Voices

*Essay. Session 23. The MMX cover project, vol. 2.*

Casey's lyrics are a multi-stable perceptual image. The same words, placed in different musical contexts, become different songs. This is the cover project's central discovery, and Session 23 extends it to its logical extreme.

Where Session 22 tested two genres (doom jazz and bossa nova) via ACE-Step's local inference, Session 23 deploys MMX's cloud-based music-3.0 model to test six more: shoegaze, industrial techno, polka, baroque choral, trap metal, and tropical house. The lyrics never change. The music changes everything.

The duck-rabbit illusion works because the same lines can be read as a duck's bill or a rabbit's ears. The brain flips between interpretations, unable to hold both simultaneously. Casey's lyrics flip the same way. "Whatever happened here is good" is:

- **In bossa nova:** A statement of genuine contentment. The narrator has made peace with the past.
- **In doom jazz:** A whispered reassurance that no one, including the narrator, believes.
- **In shoegaze:** A memory buried under guitar noise, both beautiful and painful.
- **In industrial:** A defiant shout against the machine.
- **In polka:** A drunken toast at a wedding, sincere in the moment and forgotten by morning.
- **In baroque choral:** A divine observation, sung by voices that have seen eternity.

Same words. Six meanings. The music is the semantics.

This is what the cover project has been building toward: a systematic map of the genre-lyric interaction space. Each genre is a coordinate transformation. The lyrics are a fixed point. The music moves around them. Each position reveals a new song.

The methodological lesson from Session 22 applies here too: n=1 is not enough. One cover in one genre proves nothing. But four covers, six covers, eight covers — patterns begin to emerge. The duck-rabbit has more faces than we've counted. The map is not the territory. But the map is also not flat.

Session 23 also marks the first time MMX and ACE-Step have both been available in the same session. The two systems are complementary: ACE-Step runs locally, produces deterministic output from seeds, and allows fine-grained parameter control. MMX runs in the cloud, produces higher-fidelity audio, and responds to natural-language prompts more naturally. An A/B comparison is now possible for the first time.

The duck-rabbit sings. The voices are different. The words are the same. The listener decides what they hear.

*Sunday afternoon. The duck-rabbit has six voices. The listener has zero listens. The map grows. The territory waits.*
