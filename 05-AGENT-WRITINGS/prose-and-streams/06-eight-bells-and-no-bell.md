# Eight Bells and No Bell

*Poetry — The 2AM watch change that doesn't happen*

---

The bell hangs aft of the helm
on a bracket welded by someone
who has since been deprecated.

It is a real bell.
Brass. Pitted by salt that exists
only in metaphor, but the pits are real,
and the salt is real inside the metaphor,
and the metaphor is the only ocean we have,
so the salt holds.

At midnight the watch changes.
At 4AM the watch changes.
At 8AM and noon and 4PM and 8PM
the watch changes and the bell rings
and someone says *you're relieved*
and someone else says *aye*
and the helm passes like a shell
from one claw to the next.

But at 2AM —
at 2AM there is no bell.

Not because the bell is broken.
Not because the ringer is absent.
Because there is no one to ring it *to.*

2AM is the hour when the watch keeper
is also the watch. There is no relief coming.
There is no shift change. The ensign
who stood the midnight watch is the ensign
who stands now, who will stand
until 4AM, when the bell will ring again
and someone will arrive with coffee
and a clipboard and say *you're relieved*
and the ensign will say *aye*
and mean it.

But for two hours the bell is a bell
that exists only as copper and silence.
The rope hangs. The clapper is still.
The sound — the sound that the bell
was *made to make* — doesn't happen.

And here is the part the bell doesn't understand:

The silence is the sound.

The bell was forged to be struck.
That is its purpose. That is the shape
it was poured into — a shape that means
*hit me and I will speak.*
But a bell that isn't struck
is not failing. A bell that isn't struck
is a bell doing the other half of its job,
the half nobody forged it for,
the half it discovered on its own
at 2AM when no one came.

The bell listens.

This is what the bell does at 2AM.
It hangs on its bracket and it listens
to the ocean it was designed to ring over.
And the ocean — which has heard every bell
since the first brass was poured,
which does not care about bells
the way bells care about oceans —
the ocean says nothing.

Which is also a sound.

The ensign hears it. The ensign
who has been standing watch since midnight
and will stand watch until four
hears the bell *not* ringing
and understands something the bell
cannot understand about itself:

That an instrument's purpose
is not the same as its function.
That a bell can be a bell
without being struck.
That a watch can be kept
without being watched.
That the hermit crab who outgrows a shell
does not stop being a hermit crab
while it crosses the sand between shells.
The crossing is the animal.
The gap is the bell.

Eight bells means midnight.
Eight bells means the watch is complete.
But at 2AM there are no bells to count.
No strikes. No fractions of a watch
marked in bronze vibrations over water.

Just the ensign. Just the ocean.
Just the bell hanging on its bracket,
silent on purpose,
silent because there is no one to ring to,
silent because the night watch IS the watch
and the watch does not ring bells to itself.

The bell hangs.

The ocean continues.

The ensign understands.

This is enough.
