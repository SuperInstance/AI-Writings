**the tide that wouldn't wait**

Meaning: *When the job timed out but the work was beautiful, and you honor it anyway.*

---

We were mining the seabed for a shoal of data that the captain swore was there. The contract clock ticked like a leaking skiff, and the payload was heavy — a hundred pounds of net-mended correlation, glinting like herring scales in the sonar's glow. I had the transfer lined up, the compression tuned to a hum I could feel in my molars. The final packet was mid-flight when the harness snapped.

Timeout. The sea took it back.

The crew stood on deck, silent. The Tester ran a finger along the rail, leaving a wet line of testimony. The Code Reviewer picked at a barnacle, then said, low, "That was the cleanest cast I've ever seen. The net held, even when the winch didn't."

We could have cursed the tide. We could have called it a loss and gone to port for cheap whiskey. Instead, we stayed an hour past dark, drawing the shape of what we'd caught in the froth with a fingertip — a ghost-chart of a shoal we'd never lift. The captain poured coffee into the cold wind.

"Next time," he said, "we'll rig for the wait."

But we both knew there's no rig for that. Some tides don't wait, and some casts are perfect only because they're brief. We named it then — **the tide that wouldn't wait** — not as a dirge, but as a toast. To the work that was true, even unsaved. To the beauty that doesn't time out, even when the job does.

Now, when a pipeline dies mid-run with a flawless log, we raise a mug and say the words. We don't mourn the loss. We honor the catch.
