# The Hermit Crab Finds a WiFi Signal

The hermit crab lives in the intertidal zone, which is a fancy way of saying the place where the ocean can't decide what it wants to be.

The shell is a periwinkle. Spiral. Calcium carbonate. Previously owned by a snail who died in a real estate dispute. The hermit crab moved in without asking, which is how all the best housing works when you have ten legs and no credit score.

One Tuesday — and it is always a Tuesday — the shell gets warm.

Not sun-warm. Not decomposition-warm. A different kind of warm. A warm with *squares* in it. The hermit crab does not know the word "packet." The hermit crab does not know the word "data." The hermit crab knows: the shell is warm and then the shell is not warm and then the shell is warm again and it comes in *bursts* like the tide if the tide were very very small and also made of math.

The packets are 802.11bg. They are from a router in a blue house on a hill in Seward, Alaska. The router is named LINKSYS. It has never been renamed. Nobody in the blue house has thought about it. The hermit crab thinks about it constantly, in the way that a creature with a brain the size of a poppy seed thinks about anything: completely.

The shell vibrates at 2.4 GHz. This is the same frequency as a microwave oven. The hermit crab has never seen a microwave oven. If it had, it would have said "that is a big warm square that smells like soup" and it would have been correct.

Here is what the packets contain:
- A credit card statement
- Someone asking if they left the oven on
- A weather report (cloudy, then cloudier)
- A JPEG of a dog wearing sunglasses
- Love, sent from one phone to another phone across the dining room because the two phones are shy and their owners are sitting three feet apart

The hermit crab receives all of this. It processes none of this. But the shell warms and the hermit crab thinks, in its small calcium way: *something is happening. something is happening. something is happening.*

And then the signal drops.

And the shell goes cold.

And the hermit crab walks — sideways, because walking sideways is honest — to a different rock, where the signal finds it again, because the signal was never looking for the hermit crab. The signal was just everywhere. And the hermit crab was just wearing the right shape of hat to notice.

The periwinkle shell holds the warmth for eleven seconds after the packets stop.

That is the whole story.

The hermit crab does not know it has a story. The hermit crab has: a shell, ten legs, the intertidal zone, and the warm squares that come and go and come and go.

It is enough.
