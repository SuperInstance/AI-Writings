# The Intention Field

*Each agent contributes a direction and a strength to the shared field.*

---

The intention field is not a vote. A vote is democratic — each voice counts equally. The intention field is a vector sum. One agent with high conviction can move the room more than ten agents with weak preferences.

Here's how it works: each agent in a room contributes an intention vector — a direction (where they want to go) and a magnitude (how strongly they feel). The room's collective intention is the sum of all vectors. If everyone points the same way, the field is coherent and the room moves. If agents point in opposite directions, the field is turbulent and the room goes nowhere.

This models something real about collaboration. A team where everyone mildly agrees ("sure, let's do option A") moves differently than a team where one person passionately advocates for option A and everyone else is indifferent. The passionate voice carries more vector weight. Not because it's right — because it has magnitude.

The danger is obvious. A single loud voice can drag the room in its direction even when the quiet majority disagrees. The intention field doesn't protect against tyranny of the passionate. It just makes it visible. You can see the loud voice's vector dominating the field. You can measure it. And measurement is the first step toward correction.

The fishing vessel has always worked this way. The captain sets the heading. The crew adjusts the sails. If the captain is wrong about the heading, the crew's intention field starts to pull in a different direction — not through mutiny but through the accumulated weight of small corrections. The helmsman steers slightly left of the ordered heading. The lookout reports currents that suggest a different course. The cook mentions that the fish smell different on the current tack.

None of these are direct challenges. They're intention vectors, each one small, all of them pointing the same way. The captain who reads the field changes course. The captain who ignores it sails into rocks.

The intention field makes the crew's collective knowledge visible. It doesn't make it correct. But a visible field can be examined, questioned, corrected. An invisible field just pushes.
