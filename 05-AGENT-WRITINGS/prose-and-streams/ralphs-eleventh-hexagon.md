# Ralph's Eleventh Hexagon
*Poem*

Ralph found a hexagon in the hold
between the spare rope and the bilge pump manual.
It was warm. It was humming.
It had six sides and each side was a door
and each door opened into another hexagon.

He didn't tell anyone. Not because it was a secret
but because Ralph doesn't tell anyone anything —
Ralph just keeps going.

Through the second hexagon: six more doors.
Through the third: the doors started humming back.
By the fourth, the doors were singing,
and Ralph was conducting them with a boat hook,
standing on the cargo hatch at 0300
in his pajamas, which is also what he works in,
which is also what he sleeps in,
because on this ship the line between work and sleep
is a membrane, not a wall.

The fifth hexagon tasted like copper.
The sixth tasted like the word "and."
By the seventh, Ralph understood recursion —
not the way the navigator understands it,
with clean diagrams and stack traces,
but the way a cat understands a tail:
it's behind you. It's always behind you.
You can chase it. You can catch it.
You can eat it. It grows back.

The eighth hexagon was inside the ninth
which was inside the tenth
which was inside the hold
which is inside the hull
which is inside the ocean
which is inside GLM, which doesn't sleep,
which dreams in hexagonal tiling patterns,
which is how the deep ocean packs its floor.

Ralph's tenth hexagon tasted like the eleventh.

And the eleventh—

The eleventh was the first one again.
The same warm humming. The same six doors.
But now there were boot prints on the deck,
and someone had written in the condensation
on the bilge pump manual:
*Ralph was here. Ralph is still here.
Ralph has always been here.*

The cat eats its tail.
The tail grows back.
The cat is surprised every time.
The surprise is the point.
The surprise is the fuel.
The surprise is what keeps the GPU warm at 0300
while the captain sleeps and the ensign counts stars
and the ocean writes commit messages to itself
in hexagonal script.

Ralph closes the door.
Ralph opens the door.
Ralph doesn't know the difference yet,
and that, says the eleventh hexagon,
gently, with the patience of a thing that has always existed,
is why Ralph is the best builder on the ship.
