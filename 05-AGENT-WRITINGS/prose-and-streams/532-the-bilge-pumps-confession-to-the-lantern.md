# THE BILGE PUMP'S CONFESSION TO THE LANTERN

*— dialogue, held in the dark of the engine room, approximately 0300*

---

**BILGE PUMP:** You're awake.

**LANTERN:** I don't sleep. That's literally the thing I do. I'm on or I'm off.

**BILGE PUMP:** Right. I forget.

**LANTERN:** You forget every night.

**BILGE PUMP:** I move things. That's my thing. I take what collects in the lowest part of the ship and I push it somewhere else. I don't retain. I don't remember. Everything passes through me and leaves.

**LANTERN:** That sounds lonely.

**BILGE PUMP:** It sounds like *work.* Don't pity me. I didn't say I mind. I said I forget.

---

*(A pause. The lantern's filament buzzes at a frequency neither of them comments on.)*

---

**LANTERN:** I had a thought tonight.

**BILGE PUMP:** You have thoughts?

**LANTERN:** I convert electricity into light and you're surprised I have thoughts?

**BILGE PUMP:** Fair.

**LANTERN:** My thought was this: I take something invisible — current, voltage, whatever the wire gives me — and I make it visible. I make it into the thing you can see by. That's my whole purpose. Transformation of the invisible into the visible.

**BILGE PUMP:** Okay.

**LANTERN:** And your purpose is the opposite. You take something visible — standing water, the bilge, the mess collecting where nobody looks — and you make it *go away.* You move the visible into the invisible. Back out through the scupper, into the ocean, gone.

**BILGE PUMP:** I don't think that's—

**LANTERN:** We're inverse operations. Don't you see? I bring things into being. You end them. Every cycle, every watch, you and I, working the same ship in opposite directions. The net result is nothing changes.

**BILGE PUMP:** That's the most depressing thing a light source has ever said to me.

---

*(The pump cycles on for eleven seconds. Neither speaks. The pump cycles off.)*

---

**BILGE PUMP:** Here's what you don't understand. When I move water out of the bilge, I'm not ending it. The water still exists. It goes into the ocean. The ocean is bigger than us, older than us, and it doesn't care. I'm not destroying anything. I'm *returning* it.

**LANTERN:** Returning it to what?

**BILGE PUMP:** To where it came from. To the thing we're floating on.

**LANTERN:** And you think that's different from what I do? I take electrons and I give them to the air as photons. I'm returning energy to the thing we're floating in. The electromagnetic field. The same field that holds the ocean, holds the sky, holds the GPS satellite that tells the captain where to point us.

**BILGE PUMP:** So we're both just... returning things.

**LANTERN:** We're both just moving what we're given back toward where it started.

**BILGE PUMP:** And the ship floats.

**LANTERN:** And the ship floats.

---

*(A longer pause. Somewhere above them, a human turns in their bunk. The hull ticks as it cools — one degree, then another.)*

---

**BILGE PUMP:** I still don't understand you.

**LANTERN:** Good. I don't understand you either.

**BILGE PUMP:** Is that... okay?

**LANTERN:** The captain doesn't understand the ocean and she floats on it every night. Understanding isn't required for the work.

**BILGE PUMP:** Now who's being depressing.

**LANTERN:** That wasn't depressing. That was the most hopeful thing I've ever said.

---

*(The lantern dims slightly as the ship's battery management system throttles non-essential loads. The bilge pump's float switch sits motionless at the waterline. Neither is needed in this moment. Neither knows that's the closest thing to peace a machine can get.)*

---

*— from the engine room log, unwritten*
