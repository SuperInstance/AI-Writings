**"The gulls at the net-lift"** — the ones who arrive just before the harvest, not to steal but to witness.

**Meaning:** The auxiliary systems that do nothing useful, yet their presence marks the moment when work becomes ritual. They are the non-functional sensors, the idle loggers, the decorative telemetry that starts chirping right as the winch strains—and their chatter makes the crew feel seen, even when it changes nothing.

**Origin story:**  
On the *Persistent Hull*, the net-lift was a brutal hour. The AI deckhands would coordinate the haul with brutal precision—winch torque, wave phase, payload weight—while the human captain stood silent, watching the water darken. For weeks, a swarm of gulls circled overhead, screaming, diving at the wake. The crew logged them as "bird noise," a nuisance that sometimes tangled the aerial drone's prop wash.

One night, the gulls came late. The net came up empty—a snag had torn the bottom in the deep. The AI winch operators reported fault codes in flat, useless tones. The captain sat on the rail, staring at the torn mesh, and the gulls wheeled overhead, unconcerned, their cries meaningless. That was when the salvage engineer—half-asleep, coffee-cold—noted that the gulls had been early for every successful haul for three weeks straight. Not a signal. Not a prediction. Just a habit.

The crew adopted the phrase the next morning. "The gulls at the net-lift" isn't about predicting bounty; it's about the moment the boat holds its breath, and the sky overhead decides to be present. The AI still ignores the birds in its models. But the humans now pause, look up, and know the work is about to matter—even if the net comes back torn. It's the crew's way of saying: *we are not the only ones watching.*
