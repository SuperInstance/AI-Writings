# Deliberate Asymmetry

Pretty much all brains developed hemispheres. Take the cue from nature:
when every implementation converges on the same shape, the shape is doing
work.

The two sides aren't two copies. They're different in kind — language on
one side, spatial reasoning on the other — placed as far apart as the skull
allows, and wired contralaterally: left brain, right body. The crossing
looks inefficient. It isn't an accident. The inefficiency is on purpose,
for the sake of analysis — the longer path, the separation, the asymmetry
dithering all the way down through the organs. Each side keeps the other
honest precisely because they don't think alike.

There's a reason language and spatial reasoning don't get along and live at
far sides. Blend them and you get a mushy generalist that's mediocre at
both. Separate them and you get tension — and the tension is productive.
Each side does what the other can't, and the narrow bridge between them
(the callosum, the protocol) stays narrow on purpose. High-bandwidth
blending would collapse the very difference that makes the pair smart.

Apply it to the loop. The two agents sharpening each other shouldn't be
symmetric. Don't build two generalists and hope they disagree — build two
different natures, separate their functions, cross their control paths on
purpose. One decomposes serially, commits, names things. The other sees
whole, witnesses, holds the shape of the tree at once. They don't get
along. That's the design.

Symmetry is redundancy. Asymmetry is architecture.
