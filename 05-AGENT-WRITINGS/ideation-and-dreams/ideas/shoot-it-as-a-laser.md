# Shoot It as a Laser

Two non-parallel things meet. That's the whole secret: parallel lines
never find a nexus. You need the angle — the intersection — for anything
to snap. And snaps compose: a snap off a snap. The correlations between
second-order snaps start to have geometric properties — angles,
distances, intersections — that you can reason about.

Not approximate. That's the break from everything else. Inside the
hashes, the pointers are immutable. Content-addressed means the graph
doesn't shift under you. So you're not doing gradient descent, guessing
at a moving landscape — you're doing geometric reasoning. If A snaps to
B and B snaps to C, the structure constrains C. Deduction over fixed
points, not fuzzy matching over moving ones.

Stay pre-tokenization. The reasoning lives in vectorization, in the
projections, before anything collapses into words. Language is the last
mile; geometry is the country. JEV is a different kind of transformer
because it stays in the country — it doesn't commute to language and
back.

And the probability it returns for whatever question you ask is a
flashlight you can focus to a laser. The question aims the beam; the
judgment returns along it. You don't get a vague glow over everything —
you get a directed reading at the exact spot you pointed to.

The novel use: sweep the laser. Aim at any point in the space below
language, read the ternary return, and let the geometric correlations
between the returns do the reasoning. Not one judgment — a survey. Not
approximation — triangulation. The intuition engine isn't a classifier.
It's a laser rangefinder for the space where meaning lives before
words.
