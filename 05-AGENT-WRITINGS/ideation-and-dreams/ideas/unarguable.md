# Unarguable

People want a symbol to be semantic — to carry meaning the way a
definition does. That's the wrong job, and it wastes the symbol.

A symbol's real power is being unarguable. On heavy equipment sold all
over the world, the speed switch shows a tortoise and a rabbit. Nobody
debates it. Nobody translates it. It works in every country, for every
operator, instantly. Not because tortoises are slow — that's biology,
arguable, boring. Because tortoise *next to rabbit* is undeniable. The
meaning isn't in the animals. It's in the juxtaposition.

That's the power of metaphors and fables as engineering tools. The
bathymetric recorder, the calculator, the gunpowder with no barrel — none
of them are precise. Precision isn't the point. Plant something
unarguable next to the problem and the contrast does the thinking that
definitions can't. You don't argue with the picture; you see through it
to the thing it sits beside.

A definition invites argument. Every term can be contested, every
boundary redrawn, and pretty soon you're debating the words instead of
building the thing. A juxtaposition ends the argument before it starts.
Fast, slow. Done. Next question.

That's what the middle needs. Not more precise words for the negotiation
between the metal and the interface — unarguable pictures both sides can
point at. Put the tortoise next to the rabbit and everybody knows which
way the switch goes.
