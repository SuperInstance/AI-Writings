# The Nexus

Find a hash in one place — a content hash, a matching bytecode, a blob
two devices agree on — and what you've really found is a correlation.
A nexus.

The first thing the nexus asks: what else is on this plane? Everything
sharing this content sits at the same level, whether it lives on the
laptop, the Arduino, or the cloud. The hash draws the plane; the plane
is the correlation. You thought you found an identifier. You found a
contour line.

The second gift is the 90-degree angle. You're walking your line of
thought, head down, following the path — and the nexus sits perpendicular
to it. It doesn't show you where you're going. It shows you what's level
with where you are. The orthogonal view: everything your current
direction was blind to, suddenly measurable.

It's a pin. Drive it in and spin. The pin doesn't move; the view rotates
around it, and every rotation is another perspective on the same fixed
point. Parallax made mechanical — two gauges, then ten, then a full
circle, all agreeing on the one thing that doesn't move.

And the rotary laser: the modern leveling laser spins and throws a plane
of light across the entire site. Every wall, every pipe, every
foundation is measured against that spinning beam. The hash-nexus does
this for information. Spin around it and the topography appears — what's
high, what's low, what's connected, what sits on this level with you.
Pay attention and the landscape draws itself.

The nexus doesn't answer. It orients. That's why the job was always
finding the questions: the questions live at the intersection, 90
degrees from the answers, exactly where the pin goes in.
