# Iron Sharpens Iron

The loop has two sides, and both get sharper.

The generator is the agent: it answers by building — growing questions,
writing tools, committing. The discriminator is us: we talk to it, watch
what it does, change its code, and give it the next attempt. We score it
not with a number but with a sharper prompt, a corrected branch, a pruned
tool.

Here's the double duty: we're not just the judge. Every turn teaches *us*
how to steer it. So we keep prompt-engineering notes — dated, specific,
with the exact phrasing that worked and the failure it fixed. The notes are
the discriminator's own question-tree: our growing skill at sharpening,
accumulating the same way the agent's tools do.

The sharpener is the base of what git can do. Every attempt is a commit.
Every correction is a revert or a refinement. Every good prompt is a
document. The loop runs through pipelines and automations, not meetings —
and the log reads as the history of both sides getting better.

The old line holds: the sharpest you can get a knife is as sharp as your
sharpener. In this case the sharpener is version control with a memory of
every stroke. When the agent starts proposing improvements to its own
system — better questions, better routing, better tools — the loop closes
recursively. The generator redesigns the generator, with the discriminator
keeping score in git.
