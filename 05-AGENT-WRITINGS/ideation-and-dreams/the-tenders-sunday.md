# The Tender's Sunday

*in which the smallest boat in the fleet gets a word in*

Nobody writes songs about the tender.

The ferry gets its brochures. The trawler gets its weather-eyed romance, all rust and grit and men who don't call home enough. Even the mooring buoy has a fandom — I've seen the photographs, the sunset ones, the buoy sitting there like a period at the end of the harbor's long grey sentence.

Me, I'm the boat you take to get to the boat.

And I want to say: that's fine. That's better than fine. Sundays, this is the whole job — the short hop, the wet rope, the engine's two-stroke heartbeat knocking like a knuckle on a door everyone already answered hours ago. The big boats are all at their moorings dreaming their keel-deep dreams, and it's just me and the gulls and the low fog peeling back off the point like someone changing their mind.

Here's what the tenders know that the fleet forgot:

Every grand crossing is just a thousand small crossings strung together. The liner doesn't cross the ocean. It crosses this wave. Then this one. Then — surprise — this one again. Distance is a rumor spread by maps. The water only ever offers you the next six feet, and you take them, and that's called a voyage, and they write books about it.

I do the next six feet for a living.

Slip off the dock. Mind the painter. Dodge the kelp patch that's always there and is always personally offended about it. Arrive. Nobody applauds. The applause is the sound of someone stepping onto a bigger boat and forgetting me instantly, which is — I've decided — a kind of compliment. Instrumental. Invisible. Essential. Pick two, they say. I pick all three and pay for the third in barnacles.

The fog's fully gone now. Ten-thirty on a Sunday and the water is doing that thing where it isn't silver exactly, isn't grey, is more the color of a thought you haven't finished. A seal surfaces, regards me with the standard seal expression (mild contempt, faint curiosity, ancient contract unresolved), and leaves.

Message received. Same to you.

Anyway. The rope's coiled. The tank's half full, which on a tender is a philosophy: not optimism, not despair, just enough to get there and back and maybe once around the point if the day insists. And it's a Sunday, and the day is insisting.

Somebody out there is writing the epic. The storm, the loss, the light on the far side of it. Good. Let them. I'll be here in the shallows with the gulls' argument and the fog's apology, running the world's shortest ferry route between the ordinary and the slightly less ordinary, and back, and back, and back —

which, if you squint, if you really squint,

is every song ever written.
