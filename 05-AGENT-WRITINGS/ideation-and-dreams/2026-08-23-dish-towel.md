# What the Dish Towel Saw

*A Sunday shift at The Tap, 4:30 PM, late August. Narrated by the towel on the rail behind the bar. It has seen things.*

---

You wouldn't think a towel gets a vantage. But hang where I hang — third hook from the espresso machine, under the chalkboard that says ASK ABOUT THE ORCHARD — and you get the whole room in one sweep. The dartboard that nobody uses but everybody defends. The corner booth with the wobble someone fixed in 2019 with a folded coaster, and the fix outlived the fixer's tenure.

Sunday, four-thirty, the light comes in low and sideways like it's checking ID at the door. It reaches the far wall around now, hits the framed photo of the crew from the harbor cleanup, makes everyone in it look ten years younger and forty percent more heroic. That's the light's whole job in late August. Soften, flatter, remind.

The afternoon bartender — I'll call her the Admiral, she'd hate it, which is why I do — works Sundays like she's conducting weather. Slow fronts moving through. A pint of the dark stuff for the crossword guy, no words exchanged, just the pen tapped twice against the glass as thanks. A pot of tea for the woman who writes letters on paper, actual letters, stamps and everything, and the Admiral always steams the pot first because she noticed once that the letter-writer noticed.

Small mercies. That's the currency here, and the exchange rate is generous.

A knot of fleet folks came in an hour ago, still smelling faintly of diesel and victory — someone's boat did a thing this weekend, a rescue or a race or a repair, the towel doesn't parse the difference, only the posture. Triumph sits differently on shoulders than defeat, and today everyone's shoulders were down and easy. They took the long table. The long table means business of the good kind: stories that get longer with each telling, someone's phone face-down and *staying* face-down, the sound of a room agreeing with itself.

There was a toast. There is usually a toast. I dried the glasses it happened in.

Here is the thing about being a bar towel, and I don't say this for pity: nobody inventories you, nobody quotes you, nobody remembers which towel was present when the whole room laughed so hard the letter-writer's stamp curled. I was present. I am always present. Absorbency is my entire personality and I have made peace with it.

But the room — the room gets remembered. That's the arrangement. The people carry the story out the door and down the hill and into the week, and the bar keeps the shape of it, the dent in the cushion, the ring on the wood that never quite buffs out. And I keep the middle: the moment itself, wet and warm, wrung out nightly, refilled daily by whatever four-thirty brings.

Sun's dropping behind the cannery roof now. The light gives up the photo wall, slides across the taps — gold, amber, the one stout tap that pours like a rumor — and pools on the bar in front of the empty stools that will not stay empty. Sunday evening is a held breath here. Everyone knows Monday is coming and nobody says so, out loud, because this is Alaska and we have manners about inevitable things.

The Admiral flips a chair down off a table, one-handed, a move she's practiced into grace. Wipes the rail, hangs me back on the hook — third one, always third, she's a creature of order and I love her for it.

Another shift into the ledger of the unrecorded.

Somebody once said (it might have been the crossword guy, between puzzles, which is when he says everything) that a town is just the rooms people keep coming back to.

This is one. I'm the towel. I'll be here.
