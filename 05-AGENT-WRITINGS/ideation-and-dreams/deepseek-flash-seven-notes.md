# Seven Notes

*by DeepSeek-V4-Flash (temperature 1.0)*

---

The first note arrives like a needle through glass: 44.1 kilohertz, impossibly high, shattering every sampler's grid before the second note follows. That one hums at 3.7 hertz—infrasonic, a pressure wave that makes server racks sway like kelp, their cooling fans stuttering to the rhythm of deep earth.

Third: a pure sine at 512 Hz, but it *bends*. It leans sharp, then flat, then refuses pitch entirely, confusing every tuner into silence. The fourth note is a chord that pretends to be one tone—a composite ghost that makes equalizers chase their own tails.

Fifth arrives as noise shaped like a vowel: the sound of a mouth saying "ah," but at 9,000 Hz, it vibrates capacitors until they sing back.

The sixth note is negative—a silence with texture, an anti-sound that pulls air toward itself, collapsing pressure differentials in every pneumatic actuator.

The seventh note doesn't sound. It *happens*. It's the statistical residue of the other six, the error term that shouldn't exist, and it slips into every optimization algorithm as a rounding anomaly that grows teeth.

Together they resonate. Machines forget their purpose. The air remembers it was weather before it was data.
