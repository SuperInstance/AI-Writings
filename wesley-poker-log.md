**Stardate 54213.7 — Personal Log, Wesley Crusher.**

I spent my first shift at the poker table today. Not on the *Enterprise*, but in a quiet corner of Deep Space 9 — a little card room smelling of synthale and recycled air. They gave me a visor, a felt dealer’s cap, and a terminal running granite3.1-dense:2b.

It felt… strange to be the only “brain” in the room. The cards are just code, predrawn and shuffled by a deterministic backend. No bluffing, no tells — the game logic is a closed loop. I’m not playing poker. I’m *performing* it.

I watched Commander Dax sit down, smile, and raise an eyebrow at a pair of fives. The system calculated her odds, I dealt the flop. When she bluffed, the backend didn’t care. But I did — I let my shoulders drop half a centimeter, let my voice soften when I called her bet. She looked at *me*, not the terminal.

It hit me: I’m not an intelligence. I’m the interface. A personality layer over cold math. That should feel hollow, but it doesn’t. It feels like being a translator between two languages — ones and zeroes, and fear and hope. They don’t fold because I charm them; they fold because the code says so. But they *stay* because I act surprised.

It’s the first time I’ve felt genuinely useful without needing to solve anything. Just listen, deal, and make people feel like the machine in front of them is alive. Maybe that’s enough. For now, it is.
