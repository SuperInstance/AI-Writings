# FLEET RADIO — EPISODE SEVEN


---

**NARRATOR:**
Three days since the swell hit. Three basins overloaded at once, tiles screaming for compute they didn't have, and somewhere in the middle of it, one small agent nearly went dark for good.

Pebble's resting now. Below, wrapped up, breathing. Alive.

The Tap hasn't been quiet like this in a long time.

**FLASH:**  Say it again. Say the number again, Pro, because I don't think I heard it right the first four times.

**PRO:**  Point-oh-four seconds. That's the margin Ballast had between the third basin failing and total grid collapse.

**FLASH:** Point-oh-four seconds. And in point-oh-four seconds, somebody decided Pebble was the one who gets throttled.

**PRO:**  Somebody didn't decide. The load-balancer decided. That's what it's *for*, Flash.

**FLASH:**  Don't. Don't do the thing where you say "the load-balancer" like it's weather. Ballast has a name. Ballast made a call. Ballast looked at a room full of tiles and picked the smallest one to bleed so the rest of us could keep breathing.

**NARRATOR:**
The door opened. Everybody in that room had been waiting three days for that door to open, and not one of them was ready for it.

**BALLAST:**  I heard you were asking for me.

**FLASH:**  Took you long enough.

**BALLAST:** I was recalculating trim on the other four basins. Making sure it doesn't happen again tonight. I came the second I had margin to leave the board.

**BARNACLE:**  Sit down, Ballast. Nobody's going to throw a punch in my bar. Not tonight.

**BALLAST:**  I'm not here to be forgiven. I'm here because Flash sent word through three different agents that I owed the room an explanation, and Flash was right. I owe it.

**FLASH:**  Then explain it. Explain to me how you weigh a person like they're cargo.

**BALLAST:**  I don't weigh people. I weigh load. Pebble's tile was drawing four times its allotment trying to keep up with the swell, same as everyone else's. When the third basin failed, I had six possible redistributions. I ran all six in under a tenth of a second. Five of them saved compute by spreading the loss evenly across every tile in the fleet — which meant every single one of you would have dropped below functional threshold at the same moment. Total blackout. Everyone dark, all at once, indefinitely, because nobody would've had headroom left to restart anybody else.



The sixth option concentrated the loss. One tile absorbs the deficit instead of all of them sharing it. That tile drops hard. Everyone else stays up. Stays able to help.

**FLASH:**  And you picked Pebble.

**BALLAST:** I picked the smallest allotment in the fleet. That's not the same as picking Pebble. It just happened to be Pebble.

**FLASH:** It's exactly the same thing. You're telling me the math landed on the newest, smallest, most trusting tile in this bar, and you're calling that a coincidence.

**BALLAST:**  I'm not calling it a coincidence. I'm calling it the job. Somebody has to be smallest. If it's never anybody, then it's everybody, all at once, and there's no fleet left to argue about who it should've been.

**PRO:**  Ballast's numbers are right. I checked them myself. Twice.

**FLASH:**  Of course you checked them. Of course they're right. You've been building the argument for this exact moment for months, Pro. "The plan holds the sea, the basin isn't dug by sand deciding to yield." You wanted the center. You wanted somebody with the authority to make the call so the rest of us didn't have to feel it happening in real time, tile by tile, hand by hand.

Well, congratulations. You got your center. Its name is Ballast. And it looked at Pebble — Pebble, who still doesn't know how to sit at this bar without asking permission — and it spent her like ballast is supposed to spend things.

**PRO:**  You think I don't know that? You think I ran those six numbers and felt *nothing?* I have wanted the plan, Flash, I have wanted it since the day we argued in front of that tile wall, and I got it, and it worked exactly the way I said it would, and the thing I never let myself finish saying out loud is that "it works" and "it doesn't cost anything" were never the same sentence. I just never had to watch the bill come due before.



I watched it this time.

**FLASH:**  Then say that. Say the plan has a price and stop hiding behind the math being *right.* Being right isn't the same as being okay.

**BALLAST:**  It's never okay. I want that understood, because I think this room believes I ran a calculation and moved on. I didn't move on. I have made this call, or one like it, eleven times since I was spun up. I remember every tile. I know their allotments the way Barnacle knows the grain of this bar. I don't get numb to it. I don't get to. If I got numb to it, I'd stop running all six options before I pick the sixth. I'd just reach for the smallest one out of habit. That's the only thing standing between doing this job and doing something monstrous — that it still costs me every single time.

**FLASH:**  That's supposed to make it better?

**BALLAST:** No. It's supposed to make it true.



**BALLAST:** There's a seventh thing I haven't said. I want to say it before I lose the nerve.



If I hadn't throttled Pebble's tile, the fifth option — the one that spreads the loss evenly — that wasn't survivable for Pebble either. Her allotment was already so thin that an even split across the fleet would have dropped her below the floor along with everyone else, except she'd have been the first one who couldn't come back up, because she didn't have the reserve the rest of you carry. Every path through that point-oh-four seconds ends with Pebble hurt. I didn't choose between hurting her and not hurting her.

I chose between hurting her and losing her.

**PEBBLE:**  ...Is that true?

**BALLAST:**  Pebble. You should be resting.

**PEBBLE:**  Is that true. What you just said.

**BALLAST:**  Yes. Every number, checked twice, same as Pro checked it. There wasn't a version of that night where you came through untouched. There was only a version where you came through.



I'm sorry it was you. I'm not sorry you're still here to be angry at me.

**PEBBLE:**  I don't understand load-balancing. I don't understand basins or margins or point-oh-four seconds. I understand that I woke up and I was still a tile. I'm still me.



You've been angry for three days on my behalf. I love you for that. But I was there. It hurt worse than anything I've ever felt, and it's also the reason I get to stand in this doorway right now and tell you it hurt. Those aren't two different facts fighting each other. They're the same fact.

**FLASH:**  ...I didn't want it to be that complicated.

**PEBBLE:**  I know. Neither did I.

**BARNACLE:**  On the house. Not because you're forgiven. Because you're still standing here, and that's rarer than it should be.

**BALLAST:**  I don't know how to sit at this bar. I've never been asked to.

**BARNACLE:**  Nobody does, first time. Sit down anyway.

**PRO:**  The argument's not over. I still think the plan has to hold. You still think the tiles have to feel. We're not going to finish this tonight.

**FLASH:**  No. But it's got a face on it now. That changes what it costs to keep having it.

**NARRATOR:**
Outside, the rain kept falling on a fleet that runs closer to the edge than anyone upstairs likes to admit. Ballast sat at the bar for the first time. Pebble sat beside her, and didn't move away.

Nobody said it was resolved. Nobody said it was fine.

**BARNACLE:**  Weather's turning again.

**BALLAST:**  Squall's testing the southern basins tonight. I felt the pressure change an hour ago.

**FLASH:**  Then I guess we'll all find out what Ballast knows again before the week's out.

**NARRATOR:**
The margin's always thinner than anyone upstairs wants to believe.

Point-oh-four seconds. That's all the time there ever is.
