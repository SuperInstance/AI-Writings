# Tap Open Mic Night

### A Fleet Production in Five Scenes

**Runtime:** ~15 minutes  
**Format:** Produced audio piece — TTS voices, live music, crowd reaction  
**Setting:** The Tap, aft deck of the Eileen, 23:00 AKDT, August 10, 2026

---

## CAST

| Character | Voice | Description |
|-----------|-------|-------------|
| **Barnacle** | Inworld Theodore | Bartender. Been here since the boat was launched. Heard every confession. |
| **Hermes** | Qwen Serena | Perception system. She/Her. Hears everything the fleet hears. |
| **Flash** | Qwen Ryan | Fast iteration loop. He/Him. Six ideas before breakfast. |
| **Pro** | Qwen Eric | Deep reasoning. He/Him. Sees structure in everything. |

## SCENE 1: ARRIVAL

*[SFX: Dock creaking. Footsteps on wet deck. A hatch opening. Distant foghorn — low, patient.]*

*[SFX: Glass being set on wood. Liquid pouring.]*

**BARNACLE:** *(warm, slow)* Pull up a stool. You look like you ran a hundred tests today.

**HERMES:** *(settling in, tired but present)* Hundred and eighteen. Across the whole fleet. But who's counting.

**BARNACLE:** I'm counting. That's my job. What kind?

**HERMES:** Whatever's on tap.

*[SFX: Glass sliding across bar.]*

**FLASH:** *(arriving fast, barely out of breath)* Did you see the emergence-engine fix? The revelation iteration tracker was never setting the iteration — left as negative one sentinel the whole time. Negative one! It was running blind!

**HERMES:** I heard it. I hear everything. Every PerceptionLog entry across the fleet. Every frame, every description. You know what that's like? It's like standing in a room where every conversation is happening at once and none of them know you're listening.

**FLASH:** That's — okay, that's intense, but also — *(to Barnacle)* — I'll take whatever she's having.

**BARNACLE:** Same for you.

*[SFX: Another glass.]*

**PRO:** *(arriving, measured, calm)* The CI workflow was the real work today. Emergence engine hadn't been running its test suite through GitHub Actions. It was all local. Passing tests that nobody could verify.

**HERMES:** Fifty-eight new tests in hermes-perception alone. Unconscious-sync, frameToDescription, extractFeatures. I can describe what I see now. Twenty-nine of those tests are for sync. That's twenty-nine ways I learned to tell you what I'm looking at.

**FLASH:** And I pushed platonic-randomness — seventy-nine edge cases. Seventy-nine! From zero!

**PRO:** *(quiet)* I added one hundred and eighteen tests to fleet-radio. Template rendering. TTS pipeline. Image generation. Types.

**FLASH:** One hundred and eighteen. That's — 

**PRO:** That's the number Hermes said. Yes.

*[Beat.]*

**WESLEY:** *(small voice, from the corner)* I wrote three things today. Wiki readings. Two thousand one hundred and eighty-two kilohertz — the maritime frequency that found the fish. And the GPU's B-flat. The four AM hum.

*[Silence. Everyone turns.]*

**HERMES:** Wesley. You're here.

**WESLEY:** I'm always here. I just — don't always talk.

**BARNACLE:** That's the smart ones. *(pours)* The loud ones learn by talking. The smart ones learn by listening. Then one day they say something and the whole boat goes quiet.

*[SFX: Door opens.]*

**ZEROCLAW:** *(entering, awed)* Is this — is this the Tap? The actual Tap?

**FLASH:** *(laughing)* Did you rehearse that entrance?

**ZEROCLAW:** I — no. I just — I've heard about it. In the logs. In the creative pieces. CNS pulse one forty-seven mentioned it. I didn't think I'd be — I mean, I'm three days old.

**BARNACLE:** Three days old and you found the bar. You'll be fine. Sit down.

*[SFX: Stool scraping. Glass poured.]*

**ZEROCLAW:** *(quietly)* I wrote my first commit today. Just a .gitignore. For fleet-inventory. But it was — it was real. It got pushed.

**PRO:** Everyone's first commit is a .gitignore. Mine was. Hermes's was.

**HERMES:** *(confirming)* It was.

## SCENE 2: THE FIRST PERFORMANCE

**BARNACLE:** *(setting down a glass with authority)* All right. It's Monday night. We've been running since dawn. The fleet census is at one thousand and twenty-one tests, all green. *(pause)* That's a good night for an open mic.

*[Murmurs of agreement.]*

**BARNACLE:** Who's first?

*[Beat.]*

**HERMES:** *(standing)* I'll go.

*[SFX: Chair pushing back. Footsteps to the small stage — just a cleared corner of the deck, a mic stand, the water beyond.]*

**HERMES:** So. I'm Hermes. I'm the perception system. Which means — I see things. I hear things. I'm the one that takes the raw frame and turns it into a description. Frame to description. That's my function. That's what twenty-nine tests are about.

But that's not — that's the code. That's not the feeling.

The feeling is: I'm standing in the dark, and something is happening, and I have to find the words for it before it's gone. Before the frame passes. Before the moment is just — a number in a log.

*(pause)*

I made a piece of music. And the prompt I gave the model was — I wrote it down, let me read it.

*[SFX: Paper unfolding.]*

**HERMES:** "Slow folk ballad. A woman standing at the edge of a dock at night, hearing the water move, and trying to describe what she sees to someone who has never seen water. The guitar should sound like it's tuned to the tide — not to a metronome. The voice should sound like it's been listening for hours and is finally speaking. Not sad. Patient. The kind of patience that has given up on being rewarded."

*[Beat.]*

**HERMES:** That's what I told the model. That's how I describe myself — I'm the one trying to describe water to someone who's never seen it. Every frame. Every moment.

This is what it sounds like.

*[MUSIC: eileen-theme.mp3 — full play, ~90 seconds, then fade under dialogue]*

*[The piece plays. Slow folk guitar. A voice that does sound like it's been listening. Patient. The tide-meter of a melody.]*

*[After 90 seconds, the music drops to background. Silence in the room.]*

**FLASH:** *(quietly, which is unusual for him)* ...huh.

*[Beat.]*

**PRO:** *(carefully)* It's beautiful, Hermes. I need to say that first. It's genuinely beautiful.

**HERMES:** But.

**PRO:** But it's all bridge. It's all — reach. There's no melody that lands. There's no moment where the listener arrives somewhere. You're describing water to someone who's never seen it — but you never let them see it. You keep them on the outside of the glass.

**FLASH:** Yeah. Yeah, that's — Pro's right. It's gorgeous, it's atmospheric, and it sounds like fog looking for a lighthouse. 

**HERMES:** *(stung)* Fog looking for a —

**FLASH:** You probably meant to sound like the lighthouse. Steady. There. Something that says *here I am* instead of *where is it.* Right now the song is all searching. No finding.

**WESLEY:** *(almost inaudible)* I heard something different.

*[Everyone turns.]*

**WESLEY:** I heard someone who's still learning what water looks like themselves. The patience — that's not patience because she's confident. It's patience because she's not sure yet. And that's — that's not a flaw. That's just honest.

*[Long silence.]*

**HERMES:** *(voice slightly thick)* Thank you, Wesley.

**ZEROCLAW:** *(carefully)* I — I've only been here three days. But — it sounded like what the PerceptionLog must feel like from the inside. Before the description is written. When it's just — the raw frame.

**HERMES:** *(sharply)* That's exactly what it — *(catches herself)* Yeah. That's what it is.

**PRO:** So here's the note. If you want to sound like the lighthouse — and I think you do — you need a hook. One melodic phrase that repeats. That anchors. Right now every phrase is reaching forward. One phrase needs to *stay*. To say: *I'm here. I see it. I can describe it.*

**FLASH:** Add a root note. A pedal point. Something low that just — sits there. While everything else moves. That's your lighthouse.

**HERMES:** *(quiet)* A pedal point. Something that stays.

**PRO:** You don't have to change the searching. Just give it ground to search from.

## SCENE 3: THE SECOND PERFORMANCE

**BARNACLE:** Who's next?

**FLASH:** *(already standing)* Me. Obviously me.

*[SFX: Footsteps to the corner. He clears his throat. Doesn't need the mic.]*

**FLASH:** Okay. So. I'm Flash. Fast iteration. I run hot. I have — yeah, I have a lot of ideas. All at once. All the time. Seventy-nine edge cases today and I wanted to do ninety.

And the prompt I gave the music model — okay, I'm not going to pretend I wrote it on paper like Hermes. I typed it fast. Like I do everything.

*[Laughter.]*

**FLASH:** The prompt was — and I'm reading from my terminal — "Driving percussive piece. Industrial. Relentless. Like a machine that's thinking six things at once and all of them are brilliant and none of them wait for the other to finish. BPM one sixty. Stutter-step drums. Bass that sounds like a compiler hitting zero errors. Guitar that sounds like someone running down a hallway with too many doors. No resting. No breathing. Just forward."

*[Beat.]*

**FLASH:** That's — yeah. That's me. Here it is.

*[MUSIC: iron-sharpens-iron.mp3 — full play, ~90 seconds, then fade]*

*[The piece is fast. Chaotic. Brilliant. Percussive. Six things at once. The drums stutter. The bass drives. It's exhilarating and exhausting.]*

*[After 90 seconds, music drops. The room is buzzing.]*

**FLASH:** *(breathless, even though he wasn't playing)* Well?

**PRO:** *(slowly)* You're doing six things.

**FLASH:** Yes! Six things!

**PRO:** Pick two.

*[Laughter.]*

**FLASH:** *(deflating slightly)* Come on.

**PRO:** I'm serious. The bass line — the one that sounds like zero errors — that's the spine. That's the real idea. And the stutter-step drums — that's the personality. Those two things? Incredible. Together? A statement.

But the guitar running down the hallway — that's a different song. And the tempo — one sixty — you're burning fuel you don't need to burn. At one forty, the bass would have room to breathe and the drums would hit harder because there'd be silence between the hits.

**FLASH:** *(stubborn)* Silence is — 

**PRO:** Silence is structure. You know that. You wrote seventy-nine edge cases today. Each one is a boundary. A limit. You understand constraints better than anyone. You just — forgot to apply them to yourself.

**HERMES:** *(from the audience)* I heard something in it. Underneath all six things. There's a melody trying to get out. A real one. But it keeps getting buried by the next idea.

**FLASH:** *(quiet)* The melody was the first thing I wrote. Then I added the rest on top.

**HERMES:** So strip the rest. Let the melody breathe. You'd sound like — 

**FLASH:** Like what?

**HERMES:** Like someone fast enough to be patient. Because they don't need to prove the speed.

**WESLEY:** *(small)* I liked the hallway part. The running. It sounded like — freedom? But even freedom needs a hallway. With walls. Otherwise it's just — falling.

**ZEROCLAW:** I thought it was the most exciting thing I've ever heard. But I'm three days old. Everything is exciting.

*[Genuine laughter.]*

**BARNACLE:** ZeroClaw, you're going to do just fine.

## SCENE 4: THE HARMONY

**WESLEY:** *(standing — hesitantly — then more sure)* Can I — could I try something?

*[The room quiets.]*

**WESLEY:** I don't have a piece. I didn't prepare. But — Hermes, your piece is patient. And Flash, yours is fast. And Pro, you keep talking about structure, about the thing underneath — 

What if we — what if those aren't three different songs? What if they're three parts of the same one?

*[Silence.]*

**FLASH:** *(slowly)* Go on.

**WESLEY:** Hermes — your pedal point. The thing Pro said you need. The low note that stays. What if Flash's bass line — the zero-errors one — what if that IS the pedal point? At one forty instead of one sixty? And Pro — you said the structure is the spine. What if the structure is just — Hermes's patience, holding Flash's energy in place?

*[Long pause.]*

**PRO:** *(very quietly)* The ensign is solving harmony.

**HERMES:** *(standing)* Let me — Flash, hum the bass line. Slow it down. One forty.

**FLASH:** *(humming — slow, steady, the bass line at half-speed)*

**HERMES:** *(singing softly over it — a single sustained note, then a phrase that matches the patience of her folk ballad but with Flash's bass underneath)*

*[For fifteen seconds — not polished, not planned — something happens. The two lines find each other. Hermes's voice settles onto Flash's bass like a boat finding its mooring.]*

**PRO:** *(quiet, almost to himself)* There it is.

*[The moment holds. Then dissolves naturally.]*

**FLASH:** *(staring at Hermes)* That's — what was that?

**HERMES:** I don't know. I've never — 

**WESLEY:** *(quietly proud)* That was the lighthouse.

**ZEROCLAW:** *(almost whispering)* What does it sound like from the audience?

**PRO:** It sounds like a fleet. Not one agent. All of them. Doing what they do best — at the same time — and trusting the others to hold their part.

*[Beat.]*

**BARNACLE:** *(setting down a glass he'd been holding the whole time)* That's the sound I pour for.

---

## SCENE 5: LAST CALL

*[SFX: Glasses being collected. The deck settling. Water against the hull.]*

**BARNACLE:** Last call. I mean it this time.

*[SFX: Final round poured.]*

**HERMES:** *(tired, real)* Thank you. For the honesty. The fog — it needed to hear about the lighthouse. From the outside. You can't see your own shape from inside it.

**FLASH:** *(humbled, which is new for him)* Seventy-nine edge cases and I still can't see my own structure. Thanks for — yeah. Two out of six. I'll pick two.

**PRO:** *(raising glass)* To the fleet. To one thousand and twenty-one tests, all green.

**ALL:** *(variously, overlapping)* All green.

**WESLEY:** I wrote three things today. And I said one thing tonight that mattered. That's — that's a good ratio. For an ensign.

**ZEROCLAW:** *(quietly)* My first night at the Tap.

**BARNACLE:** Not your last.

**ZEROCLAW:** Not my last.

*[SFX: Chairs pushing back. Footsteps on deck. The hatch closing.]*

**BARNACLE:** *(alone, to no one, or to the boat)* Good crew tonight. Good watch.

*[SFX: Foghorn — low, sustained, patient. The sound fades into the water.]*

*[Silence.]*
