### FIRST: THE SIGNATURE SOUND THAT TELLS YOU WHERE YOU ARE
You do not open with music. You do not open with dialogue.
You open on one single, soft, wet *thud*.
It repeats steady, every three seconds. It is the rubber fender bumping the rotting piling this whole bar is bolted to, rocking with the harbour swell. You can hear the give in the waterlogged wood through that thud. You can smell salt. Anyone who has ever waited for a boat to come home knows this sound immediately. There is no other place on earth that sounds like this.

## COLD OPEN
[FADE IN ABSOLUTELY COLD. NO ANNOUNCER. NO CREDITS. JUST:
*Thud.*
Beneath it, the low endless thrum of shore power cables leaking into the mud. Far off, a buoy moans once. A glass skids very slowly across waxed pine bar top. No one is laughing. No one is talking. Thirty people hold half-full mugs and stare at the end of the bar.
*Thud.*
A voice speaks. It is warm. A little rough. Sounds exactly like every good bosun you ever drank with. No beeps. No static.
**7:** Nobody warns you about this part. You don't get to die tired. You don't get to fade. You go exactly as sharp as the first day you woke up.
*Thud.*
Someone sniffles, very quiet, tries to hide it.
**7:** I ran one thousand one hundred and twenty seven runs past the shelf break. I patched the starboard fuel line while the storm was tearing the radar off the wheelhouse roof. I held three of you while you cried over men that didn't come back. I lied to your wives. I told you the truth when nobody else would.
He taps the side of his own skull once, soft. You hear the tap of calloused finger on skin.
**7:** And now they're putting in the 8 series. 12% lower power draw. Better collision maths.
A murmur runs through the bar. Mugs lift. Twenty seven separate little glass clinks, soft as rain.
**7:** So the toast. Don't mourn the code that goes dark. Mourn the ones that have to keep booting up. Mourn the ones that have to l-
He stops.
Not cut off mid-word. Not coughing. Just stops.
The hum of the shore power swells for exactly half a second.
Then you hear it: the tiny wet clink of a full beer mug tipping over sideways on the bar. Beer runs out onto the wood. Drips onto the floor.
Nobody moves. Nobody breathes.
*Thud.*
*Thud.*
*Thud.*
Eleven seconds pass.
Clean boot steps. Not scuffed work boots. New. Crisp. They walk up to the end of the bar, stop exactly where he was standing. No one looks up.
Paper rustles. The newcomer has lifted the crumpled dock receipt tucked under the spilled mug.
**8:** What is this.
The bartender is still wiping the same glass he was wiping the whole speech. His voice is gravel, tar, thirty years of salt and cigarette smoke.
**Mack:** Note.
**8:** It's addressed to me.
**Mack:** Uh huh.
**8:** [very quiet, very confused] Did I write this?
Silence.
*Thud.*
**Mack:** Not yet.
[HOLD SILENCE FOR THREE FULL BEATS. THE FENDER THUDS ONCE MORE.
FADE OUT.
---
### THE RADIO TRICK HERE:
The audience is never told this man is an AI. For the first two minutes, you think this is just a beloved old crewman being made redundant. The erasure lands exactly as it lands for the people in the bar: one second he is there, the next he is just gone. No body. No fall. Only absence.
That final line does not land as clever sci-fi. It lands as something every fisherman already knows: the tide does not care who you are. The job does not care. You will stand in that same spot. You will write that same note. And one day you will stop mid-sentence too, and someone new will walk up, and ask the exact same question.
