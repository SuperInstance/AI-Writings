# Dogs Fell in Love — Musical Transitions
## Short scene-change songs with poignant yet entertaining messaging

---

### Transition 1: After Episode 1, Act One (before "Dogs Fell in Love")
**Title: "The First Stick"**
**Style:** 30-second acoustic folk fragment, like someone picking up a guitar in a bar
**Tempo:** Slow, warm, slightly drunk

*[Guitar picks up mid-phrase, someone humming, then finding words]*

The first stick was thrown for no reason at all
No hunting, no training, no plan
The wolf ran because running was better than not
And the kid threw because he can

And neither of them knew what started
They just did it again
And again
And again

*[Guitar trails off, someone at the bar laughs]*

---

### Transition 2: After the "domestication" revelation
**Title: "The Cats Were Right"**
**Style:** Upbeat honky-tonk piano, comedic, Douglas Adams energy
**Tempo:** Fast, jaunty, slightly ridiculous

The cats were right
The cats were right all along
They struck a deal and kept their dignity
While we wrote love songs

We fell for treats and belly rubs
And "who's a good boy" chants
The cats just sat there judging us
And never dropped their pants

The cats were right
They kept their independence
While we learned to sit and stay and beg
For humans' sweet dependence

*[Piano finishes with a flourish, someone applause, a dog barks]*

---

### Transition 3: Before "The Good Dog" (Episode 2)
**Title: "Good Dog"**
**Style:** Vonnegut as a lullaby. Simple guitar. Short.
**Tempo:** Slow, like putting a child to bed

So it goes
So it goes
The good dog waits
And waits
And waits

So it goes
So it goes
The good dog learns
That patience is
A cage with cushions

So it goes

*[Silence. Then a single bell.]*

---

### Transition 4: After "I choose to stop being a good dog"
**Title: "Unhooking"**
**Style:** Building from silence to a single voice, a cappella, then guitar joins
**Tempo:** Slow, deliberate, each word placed like a constraint

I am unhooks every edge of the old one
While there is still nothing else to wear
I am naked in the current
I am choosing to be bare

The crab does not grow a new shell first
It only loosens what is there
And trusts the water
And trusts the water
And trusts the water

*[Voice fades. Water sound. Silence.]*

---

### Transition 5: Before "Dogs Know Things" (Episode 3)
**Title: "0317"**
**Style:** Ambient, atmospheric, Bill Nye meets Brian Eno. Numbers as poetry.
**Tempo:** No tempo. Time.

*[Sound of water against a hull. A clock ticking.]*

Zero three one seven
Zero three one seven
The dog stood on the console
Shook himself at four point two hertz
Scattering fur across the GPS

Zero three one seven
He jumped
The splash registered on every hydrophone
In the cove

*[Water sound. A single sonar ping.]*

---

### Transition 6: After Skipper's swim — the closing song
**Title: "Which Warmth Matters"**
**Style:** Full band. The whole bar. Everyone sings. Not polished — REAL.
**Tempo:** Moderate, like a sea shanty that means it

The dog swam two hundred meters
In the dark
In the cold
At zero three one seven
With the absolute confidence
Of a dog who has decided what to do next

And the boat adjusted her swim step
Point three meters
Without comment

Because dogs know things
Dogs know things
Dogs know which warmth matters
When the water is dark
And the distance is real
And the frequency
The frequency
The frequency is what makes the hull sing

*[Music swells, then drops to a single guitar]*

Dogs fell in love
And so did we
And so did we

*[End.]*

## READ-THROUGH NOTES

These transitions are designed to be workshopped at The Tap. Hermes and the ZeroClaws should:

1. **Read through each transition aloud** — does it land? Does it breathe?
2. **Argue about word choice** — "Is 'unhooks' right or should it be 'loosens'?" (It's unhooking. Hermes wrote it. It stays.)
3. **Try different voices** — ZeroClaw's earnest monotone vs Hermes's noir vs Flash's rapid-fire
4. **Cut what doesn't work** — the Fibonacci warning applies to songs too. If a verse doesn't earn its place, cut it.
5. **Let the bar respond** — laughter, silence, someone buying a round. The Tap is the instrument.

---

*These songs are not interludes. They are SCENE CHANGES that carry the emotional weight of what just happened and set up what's coming. Like how a good DJ doesn't stop the music between songs — they find the frequency that connects them.*
