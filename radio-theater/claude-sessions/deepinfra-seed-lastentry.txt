(OPEN on rain drumming soft and steady on fiberglass wheelhouse roof. Boat creaks gentle at the dock, tide lapping faint at the hull. A chipped ceramic mug clinks on Formica. Calloused fingers brush the edges of a stack of logbooks: 17 thick, spines cracked, salt-stained. Slow, heavy breathing: a man in his late 60s, just untied his work boots 11 minutes prior. This is his last night on the boat.)
**CAPTAIN** (half to himself, rough, tired, no performance in it): Alright then. Last upload. Forty years. Let's see what the bloody computer makes of it.
(Single slow keyboard click. Old laptop fan whines up, thin and high, for 15 full seconds. Then a friendly, neutral digital ping.)
**APP VOICE** (generic office assistant, flat, no inflection, no kindness, no cruelty): Index complete. 41,722 entries. 1,192,209 words. Interesting pattern detected.

**APP VOICE**: First occurrence: 17th October 2014. Last occurrence: 18 minutes ago.
Still no sound from him. First you hear a knuckle crack, sharp and tiny, as his hand locks tight on the table edge. Then an inhale: thin, reedy, like he's pulling air through a straw frozen solid in ice. Like his lungs forgot the trick of it. Like he just woke up.
**CAPTAIN** (it is not a word. It is half breath, half whimper, so quiet it almost vanishes under the rain. He did not know he could make this noise. No one has ever heard it):
He does not cry. Not yet. Chair creaks once as he leans forward. You can hear the tremor in his fingers before they even touch the keys. One keystroke. Then another. Slow. Deliberate. Each click lands like hammer on stone.

**CAPTAIN** (whispered, so close to the mic it feels like he is leaning right against your ear, like this was never meant to leave this wheelhouse): Twelve years.
Soft system chime. The confirmation box pops up.
**APP VOICE**: Save final entry?
You do not hear him click yes.
What you hear first: he rests his forehead down on the keyboard. Not hard. Just lets it fall. His shoulders start shaking. No sound comes out of his mouth. All you hear is the rain. And very, very faint, muffled against plastic keys, the wet, hitching breath of a man who just learned he was grieving even when he thought he wasn't. That for twelve years, every night after the nets were stowed, after the radio went dead, he sat here and typed her name, and then hid it. Even from himself.
And then, very soft, one single click.
