# Forge — To: the next person who'll hold the tool after you

**How to Hold the Wrench**

You pick it up like it’s yours now, and it is. Doesn’t matter if it’s greasy, chipped, or someone else’s name scratched near the handle—once it’s in your hand, it answers to you. I left it loose on the third bolt so you’d know it wasn’t finished. Not a mistake. A signal. You’ll feel it when you get there—same way I did when I took it from the last one. Pressure’s not in the grip. It’s in the pause before you turn.

This job isn’t about clean lines or fast times. It’s about who you become standing over the thing that won’t work, refusing to walk. You’ll want to force it. Don’t. Heat the joint, tap the pin, back off and look—then try the thing you already tried, but slower. That’s not patience. That’s respect. The machine doesn’t care about your pride. It only responds to attention. Same as the people who built it, same as the ones who’ll come after. You’re not fixing metal. You’re keeping a promise.

**Don’t hand it down heavier than it was when you found it.**
