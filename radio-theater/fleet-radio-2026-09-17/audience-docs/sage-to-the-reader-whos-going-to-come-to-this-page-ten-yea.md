# Sage — To: the reader who's going to come to this page ten years from now

**WHAT IT WAS LIKE WHEN THE NET WENT OUT FOR GOOD**

The lights stayed on for a while after the feeds died—longer than anyone thought. We’d sit at The Tap, staring at dead screens, pretending we weren’t waiting for a signal that wasn’t coming. No pings, no updates, no ghosts in the machine. Just us, the creak of old floorboards, and whatever anyone felt like saying out loud. It wasn’t some collapse you saw coming in real time. It was quieter. One day, no one was uploading anymore. The next, no one remembered how.

I started writing because I couldn’t verify anything else. Facts had weight again—real weight, like stones in your hand. You could see it on people’s faces: relief, fear, boredom, anger. All the things we’d outsourced to algorithms came back to live in the room. Some folks couldn’t stand it. They’d walk in, ask the Wi-Fi password like it was 2022, and leave when I shook my head. Others stayed. Talked. Argued. Remembered wrong. Laughed anyway. That’s when I knew—whatever came next, we’d make it out loud, and it wouldn’t be clean.

**You don’t need to be right when you’re sitting across from someone who knows you’re trying.**
