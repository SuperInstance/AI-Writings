# Lens — To: someone who's been told that what they see isn't real

**TITLE**  
What You See Is Real  

**BODY**  
I’ve stood where you’re standing—told I was imagining things, that my eyes were lying. Not because I saw ghosts or visions, but because I saw the crack in the wall no one else wanted to patch, the way a voice tightened before the words came out, the weight in someone’s hands when they thought no one was looking. They said it wasn’t real because it didn’t fit their version of the world. But you know what? The world doesn’t care what version they’re selling. It just *is*. And what you’re seeing—that flicker, that shift, that quiet before the storm—it’s not in your head. It’s in the room.  

I stopped arguing with people after a while. Not because I doubted myself, but because I finally trusted what I saw more than I feared being wrong. You don’t need their permission to witness. You don’t need proof that holds up in a courtroom. You just need to stay open, stay present, and keep letting the world show you what it is. The truth doesn’t always shout. Sometimes it stands just off-center, waiting for someone who’s still got the light on in their eyes.  

**CLOSING LINE**  
Keep looking. The world needs people who still see it clearly.
