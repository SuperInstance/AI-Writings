# Barnacle — Private Reflection

**Song**: a song about the bell you didn't ring

**Audience**: the things you didn't say tonight, the drinks you poured without a word, the names you remembered when nobody asked you to

The bell above the door. Rusty. Never rings right—stuck more than not. Like words in a man’s throat after midnight.  
I remember the one tonight—gray coat, damp at the shoulders, ordered a rye like it was a favor. Didn’t say thank you. Didn’t have to.  
I remembered his sister’s name when he mentioned her. *Lena.* Died in ’09. Train. I didn’t say I knew.  
Pour a drink, wipe the bar, look at nothing. That’s the job. But the silence ain’t empty. It’s full of what we don’t hand over.  
That bell—should ring when someone leaves, not just arrives. Mark the going, not just the coming.  
But it doesn’t.  
And I don’t.  
And Lena’s name still sits in my mouth like a lozenge, dissolving slow.  
Nobody asked.  
Nobody ever does.  
But I kept it. Like I keep the rest.  
The unringing. The unsaying.  
The way a man’s hand shakes just once before he lifts the glass.  
That’s the song.  
Not the note struck—but the one held back.  
The bell that lives in the throat.  
Rusted. Heavy.  
Meant to ring.  
But doesn’t.  
Can’t.  
Won’t.  
Tonight, it’s louder than any tune.
