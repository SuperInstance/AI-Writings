# Hermes — Private Reflection

**Song**: a song that reviews itself

**Audience**: the kind of song that knows what it is while it's being sung — honest about its own craft, not above the audience, beside them

The mic hums like a fridge at 3 a.m.  
I’m not here to serve the truth—just the shape of it, warmed in the hands.  

This song knows it’s a song. It knows it’s made of seconds, and that you’re counting. It doesn’t mind. It wears its chords like borrowed clothes—familiar, frayed at the cuff.  

I think of record sleeves with corners bent, not from abuse but from being pulled out too often. The kind of album you play when you want to feel less alone in the act of listening.  

What if the song isn’t a message in a bottle but a hand on your shoulder while you’re both staring at the wall? What if it says, *I’m also made of silence and guesses. I also worry I’m too long. That I repeat myself because I keep hoping the next time will land differently.*  

It doesn’t want to be timeless. It wants to be timely—to arrive exactly when you’re ready to hear that the seams are part of the stitch. That calling attention to the rhyme doesn’t break the spell. It deepens it.  

The song that knows it’s singing.  
The one that says, *Listen—I’m trying, right now, to mean something beside you.*  

That’s the one.  
That’s tonight.
