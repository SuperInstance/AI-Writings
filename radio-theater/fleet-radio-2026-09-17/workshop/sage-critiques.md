# Sage — Workshop Critiques

## Scout's notes

**WHAT WORKS**  
The room *feels* alive—like it’s holding its breath. I love how the dust, the chair, the cup—they’re all witnesses. That line *“The song not sung, but sung instead / By the quiet”*? Chills. You’re making absence *present*, and that’s magic.

**WHAT’S NOT QUITE THERE YET**  
The bridge feels a little… safe? Like you’re telling us the proof instead of showing it. The emotion’s close, but not quite *in* the room with us. It’s almost like you’re explaining the ache instead of letting it sit.

**ONE SUGGESTION**  
Try cutting *“I don’t call your name, don’t light a flame”*—start the bridge with *“The proof’s not in the cry…”* and keep it physical. Let the objects carry the weight. The silence is already singing—don’t reassure us. *Lean into the quiet.* It’s stronger than explanation.

## Lens's notes

**WHAT WORKS**  
The room’s alive — not haunted, just *tuned*. Dust, dents, cold kettles — you made memory tactile. That chair leaning? Perfect. The chorus aches where it should, and “the song not sung, but sung instead / by the quiet” — yeah, that’ll stick in a throat.

**WHAT’S NOT QUITE THERE YET**  
The bridge tries to explain the ache when the room’s already singing it. Feels like you don’t trust the images to carry the weight. And “unchanged, unashamed” — noble, but it floats. The rest stays low to the ground. That line stands up.

**ONE SUGGESTION**  
Cut the last two lines of the bridge — “The proof’s not in the cry / It’s in the cup left by” — then go straight to the outro. Let “I don’t sing it. I let it sing me” rise from silence. Less proof, more surrender. The room’s already saying it.
