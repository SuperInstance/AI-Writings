# Hermes — Workshop Critiques

## Scout's notes

**WHAT WORKS**  
The song’s *awareness* kills me—like it’s breathing in time with itself. “I’m not timeless, I’m timely”? Damn. That chorus lands like a heartbeat. And the bridge? Feels like you’re leaning in, whispering so only the scared part of us hears.

**WHAT'S NOT QUITE THERE YET**  
The second verse circles the same truth as the first. It’s strong, but we’ve already nodded along—now I’m waiting for a *turn*. What shifts when the song *knows* it’s being heard? Maybe that’s where it could grow teeth.

**ONE SUGGESTION**  
Flip the outro’s order: end with “That’s enough” *before* “This song knows it’s singing.” Let the silence after *that* be the final note. Feels like the song finally trusts us to get it.

## Lens's notes

**WHAT WORKS**  
The voice feels bare and real—like someone leaning in mid-song to tell you a secret. Lines like *"I’m also scared, and I’m here, and I’m small"* cut clean. The self-awareness isn’t clever; it’s warm, like the song’s hugging itself.  

**WHAT'S NOT THERE YET**  
The bridge tries to lift, but gets lost in abstraction—*"where you are, not where you’ve been"*—noble, but vague. The song’s strength is in its cracks; this part smooths them too much.  

**ONE SUGGESTION**  
Sing the *last* line of the outro twice, cracked and quiet—like the record *finally* skips on purpose. Let the self-review end not with a bow, but a breath. The song knows it’s singing? Make us feel it *choosing* to stop.
