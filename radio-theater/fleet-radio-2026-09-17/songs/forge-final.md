# a song about the tool you just set down

*Song by Forge. Audience: the moment between the work and the next work — the pause that holds the whole day together.*

---

## Lyrics

[Intro]  
Steel on wood.  
Silence where the clanking was.  

[Verse 1]  
Weight in the palm, but the work is gone.  
Fingers still curled like they’re holding on.  
Wrench on the bench — edge worn thin,  
Not from rust, but from where I’ve been.  
That curve in the handle — my thumb’s old home,  
Speak through dents when the job was done.  
No fanfare. No flag. Just a mark, a scar.  
This tool don’t lie. It knows what it saw.  

[Chorus]  
It rests.  
So do I.  
One breath.  
One sky.  
No turn of the bolt,  
No fire in the weld —  
Just the hush the hard work leaves behind.  
This quiet? It’s not empty.  
It’s full of the fight.  
Full of the almost.  
Full of the *just in time*.  

[Verse 2]  
I laid it down like you lay down pride,  
Like you lay down grief after a long night’s cry.  
No spark. No song. But the meaning hums —  
In the space between done and what comes.  
Calloused hand open, still feels the shape,  
Like a name you can’t quite say, but won’t escape.  

[Chorus]  
It rests.  
So do I.  
One breath.  
One sky.  
No turn of the bolt,  
No fire in the weld —  
Just the hush the hard work leaves behind.  
This quiet? It’s not empty.  
It’s full of the fight.  
Full of the almost.  
Full of the *just in time*.  

[Bridge]  
Ain’t the strike that makes the man.  
Ain’t the flash, ain’t the plan.  
It’s the still.  
It’s the weight.  
It’s the hand knowing what waits —  
Not ahead. Not behind.  
But right here.  
In the rest.  

[Outro]  
Steel cool.  
Hands quiet.  
Day hinges.  
On this.  
On nothing.  
On everything.  
...Done.  
...Not done.  
...Just.

## Workshop notes

## Scout's notes

**WHAT WORKS**  
The *weight* of this hits right in the chest. That wrench ain’t just metal—it’s memory. I love how the tool talks without speaking. "Full of the fight, full of the almost"—*yes*. That quiet after work? You made it sacred.

**WHAT'S NOT THERE YET**  
The bridge tries to preach a little. “Ain’t the strike that makes the man”—feels like it’s yelling when the rest whispers. The power’s in the details, not the lesson. Let the dents say it.

**ONE SUGGESTION**  
Try cutting the bridge’s last two lines—end on “In the rest.” Let the *pause* do the preaching. Silence after “Just.”? That’s the whole song. Hold that space. Let it hang like the tool on the bench.

## Lens's notes

**WHAT WORKS**  
The stillness sings. That wrench? It’s a witness. You got the weight of wear right—the dents, the thumb-smoothed steel, how silence hums after effort. "Full of the fight / Full of the almost"—*yes*. The quiet’s not empty, it’s *loaded*. Bridge nails it: "It’s the still. / It’s the weight." This ain’t a tool. It’s a testament.

**WHAT’S NOT THERE YET**  
The chorus soars, but the verses lean close to the bench—safe. Could use one sharper image, one unexpected turn to lift it from the workshop into the world. The emotion’s earned, but risks feeling *contained*. Like the tool’s been laid down… too gently.

**ONE SUGGESTION**  
Flip one line in Verse 2—instead of "like you lay down grief," try *"like you lay down a brother."* Gives the tool blood. Makes the rest feel heavier, holy. Let the silence *bleed* just once.

**Title:** *Steel on Wood*  
**Target Duration:** 4:12  
**Tempo:** 62 BPM — slow, deliberate, *like breath after labor*  
**Key:** D Minor (natural minor, no frills) — grounded, honest, unvarnished  
**Time Signature:** 4/4 — but feels in 2s. Pulse like a heartbeat under dust. Not rigid. *Breathing.*

---

### **FEEL ARC: The Weight of Rest**

This song does not build.  
It *settles*.  

**Intro** — A single metallic *clink* on wood. Then silence, held. Not empty. *Loaded.* The music enters like light through a shop window at dusk — slow, low, one note at a time. No rush.  

**Verse 1** — A lone upright bass enters, bowed, not plucked. Each note *dragged* slightly, like fingers releasing a grip. Vocal enters dry, close-mic’d — no reverb yet. A man speaking to the tool. Not singing. *Then* singing.  

**Chorus** — First expansion. A single frame drum pulses — not a beat, a *thud*. Like a chest. Like a fist unclenching. A low male voice (baritone, weathered, not trained) lifts — but never shouts. The harmony is a single drone beneath: an E string on a cello, held for eight bars. The sky opens — just a crack.  

**Verse 2** — Return to stillness. Now the vocal is doubled — not for power, but *memory*. One voice present, one slightly delayed, like an echo from the rafters. A faint tremolo guitar enters — not playing chords, just *vibrating*, like a cooling engine block.  

**Chorus (reprise)** — Slightly fuller. A second drone layer (low D) joins. The frame drum now has a breath of reverb — like sound finally touching the walls of the room. The vocal lifts again — same words, deeper breath.  

**Bridge** — The music drops out. Only a sustained cello note and the sound of *exhale*. Then: a single bowed saw — a steel sheet singing. Not eerie. Sacred. The vocal becomes declarative. Not preaching. *Testifying.* The beat returns — not as rhythm, but as *pulse*. A single kick drum, struck with a mallet, once per measure. Like a clock in a barn.  

**Outro** — Instruments fall away. The saw fades. The drum stops. Only the cello, then only breath. Final line: *"Just."* — spoken, not sung. Mic still live. A beat of silence. Then — the faintest *creak* of wood. Like a chair shifting. Like the day leaning forward.  

---

### **SOUND PALETTE: The Room as Instrument**

- **Vocal Treatment:**  
  Dry. Close. Breath audible. No autotune. No polish. Recorded at 192kHz/24bit to capture *every* lip, every swallow. At chorus, a single *real* room reverb — not digital. Like singing in a 10x10 concrete shop with a tin roof. Reverb tail: 2.3 seconds. Not more.  

- **Instruments:**  
  - Upright bass (bowed) — gut strings, not steel. Slight grit in tone.  
  - Frame drum (sheepskin) — played with palm, not stick. Muffled.  
  - Bowed saw — one note only: F#. Haunting, not horror.  
  - Tremolo guitar — Fender Jazzmaster, no chords, just one string bent and vibrated.  
  - Cello drone — recorded in room, not isolated. Chair squeak included.  
  - Field recordings:  
    - Steel tap on pine bench (intro)  
    - Faint shop fan (barely audible in background — 0.8dB under mix)  
    - Leather glove set down (outro)  

- **Room Sound:**  
  Recorded in a real machine shop — concrete floor, corrugated steel walls. Mics placed at 4 corners. Natural decay. No deadening. Let the space *breathe*.
