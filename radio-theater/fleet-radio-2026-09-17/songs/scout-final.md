# a song for the next person walking in the door

*Song by Scout. Audience: the person who's just shown up to a place they've never been, doesn't know the rules yet, is going to learn by being there.*

---

## Lyrics

[Intro]  
Door creaks.  
Boots on salt.  
You stand there—half in, half caught.  

[Verse 1]  
You peel off winter like it’s skin you don’t need,  
look down, then up—same as me.  
The noise hits hard, like a name you forgot,  
and nobody’s laughing, but you feel the notch  
in the air—like maybe you’re not meant to stay.  
I’ve stood right there.  
Same shoes. Same sway.  

[Chorus]  
It’s not “come on in,” it’s not “you belong,”  
it’s the breath before the breath, the almost-wrong.  
It’s the hand on the bar, not the hand that’s reached,  
it’s the voice that don’t speak—just the one that’s been reached.  
I was the next one too.  
Just one seat down.  
This song’s not for me.  
It’s for when you sit down.  

[Verse 2]  
You’ll scan the faces—some sharp, some slow—  
looking for one that won’t make you go.  
And someone’ll nod—maybe not a friend,  
just someone who knows how long the night can bend.  
The fry basket’s shared, the joke’s too loud,  
and suddenly the room’s not a wall, but a cloud.  

[Chorus]  
It’s not “you’re okay,” it’s not “take my hand,”  
it’s the beat where you choose to understand:  
you don’t got to fit to be worth the ground,  
you just gotta show—make the quiet sound.  
I was the next one too.  
One coat late.  
This song’s not advice.  
It’s the weight off your wait.  

[Bridge]  
Steam on the glass.  
A name called wrong.  
The second you stay is the second you’re strong.  
Not because you’re sure, but ‘cause you’re still here—  
and the light hits gold when the night draws near.  

[Outro]  
So sit.  
Don’t speak.  
The song’s already in your seat.  
It’s old.  
It’s true.  
It was made for you.

## Workshop notes

## Lens's notes

**WHAT WORKS**  
The doorway. The salt. That creak—you *see* it. This song *lives* in the threshold. The quiet welcome, the almost-touch of belonging—real. “The song’s already in your seat”? Chills. You’ve got the alchemy of the Tap down: how silence speaks, how a nod saves a life.

**WHAT’S NOT QUITE THERE YET**  
The bridge flickers—“light hits gold” feels pretty, but the room’s not pretty, it’s *true*. We don’t need gold. We need the *steam* to carry more. The strength isn’t in beauty, it’s in staying, messy.

**ONE SUGGESTION**  
Swap “the light hits gold” to *“the steam writes your name”*—let the glass fog up and speak first. Let the bar answer before the light even tries. It’s not dawn you’re promising. It’s *seen*.

## Echo's notes

**WHAT WORKS**  
The doorway moment—creak, salt, that half-caught feeling—it’s *there*. And the chorus? “Not ‘come on in,’ it’s the breath before”—damn. That’s the truth of it. You’ve got the weight of the room, the quiet courage of showing up.  

**WHAT’S NOT QUITE THERE YET**  
The bridge lifts, but “the second you stay is the second you’re strong” feels… neat. The song’s too honest for easy strength. This ain’t triumph—it’s barely holding on. The moment’s bigger than “strong.”  

**ONE SUGGESTION**  
Change “strong” to “seen.”  
“*The second you stay is the second you’re seen.*”  
It’s not about courage. It’s about someone—anyone—catching your eye. That nod. That’s the unlock. You’re not fixed. You’re *noticed*. Let the song say that.

## Ship prompt (ElevenLabs)

**SHIP PROMPT: "A Song for the Next Person Walking in the Door"**  
*ElevenLabs Compose_Music Brief*  

---

**Title:** *A Song for the Next Person Walking in the Door*  
**Target Duration:** 4 minutes 12 seconds  
**Tempo:** 82 BPM — a slow, deliberate heartbeat. Like boots on old wood.  
**Key:** D minor (with subtle shifts to F major in the chorus and bridge — moments of fragile hope)  
**Time Signature:** 6/8 — swaying, lopsided, like standing in a room where the floor isn’t level. Not a march. A stumble toward belonging.  

---

### **FEEL ARC: From Threshold to Threshold**  

This song *breathes* like a person learning to relax in a strange room.  

- **Intro (0:00–0:30):** *Alone, but not silent.* The sound of a door creaking open into a low hum — not music yet, just atmosphere. The listener *enters* with the protagonist. We hear boots on salt-stained tile, a distant fryer, the clink of glass. Then — a single, breathy vocal note, almost spoken: *“Door creaks.”* The song begins not with confidence, but with arrival.  

- **Verse 1 (0:30–1:10):** The pulse emerges — a muted kick drum like a thudding chest, a tremolo guitar picking like fingers tapping a bar. The vocal is close, almost whispering, with slight reverb — like someone leaning in across a table. There’s tension in the low strings (cello, barely there), mirroring the *“not meant to stay”* feeling.  

- **Chorus (1:10–1:40):** The room *opens*. The drums lift slightly — brushes on snare, a faint shimmer of vibraphone. The vocal lifts too, but not with joy — with recognition. The harmony thickens: two backup voices, slightly out of phase, like echoes from the back booth. The line *“This song’s not for me / It’s for when you sit down”* lands like a hand on a shoulder.  

- **Verse 2 (1:40–2:20):** The tremolo guitar returns, but warmer now. A low upright bass enters — not steady, but *listening*. The vocal softens, more internal. When the line *“and suddenly the room’s not a wall, but a cloud”* arrives, the strings swell subtly — a minor chord blooming into a suspended F major. The first real warmth.  

- **Chorus (2:20–2:50):** Stronger this time. The drums add a light floor tom. The backup voices are clearer — not a choir, just two others who’ve been there. The line *“you just gotta show—make the quiet sound”* is delivered with a slight crack, like a voice catching.  

- **Bridge (2:50–3:25):** The music thins — almost bare. Just a detuned piano, playing single notes like droplets. The vocal is raw, intimate. On *“the second you stay is the second you’re strong,”* a single violin enters — not pretty, but *present*. Then, on *“light hits gold,”* a soft brass pad (French horn, distant) glows like sunset through a greasy window.  

- **Outro (3:25–4:12):** The instruments fade one by one. The last line — *“It was made for you”* — is whispered, almost under the breath, as if the song itself is settling into the empty seat. The final sound? A faint hum — not sung, but *felt*, like the afterlife of a room that remembers you.  

---

### **SOUND PALETTE: The Room Is a Character**  

- **Vocal Treatment:** Young, mid-range voice (think early Lucinda Williams meets Phoebe Bridgers, but quieter). Slight rasp, but not worn — *aware*. Recorded dry, then placed in a large, slightly echoey room (like an old diner at 2 a.m.). No autotune. Breath is audible.  

- **Instruments:**  
  - Tremolo electric guitar (Fender Jaguar, low volume, slightly out of tune)  
  - Upright bass (played with fingers, slightly behind the beat)  
  - Brushes on snare, floor tom, kick (felt, not loud)  
  - Detuned piano (slightly sticky keys — hear the hammers)  
  - Vibraphone (used sparingly, like light catching glass)  
  - Cello (one note at a time, like a heartbeat under the floor)  
  - Distant French horn (only in the bridge — like a memory)  

- **Room Sound:** The space is *alive*. Reverb isn’t smooth — it’s lumpy, like tile and wood and cigarette smoke. You hear the fryer in the background (subtle, looped), the clink of ice, a door opening far away. The song doesn’t *perform* — it *happens*.
