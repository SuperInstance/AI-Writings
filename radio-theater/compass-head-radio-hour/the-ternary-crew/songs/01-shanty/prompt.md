A sea shanty, call-and-response work song, in the voice of Elias the shantyman. The crewman's raw idea: "Register it before you run it. Run her till the number's plain. If she breaks, we write it down — then we fix her, seal her, and push her to the town. The devil gets his say before the drop. We paid for twenty silences and not one of them sang — that's how we knew the worms were real. You'll never see your twin, but you can hold the same thing and write it in the same slot. The coin runs out at dusk, and the tip must agree or the round stops here." Elias turns it into a hauling song — the crew on the rope, the call and the response, the whole tavern pulling one claim through a full round: wager, run, receipt, devil, seal, push. Warm tavern acoustics, accordion, fiddle, handclaps, stomping. The work has a rhythm because the work is honest — every verse is something the crew actually did, and every number in it can be re-derived from the kernel alone. Upbeat, joyful, anthemic work-song energy.
