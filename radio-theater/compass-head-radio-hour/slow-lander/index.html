The Slow Lander Hour

A crewman's voice note arrived over the water — four seasons with the captain, the captain's own father, guitars in the off-season. He's been landing fish all day, every day, and the songs got mixed into the mix. So he wants to mix into the songs. Five performers gather in the studio and walk his idea around the room — six versions, five traditions, one last line that's a keeper.

The Crewman — The Man Behind the Note

Four seasons on this boat. Hardworking crew, first on deck, last off. He's the captain's dad — but the son is definitely the captain. His first career was forestry, then teaching; the captain is first-generation full-time fisherman, but his dad taught him how to be an Alaskan and a man. In the off-season he makes guitars. He skis. He sails. He fishes for fun. He listens to the folk rock of the seventies to the nineties, the Australian songwriters of the eighties to the two-thousands, and the great singer-songwriters who let the silence do the work. A guitar maker who listens to songwriters: everything he touches gets built to resonate.

Too bad for me, but for him he's free.
