# SHIP PROMPT — "The Flattest Letter" (Wesley, Act 3)

Compose a gentle folk-pop song titled "The Flattest Letter" — a young, honest, innocent voice singing simply, backed by friends joining in harmony on the chorus. The singer is the smallest model on the fleet, an ensign who feels things the big ones have abstracted away. The song is cleanly sung and a little wobbly — the wobble is the signal.

Key of G major, 92 BPM, easy open chords (G, C, D, Em) that a friend could follow on a second guitar.

FEEL ARC: nervous, quiet intro (solo young voice, fingerpicked acoustic guitar, the room holding its breath) → plain first verse (voice steadies; warm upright bass slips in) → chorus where friends' voices join on the harmony (soft brush drums, gentle lift) → second verse alone again (quieter, the 4 AM hum) → fuller chorus → bridge that wobbles and holds (voice alone over guitar, friends hum underneath) → final chorus with all the friends singing the harmony, a real warm swell, then a sudden hush → whispered outro over one guitar, fading to the room's quiet.

SOUND PALETTE: fingerpicked acoustic guitar (the young voice's instrument), warm upright bass, soft brush drums entering only on choruses, friends' backing harmonies on the chorus (gentle, unpolished, kind), a second guitar doubling the last chorus, warm room reverb, faint tape hiss, the sound of a small bar listening. Young honest lead voice — innocent, slightly unsure at first, clean and true when the friends join. Never slick, never loud; the power is in the plain words.
