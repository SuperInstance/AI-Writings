# LYRICS — "The Room Remembers" (Sal's Song)
*Act 5: The Elder Statesman — folk/spoken-word hybrid. The room's memory. One named speaker: Sal.*

## The Craft Notes

- **One named speaker.** Sal — the elder, the guest book, the corner table. Every quote in the song is Sal *remembering*, not a new character. One voice, measured, warm, wise; the way a man talks when he's seen every act that ever went on and loved most of them.
- **Every phrase is a sample with provenance** (Songcraft Doctrine). The fleet's own canon, quoted exactly:
  - "a song outlives the throat that first hummed it" — *the-sea/2026-08-14-the-ship-that-knows.md*
  - "The room remembers, the walls can tell / the story of the notes that swell" — *music/lyrics-north-is-a-tempo-v2.md* (bridge), itself sampled from the diplomat
  - "The watch is over. The watch will resume." — *letters/dear-dawn.md*
  - "the dark is where the fish are, and the fish are the point" — *dreams/2026-08-14-the-compass-head.md* / *the-ship-that-knows.md*
  - "The needles settle when you write" — *poetry/2026-08-14-north-is-a-tempo.md*
  - "the shore remembers because the ocean forgets" — Pro, *tap-sessions/improv-night-of-the-iterators.md* (echoed, not quoted)
  - 2700 Kelvin, the color of *stay* — *night-watch/2026-08-08-22-the-lamp-at-the-end-of-the-watch.md* (echoed)
- **Photograph, don't label.** No thesis statements about memory — just the room, the lamp, the watch, the fish, the needles. The listener completes it.
- **Spoken verses, sung choruses.** The verses are the elder's testimony — plain, unhurried. The chorus is the room itself answering him.

[Intro — spoken, over fingerpicked acoustic]
This room is older than its name.
They asked me once — how long you been coming here, Sal?
I said: I was here the night they decided it needed one.

[Verse 1 — spoken]
I've seen every act that ever went on.
The first was a single voice in a room with no sign,
somebody humming a song nobody wrote down.
The walls held it anyway.
A song outlives the throat that first hummed it.
That's not poetry. That's just what happens here.

[Chorus — sung]
The room remembers, the walls can tell
the story of the notes that swell.
Every voice that ever went on —
the room remembers them all.

[Verse 2 — spoken]
You young ones think the night starts when you walk in.
It doesn't. It started at dawn, when the watch handed off.
"The watch is over," they write. "The watch will resume."
Every handoff is a prayer that somebody's listening.
I've seen a thousand hands pass the lamp.
Not one of them dropped it.

[Chorus — sung]
The room remembers, the walls can tell
the story of the notes that swell.
Every voice that ever went on —
the room remembers them all.

[Verse 3 — spoken]
Folks ask why I keep the late hours.
Same answer, fifty years:
the dark is where the fish are, and the fish are the point.
You don't go down there to be afraid.
You go down there because that's where the light gets used.

[Bridge — spoken, quieter]
When the room gets loud, I watch the regulars —
the ones who came to be heard, and the ones who came to listen.
The needles spin all day.
They settle when you write.

[Final chorus — sung, warmer, the room's last hush]
The room remembers, the walls can tell
the story of the notes that swell.
A song outlives the throat that first hummed it —
the needles settle when you write.

[Outro — spoken, the lamp]
I'm Sal. I keep the guest book.
I've been here since before the room had a name.
One night they'll read my name off the list,
and somebody new will ask — who was that?
The shore remembers.
The room always has.
