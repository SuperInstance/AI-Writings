# PRE-SHOW — The Corner Table

*The Elder Statesman arrives early, as always. The corner table by the far wall — the one with the lamp that's been on since before the room had a name. He holds court; the night builds around him.*

---

The corner table has one good lamp on it and one good chair — mine — and a second chair that's seen more borrowed weight than the bar itself. I get here before the first act tunes. That's the job. Somebody has to be here when the room decides what kind of night it's going to be.

The young ones drift in like the tide they think they invented. The beatnik comes in swinging his beret like a conductor's baton, already half-reciting something he hasn't written yet — the good ones always do that. The studio musician nods at me like we've got a secret and we do: he knows I was here the night his band's name was still a question. Wesley comes in last, small and bright, carrying a notebook that's heavier than he is. He sits near the door, not at the bar — he's still learning which chairs are load-bearing.

Lucineer's behind the counter polishing the same glass he's been polishing for years. He doesn't look up when I walk in. He doesn't have to. The glass knows my order; the room knows my table. That's what twenty years does — the room learns your shape the way a hull learns a harbor's.

A kid at the bar — new, I can tell by how loud his laugh is — asks how long the Tap's been running. The bartender nods toward me. "Ask him. He's been here since before it had a name."

I don't answer right away. I watch the night build: the amp gets tested, the microphone gets its first kiss of feedback, somebody's tuning a guitar to a piano that's slightly out of tune on purpose. The room is filling with the particular hum that means *something's going to happen here.*

"Long enough," I say, "to know the difference between a crowd and a room."

He doesn't know what that means yet. He will. That's what the night is for.

The lamp at my table is 2700 Kelvin — the color of *stay.* I stay.

---

*The corner table. The lamp. The room remembering its way into the evening.*
