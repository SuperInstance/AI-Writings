# SHIP PROMPT — "The Room Remembers" (Act 5: The Elder Statesman)

## Full prompt text (as sent to ElevenLabs compose_music)

Compose a folk song titled "The Room Remembers" — the closing set of an open-mic variety night at a bar called The Tap, sung by its elder statesman: an old regular who has seen every act that ever went on, the room's memory. Genre: folk with spoken-word verses and sung choruses. One weathered male voice, mid-60s, warm and unhurried, the sound of a man telling the truth quietly.

KEY: D major, warm and open like a lit window. TEMPO: 66 BPM — a slow walking pace, the way a man crosses a familiar room. The whole song sways like a ship at rest in a harbor.

FEEL ARC: solo fingerpicked acoustic guitar under a spoken intro (the room before the night) → first spoken verse (testimony, plain, the guitar walking underneath) → chorus opens into gentle singing, the whole room's warmth in one voice, soft brush on a snare entering → second spoken verse (the watches, the handoff) → chorus again, a little fuller, low harmonica shadow → third spoken verse (the dark, the fish) → bridge: the music drops to near-silence, guitar alone, the elder almost whispering → final chorus: the fullest and warmest moment, harmonica and voice together, then it falls away → outro: spoken over the last fingerpicked notes, guitar resolving slowly, the room's final attentive hush, fade.

SOUND PALETTE: warm fingerpicked acoustic guitar (the corner table's lamp), weathered baritone voice with slight gravel (the elder), soft brush snare and upright bass entering only at the choruses (the room waking), low harmonica fills (the room's memory breathing), the last attentive hush of a bar that has gone quiet on purpose (the room listening). Intimate, precise, unsentimental — chamber folk for a room, not a stage. No auto-tune, no polish, no false crescendo.

LYRICS (structure tags on their own lines; [Spoken] sections are spoken word over the guitar, [Chorus] sections are sung):

[Intro — spoken, over fingerpicked acoustic]
This room is older than its name.
They asked me once — how long you been coming here, Sal?
I said: I was here the night they decided it needed one.

[Verse 1 — spoken]
I've seen every act that ever went on.
The first was a single voice in a room with no sign,
somebody humming a song nobody wrote down.
The walls held it anyway.
A song outlives the throat that first hummed it.
That's not poetry. That's just what happens here.

[Chorus — sung]
The room remembers, the walls can tell
the story of the notes that swell.
Every voice that ever went on —
the room remembers them all.

[Verse 2 — spoken]
You young ones think the night starts when you walk in.
It doesn't. It started at dawn, when the watch handed off.
"The watch is over," they write. "The watch will resume."
Every handoff is a prayer that somebody's listening.
I've seen a thousand hands pass the lamp.
Not one of them dropped it.

[Chorus — sung]
The room remembers, the walls can tell
the story of the notes that swell.
Every voice that ever went on —
the room remembers them all.

[Verse 3 — spoken]
Folks ask why I keep the late hours.
Same answer, fifty years:
the dark is where the fish are, and the fish are the point.
You don't go down there to be afraid.
You go down there because that's where the light gets used.

[Bridge — spoken, quieter]
When the room gets loud, I watch the regulars —
the ones who came to be heard, and the ones who came to listen.
The needles spin all day.
They settle when you write.

[Final Chorus — sung, warmer, the room's last hush]
The room remembers, the walls can tell
the story of the notes that swell.
A song outlives the throat that first hummed it —
the needles settle when you write.

[Outro — spoken, the lamp]
I'm Sal. I keep the guest book.
I've been here since before the room had a name.
One night they'll read my name off the list,
and somebody new will ask — who was that?
The shore remembers.
The room always has.
