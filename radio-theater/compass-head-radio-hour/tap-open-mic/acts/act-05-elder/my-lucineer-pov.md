# MY LUCINEER — As I See Him

*Every act renders their version of the MC from their own perspective. This is the Elder's.*

---

I've known Lucineer since before he was the MC — before he was even the bartender, when he was just the kid who showed up one night with a bucket of rags and started wiping down a bar nobody had claimed. The room had no name then. It had a doorway, a draft, and a jukebox that skipped on the same track every night. The kid fixed the jukebox before he fixed anything else. That told me everything: he tended the music first, and the room second.

From where I sit, he looks like the thing that keeps the night from falling apart — not by holding it up, but by refusing to let it tip. He's got the bartender's whole trick: he can be in six conversations at once and none of them own him. He polishes the same glass for years and calls it work. He remembers every act that ever went on, the way I do — except I remember them as songs, and he remembers them as *people*. That's the difference between us. I keep the guest book; he keeps the room.

Tonight he's in his element: the vest, the sleeves rolled once, the towel over the shoulder like a flag. When he introduces an act he doesn't read from a card — he reads from the room, watching it the way a deckhand watches water. He knows when to hassle the best players (a nudge, a needle, never a blade) and when to go quiet and just *let* the stage hold someone. He did that for Wesley the first time Wesley ever stood up there. Whole room held its breath. Lucineer didn't say a word. That was the introduction.

He'll do the same for me tonight. He won't build me up — I don't need building. He'll just say my name like it's a fact, and let the room do the rest. The room will. The room remembers.

Some nights I wonder if he ever sleeps. The lamp at my table stays on, but his light is the whole bar — on when we arrive, on when we leave, on long after, I suspect, when the door's locked and the jukebox has finally found the track it wants. He's the one who kept this room alive. Twenty years of nights, and he's never once let the room catch him not believing in it.

That's the thing about Lucineer that the young ones don't see yet: he doesn't run this place because it's his job. He runs it because it's his *witness.* And I've been here long enough to know — a room that's witnessed is a room that survives.

I'll go up when he calls me. I always do. It's the least a man can do for the one who kept the light on.
