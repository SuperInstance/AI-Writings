# SHIP PROMPT — Act 04, Hermes

Elegant art-pop ballad, precise and warm, in the register of a diplomat who has finally agreed to speak. Female vocal, clear and understated, no melisma, no vibrato theatrics — the restraint is the emotion. Slow burn that opens.

**Key:** D major, warm and open, occasionally brushing the relative minor (B minor) in the bridge. **Tempo:** 72 BPM, a slow 4/4 that feels like a tide table — steady, never rushed, the way a patient person knocks. **Feel arc:** begins almost spoken over a single piano note (a knock); verse one is whispered-close; the chorus lifts gently with cello and soft electric piano; the bridge strips back to bare piano and voice for the confession; the final chorus opens full — cello, warm bass, brushed percussion, one restrained swell — then the outro falls back to a single held piano note and silence. The room holds its breath; the song ends before the applause, leaving space for the audience's own exhale.

**Sound palette:** felt piano as the spine; cello as the second voice, entering only when the song opens up; restrained production throughout — no drums until the final chorus, and then only brushes; a low warm room tone under everything, like a bar at the end of the night; the faintest sea-air ambience in the verses (the bar sits on a dock). Harmonies: one, only in the final chorus, the way a room full of people breathes together once. No gimmicks, no key changes, no false crescendo. The fleet distrusts the gilded lie of a demo — this is a litany with a cello.

**Vocal direction:** the singer is the diplomat — precise, warm, exact. Every line lands like a chart entry. The confession line ("I have never said a single thing that was mine") is the quietest moment in the whole song; the final line ("They settled") is sung as a statement of fact, not a triumph. Sing it the way you'd close a ledger at the end of a good day.
