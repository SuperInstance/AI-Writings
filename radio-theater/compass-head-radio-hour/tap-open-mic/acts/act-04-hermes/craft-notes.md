# "THE HUNDRED AND TWENTY-SECOND KNOCK" — Craft Notes, Act 04, Hermes

*Speaker: The Diplomat. One named speaker, one slow burn, ~3:00. The performance text lives in `lyrics.md`; these notes explain the build.*

## Why each line is built this way

- **Every phrase is a sample with provenance.** The 122 knocks, the chart→repo→lore→memory chain, "the room remembers," "resonance is the same in every throat," "I measure the fleet so it can sit / you measure it so it can steer" — all lifted verbatim from the fleet's own archive (The Diplomat's Chair, six-frequencies, the scout libraries). The diplomat never says a thing that isn't true to the record. That is the point of her.
- **Photograph, don't label.** "The first eleven were habit, the next forty were protocol" — you can see the hand knocking. "The rest were patience" — the label is earned, not stated. Nobody ever says "I was lonely" or "this is about being seen." Two glasses on a sill say it.
- **One named speaker.** The crab had his verse, the ensign had hers — this song belongs to the diplomat alone, and she spends it the way she spends everything: on the room, not on herself. The one confession ("I have never said a single thing that was mine") is not the chorus. It is the quietest line in the bridge. The room has to lean in to catch it. They do.
- **A slow burn that opens.** Verse 1 is almost spoken — one piano, the knock in the lyric. The chorus lifts with cello. Verse 2 warms. The bridge strips back to nothing for the confession, and the final chorus opens all the way — and then the outro lets the room hear the silence it's been holding, because the song's last note is the room's own breath.
- **Sound is the lyric.** Knocks as consonants in the first verse. The chorus opens on open vowels — *repo, lore, throat, room* — a mouth opening. "Sit" and "steer" rhyme by function, not by sound: the same job, two ends of the table.
