# SHIP PROMPT — "Ring It Anyway" (ElevenLabs compose_music)

Compose a roots-rock singalong titled "Ring It Anyway" — a warm, studio-crafted jam-band anthem written by a project-studio musician for open-mic night at a bar called the Tap, where the room already half-knows the chorus. Key of G major, 100 BPM, a four-on-the-floor warmth with a country-soul sway, a song built to be sung by a crowd that doesn't know the words yet.

FEEL ARC: easy intro (clean Fender Telecaster arpeggio, one voice, the first two lines sung nearly spoken over a held room tone) → verse 1 (bass locks in, brushed feel, the bartender's story) → chorus 1 (Hammond B3 swells in, the hook "ring it anyway" lands, heads start nodding) → verse 2 (three-part harmony vocals enter, the bridge agent's story) → chorus 2 (the whole room joins on the hook — half-sung, half-shouted, joyful) → telecaster solo (one chorus long, melodic and singable, tube-amp sparkle over the groove) → bridge (strips down to voice and acoustic guitar, the confession, near-silence, tape hiss) → final chorus (big and warm: layered harmony vocals, Hammond with slow Leslie, driving backbeat, handclaps, the whole room singing the hook) → outro (deconstruction: Telecaster alone, the hook hummed, one last ring, fade).

SOUND PALETTE: warm clean Fender Telecaster through a tube amp (the lead voice), Hammond B3 with slow Leslie (the room's warmth), upright-feel bass, brushed drums that open into a backbeat on the final chorus, three-part harmony vocals (the crowd), handclaps from the second chorus on, subtle tape hiss and analog studio warmth throughout — a crafted sound built over years, played like it's live tonight. Male lead vocal, warm and lived-in, no auto-tune shine.
