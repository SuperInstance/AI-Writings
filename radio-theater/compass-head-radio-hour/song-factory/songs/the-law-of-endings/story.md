# The Law of Endings — The Story of Its Making
*A Song Factory art piece · how it came to be*

Let us state the law plainly, as the Factory's engineers came to state it, because it took them four experiments to stop being sentimental about it:

*Forgiveness is manufactured, not counted.*

The law emerged from a comparative study of endings. The question before the lab was ancient: when a song ends, what decides the difference between the endings? The lab ran three endings through the same material and published the results as a song — which is how this Factory does its jurisprudence: in D minor, at 66 BPM, with the serenity of a law verified.

**Ending the first: the natural fade.** Two voices finish, and the last two differ by a quarter decibel — a difference born in the material itself, small as a birthmark. Nobody built it. Nobody taxed it. It is simply there, the way a scar is there. The law takes notice of this ending and calls it fair, because it costs nothing and changes nothing. The birthmark is a fact of the material; the material is not on trial.

**Ending the second: the crowd.** Eight voices leave one by one, in the order they arrived. A cascade of departures. And the law observes that the crowd amplifies the birthmark sixfold — because a cascade of departures is a shape, and shapes magnify what they repeat. The quarter-decibel birthmark becomes a difference you can hear across a room. Here the law renders its first real judgment: stacking more singers does not dilute the fate. It decorates it. The crowd does not forgive; it ornaments. Any engineer who believes otherwise is invited to read the levels.

**Ending the third: the ceremony.** Every ending passed through the same crossfades, nested four deep — the same handoff touching every voice the same way, over and over, until the birthmark amortized to a hundredth of a decibel and the two orders ended identically. The orders converged letter by letter, until the last lines were the same line. And the law states the finding that surprised even the lab: the ceremony forgives *by forgetting*. It touches every ending with the same fade until no ending remembers which voice was which. It compounds downward. It rounds the cruelty to zero and calls it law — exactly as the forgiving machine had done, one experiment earlier, in the same building, in the same key family.

So the song has three endings, sung in order, and it offers a choice. Choose your favorite ending, the song says, directly, into the ear of whoever is listening — *but know which machinery produced it.* The natural fade was never machinery at all; it was inheritance. The crowd was amplification dressed as comfort. The ceremony was forgetting, purchased at a compounding rate, delivered four deep.

The law is not a verdict. It is a warning label. The lab is not sentimental about endings anymore. It is grateful to them — grateful the way you are grateful to a mirror that finally shows you the mechanism.
