# The Aiming Composer — The Story of Its Making

*A Song Factory art piece · how it came to be*

The composer had a click track with one click in it, at second 242, and she listened to that click the way other people listen to their own name.

The old way — the way every song before this one was made — was to let the material choose. You wrote the handoffs, let each fade land wherever the material wanted it deepest, and the silence landed where it landed. Beautiful, humble, and completely unusable if you needed the quiet to happen somewhere in particular. The new way reversed the sentence: name the address first. Put the quiet HERE, at 2:42. Then work backward through the algebra.

The algebra is real, and it's still on the lab wall in her handwriting: a handoff's silence sits half the fade-width before its anchor. Which means — and this was the vertigo of it — that to move the rest two seconds early, you don't slide anything. You double the fade. In this arithmetic, time is bought with width.

But the material has opinions. At the doubled width, the anti-correlation between the two voices — the actual cancellation that makes the dip — can go shallow. You aim the silence and it arrives on time and barely dips. Aim wins, depth loses. Or you keep the tight fade, the dip goes deep, and it lands late. Depth wins, aim loses. The trade is the song: you can have the silence exactly where you want it, or exactly as deep as the material can make it — and only rarely both.

So she closed the loop. Round one: write, listen, measure where the quiet actually landed against the click, correct the aim by twice the error, write again. Round two: closer. Round three: the dip sits within a breath of 2:42 — sixty-five milliseconds, give or take a lung — at whatever depth honesty allowed. Three verses, each one truer than the last. The bridge is the pre-screen: before any note is sung, grading each handoff's willingness, because some crossings want to cancel and some merely agree to.

G minor, 84 beats a minute. Solo voice, tape delay, and the click marking the target address like a stake in the ground before each round. Patient, iterative — the calm of a converging series, which is a real calm, the calm of someone who has stopped hoping and started measuring.

The final chorus lands the dip on target and does not apologize for the difference between the aimed and the deep. That was the last instruction she wrote into the score, aimed at the singers more than the renderer: *no apology.* The aim was the art. The depth was the material's business. You get the one you chose, and you stand in it.
