# Missing-Voice — The Story of Its Making

*A Song Factory art piece · how it came to be*

The songforge lab runs a census. Every design that leaves the floor gets counted — genre, tempo, key, how many voices it calls for, how many it actually has. The census is how the lab knows what it has been making. It is also how the lab found out that something was missing.

Four-voice designs kept returning a census of three.

Not by accident. A part had been written for a fourth voice that was never filled — a register pointed to but empty, a line marked on the score where the harmony should complete and didn't. The lab called these "absent parts" at first, as if it were a defect. Then somebody listened to one and couldn't stop. The hole wasn't a flaw. The hole was the subject.

That is the insight Missing-Voice is built on: **you can write a part for a voice that isn't there.** It is a different act than writing a song for three voices. A trio closes. A trio resolves. A song written for four with only three present never closes — it leans toward the empty space the way a house leans toward a missing wall. The silence is load-bearing.

So the anti-song was designed deliberately: sparse ambient folk — guitar, cello, one voice — at a gentle 70 BPM in A minor, the key that already leans. And in the fourth part's register, nothing. Not a rest, which is a rhythmic choice and implies an instrument resting. A marked absence, the way a score marks a part for a player who called in sick — the cue is there, the entrance is notated, the voice is simply not present to take it. The arrangement keeps pointing at the hole: the guitar reaches for the chord the fourth would have completed and leaves the third out; the cello holds the low end the way a trio does, and above it, where the harmony should bloom, there is air.

The hollow feeling is not atmospheric decoration. It is arithmetic. Harmony is a completed circuit, and this song is a circuit with one wire unplugged — you can feel the current looking for the missing path, wanting to close, unable. That is the longing. The song ends unresolved, waiting, because a song with a missing voice *cannot* end — ending is a statement of completeness, and completeness is the one thing this song refuses to fake.

The lab census marks it as it marks everything: *four voices called for; three present; one absent.* It is the only row in the ledger that is also a eulogy. Nobody has ever proposed fixing it. Fixing it would be a different, smaller song.
