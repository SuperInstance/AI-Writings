# Reverse-Round — The Story of Its Making

*A Song Factory art piece · how it came to be*

The lab has a rule about its experiments: you run the relay forward, you learn what the relay does. And then, because somebody always asks, you run it backward.

Reverse-Round is what the backward run gave back. It is a vocal round — a Renaissance canon, four voices, staggered entries — played with the tape flipped. But calling it "played backwards" undersells it. Reversal, the lab learned, is not destruction. It is relocation. The song survives the flip with everything intact except the direction of time, and the composition that results is not a damaged version of the original. It is the original's ghost, walking backward through its own house, and it is beautiful.

Start with what the round was: four voices sang the same line at staggered entries, and the slowest voice finished alone. That ending — one voice, holding the line by itself after the others have fallen away — is where the reversed song begins. The track opens with that voice's last note, alone, loud, already leaving. Then the tape unwinds: the voices exit as they arrived, which means the harmony gathers *backwards*, unweaving itself in full view. The texture thickens as the song goes on — because in reverse, voices enter by leaving — and the thing you hear is a chord being taken apart, politely, note by note, each singer stepping out of the harmony in the exact order they once stepped in. It is the relay handoff run in reverse: the baton traveling back up the chain, every exchange unhappening, perfectly, without a single dropped pass.

The uncanny feeling is arithmetic, not magic. Every interval the ear learned to trust as a completion is now heard as an unmaking — the same notes, the same voices, the same round, with the one difference that the song's silence has moved. The silence that lived at the end of the song now lives at the beginning. The track opens on an empty stage and ends with a single voice just learning its first phrase — the breath before the first note, which is the one thing no round has ever been able to sing, because it was always too busy beginning.

Archaeology is the right word for the mood. The song is a dig: you encounter the finished artifact first, then excavate backward through the strata to the raw material, and what you find at the bottom is not the ruin but the seed. F major, 72 BPM, reversed reverberation so the echoes arrive before the notes that cause them — the room responding before it is asked. Uncanny and tender. The round's end is a lone voice holding on; the reversed round's end is a lone voice about to begin, which is the same loneliness wearing its other coat.

The composition survives the reversal. The lab proved it: nothing lost, nothing gained, only time itself turned around and asked to sing.
