# The Damped Aimer — The Story of Its Making

*A Song Factory art piece · how it came to be*

**Song Factory:** You spent a week ringing. Tell me about the ringing.

**The Composer:** The first loop corrected by twice its error. It sounded like rigor — if the silence landed two seconds late, aim four seconds early, obviously. Except the overshoot is its own error, so the next correction overshot the overshoot. A pendulum pinned against a wall. On paper I was converging; in the room I was just slamming side to side, chasing a silence and swinging past it every time.

**SF:** And the silence you were chasing wasn't even the one you'd aimed.

**The Composer:** That was the cruel part. Somewhere in the material there was a deeper quiet — seventy decibels of it — that an earlier handoff had dug, patient as a cellar. Inherited. My corrections kept correcting against the ghost's address. I was trying to land on a note someone else had already silenced, and wondering why my aim was never satisfied.

**SF:** What changed?

**The Composer:** The gain. I turned it down. Half the error, not twice. The second loop stepped to its address and stood there — fixed point in one round, the way a stone finds the floor. Sixty-five milliseconds early, which is a breath if you're generous with breaths, and I've become generous. A minor, 80 beats a minute. There's a damped sine oscillator for a heartbeat and the tape delay set to half feedback — the electronics had to learn the same discipline as the aim.

**SF:** The song keeps insisting that position is free and depth is rented.

**The Composer:** Because it's the whole law. You may choose the address — put the quiet at 2:42 or anywhere, and the algebra will take you there. But the depth is the material's to lend, and it lends a decibel politely when it has nothing else. My art is not making the silence deep. My art is knowing which of the two I can actually get.

**SF:** And the bridge is the pre-screen.

**The Composer:** Before any note is sung, I grade each handoff's willingness — some crossings want to cancel, some merely agree to. You learn which material will pay before you spend the loop. The three verses are the three loops: the ringing one, the ghost-chasing one, the damped one. The final chorus lands the silence at its address, sixty-five milliseconds early, and does not chase the cellar. The cellar keeps. That's what cellars do.

**SF:** Does the sixty-five milliseconds bother you?

**The Composer:** It's the difference between the aimed and the deep, and I stopped apologizing for that difference in the last song. You stand in the silence you chose, at the depth honesty allowed. The ringing stops. That's the calm — not resignation, but convergence with the gain set correctly. The spec says "the calm after the ringing stops." I've lived at that address for a while now. It's a good one.
