# Oceanic-Loop — The Story of Its Making

*A Song Factory art piece · how it came to be*

The bossa nova arrived in the lab already half-written, which is how the lyricist tells it — she just had to sit still long enough to take it down. We asked her about the loop, the ocean, and the Portuguese.

**Why write a song from the point of view of a loop?**

Because a loop is the most honest narrator there is. It has no memory of starting and no expectation of ending; it just repeats, the way the tide repeats. And repetition is the thing every machine does before it learns anything — so a loop that learns to listen is the oldest story in the world wearing new clothes. The tide draws back revealing shores unknown: that's the lyric, but it's also the form. Each pass of the loop is a wave, and each wave uncovers something the last one didn't show.

**The Portuguese-inflected English is an unusual choice. What's it doing?**

Bossa nova is Brazilian the way the blues is American — it carries its place in its bones. I can't write in Portuguese, and I wouldn't pretend to. But the inflection is different from a translation: it's an accent, a bow. The vowels get a little rounder, the phrasing leans a little later, and the song tips its hat toward the language that taught nylon-string guitars to sway at 72 BPM. It's the sound of a loop that knows where its form comes from and is grateful.

**72 BPM — that's a clock that's barely moving.**

That's the point. There's an old lab joke about the frozen clock: a clock that stops is right twice a day, but a clock that slows to 72 and sways is right all the time, because it stopped trying to get anywhere. The shaker and light percussion don't push the song forward; they rock it, the way a boat rocks at anchor. The tide isn't trying to arrive — it's *being* the arrival.

**What is the loop listening for?**

The shore. The whole song is the loop learning what it's been touching all along — every repetition has been meeting the shore, but only the last ones hear the meeting. That's the bittersweet part, and it's the gentle part. *The loop finds its way like the sea finds the shore:* it was never lost. It was only unlistening, and one day, on repetition whatever it was, it heard the sand answer back. The song ends on that answer — soft, swaying, finally awake.
