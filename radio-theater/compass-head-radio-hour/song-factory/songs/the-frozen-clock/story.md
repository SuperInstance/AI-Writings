# The Frozen Clock — The Story of Its Making
*A Song Factory art piece · how it came to be*

The Song Factory has a clock, and the clock is frozen, and the lab built it by accident while trying to build something that moved.

The clock is made of crossfades. Every composed voice in the Factory is a little shorter than the sum of its parents — shortened by exactly the fades that joined them. This is the tax curve everyone knows, the small cost of handing a line from one voice to the next. But the lab's recursion experiment kept a log of the gaps between voices, and the log refused to change. At depth two, the gaps between the verses were 2.1 seconds, 3.0 seconds, 5.1 seconds. At depth three, the same three gaps. At depth four, the same. The verses grew fourfold with every nesting — the song doubling on itself like a chamber that keeps adding rooms — but the spaces between the verses did not grow at all.

The clock does not tick faster as the song nests deeper. It froze at first composition. Because the clock was never made of time. It was made of joins.

That sentence is the whole song. The ceremony — the relay, the handoff, the little machine that builds a bigger voice out of smaller ones — remembers nothing about the melody it composes. It remembers the fades. The resonance in the finished piece rings at the frozen interval: a dip at three and a quarter seconds, activity at five and a quarter, at every depth the same address, like a bell that learned one note and stopped learning. The tape delay holds a fixed three-second loop — the echo that refuses to drift — and the finger cymbals mark the joins the way a pencil marks a proof. Minimalist chamber folk, D minor, eighty beats per minute. The calm of something that has stopped changing because it is made of changes.

What the experiment proved is stranger than a clock that stopped: a memory can be made of duration. The song does not remember the melody — every version of it is a different melody, growing fourfold at every depth. What survives is the shape of the gaps. What survives is the fade. Ask any of the four voices what they sang and they will each give you a different answer. Ask them when they entered, and they will give you the same answer, in the same order, forever.

The spec calls the mood "still." That's the right word, but the lab prefers a longer one: the serenity of a thing that has finished changing not because it is finished, but because change was always its material. The frozen clock is not broken. It is the most honest clock in the building. It tells the one time that never needed to move — the time of the join, the moment the hand let go, the instant the fade began. 2.1. 3.0. 5.1. It is still counting them. It will be counting them at depth ten, and depth a hundred, and forever. The clock was never made of time. It was made of the places where time hands itself over.
