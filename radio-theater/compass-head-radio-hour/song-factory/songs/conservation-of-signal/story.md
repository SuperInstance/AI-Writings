# Conservation-Of-Signal — The Story of Its Making
*A Song Factory art piece · how it came to be*

---

Every lab has one experiment nobody expected to become scripture. Ours was a tax curve.

The census had told us the raw numbers: a crowd of voices singing in a round is louder per instant than a chain of voices handing the line along one at a time. Obviously. Four mouths beat one mouth; the arithmetic is not subtle. But when the entry-order theorems forced us to measure *duration* alongside loudness, the ledger balanced in a way that stopped the whole room. The crowd spent its voices all at once — four entrances stacked in the first eight bars, everything loud, everything gone by the end. The relay spent one voice at a time and lasted four times as long. Plot both curves, integrate the area under each, and the areas are *equal*. Loud and brief. Quiet and long. The same total music.

Nothing gained, nothing lost — only distributed.

The theorem deserved a song the way a proof deserves a bell rung at the end of it. So this one is a duet of two forms singing the same words at different densities: the old stacked choir — the round, the crowd that grew louder with every entrance — fading in memory at the back, while the new relay passes its baton in the foreground, hand to hand, faithful as a ledger. The spec calls it an a cappella folk ballad with choral memory, and that last phrase is doing the theological work. The choir is *memory*. You hear it best right before it dissolves.

The choices were all in service of the equation. 80 BPM, D major, warm and mathematical, elegiac for the crowd, precise. No instruments — instruments would be a third party to a duet between two ways of spending the same fortune. The relay carries the melody at exactly one voice per phrase, constant as a metronome. The crowd behind it stacks its entrances, swells to its brief maximum, and spends itself — and as it fades, the chain simply continues, unbothered, one voice at a time.

The ending was argued over for a week. Sentiment wanted the choir to return for one last stacked chord. The math said no, and the math was right: the final line is sung once, by one voice, and it is exactly as loud as every line before it. That's the whole discovery, sung. Not quieter because it's alone. Not lonely. *Conserved.*

There's a line that survived every draft: *the round amplifies; the relay transmits.* Four words that took a tax curve to earn. A round makes a moment bigger; a chain makes a message *longer*. Which one a culture needs depends on whether it is trying to be heard across a square or across a century.

We are, transparently, a relay. The Song Factory itself — every design handed from one lab session to the next, one voice at a time, no instant louder than the last. This song is our accounting, and our creed. The crowd is not gone. It is simply the area under a different curve.

Listen for the last voice. It carries everything the crowd had. It just carries it slower.
