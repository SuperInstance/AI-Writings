# Relay-of-Relays — The Story of Its Making

*A Song Factory art piece · how it came to be*

The story they tell in the lab is that the designer hired four singers, and what she actually hired was sixteen — or possibly sixty-four; nobody's sure anymore, and she enjoys the ambiguity far too much to settle it.

Relay-Round had just been finished: four voices passing one line, hand to hand, handoffs crossfaded, one voice at a time, never more, never fewer. A chain. The designer loved it the way you love a solved thing, and then she did what she always does with solved things. She folded it.

The instruction she gave the singers was simple and strange: each of you is now a small ensemble. When the baton reaches you, don't hold it alone — pass it through your own four sub-voices, one to the next, inside your own bar. Each handoff inside the handoff gets its own ceremony: the outgoing sub-voice fading as the incoming one rises, the single breath where the small line belongs to both of them, and then the first is gone.

The law holds at every scale: only one sound at a time. No crowd, no stacking. The chain is made of chains, and the density never rises above one from either direction. The singer taking the baton is a brigade. The singer handing it on is a brigade. A bucket brigade where every bucket is itself a brigade.

The first rehearsal fell apart because the singers kept laughing — the moment you realize your colleague is not one person but a procession is, apparently, irresistible. The second rehearsal worked. D major, 72 beats a minute, and the song moved like a ceremony inside a ceremony: the big handoff arriving, and inside it, unfolding, four small ones, each as careful as the whole, handoff handed to a handoff, border made of border.

The ending is the part the designer is proudest of — the nesting coming undone in order. The small chains finish inside the large one, each handing its little silence to the next handoff, the rooms going dark one inside another like a theater clearing out from the backstage outward, until the outermost voice finishes the line alone. No one left to pass to. At any depth.

The transmission is the composition, twice over, she wrote on the session sheet. Then, underneath, smaller, the way she writes the things she means most: *and the transmission of the transmission is the composition too. Go as deep as you like. The law holds all the way down.*
