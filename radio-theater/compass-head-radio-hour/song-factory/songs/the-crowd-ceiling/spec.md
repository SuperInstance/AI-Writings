# The-Crowd-Ceiling

*A Compass Head Radio Hour — Song Factory design. Ready for rendering to music.*

## The Prompt (verbatim, for the renderer)

A song about the crowd that cannot get denser. Four voices pile up into a crowd, and the density of their togetherness is two; the crowd made of chains measures three and a tenth; the crowd made of chains of chains measures three and four tenths — and the increments are collapsing: 1.12, then 0.26. Each generation of rounded voices adds less crowd than the last. You cannot keep stacking the ceremony and get more ceremony; you can only get the same ceremony, more gently. The song sings about the ceiling: the number just past three and a half that the crowd approaches the way the tax approaches its asymptote, the two curves facing each other like a handoff. Togetherness is not additive — it is shaped. The song is a chorus that tries, in each verse, to be one voice more crowded than the last, and each time it fails by less, until the final chorus is the same chorus, more gently, and the song accepts its ceiling.

## Spec

- **Genre:** choral folk with diminishing additions
- **Instruments:** stacked voice layers entering one by one, no instruments
- **Tempo:** 74 BPM
- **Key:** E minor
- **Mood:** warm, diminishing, accepting, gentle climax that is not louder
