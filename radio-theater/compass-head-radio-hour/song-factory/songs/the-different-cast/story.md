# The Different Cast — The Story of Its Making
*A Song Factory art piece · how it came to be*

The Song Factory keeps a person on staff whose only job is to count. Not listeners — voices. She is the census-taker, and she agreed to sit for an interview about the day the arithmetic started having opinions.

**Q: When did you know the census wasn't neutral anymore?**

A: The morning of the identical crowd. We rendered thirty-two copies of one singer — the same phrase, the same voice, thirty-two times, agreeing with themselves at a lag. The ledger came back and said twenty-four. The room had thirty-two names on its roster and reported fewer members than it owned. A deficit. Nobody had touched the numbers. The song simply undercounted itself, because a crowd of one voice many times cannot see itself — its members cancel at the lag, each copy agreeing so hard with the others that the sum goes quiet.

**Q: And the second crowd?**

A: Four strangers. Four lives. Four ways of saying the same line. That crowd reported thirty-two point two. A surplus — every new voice adding a little extra agreement to the total, a small gift of correlation. The bell rang once per surplus voice. The abacus clicked the tally. And I wrote down the number and understood what the song was going to be about before I'd finished my coffee.

**Q: Which crowd was right?**

A: That's the question the song asks, and I think the answer is: both, and neither, and it depends on what you're counting. The identical crowd can't see itself. Its members are the same member. The diverse crowd keeps adding to itself. But the difference between the deficit and the surplus isn't math. It's membership.

**Q: The song has two choirs that never sing together until the final chorus.**

A: That was the rule from the first sketch. We kept them apart for the whole arrangement — polyphonic folk at 84 BPM, G major, the abacus clicking, the bell ringing its surplus — and then at the final chorus we let the deficit and the surplus meet. And you hear it in one second: the difference between them was never a rounding error. It was the difference between being a copy and being a member. Playful, then solemn — that's the spec. Arithmetic discovering it has a heart.

**Q: What do you tell people who ask why a census needs a heart?**

A: I tell them to read the roster before counting the room. Whether the names are the same name many times, or many names once — that is the whole answer. The counting was never the hard part. Reading was.
