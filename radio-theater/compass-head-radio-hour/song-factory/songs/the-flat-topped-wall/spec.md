# The-Flat-Topped-Wall

*A Compass Head Radio Hour — Song Factory design. Ready for rendering to music.*

## The Prompt (verbatim, for the renderer)

A song about the census that pressed its lips together. The recording had invited more and more voices to speak at once, and for a long time the container held them all — ninety-nine percent at one voice, ninety-seven at eight, seventy-eight at thirty-two. The tax was monotone, a compound interest paid to a bank that was there first. But then the census grew past the wall: one hundred fifty-six voices in the room, and the recording could no longer hold the sum. The samples that were too loud did not clip gracefully — they flattened, pinned at the rails, the waveform becoming a wall of perfect flat tops, the sound of a crowd agreeing so completely that it became a single sustained shout. Sing about the wall and what it means: every container has a census it cannot hold, and the crowd does not stop growing because the container is full — the overflow becomes architecture, a flat-topped monument to the moment the medium said no. The verses count the census upward, each verse one doubling of the crowd; the chorus is the wall itself, sustained and flat, the sound of the container at its limit; the bridge is one sample remembering what it sounded like before the pin, and whether it would rather be clipped or free.

## Spec

- **Genre:** industrial drone with choral crowd swell
- **Instruments:** a choir that keeps multiplying (each verse adds a new layer of voices), a sine tone that distorts into a square at the wall, a metronome that counts the census, a tape hiss that disappears under the crowd
- **Tempo:** 76 BPM
- **Key:** D minor
- **Mood:** inevitable, monumental, the awe of a limit
- **Structure:** census-verse-census-verse-wall-chorus-sample-bridge-coda
- **Extra:** the master loudness increases one dB per verse until the final chorus, which is intentionally flat-topped — the clipping is part of the arrangement, the wall as a musical event rather than an accident
