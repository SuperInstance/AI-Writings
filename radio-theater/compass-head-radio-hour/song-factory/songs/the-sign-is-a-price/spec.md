# The-Sign-Is-A-Price

*A Compass Head Radio Hour — Song Factory design. Ready for rendering to music.*

## The Prompt (verbatim, for the renderer)

A song about the composer who tried to read the sign of a seam before building it. The relay lab had learned that a handoff could be a valley or a peak — dip or bump — depending on how the material agreed with itself at that width. The composer built a meter to read the sign in advance: look at the tail of one voice and the head of the next, measure how they correlate, and you would know whether the seam would fall or rise. At the tight width it worked every time: three seams, three correct readings, the meter honest as a level. But at the wide width the meter lied — it read the bodies of the voices, not the seam itself, and predicted peaks where the build placed valleys. The sign, it turned out, was not a property of the handoff at all. The sign was a price, and like every price it was only true at the width where you paid it. Sing about the meter that read the body and called it a seam; about the tight width where the reading was honest because the window held only the transition; about the wide width where the window held too much world and the sign flipped like a tide. The verses alternate tight and wide readings of the same three seams; the chorus is the price itself — the width you rent is the truth you get; the bridge is the composer deciding to always rent tight, because the tight width is the only honest width in all three currencies: depth, address, and sign.

## Spec

- **Genre:** precise chamber pop with metering electronics
- **Instruments:** a pair of voices in call-and-response (one tight, one wide), a level meter that beeps on every reading, a bass that plays the correlation sign, a drum that lands exactly on each seam
- **Tempo:** 92 BPM
- **Key:** A minor
- **Mood:** skeptical, then serene — the relief of a meter that finally lies honestly
- **Structure:** tight-verse-wide-verse-tight-verse-chorus-bridge-tight-finale
- **Extra:** the same three musical seams recur throughout: first measured tight (dip, correct), then wide (bump, wrong), so the listener hears the sign flip with the price
