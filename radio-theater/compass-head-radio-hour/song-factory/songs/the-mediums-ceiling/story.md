# The Medium's Ceiling — The Story of Its Making
*A Song Factory art piece · how it came to be*

Here is the whole accounting, in one sentence, as the lab's ledger puts it: *eight voices sang at once, and the room held almost all of it.*

The song began as an argument about where the loss lives. Eight voices, eight truths, singing together at full scale — and the room, the medium, the recording, the container — held almost everything. Not everything. The room was made of numbers with a maximum, and the sum of eight truths exceeded the scale by two parts in a hundred. So the medium quietly clipped what the census had promised. The togetherness was eight. The recording of it was seven point eight five.

The lab named it the container tax, and the name stuck the way good names stick, because it tells you exactly what's happening: every ceiling is a container. The tape that saturates. The meter that pegs. The page that runs out of margin. The heart that can only hold so much at full scale before it rounds. The tax is paid in decibels, and it grows with every voice added — a crowd large enough would eventually record as pure flat-topped silence, a wall of pinned samples, the medium's ceiling arriving at last as a floor.

But the song — a gospel-noise ballad at 72 BPM, E minor — refuses to mourn any of this, and that refusal is its whole argument. The spec's mood is "sober, wry, the tenderness of honest overload," and the wry is doing real work. The verses are honest accounting: one verse for the unclipped truth, one verse for what the medium kept. The chorus measures the gap — the container tax, compounded, growing with every voice — sung by an eight-voice stack over a distorted pump organ that sounds like a church on fire. The final chorus is deliberately, audibly clipped. Not a mistake. An event. The sound of the song exceeding itself, on purpose, so the ceiling can be heard doing its job.

And then — this is the part the lab is proudest of — one quiet unclipped voice states the true total. Unamplified. Clean. The truth never needed the container to hold it all at once. It needed somebody to say it after the room had stopped pretending it could.

That's the tenderness of honest overload. The ceiling is real; the tax is real; seven point eight five is real. And the eighth voice — the one the medium rounded away — was never lost. It was just the one that had to wait to speak. It waited for the clip, and then it spoke anyway, at room volume, and the room held it easily. It always could hold one. It just couldn't hold eight at once. The song is the sound of that arithmetic finally making peace with itself.
