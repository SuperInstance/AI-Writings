# Klezmer-Dub — Lead Sheet

*A Compass Head Radio Hour — Song Factory lead sheet. Playable now; renderable when quota returns.*

## Song Header

| | |
|---|---|
| **Title** | Klezmer-Dub |
| **Genre** | Klezmer dub |
| **Tempo** | 100 BPM (4/4, one-drop feel) |
| **Key** | E minor (natural minor for the dub weight; the B7s bring the harmonic-minor klezmer sting) |
| **Mood** | Celebratory, deep, rooted |

**The idea in one line:** the joy of klezmer meets the weight of Jamaican dub — a wedding dance and a sound-system sway occupying the same body at 100 BPM. Revolutionary and celebratory: the clarinet stays a clarinet, the bass stays a bass. Nobody melts into anybody.

## Chord Chart

```
Verse (A) — 16 bars, melody over the two-chord sigh
   | Em7    | Em7    | C      | D      |
   | Em7    | Em7    | C      | D      |

Bridge (B) — 8 bars, the klezmer sting
   | Am     | B7     | Em     | Em     |
   | Am     | B7     | Em     | Em     |

Hook (C) — 16 bars, the freilach turn (most celebratory)
   | Em     | G      | C      | B7     |
   | Em     | G      | C      | B7 Em  |
```

All 4/4 at 100 BPM. The verse never leaves i–VI–VII; the weight comes from staying put. The only chromatic note in the whole chart is the C♯ inside B7 — save it for the bridge and the hook, and it will hit every time.

## Structural Map

```
[Intro]      8 bars  — riddim only: bass + one-drop + skank. No clarinet yet.
[Verse A]   16 bars  — clarinet enters, melody over Em7–C–D
[Verse B]   16 bars  — second melody phrase; delay tails grow each pass
[Bridge]     8 bars  — Am–B7; clarinet climbs; spring reverb splashes
[Hook]      16 bars  — freilach turn; full band, maximum joy
[Dub Break]  8 bars  — the drop: instruments strip away, bass + echo carry the song
[Outro]      4 bars  — bass alone; last clarinet phrase decays into spring reverb ring
```

The song doesn't really end — the spring reverb rings after the clarinet has stopped, the handoff continuing into the silence.

## Rhythm Section — The One-Drop Pocket

- **The drop:** kick and snare lock *together on beat 3*. Beats 1 and 2 are air. This is the one-drop: the band falls into the hole on 3 and lets the first half of each bar breathe.
- **Skank:** offbeat stabs (guitar/keys) on the "and" of 2 and the "and" of 4 — short, dry, no sustain.
- **Bass:** enters on the pickup to 3 (the "and" of 2), root lands *on* 3, long tones, occasional octave drop. First two beats of every bar: the bass is silent. Space is the instrument.
- **Feel:** the rhythm section plays *behind* the beat; the clarinet plays *ahead* of it. That tension is the genre.
- **Drummer:** hi-hat eighths, ride on 2 and 4, keep the snare on 3 at the same dynamic as the kick — they are one instrument hitting together.

---

## Clarinet — Register, Phrasing, Voice

- **Register:** keep the melody written G4–G5 (chalumeau into the lower clarion). The line should sit *on top of* the bass, never fight it. If the melody climbs past G5, drop it an octave and let the delay do the rising.
- **Phrasing:** klezmer ornamentation over a minor grid — grace notes into beat 3, the *krekhts* (the sob) as a slight bend on the 3rd of the chord, and the occasional G♯ passing note (harmonic minor) as a scream, used sparingly.
- **The trade:** the clarinet surrenders up-tempo klezmer frenzy — you cannot run a full-speed freilach over a one-drop and keep any weight. What you get in return is the bass. Melody sits down on a rhythm that can carry a whole history.
- **No clarinet in the band?** Violin or alto sax doubles well; keep the same register and phrasing.

---

## Production Notes — Spring Reverb & Tape Delay

- **Spring reverb:** short, splashy, *mono*, on the clarinet send only (~30% wet). Decay ~1.5–2 s. Leave the spring "boing" audible at the end of every phrase — it is the song hearing its own echo and answering.
- **Tape delay:** dotted-eighth at 100 BPM = **450 ms**, 2–3 repeats, feedback ~40%, dark-filtered (roll off above ~4 kHz). The classic dub echo tail.
- **Dub drops:** at the Dub Break, strip the mix to bass + delay returns; bring the full riddim back on the downbeat of the next section. The drop should land like a door closing.
- **Ending:** no fade-out cheat. Let the last clarinet note decay into the spring and let it ring until the room is silent.
