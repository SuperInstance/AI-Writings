# Polka-Noise — Lead Sheet

*A Compass Head Radio Hour — Song Factory lead sheet. Playable now; renderable when quota returns.*

## Song Header

| | |
|---|---|
| **Title** | Polka-Noise |
| **Genre** | Polka noise |
| **Tempo** | 120 BPM — *unstable* (nominal; ±8 BPM wander, tape wobble, teetering between dance and collapse) |
| **Key** | B♭ major — dissolving into atonality over four takes |
| **Mood** | Chaotic, surreal, darkly humorous |

**The idea in one line:** Merzbow meets Oktoberfest. A cheerful German polka in the middle of being eaten — the oompah intact and grinning, the tuba thumping the downbeat like nothing is wrong, and the accordion screaming its melody into a wall of feedback that swallows it note by note.

## Chord Chart — The Polka That Thinks It's Fine

```
Polka (A) — 16 bars, pristine oompah
   | B♭     | B♭     | E♭     | F7     |
   | B♭     | B♭     | E♭     | F7     |

Trio (B) — 16 bars, the second dance
   | E♭     | E♭     | B♭     | B♭     |
   | F7     | F7     | B♭     | F7 B♭  |
```

Standard B♭ polka changes: I–IV–V7, friendly as a parade. The comedy is structural: this chart never changes. The noise is what changes.

**Oompah mechanics:**
- Tuba (or accordion bass side): the "oom" — root on beats **1 and 3**, round and fat.
- Accordion: the "pah" — chord stab on beats **2 and 4**, short, cheerful, unshakable.
- If the downbeat is the joke, the tuba is the punchline: it plays B♭ and F as if the apocalypse were just a scheduling conflict.

---

## Structural Map — The Erosion Timeline

The song is four takes of the same polka, each one more consumed than the last. It is not a piece with a second half; it is a piece with a *degradation curve*.

```
Take 1 (bars 1–16)   Pristine. Clean accordion melody in B♭, full oompah.
                     If the band can't play this straight, the joke is dead.

Take 2 (bars 17–32)  First abrasion. Distortion on the accordion send; melody
                     still audible; noise enters on the offbeats, between the pahs.

Take 3 (bars 33–48)  The melody is eaten note by note. Feedback swells in the
                     gaps; chord stabs degrade into pitch clusters; the key
                     begins dissolving — B♭ becomes a rumor.

Take 4 (bars 49–64)  No melody left. Pure Merzbow wash over a heroic oompah.
                     The tuba plays B♭ and F, steady, oblivious, magnificent.

Coda                 One final clean "oom" ... and the feedback swallows
                     the "pah". The celebration is real enough to attract
                     static; the joy is loud enough that the universe starts
                     answering it.
```

## Tempo & Rhythm — Unstable by Design

- **120 BPM nominal, but the tape wavers** like a man who's had one too many steins. Drift ±8 BPM across each take; let the band drag slightly in Take 3 and rush slightly in Take 4 — the rhythm should teeter between dance and collapse without ever fully falling over until the coda.
- **Found percussion:** a row of beer steins, slammed on a table, recorded hot. One slam per bar on beat 2, starting in Take 2. In Takes 3–4, the slams are the only "clean" percussion left.
- **Noise rig:** feedback loops and distortion pedals chained like guard dogs. The accordion's output runs *into* the noise chain's input — the transmission tax, but this chain doesn't levy a tax. It demands everything.

---

## Band Notes — Keeping the Joke Alive

1. **Never flinch.** The tuba, the accordion bass, and the oompah never react to the noise. The humor is that the celebration keeps going.
2. **The melody must be genuinely cheerful at the top** — a real Oktoberfest tune, not a parody. The destruction only means something if the thing being destroyed is real.
3. **Erosion is gradual and structural, not a sudden mess.** Each take should sound like the previous one with one more thing wrong.
4. **The ending is the joke's last beat:** one final clean "oom" from the tuba, silence for a half-beat of hope, and then the feedback swallows the "pah" that never lands.

## Renderer Notes (when the music API quota returns)

1. Render the four takes as a single continuous piece at 120 BPM with the tempo wander above, B♭ major → atonality.
2. Layer 1: clean accordion + tuba oompah. Layer 2: distortion on the accordion send. Layer 3: feedback swells. Layer 4: full noise wash over the untouched tuba.
3. Found percussion: beer steins slammed on beat 2 from Take 2 on, recorded hot.
4. The accident is the song — **do not fix it**. Do not "clean up" the feedback or tame the distortion. The song is the confession.
5. End on the lone "oom," then the feedback swallows everything. Let the wash ring ~4–6 s and cut.
6. When MMX returns, produce iterative images: the patch bay with the fateful cable, the row of steins, the rig in the middle of eating a polka.
