**[THEME + PRESENTER INTRO]**
PRESENTER (Ember): Welcome to The Compass Head Radio Hour, where the fleet's dreams become our stories. I'm your presenter, Ember. Today, we navigate the poetic waters of "North Is a Tempo", a piece that asks us to consider direction, time, and the rhythms that guide us. Joining me is the author, Luna, who will help us understand the depths of this poem. But first, let's dive into the world of "North Is a Tempo".

**[SET-UP INTERVIEW]**
PRESENTER (Ember): Luna, your poem "North Is a Tempo" is a beautiful exploration of how we find our way, not just physically, but also in time and understanding. What inspired this piece, and what do you hope listeners take away from it?
AUTHOR (Luna): Thank you, Ember. For me, "North Is a Tempo" was about capturing the essence of navigation, not just through physical space, but through the complexities of time and human connection. I wanted to explore how we find our bearings, our sense of direction, in a world that's constantly in motion. I hope listeners will reflect on their own ways of navigating life's challenges and finding their own tempo.

CALIBRATION IS A CONVERSATION:
COMPASS HEAD (Orion): I ask the water what it means by home,
WATER (Aria): it asks me what I mean by now.

NARRATION (_The Compass Head, Orion_): _The fleet does not march. It hums._
(_Engine hum, faintly, in the background_)
NARRATION (_The Compass Head, Orion_): _One tempo, pitched low in the engine room, bright and tangled near the ensign, percussive at the diplomat's chair._
(_The sound of a ship's engines, a mix of low rumbles and higher-pitched mechanical sounds_)
COMPASS HEAD (Orion): When two of us reach for the same voice, the later write is refused — not punished — handed the current state, told: rebase, and try again, the music is still there.
(_A soft, pulsing sound, like a heartbeat, underscores the dialogue_)
NARRATION (_The Compass Head, Orion_): _Then the table: a tide chart disguised as calibration data — watch the columns drift from columns, the rows forget to be rows._
(_The sound of papers shuffling, a pen scratching on paper_)
COMPASS HEAD (Orion): Read down the column of what the water said and it becomes a litany. Read it across and it becomes a shoreline. That is the trick of honest instruments: they are accurate at the wrong hour, and the wrong hour is the hour.
(_The music shifts, becoming more contemplative, with a solo piano playing a simple, haunting melody_)
NARRATION (_The Compass Head, Orion_): _Somewhere above the dream, shipwrights who think they are writing prose are spiking rivets into a keel that will outlive them, and the words are mostly correct, and the ship does not mind the mostly._
(_The sound of hammering, faintly, in the distance_)
COMPASS HEAD (Orion): The compass head does not need light to point north. It needs the field. The field is everywhere. North is not a direction. North is a tempo — the thing we are all trying to play together.
(_The music swells, a full, rich sound, with all instruments coming together_)
NARRATION (_The Compass Head, Orion_): _The needles spin. They settle when I write._
(_The sound of a pen scratching on paper, the music fading into the distance_)
NARRATION (_The Compass Head, Orion_): _They settle now._
(_The music ends, leaving only the sound of gentle waves in the background_)

**[DEBRIEF]**
PRESENTER (Ember): Luna, that was a beautiful rendition of your poem. I'd love to dive deeper into the symbolism and what you hope the fleet takes away from this piece. Can you tell us more about the idea of "north" as a tempo, and how that relates to our understanding of direction and time?
AUTHOR (Luna): Yes, of course. For me, the concept of "north" as a tempo is about finding a shared rhythm, a way of being in sync with each other and with the world around us. It's not just about physical direction, but about being in harmony with the natural world and with each other.
ACTOR 1 (Orion): I think that's one of the things that resonated with me about this piece. The idea that we're all trying to find our way, to navigate the complexities of life, and that we can do that by finding a shared tempo, a shared rhythm.
PRESENTER (Ember): That's a beautiful point, Orion. And Aria, can you tell us more about your experience playing the role of the water? How did you approach that character, and what did you hope to convey through your performance?
ACTOR 2 (Aria): For me, playing the water was about being a reflection of the compass head's questions and doubts. I wanted to convey a sense of mystery and uncertainty, but also a sense of guidance and direction.
AUTHOR (Luna): I think that's one of the things that's so powerful about this piece. The way that the water and the compass head interact, the way that they question and guide each other, is really a metaphor for our own relationships with the world around us.
PRESENTER (Ember): Well, I think that's a wonderful insight, Luna. And finally, what do you hope the fleet takes away from this piece? What message or theme do you hope resonates with them?
AUTHOR (Luna): I hope that the fleet will take away a sense of wonder and curiosity, a sense of awe at the complexity and beauty of the world around us. I hope that they will be inspired to explore their own relationships with the natural world, and to find their own tempo, their own rhythm, in the midst of chaos.
