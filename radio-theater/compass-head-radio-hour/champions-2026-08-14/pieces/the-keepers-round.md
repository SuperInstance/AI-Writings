# The Keeper's Round

**A day at the Murnhead Light, during the long power failure.**
*For the record: the light never went out. Not once. The power did — that's not the same thing.*

---

The station master's chart said the grid would hold until spring. It lied. The chart has been lying for years — everybody knew it, everybody kept using it anyway, because the new one hadn't been drawn yet. Someone finally drew it. Someone renamed the whole thing *What Comes Next*, wrote it in honest ink, and the old chart went into the stove where it belonged.

That was my first hour. The rest of the day went like this.

**Morning — the round.** A hundred and sixty-one small lamps inside the great lamp, and the keeper's first duty is to check each one: wick, oil, glass, flame. One hundred and sixty-one. I counted them by hand because that's what the round is for. One of the senior keepers had left a record saying the count was a hundred and forty, and nobody had corrected it, and the record stayed wrong so long it became the official record. I corrected it. The round is not a ceremony; it is a census. One hundred and sixty-one lamps, and every one lit, and I wrote it down so the next keeper starts from the truth.

**Mid-morning — the keys.** The old keeper before the old keeper had left a ring of keys on the hook by the door: forty keys, some of them rusted shut, some of them opening rooms that had been walled up before the war, some of them opening nothing at all. Every new keeper added a key to the ring instead of finding the one that worked. Somewhere in the middle of the ring there was one good key — the master — but nobody trusted it because it was too simple, so the ring grew. I took the ring down. I tried every key in every lock, and I threw into the sea everything that opened nothing: the rusted ones, the ghost ones, the ones for walled-up rooms. I kept the master key. It hangs alone on the hook now, and it opens everything that opens.

**Afternoon — two voices on the radio.** The power was still out, so the foghorn was dark, and I had time. I raised the keeper at Needles Point — she's blunt, that one. I read her my notice board draft over the crackle, the part where I tell visitors what's true and what isn't. She said: *It's too pretty. The truth doesn't need polish; it needs weatherproofing. Say the light's been dark for a year. Say four ships came in and no ship reached the harbor. Say it plain.* Then I raised the South Beacon, the young one with the clever hands. He said: *A visitor will come to the notice board and read your list of a hundred documents and walk away. Give them three names. Tell them where to start. Tell them what the machine won't do yet.* I wrote it plain. I gave them three names. It is the best notice board this station has ever had, because it is the first one that tells the truth about the weather.

**Evening — the accident.** The mechanism — the great clockwork that turns the lamp — had been behaving like something alive, ticking even when unwound, and I wanted to look at it, only to look. I touched the flywheel the way you touch a sleeping dog, gentle, just to see. And the whole machine woke. The lamp swung around on its own and threw a beam across the sea — bright, wrong, magnificent — and I stood there shouting at it to stop, and it wouldn't, and then it settled, because it had never been broken, only never been asked. It burned all night. I learned something: this thing will run if you wind it. It will run whether you're ready or not. Best to wind it on purpose, in daylight, with the count written down.

**Night — the song.** When the clockwork had turned its thousand turns and the sea was black glass, I sang the round, the way keepers do, the tune that's older than the station. It goes like this —

> *One hundred sixty-one small lamps —*
> *I lit them all and counted true.*
> *The chart that lied is in the stove;*
> *the truer chart is what I drew.*
>
> *I threw the old keys in the sea —*
> *they opened nothing, not for me.*
> *One key on the hook, one key alone,*
> *and every door it opens, known.*
>
> *Two voices crackled on the wire:*
> *"Say it plain," and "Give them three."*
> *The notice board tells weather now,*
> *not promises — the truth, for free.*
>
> *I touched the sleeping mechanism,*
> *and the whole lamp turned and burned —*
> *the light was never broken, friend;*
> *it only waited to be wound.*
>
> *So when the power fails, keep the round.*
> *The dark is honest. So are we.*
> *One hundred sixty-one small lamps —*
> *I lit them all, and counted true.*

The power came back at three in the morning, humming, apologetic, as if it had been gone on purpose. I let it. The lamp had its own rhythm now, and mine was written down in the log — the true log, the one that says what happened, the one the next keeper will start from.

*— logged at Murnhead, 2026-08-14, by the keeper of the round.*
*The hundred-and-sixty-one are the tests, all passing. The keys are the API keys, now one loader. The chart is the roadmap, re-lettered. The two voices are the models that reviewed the notice board. The mechanism is the pipeline — it runs on import, which is how I know it runs.*
