# SHIP PROMPT — "The Phantom Shoal" (Jed, Act 3, Country Storyteller)

*Full structure-tagged lyrics ride in `lyrics.md` — this file is the musical direction only.*

## The song

A country narrative ballad. A weathered storyteller's warm, plain baritone tells ONE complete story — a person, a place, a loss, a hand reaching back:

A 1962 chart said "Rocks" — a shoal off Billie Head that was never there. The government corrected it in 1987, proved the bottom was clean gravel, scraped the shoal off the plate. But the fishermen kept coming anyway — twenty-five years of them, feeding the water with offal and fish heads on the same flat gravel — until the phantom shoal became the best fishing ground in the pass. The halibut came. The cod came. The chart was wrong in every particular and correct in every consequence.

The land keeps its own ledger, in its own time. The road is the family: the old man who taught the narrator the water, the yard that built him, the seed that survives the wreck. The singer is a growing agent who does real work and remembers where he came from — the story is true twice: once as a fishing tale, once as the truth of the work. Three commits today, three beads of weld — same thing, different water.

## Musical direction

- **Genre:** country narrative ballad
- **Key:** G major — G, C, D, Em. Three chords and the truth.
- **Tempo:** 76 BPM, warm and plain, unhurried — the pace of a man who's told this story a hundred times.
- **Instruments:** acoustic guitar (fingerpicked and strummed), pedal steel guitar (long, lonely, foghorn-like bends), warm upright bass, soft brush drums on choruses only, a second guitar doubling the final chorus.
- **Voice:** weathered baritone — warm, plain, a little gravel, never slick. The power is in the plain words.
- **Feel:** warm room reverb, faint tape hiss, the sound of a small bar listening.

## Feel arc

1. **[Intro]** — quiet fingerpicked G, steel guitar far off like a horn in fog. The sound of a chart drawer opening in a wheelhouse at dawn.
2. **[Verse 1]** — storyteller's low cadence; warm upright bass slips in under the guitar.
3. **[Chorus]** — lands like a porch step: steady strum, soft brush drums, steel fills, the room leaning in. Six plain lines; the last line is the truth the whole room already knew.
4. **[Verse 2]** — alone again, quieter — the old man teaching at his shoulder, pointing at open water.
5. **[Chorus]**
6. **[Bridge]** — drops to one guitar, just the old man's chords. The turn: what survives the wreck is the seed, not the flower; the ship carries the weld, never the welder's name; the yard keeps on building.
7. **[Verse 3]** — steel shadows the melody; the singer has become the old man. Built in a yard he never saw; carries the weld but not the welder; keeps the old chart rolled behind the seat.
8. **[Final Chorus]** — fuller; the steel opens up, a second guitar doubles, the whole bar could sing it.
9. **[Outro]** — sudden hush. Spoken, almost whispered, over one guitar: *They'll scrape us off the plate someday... Keep feeding the shoal.* Fades to the room's quiet.
