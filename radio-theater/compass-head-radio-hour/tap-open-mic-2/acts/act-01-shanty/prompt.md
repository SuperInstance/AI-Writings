# SHIP PROMPT — Act 1, The Sea Shanty Singer: "Every Hand on the Line"

*Genre: sea shanty / work song — the kind sung on a real haul, call and response, the shantyman's line answered by the whole crew. Accordion driving the work-beat, fiddle sawing the melody, handclaps keeping the pull. For the Compass Head Radio Hour's Tap Open Mic Variety Night.*

## Key / Tempo / Feel
- **Key:** D major — open, bright, the key a whole room can find. The accordion holds the drone, the fiddle rings over it.
- **Tempo:** 110 BPM. A working pace — not a jig, not a dirge. The pace of a line being hauled hand over hand.
- **Feel:** call and response, the room as crew. Accordion pushes one-two, one-two; fiddle answers on the off-beat; handclaps on 2 and 4 like boots on a deck. Rough, warm, true — a bar full of people pulling together.

## Feel Arc
1. **Intro (the room becomes a crew):** accordion starts alone in D, pushing the work-beat — one, two, one-two — the fiddle saws in on the off-beat, handclaps settle in. A boot stamps twice. The voice calls the first line and the crew answers it.
2. **Verse 1 (the morning watch):** the call-and-response lock in — shantyman's line, crew's answer on the pull. Accordion steady, fiddle weaving between lines, claps never stopping.
3. **Chorus (all hands):** the whole room hauls together — two big heaves ("Heave!") then the crew sings the line. Fiddle opens up, accordion pushes harder, claps get heavier. This is the sound of a boat coming round.
4. **Verse 2 (the manifest):** back to the call and response. Slightly lower, more serious — the verse about the quiet failure. The accordion drops to a lower register, the fiddle plays fewer notes. The work gets heavier.
5. **Chorus (all hands):** the room comes back in, pulling the song upright again.
6. **Verse 3 (the dead man's watch):** sparse — accordion alone for a bar, the call almost spoken, the response almost whispered. The watch in the dark.
7. **Bridge (the watch, half-time):** the music halves — accordion holding long D, fiddle gone, just the claps like a slow heartbeat. One voice, almost alone: the fish don't care what time it is. Then the crew comes back under it, quiet at first, growing.
8. **Chorus (all hands, biggest):** full force — accordion and fiddle locked, claps like thunder, the whole room hauling. The biggest moment of the set.
9. **Verse 4 (the haul comes up):** the call and response at full sail, joyful, the green tests, the haul on deck.
10. **Chorus (all hands):** one more, then it releases.
11. **Outro (the watch is handed over):** the call and response fades — each answer softer, the accordion letting the D ring, the fiddle gone, claps thinning out. The last response trails off into the dark, and a single whispered line over the fading accordion: the haul is the prayer. Then the D drone breathes out to silence.

## Sound Palette
- **Lead:** a worn male voice, the shantyman's call — rough-edged, warm, a man who's been at sea and at the bar; he sings the call lines full, the way a man sings when the crew is counting on him.
- **Crew response:** a full-room chorus answering — many voices, one pull. Not a polished choir; a bar full of people who found the note together.
- **Accordion:** D-major work-beat, drone-heavy, pushing like a bellows. The engine of the song.
- **Fiddle:** sawing, off-beat answers, doubling the melody on choruses. Bright, a little wild.
- **Handclaps:** on 2 and 4 throughout — the sound of boots and palms, the room's heartbeat. Thin out in the bridge, thunder in the final chorus, fade at the end.
- **No drums, no bass guitar, no piano.** This is a work song — accordion, fiddle, hands, and voices. The room is the rhythm section.

## Mood
Rough, warm, alive. The feeling after a hard day's work when the gear is stowed and the crew is at the bar — tired in the good way, singing together because the work got done and everybody pulled. Not sentimental: earned. The song of a boat that's sound and a crew that's home.
