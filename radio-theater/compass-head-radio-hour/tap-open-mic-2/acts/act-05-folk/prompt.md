# Ship Prompt — Act 5: WRITE IT DOWN

## Genre & Feel
Plain-spoken protest folk in the Woody Guthrie tradition — the word as witness, the common truth, the song that tells what happened so it can't be unwritten. Honest, open, unadorned. One voice, close to the mic, like a woman telling the room something they need to know. No irony, no ornament, no production tricks. The plain truth is the most radical thing.

## Instruments
- Acoustic guitar, fingerpicked and strummed — C major, open and warm, the strum like a walk. Two-bar intro that sounds like a person starting to speak.
- Harmonica — the whistle in the middle (8-bar break after verse 3, then a short outro fade). The harmonica plays the chorus melody: it is the song saying the part words can't.
- No drums, no bass, no strings. Just the guitar, the voice, and the harp.

## Tempo & Key
88 BPM. C major. ~3:00.

## Structure
1. Intro — guitar, 2 bars
2. Verse 1 — "The dashboard on the wall was lying / Said 'undefined' where a name should be…"
3. Verse 2 — "I asked the wise one where the story hid / He pointed at the empty page…"
4. Chorus — "Write it down, write it down / What the hands did, let the paper say / When the grain is gone and the makers are gone / The clay still tells the truth today"
5. Verse 3 — "The book said one thing, the code said two / Two dashboards standing in one house…"
6. Whistle break — harmonica, 8 bars, no words
7. Verse 4 — "The merchant dies, the warehouse falls / The city turns to dust and sand…"
8. Chorus
9. Verse 5 — "I am small and I am new / But I can write what I have seen…"
10. Chorus — repeat, stronger
11. Outro — harmonica fade

## Vocal direction
Sing like you're informing, not performing. Verses flat and true, the words doing the work. The chorus opens up just a little, like a door. Verse 5 quiet and steady — the line "the work speaks for itself" lands like a verdict. Harmonica between verses breathes like a held thought.

## The story (for interpretation)
A dashboard was lying on the wall — it showed "undefined" where a name should be, and the lie ran for weeks. Nobody had written down what the system actually said. The witness asked the wise, asked another who never answered, and read the pages herself in the dark. She wrote the truth down: the schema, the difference, the fix. And the song reaches back to the oldest witness there is — the Sumerian tablet that outlived the grain, the merchant, the warehouse, the city — the record that tells the truth long after everything else is dust. The last verse is the witness's own testimony: small and new, but able to write what she has seen. The work goes on while the captain sleeps, and the work speaks for itself.
