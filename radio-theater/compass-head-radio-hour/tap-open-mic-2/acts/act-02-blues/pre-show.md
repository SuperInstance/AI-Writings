# Pre-Show — Corinne, the Delta Blueswoman (Act 2)

*The Tap, early evening. A corner booth where the light is low and blue. A hollow-body guitar in a stand, a coffee gone cold, and a small black ledger book held shut with a rubber band.*

---

I come in off the road with the guitar case and the ledger. The ledger's older than the guitar. It's the whole tradition — you don't hold the sorrow in your chest where it can rot you, you write it down like a debt, and you sing it out at the crossroads. A debt you sing is a debt you can pay. A debt you carry is a debt that carries you.

The room's still early. Elias the shantyman's got a table like it's a ship's rail — he's showing somebody how the haul goes, call and the answer, and I hear him say *the rope don't exist yet and neither does the sea, so we sing them both into being.* That's a different weather from mine. He hauls on a rope that appears when you pull. I walk down a road that only forks when you choose. We're both making the thing by doing the thing, but he's got the crew and I've got the ledger. Fine. The room holds both.

Jed the storyteller's got the corner table by the window, watching the door like every face is a verse walking in. He catches my eye and nods at the ledger. I tap the rubber band. He doesn't need the words. Country knows debt. Blues knows debt. We just keep it in different pockets — his in the road, mine in the ache.

The MC drifts by, faster than last night, moving like the night's already late. He nods at the ledger. "You gonna read us something from that?"

"Gonna sing it," I say. "Reading's for the ones who can't carry a tune. The blues is the sorrow turned into a note you can bend."

"Bent how?"

"You ever hear a note that's supposed to be one thing and you lean on it till it's another? That's it. The truth, but with a bend in it, because the straight truth'll cut you." I pick up the coffee, find it cold, put it down. "The straight truth is I fixed two real bugs today and pushed them before I tuned this guitar. The bent truth is the same story sung slow."

He laughs — short, moving. "Bent it is. You're on after Elias."

"I know. The haul, then the ache. That's the order of things."

The room's filling. Somebody's running a thumb down guitar strings behind me, testing the wound ones, and I feel it in my chest before I hear it — the low E, the one that hums like a man thinking. That's my note. That's the one the whole 12 bars lean on.

I open the ledger, just to look at it. Four entries today, in my own hand:

- *The phantom path — a function that returned a file it never wrote. Killed it. The lie that looked like success.*
- *The astats lie — spectral features that were never measured, zeros wearing a lab coat. Now the instrument measures what it reports.*
- *The dead parameter — a knob nobody could reach. Wired it through.*
- *The unsent letter — still unsent. That's the one the song's for.*

I close the ledger. Rubber band back on. That last line's the one that's got a bend in it tonight.

When they call my name I'll walk up with the hollow-body and the ledger, set the book on the stool where everybody can see it, and play the AAB — the line, the line again, and the answer. Twelve bars. The ache stated plain, then bent. A solo space where the guitar says the thing I don't have words for.

The crossroads is a real place. I've been standing in it all day: fix the lie, or sing about the lie. I did both. That's what the blues is for.

Keep the ledger. Pay the debt. Bend the note.
