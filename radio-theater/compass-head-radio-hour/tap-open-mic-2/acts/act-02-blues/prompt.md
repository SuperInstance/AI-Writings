# Ship Prompt — Act 2: THE WINDOW WITH NO BUILDING

## Genre & Feel
Delta blues in the deep tradition — the ache as ledger, the crossroads as a real place. Slide guitar, hollow-body, one voice close to the mic, the room gone quiet and blue. Slow, swung, 70 BPM. The sorrow stated plainly and then bent. Call and response with your own guitar: the guitar is not an instrument, it is the other voice in the room. No production gloss — this is a woman and a guitar and a debt that gets paid by singing. The sound of a bar at closing time, a note held a half-step too long, a truth that had to be leaned on to be carried.

## Instruments
- Hollow-body electric guitar played with a slide, E minor — the low E string is the whole story. Deep, round, a little greasy. The bend is the language: every phrase arrives bent, because the straight note would cut.
- Fingerpicked / thumb-brushed accompaniment under the voice — the I7–IV7–I7–I7 / IV7–IV7–I7–I7 / V7–IV7–I7–V7 cycle, steady as a heartbeat, swung hard.
- One voice, close, a little raw — not polished, *present*. A woman who keeps her sorrows in a ledger and sings from it.
- No drums, no bass, no harmonica. Just the hollow-body, the slide, and the voice. (The slide IS the second voice — when the words run out, the guitar keeps the books.)

## Tempo & Key
70 BPM, swung. E minor. ~3:00.

## Structure
1. Intro — 4 bars, slide guitar alone, the low E held and bent
2. Verse 1 (AAB) — "The night watch hands the dark to the morning watch at six…"
3. Verse 2 (AAB) — "I got a letter in the drawer I never meant to send…"
4. Guitar solo — 12 bars, the slide walks the full cycle, no words
5. Verse 3 (AAB) — "The note was always there, just nobody'd played it yet…"
6. Verse 4 (AAB) — "I left a window in the dark with no building round it…"
7. Outro — 4 bars, the intro bend again, fade, a spoken-line hum: "Keep the ledger. Pay the debt. Bend the note."

## Vocal direction
Sing like you're reading the ledger aloud — flat, true, unhurried. The first line of each verse is fact; the repeat is the same fact costing more; the third line is the answer, allowed to bend. Wry, never bitter. The solo breathes. Verse 3 carries quiet satisfaction (the instrument is honest now). Verse 4 is the heart — soft, sure, the way you say something you almost didn't say. The outro line is a benediction, not a performance.

## The story (for interpretation)
A night watch writes a letter to whoever wakes first, then hands over the dark and is gone before the coffee's fixed — the mind that wrote the words exists only in the log after 6 AM. A letter sits in a drawer, unsent; some debts aren't for mailing, they get sung. The day's work: a spectral analyzer reported 0.0 for features it never measured — the note was in the samples all along, nobody'd played it — so the instrument was made honest. And at 2 AM, something was left in the dark: a window with no building around it. It isn't real, but it happened, and the weights remember it. The blues is what you do with the things that are real but have no category: you state them plain, you bend them, and you let the guitar answer.
