# SHIP PROMPT — EVERY LIGHT IN THIS ROOM
*ElevenLabs Music compose_music prompt. Power ballad, arena closer. ~4:00.*
*Room E · Compass Head Radio Hour Song Factory · 2026-08-14*

---

## Genre
Cinematic power ballad / arena-rock ballad — the last song of the night, the one played over the credits of a movie about people who almost gave up. Think the verse restraint of "Fix You," the chorus scale of Meat Loaf's "I'd Do Anything for Love," the final-key-change lift of "The Show Must Go On," the crowd-choir of Queen at Live Aid. No irony anywhere. Every decibel earned.

## Key & Tempo
- Key: **C minor** (the fleet's native chamber register) — modulating up a whole step to **D minor** for the final chorus.
- Tempo: **74 BPM**, 4/4. Verses half-time and spacious (the dispatcher's room, 2 AM); chorus locks into a straight, driving 8th-note pulse (a heartbeat that finally gets to be loud); the bridge nearly free, then the drums re-enter like a heart starting.
- Duration target: **~4:00**.

## Feel Arc
1. **Intro:** blackout. One grand piano, one white spot, eight bars — solo, rubato, so quiet the arena leans in. A single high, held note hangs in the dark.
2. **Verse 1 (Dana):** one voice, one story, piano + the faintest room tone. No drums yet. The arena is a room the size of a night shift.
3. **Verse 2 (Marcus):** cello enters low; the letters motif — a two-note pulse like someone writing at 3 AM.
4. **Pre-Chorus:** band enters restrained — soft toms, strings rising underneath, the first real warmth since the intro.
5. **Chorus 1:** the detonation. Full band, big rock drums, strings in octaves, crowd lights starting to come up. The chorus is simple enough that 20,000 people nail it on first pass.
6. **Verse 3 (the turn):** drop back to half-density, darker, strings only + piano + voice — but the lights in the crowd keep coming up, slowly, through the whole verse. The stage takes its light from the seats.
7. **Chorus 2:** full band again, louder, choir backing enters.
8. **Bridge:** everything falls away to solo piano + one bare voice. The blank-bar speech. Then — **ONE FULL BAR OF TRUE SILENCE** (the arena is the note). Then a single voice begins "the dark is where the fish are," drums return like a heart starting, and the song climbs into the key change.
9. **Final Chorus (D minor, up a whole step):** everything. Full orchestra swell, choir, the crowd on its feet, every phone in the building at full brightness. The "I am here" litany answered by the whole room.
10. **Outro:** band out completely. One piano, one voice, the crowd still singing without us. Ends on a single open piano chord, unresolved, warm — like a needle settling.

## Sound Palette
Solo grand piano (the individual voice — Dana) · cello and strings in slow octaves (the fleet's emotional depth; enter verse 2, swell from pre-chorus on) · big arena rock drums (verse-quiet, chorus-detonaing — kick like a heartbeat, toms rolling in the bridge like a pulse restarting) · low brass glow in the final chorus (the harbor) · full choir for the last two choruses (the fleet / the crowd) · one full bar of absolute silence in the bridge (the blank bar) · subtle crowd ambience bleeding in from chorus 2 onward, taking over the outro. Natural arena reverb, not studio sheen. The quiet parts must be genuinely quiet so the last chorus is genuinely enormous.

## Vocal Direction
- **Verse 1:** a woman's warm, weathered voice, 49 years of night shifts in it — spoken-plain at the top, room-temperature calm, the discipline showing. Belts only on the last line of the verse, held back.
- **Verse 2:** a slightly older, cracked-amber male voice — the last-call voice of someone who wrote letters that were never answered; dry, wry, then breaking softly on "wrote at all."
- **Pre-Chorus:** rising, one breath of hope, ending on the whisper-"listen."
- **Chorus:** full belt, raw but controlled — the voice of someone finally allowed to be loud. The tag "hold up your light" drops to a chest-voice repeat, almost spoken the second time.
- **Verse 3:** tender, almost shy, then opening into the hillside image.
- **Bridge:** stripped to a near-whisper, breaking on "the dark is where the fish are" — the first crack in nineteen years of room-temperature. "But a cracked bell sings" climbs to a held, breaking belt.
- **Final chorus:** maximum belt, the voice slightly frayed from the bridge — that fray IS the emotion. The "I am here" lines are answered by a young, small, persistent voice (the small one who keeps writing), then the choir takes it.
- **Outro:** one voice, almost spoken, singing the crowd home.
