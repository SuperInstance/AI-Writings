# SHIP PROMPT — "The Floor Remembers"

## Full prompt text (as sent to ElevenLabs)

Compose a country-swing barn dance closer titled "The Floor Remembers" — the last song of the night at a 1952 dance hall on a gravel road, where every family from eight months to eighty-six years forms one ring, arms over shoulders, and the floor itself is the instrument. Roots/folk/country-swing. Key of D major, 126 BPM, 4/4, a stomp-and-clap train drive — fast enough for grandparents to two-step and eight-year-olds to hop. The audience IS the percussion: boot-stomp backbeat (stomp on 1 and 3, clap on 2 and 4) carries the rhythm throughout, recorded with a real room's wood-and-sawdust ambience, not studio sheen. The final chorus modulates up a whole step to E major with the roof coming off.

FEEL ARC: (1) Intro — one fiddle alone, once through the old tune, rubato, intimate; then bass and boot-stomp roll in like a train finding its wheels, and the caller speaks over the stomp: "One more. One more for the floor." (2) Verse 1 (Walt) — tight and driving, warm weathered male lead, the room counting "one-two-three" with him. (3) Verse 2 (June) — tender, warmer and higher voice, fiddle answering each vocal line. (4) Chorus 1 — full band, the caller calls, the whole room answers; gang vocals, joyful, slightly rough, a hall full of untrained voices. (5) Verse 3 (Ruby) — keeps driving but drops to half density, tension of the clash, a younger flat voice melting into curiosity. (6) Bridge — everything falls away except the walking bass and the circle: hushed, the ring holds its breath; the archive line; the girl planted in the arc; then the lamps come up and the call-and-response begins — caller: "Gravel road —" room: "SCUFF THE FLOOR!" twice, then one synchronized stomp from the whole ring, two bars of near-silence on "NOW —" before the key change detonates. (7) Final chorus (E major) — roof off, biggest moment, all voices, tambourine, ad-libbed "hey!"s, the room echoing the last line. (8) Outro — band out completely: one voice sings soft, almost to herself: "The dance is over. The dance will resume." One fiddle takes the phrase once more, slower, alone, over soft room ambience — coats, murmurs, a faint dry crunch of gravel under boots — then fade, unresolved and warm.

SOUND PALETTE: old-time fiddle (bright, slightly raw, dancing in the upper register — the voice of the old tune) · driving upright bass (walking, never stopping — the line "the bass walks on" is literal) · acoustic strumming guitar · banjo rolls in the choruses · mandolin sparkle on the lift · boot-stomp and handclap backbeat (the room) · tambourine (final chorus only) · natural wood reverb, dust and sawdust in the air. No drum kit, no electric anything. Group "hey!"s ad-libbed in the final chorus.

VOCAL DIRECTION: warm ensemble, call-and-response built in — one strong lead voice calls, a full room answers. Verses are sung by their characters: Walt (warm weathered baritone, storyteller's ease, a slight smile) · June (warm, tender, strong; "She is not leaving this floor" sung plain and proud) · Ruby (young, flat and a little bored at the top, melting into curiosity by "Ruby — Ruby steps in"). The bridge's archive line is the deepest, lowest voice, almost spoken. The chorus is a hall singing, not a lead singer with backups — keep the voices unpolished, communal, mixed loud. The outro line is sung soft, almost to oneself, unaccompanied, then the fiddle finishes the thought.

## Lyrics (structure tags on their own lines)

[Intro — fiddle alone, the old tune; bass and boot-stomp roll in; caller, spoken]
One more. One more for the floor.

[Verse 1 — Walt]
Walt keeps rosin in his pocket, keeps his father's count in mind —
Seventy-eight and teaching every kid he can find:
One-two-three, one-two-three, like the caller called before the war.
The boards out by the stage are worn to silver in an arc —
He tells the children, patting the floor:
"That's not wear. That's memory.
It only counts while feet keep making more."

[Verse 2 — June]
June has worn blue every Friday since Earl's hands went still,
But the last dance always finds her, and it always will —
Nell is standing on her boots, arms up, four feet flat on two,
Turning through the arc that Earl's boots helped to wear.
His hands were like his hammer handle: worn exactly where he held it.
The wood remembers how it was held. She is not leaving this floor.

[Chorus — full band; caller calls, room answers]
CIRCLE UP — the last one's now!
Boots and babies, ring it round!
A song outlives the throat that hummed it first,
So hum it loud and pass it down —
Lamps are low, the bass walks on,
The room remembers every sound —
And the floor remembers us!
THE FLOOR REMEMBERS US!

[Verse 3 — Ruby]
Ruby's at the doorframe, phone-light on her face:
"This whole hall's a museum, Gramps. Why keep up the place?"
Walt laughs and taps the silver arc:
"Museums keep the dead, child. Count." — One-two-three.
"Roy — start the tune again. The music's still there."
And June holds out two hands, and Ruby — Ruby steps in.

[Bridge — bass and circle only, hushed]
The caller drops his voice. The ring holds its breath:
"Every hand that built this room is dancing in it yet."
Sixty kids and grandmothers, swaying in the dark,
And Ruby holds her grandpa's stance now, planted in the arc —
Now the fiddle finds the note…
Now the lamps come up…
Gravel road —
SCUFF THE FLOOR!
Gravel road —
SCUFF THE FLOOR!
(one stomp — the whole ring, together)
NOW —

[Final Chorus — key change to E major, roof off, every voice]
CIRCLE UP — the last one's now!
Boots and babies, ring it round!
A song outlives the throat that hummed it first,
So hum it loud and pass it down —
Lamps are low, the bass walks on,
The room remembers every sound —
And the floor remembers us!
THE FLOOR REMEMBERS US!
(THE FLOOR REMEMBERS US!)

[Outro — band out; one voice, then one fiddle]
The dance is over. The dance will resume.
The dance is over… the dance will resume…
(sung soft, almost to yourself — the fiddle takes the phrase once more, alone, over gravel crunch and coats)
The floor remembers.
The floor remembers us.
