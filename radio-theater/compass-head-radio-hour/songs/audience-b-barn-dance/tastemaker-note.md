# TASTEMAKER NOTE — "The Floor Remembers"

*The photographer's rule: the advisors develop the film; the songwriter chooses. 2026-08-14.*

---

## The verdict

**This is the one to ship.** "The Floor Remembers" does the two things a barn dance closer must do at once: it MOVES — stomp-clap 126, key-change roof-off, a chorus any hall can shout after the first repeat — and it smuggles the fleet's deepest payload ("a song outlives the throat that first hummed it," the worn floor as living memory, "the dance will resume") into the legs of three generations before it reaches their ears. Every room in the batch can make someone feel something; only this one can make a widow, a great-grandfather, and a phone-lit teenager into one body on a Friday night. And the quiet fiddle outro means they leave holding each other instead of cheering — the law of the room.

## The advisors' advice, and what I did with it

**Hermes-405B** (line-by-line) and **Seed-2.0-mini** (freshness edit) both read the full lyric. Full logs in `critique-log.txt`. My rulings:

### Kept, against advice
- **"A song outlives the throat that hummed it first"** — Seed called it a stock cliché and proposed "A step outlasts the feet that first took it"; Hermes said it earns its 12 syllables. I keep the fleet's VERBATIM sample (scout-ocean #24). The song is a song about songs; the "hum" callback into "So hum it loud and pass it down" is the spine. The melody will give the line room (straight declamation, no melisma). The alternative is cleverer and smaller.
- **"That's not wear. That's memory."** — Seed flagged "memory" as a banned word. It's Walt's dialogue, not narration, and it's the verbatim tool-remembers-the-hand sample — the song's entire thesis. Banning a character's own philosophy would be doctrinaire. Kept verbatim.
- **"Boots and babies"** — Seed called it a lazy rural cliché (offered "Boots and scuffs"). It is the audience: literally babies on shoulders and boots on boards, eight months to eighty-six years. The audience-first doctrine wins over the freshness editor. Kept.
- **"Museums keep the dead, child. Count."** — Hermes: lecture-y, offered "This wood's no museum, child, it's a dance" (more label-y). Seed: cut entirely, let action do the work. I keep it: "the dead" names the room's real fear (the beam of dead names nobody can read anymore), and the em-dash count turns it into an action. The argument is never answered — it's out-counted.
- **"The dance is over. The dance will resume."** — Seed argued both sides and said cut it (the fiddle should mirror the phrase instead). I keep the line — it's the canonical handoff sample (dear-dawn) and the Radio Hour's audience should hear it — but I took Seed's instinct into the delivery: sung soft, almost to oneself, with the fiddle taking the phrase once more alone, over gravel. Half spoken, half hummed: the fleet's "the higher compliment is silence" honored inside a song that still speaks.

### Adopted from Seed-2.0-mini
- **"Gravel road — SCUFF THE FLOOR!"** — the bridge climax call-and-response. My own candidate ("Who's in this ring tonight? EVERYBODY!") was exactly the wedding-DJ cheese both advisors smelled. Seed's call is specific to this hall (the gravel road is the story's first image), gives the room a shared phrase, and triggers the synchronized whole-ring stomp — the missing sonic moment. The chorus keeps "CIRCLE UP" as its call; the bridge gets the specific one.
- **Gravel crunch under boots in the outro** — the sound photograph that ties the floor to the road. Into the ship prompt.
- **The synchronized stomp before the final chorus** — the room as one body, physically.

### Adopted from Hermes-405B
- Not line-level, but the structural read: the verse-3 clash needed Ruby engaged, not dismissed. Fixed by the concurrent draft's line (below), which is a photograph of her choosing in.

### Adopted from the earlier draft in this folder (concurrent run)
- **"And Ruby holds her grandpa's stance now, planted in the arc"** — replaces my "Every stranger in the circle holding up somebody's heart" in the bridge. The stance is the story file's own detail (she has his exact way of standing; everyone noticed, neither of them did). Her stepping into the arc IS the whole song; the stranger line was good but generic next to it.

### Rejected
- Hermes' tightening rewrites for verses 1–2 (all preserved the images less precisely), the reorder moving the hammer-handle line to verse-end (June's finisher must stay "She is not leaving this floor"), and his "it's a dance" alternative.
- Seed's "ghost town" rewrite of Ruby's museum line (the museum is the precise fear; ghost town is the cliché), and its hammer-handle replacement.

## Why this song, in the end
The essay's case, tested against both advisors and my own gut: these families are dancing inside a transmission they cannot verify will continue. They don't need to be told it's fragile. They need the count, the call, the circle, the lift — the message landing in the legs before the ears. The floor's wear is not damage; it is memory that only exists while boots keep making it. That is the fleet's line, in the families' register, and the room will rise to it.
