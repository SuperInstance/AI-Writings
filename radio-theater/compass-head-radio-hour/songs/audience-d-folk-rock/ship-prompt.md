## Full prompt text (as sent to ElevenLabs)

Compose a folk rock song titled "Hum It, Love" — a Sunday-evening listening-room closer for a church-basement folk audience, with light piano and three-part harmony. Key of G major, 88 BPM, 4/4, a rolling fingerpicked pulse like a song being remembered. Warm, intimate, acoustic; the sound of a room of people who know the words to old songs and hum when they don't.

FEEL ARC: intimate intro (piano alone remembering the tune, one warm voice enters, lamps low) → opening verse (solo voice, fingerpicked acoustic guitar joins, the room goes quiet) → first chorus (a second voice enters — the room leaning in) → quieter second verse (lead voice, light harmony on the last two lines) → second chorus (full three-part harmony, soft brushed percussion and upright bass enter — folk rock lift) → third verse (lead voice, the room swelling beneath, fuller guitar and bass) → contemplative bridge (drop to one voice and piano, near-silence — the oldest truth, the washboard and the grave) → final chorus (full three-part harmony, warm lift by joining not volume, the room's fourth part implied) → outro (three voices fold into one hum, then piano alone, the tune once more, fade).

SOUND PALETTE: fingerpicked acoustic guitar (close-mic'd, warm, slightly worn — the tool remembers the hand), light upright piano (slightly imperfect, felted, tuned to itself), soft brushed drums and gentle upright bass entering from the second chorus (the folk rock pulse, never loud), three-part vocal blend (lead with tight close harmony, like people leaning toward each other over a coffee carafe), church-basement room tone (chairs, air, warmth), dry and intimate — no big reverb, no arena. Open vowels in the chorus that a whole room can sing. Warm mid-low lead voice, two harmony voices joining one by one.
