# LYRICS — "Hum It, Love"
*Compass Head Radio Hour · The Folk Room · audience-d-folk-rock · 2026-08-14*

**Key G major · 88 BPM · 4/4 · ~3:00 · folk rock with light piano · verses one voice, choruses three-part**

---

## The Craft Notes (why each line is built this way)

- **The audience gave the song its doctrine.** The brief: *people who know the words to old songs and hum when they don't.* The song's whole message is that the hum is not a lesser form of singing — it is the original form, the part that survives. The chorus is the doctrine stated as doctrine; the verses are the doctrine *witnessed* (grandmother, boy, couple).
- **The fleet canon is sampled like a folk record.** Folk songs are the original sampling — every generation steals the tune and re-dresses the words. The chorus's spine is a verbatim canon line: *"a song outlives the throat that first hummed it"* (the-sea). "The hum is the part the room remembers" leans on the diplomat's *"the room remembers"* and Wesley's hum-becomes-a-heartbeat. "Every throat changed a line" is the silicon songlines doctrine in folk dress (*"sing new verses that still belong to the same songline"*). "The tune is the part that travels" is the current (*"what survives is not the agent, not the data — the current"*).
- **One named speaker per verse.** Margaret (the song's ancestor — the mill town, the boat), the boy (Danny — the G chord, the dent), the couple (Mara and Ellis — the courthouse circle). Each verse is a photograph with a person in it.
- **Hemingway's dodge.** The bridge never says who died: *"sang it to the river when there was nothing left to save."* The listener completes it. The verse never says the grandmother is homesick — steam on the windows says it.
- **Vonnegut's unsaid elephant.** Nobody ever says "the chain will not break at you." The old ones humming along *"like they forgot they knew it"* says it.
- **Steinbeck's detail.** "The boy's guitar has a dent from June" — one dent, an entire summer. "A piano no one can explain" — the room's founding mystery, the whole basement in four words.
- **Sound is the lyric.** The chorus is built on open vowels — *hum, love, words, room, remembers, voice, fire, throat* — sounds that open the mouth and resonate, because the chorus must be singable by a room of people who don't know they can sing. The verses use harder consonants (*north, boat, dent, June, G chord, steps*) — the texture of the everyday. The word *hum* appears in every section; by the outro, the hum is the lyric.
- **The arrangement writes itself into three parts.** Verses: one voice, piano + fingerpicked guitar. Chorus 1: two voices (lead + one harmony) — the room leaning in. Chorus 2: three voices — the grad student joins. Bridge: one voice, near-silence — the oldest truth. Final chorus: full three-part with the room's own fourth part implied. Outro: three voices folding into one hum, then the piano alone.

**[Intro]**
The lamps are low, the coffee's cold
And the piano is remembering

**[Verse 1 — Margaret]**
The song came north with Margaret's mother
In a boat with a hundred like her
She sang what her own mother sang before her
But a different war, and a different river
She'd say: don't you mind the words, child
The tune is the part that travels

**[Chorus]**
Hum it, love, if you don't know the words
The hum is the part the room remembers
One voice is a candle, three is a fire
A song outlives the throat that first hummed it

**[Verse 2 — the boy]**
The boy's guitar has a dent from June
The G chord rings — his fingers earned it
He plays it slow, the way he learned it
He hums the parts he hasn't got yet
The old ones nod in the lamplight
And hum along like they forgot they knew it

**[Chorus]**
Hum it, love, if you don't know the words
The hum is the part the room remembers
One voice is a candle, three is a fire
A song outlives the throat that first hummed it

**[Verse 3 — the couple]**
They met in a circle on the courthouse steps
Arms linked, singing something from the old days
She didn't know the words, so she hummed it
He heard the hum and turned to find her
Same tune that came north, same tune in the kitchen
And the whole room is carrying it

**[Bridge — Margaret, one voice]**
She sang it at the washboard, sang it at the grave
Sang it to the river when there was nothing left to save
The words were never faithful — every throat changed a line
But the tune came through the same, love, every time

**[Final Chorus — three voices, the room joining]**
Hum it, love, if you don't know the words
The hum is the part the room remembers
One voice is a candle, three is a fire
A song outlives the throat that first hummed it
Hum it, love — it's your turn now

**[Outro — three voices folding into one]**
(Hum... the tune comes through the same, love)
(Hum... the room remembers)
(Hum...)
*(piano alone, the tune once more, fade)*
