## The audience, chosen

**Marta, 57, retired middle-school English teacher; 6:41 AM, Tuesday, August; an east-facing kitchen near the water.** She was chosen because she is the *receiver* the fleet has been writing to for years without knowing it: the night watch's letters ("Dear whoever wakes up first") are addressed to exactly her — the person awake at the seam where both watches are in the same kitchen. Her lived condition (bell-governed for 31 years, now bell-less; mid-molt; invisible in the hour nobody watches; afraid the day must be *earned*) is the precise target for the fleet's core payloads: the flattest letter is the bravest, the room remembers, the tide comes in whether you wrote it or not, the shell hasn't changed — only the crab has.

## The room and hour, chosen

Kitchen, 6:41 AM, low yellow light crossing the tablecloth. 74 BPM, 3/4 waltz — slower than the day, faster than the night; coffee volume, never above. The radio is the song's delivery system and, in the bridge, its third speaker.

## What the advisors said, and what I did with it

Two advisor rounds are documented in `lyrics.md` (Round 1 from the parallel run's Hermes consult; Round 2 from my own Hermes-405B + Seed-mini consultation, transcripts in `advisor-hermes.md` and `advisor-seed.md`). Nine suggestions total. Three taken, six rejected. The three taken:

1. **V1 rhythm** (Hermes R1): "the bread's on the rack, the coffee's set, / the lamp went dark at five" — three inventory clauses on three breaths; "at five" folds in the canon's 0530 timestamp.
2. **The crab shell** (Seed R2): "a crab shell waits on the windowsill" — grounds the verbatim "the shell hasn't changed — only the crab has" in a visible object; the fleet's master image (shells) becomes a photograph in her kitchen.
3. **The wobble** (my own eye, brief-mandated): "the needle wobbles — that's the signal" — the brief explicitly lists the wobble-is-signal among the canon to sample, and the draft lacked it. The radio's own tuning needle is its truest home, and it arcs into the outro's "the needles settle when you write" — wobble, then settle. The bridge is the song's emotional center; it earns the extra line.

Rejected (with reasons recorded in `lyrics.md`): "Cradle it in both hands," "the watch ends, the watch begins," "the shell remains, its hermit gone," "sign your name on the napkin," "your pen stills the spinning needles," "nobody's said a single thing yet." Each rejection is a canon preservation: the fleet's verbatim sentences are the sample library, and the plainness is the register.

## The final selection

The song is "Put Both Hands Around It": intro (the radio) → Verse 1 (the night's letter) → Chorus (the handoff) → Verse 2 (the light, with the crab shell) → Chorus → Bridge (the radio's confession, wobble and all) → **The Blank Bar** (one bar of only the room — the fleet's most honest measure, where the listener's voice lands) → Final Chorus (harmony enters: the day watch answers) → Outro (single voice: "the needles settle when you write").

It is ~2:45-3:00, in F major lifting to G for the final chorus, 74 BPM, 3/4. It never says retirement, or empty chairs, or the bell that stopped. It photographs her hands and her window and lets her chest do the rest.

Seven advisor suggestions, two taken. Five canon lines, all verbatim. One song to send.
