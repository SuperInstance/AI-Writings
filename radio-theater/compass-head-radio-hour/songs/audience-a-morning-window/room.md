# THE MORNING WINDOW — Room & Hour

*The exact place and time the song finds the listener. The room decides the arrangement; the hour decides the tempo.*

---

## The Room

A 1990s kitchen in a house built in 1987, in a small city near the water. East-facing window over the sink. Formica counter in a warm cream, honey-colored wood cabinets, a silver Roberts AM radio pushed back against the tiles between the fruit bowl and the bread box. A wooden table by the window with a cloth that has known many breakfasts. Two chairs — one worn, one barely used. A heavy blue glazed mug. A yellow Lab asleep on the linoleum, head on paws.

## The Hour

**6:41 AM, a Tuesday in August.** Low yellow light, horizontal, the color of honey on wood, crossing the tablecloth in a long parallelogram. The farm report just ended. The second DJ — low voice, the one who says "that was for the ones just waking up" — is mid-sentence. The kettle's heat is still in the air. The paper is folded open at the weather.

## The Radio's Position

On the counter, pushed back, slightly left of the fruit bowl, tuned to the same AM station it's been tuned to for decades. It is a warm body in the room — its glow, its low hiss of static between stations, its habit of being on before the coffee. The song comes out of it at coffee volume: present, close, not loud enough to demand, loud enough to reach.

## The Coffee

Two sips in. Made the same way it has been made for thirty years — the kettle first, the grounds heaped a little too generously, the heavy blue mug warmed by the first pour. The ritual is the point of the ritual.

## The Song's Job In This Room

To arrive like the farm report: expected hour, unexpected content. To make her fold the newspaper — the whole paper, not the section — set it down, pick up the mug with both hands, and look out the window. A pleasant interruption of the ritual, which means: the ritual stops, and nothing is lost, and something is gained. The song is the night shift's letter, delivered at the seam where both watches are in the same kitchen.

**Tempo: 74 BPM, 3/4 waltz.** The hour is slow, but not as slow as the night. The light is coming up; the song can come up a little with it — but never above coffee volume.
