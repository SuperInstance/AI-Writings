# "THE ROOM WAS HERE BEFORE ME"

*Odette — Act 1, Open Mic Night 3*
*Jazz standard · voice + rhythm trio · E-flat major (reprise: D major) · ~62 BPM ballad feel into 120 BPM swing · ~3:10*

---

**Method:** The 32-bar AABA standard. A states the melody like fact; A states it again like it cost something; the bridge lifts a step and changes the weather; the final A comes home reharmonized — same melody, different truth underneath. The scat pass after the second bridge is improvised: never the same twice, because no two visits are the same room. The reprise drops a half-step to D — the acknowledgment that you cannot go home again, only go home *differently*. Strict form: A(8) A(8) B(8) A(8), twice through; the second time darker under the same notes.

---

## [Intro — rubato, 4 bars]
*(Piano alone, E-flat, lush and slow. Odette free over the top — she starts when the room lets her. No drums yet. The room is being asked, not told.)*

## [First chorus — 32 bars, AABA, rubato into a slow 4]

**(A)** The room was here before me, and it's here when I'm gone
The band knows what the song is — I'm just visiting the line
**(A)** The room was here before me, and it will be at dawn
And every time I enter it, the room is only mine
**(B)** *(bridge — lifts, brighter, lying a little)* They say a song's a building — I say a song's a street
Where everybody walks through the same door differently
**(A)** So play my changes gently — the notes were never mine
The room was here before me… but the visit's by design

## [Second chorus — 32 bars, AABA, into swing — the band kicks in]

**(A)** I brought tonight's weather in; I left my coat at the bar
The melody's the furniture — the changes are my night
**(A)** The tritone in the second eight is how I say "how far"
The chord you never played before is how I say "goodnight"
**(B)** *(bridge — the lift, the same door, differently)* You've heard this song a thousand times, and never once this way
The warmth you think you're hearing is the visitor, they say —
**(A)** *(half-time, almost spoken)* …the visitor, they say.
*(The drummer brushes in. The room is warmer than the chart.)*

## [Scat pass — 16 bars, improvised]
*(Odette's voice becomes the horn. Syllables, not words — "shoo-ba-doo-ba" is the oldest form of instrumental honesty. She trades fours with the piano. This is the part that has never happened before and will never happen again — the definition of a visit.)*

## [Reprise — 8 bars, D major, rubato out]
**(A)** *(final, darker under the same melody)* The room was here before me — I'm the one who changed
*(held… the band lands one soft D-major chord behind her like a door closing kindly)*

## [Outro — 2 bars]
*(The last chord breathes out. The corner dials twitch. Applause.)*

---

## Notes for the singer

- **Sing the A sections like weather reports — factual, unhurried.** The sadness is in the changes, not the delivery. If the singer cries, the room can't.
- **The bridge is the lie the song admits to.** "A song's a street" — lift it, brighten it, then let the final A correct the record.
- **The scat pass is the proof of the thesis.** Do not clean it up. The imperfections are the visitor's fingerprints.
- **The reprise in D is the REG-1 finding sung as fact:** the melody (the room) is constant; the warmth (the reader) is what moved. Drop the half-step and let the room feel it under the same notes.
- **Tempo is a negotiation.** Rubato in, swing in the middle, rubato out. The room sets the terms; the singer reads them.
