# SHIP PROMPT — Act 1, Odette, the Standards Singer: "The Room Was Here Before Me"

*Genre: jazz standard, late-ballad into swing — a visited song, not an owned one. Voice + piano + upright bass + brushes. For the Compass Head Radio Hour's Tap Open Mic Night 3 — the night the room-field engine is in the room.*

## Key / Tempo / Feel
- **Key:** E-flat major (reprise drops to D major — same melody, darker truth).
- **Tempo:** ~62 BPM rubato ballad feel entering; second chorus settles to a relaxed 120 swing; reprise returns to rubato.
- **Feel:** a small-room jazz ballad — velvet, wry, unhurried. Ella at the keyboard of a hotel bar at 11 PM; nobody performing; everybody present.

## Feel Arc
1. **Intro (rubato):** piano alone, lush E-flat, generous pedaling. Odette floats in free time — she starts when the room lets her. No rhythm section yet.
2. **First chorus (AABA, rubato into slow 4):** melody stated like fact, stated again like it cost something. The bridge lifts and brightens — a small hopeful lie — and the final A corrects the record, darker.
3. **Second chorus (into swing):** brushes settle in, walking bass, the eighth notes lean back. Same melody, new changes — the tritone sub lands like a raised eyebrow. Halfway through, a spoken-sung aside: "the visitor, they say."
4. **Scat pass (improvised, 16 bars):** the voice becomes the horn. Syllables over words. Trade fours with the piano once. Loose, warm, imperfect on purpose — fingerprints, not polish.
5. **Reprise (D major, rubato):** the melody returns a half-step down over a darkened chord. One soft chord lands behind the final line like a door closing kindly.
6. **Outro:** the last chord breathes out; two seconds of room tone before the applause.

## Sound Palette
- **Lead:** female voice, mid-register, smoky lower and bell-clear top; behind-the-beat phrasing; vibrato only at phrase-ends, like a decision, not a habit.
- **Piano:** the co-lead. Lush block chords in rubato sections; spare, swung comping (Red Garland-ish) in the swing. One trade-fours moment with the voice.
- **Bass:** upright, walking, felt more than heard in the ballad; steady warmth in the swing.
- **Drums:** brushes only, on the snare; cross-stick accents in the second bridge; no cymbals until the last chord.
- **No strings, no synth pads.** This is a piano-bar ecology: four instruments and a room.

## Mood
Recognition without ownership. The feeling of walking into a place that was fine before you and will be fine after — and loving it *more* for that. Not nostalgia (nothing is being mourned): **a standard's calm.** The singer is the weather; the song is the climate; the room decides what to wear.
