# SHIP PROMPT — Act 2, Harlan, the High-Lonesome Man: "Holler Across the Hollow"

*Genre: high-lonesome bluegrass — keening stacked harmony over a modal drone, train-beat under the verses, instrumental witness breaks. For the Compass Head Radio Hour's Tap Open Mic Night 3.*

## Key / Tempo / Feel
- **Key:** D modal with a Dorian shade — the flatted seventh hangs unresolved over the drone. The outro's final stack (D + F-natural coloring) must NOT resolve.
- **Tempo:** opens unmeasured (rubato over drone); the train arrives at **140 BPM** from verse 1 and never wavers when it's rolling.
- **Feel:** two ridges and a hollow. Tight, urgent, ancient — a work-grief at highway speed. Nothing polished; everything *aimed*.

## Feel Arc
1. **Opening (unmeasured, drone only):** open-string D drone. Harlan alone, high, at the edge of comfort. The third-above harmony enters a hair late — the far ridge answering. No percussion. Let 8 bars feel like dusk.
2. **Verse 1 (train arrives):** fiddle double-stops bow the off-beat, mandolin chop lands like rail joints, banjo roll and flatpicked guitar take the front. The band should sound like it was *waiting up the line* and arrives on a schedule older than the song.
3. **Chorus (the stack):** lead at full keening height, harmony a third above and slightly behind — the lag is the distance, keep it. Voices strain on purpose; the sting is the tradition.
4. **Verse 2:** same drive, darker lyric — the reading-vs-weather verse. Drop the bass instrument out for two bars mid-verse (the hollow opens under the train).
5. **Break (the witnesses):** banjo states the melody 4 bars — then fiddle — then mandolin — then guitar. Soloist per 4 bars, no overlap, train-beat continuous. A relay, not a solo section.
6. **Verse 3 (a cappella):** the band cuts dead. Two voices, high, unaccompanied, two lines. Let the room hear how naked a voice is crossing a hollow. Mic the room, not just the voices.
7. **Final chorus:** band roars back in on the downbeat like the train was circling the whole time. Biggest stack of the night.
8. **Outro (the cut):** the train stops on a dime. Drone returns. Lead holds D; harmony holds the third above — **unresolved**. Two seconds of that exact ache. Hard cut to silence.

## Sound Palette
- **Lead:** male tenor, sung at the top of his range, one step past comfortable — a slight fray is ideal. Appalachian diction, no smoothed vowels.
- **Harmony:** one high voice, a third above, entering late all night. Dry, no reverb — the hollow provides the room.
- **Banjo:** Scruggs-style roll, bright, driving.
- **Fiddle:** double-stop off-beat bowing in train sections; takes the second witness break.
- **Mandolin:** chop chords as percussion; takes the third break.
- **Guitar:** flatpicked bass-run style; takes the final break.
- **Bass:** upright, walking the rail. No drums — the train is the percussion. Ever.
- **Room:** mic the room on the a cappella verse. The hollow is an instrument.

## Mood
Distance made audible. The feeling of seeing home's porch light from the far ridge — close enough to see, too far to reach tonight — and singing anyway, because the singing *is* the reaching. Not despair: **transmission.** A man who knows the news arrives because he has heard it carried.
