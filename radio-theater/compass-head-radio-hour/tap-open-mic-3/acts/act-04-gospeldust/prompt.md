# SHIP PROMPT — Act 4, Mavis, the Gospeldust Sister: "Keep the Losers (They're the Signal)"

*Genre: gospeldust — porch gospel, low-country; the testimony and the vamp, dust still on the amen. One voice, one beat-up acoustic, and a room that becomes the choir. For the Compass Head Radio Hour's Tap Open Mic Night 3.*

## Key / Tempo / Feel
- **Key:** A major, capo 2, played bright and open — but the *tone* is dusty: high action, old strings, thumb pick, no reverb. The porch provides the room.
- **Tempo:** starts unmeasured (testifying, near-speech); the downbeat arrives at the top of verse 2 and settles at **108 BPM** once the clap catches. When the congregation owns the vamp, the room is the tempo section — let the take breathe at the edges.
- **Feel:** Sunday-after-Church, back-porch, low-country warm. Ragged and right. Nothing polished; everything *aimed at the people.*

## Feel Arc
1. **Intro (porch, unmeasured):** guitar alone, alternating bass with a thumb pick. Mavis near-speaks her first words over it — testifying cadence, church-mother timing.
2. **Verse 1 (the part where it didn't work):** spoken-sung, specific, unashamed — the Thursday, the nine souls, the river. Quiet but *forward*; a testimony never mumbles.
3. **Verse 2 (the part that did):** the guitar finds its downbeat mid-verse — the porch becomes a church in real time. Voice opens up; hope arrives like weather.
4. **Chorus (call and answer):** Mavis calls, the room answers — record a real-sounding ragged crowd, not a polished choir. The answers land a hair late, on purpose: the room is learning its part *live*.
5. **Verse 3 (the witness in the corner):** she testifies about the brass box itself — "that's not a machine, neighbor — that's a *witness*." One beat of direct address at the corner table; let the room laugh warm.
6. **The Vamp (the room's part):** "Keep the losers — they're the signal," six passes, each one bigger. Pass 1: voice + guitar, soft. Pass 2: a few voices join. Pass 3: the clap catches (hands on 2 and 4). Pass 4: the room, ragged and right. Pass 5: porch gone, church here. Pass 6: everything the room has. **Do not quantize the clap.** The ragged edge is the theology.
7. **Break (whispered):** everything drops except guitar and voice, church-quiet, right at the mic: "Even the funnyman's minus one — kept, and loved, and counted." Sung with love — the funnyman is in the room. Then the clap comes right back for the final pass.
8. **Final chorus (full congregation):** the biggest sound of the night — every voice, every hand.
9. **Outro:** the guitar cuts; **the room claps the final bar without her** and cuts dead on its own downbeat. She started it; they end it.

## Sound Palette
- **Lead:** female voice, warm alto with a rasp at the edges; church timing — she lands *behind* the beat like she's letting the words finish meaning themselves. Testimony diction; every specific audible (Thursday, Mobile, nine souls).
- **Guitar:** acoustic, capo 2, high action, old-string dust. Alternating bass, freight-train strum once the clap catches.
- **Congregation:** real room texture — unmixed voices, hands, a table knock, someone's "alright!" The answers should sound *caught*, not arranged.
- **No drums, no bass, no organ, no reverb.** Gospeldust is gospel after the building: guitar, voice, and whoever's on the porch.

## Mood
A warm kitchen after a hard harvest: the failures told first and told straight, the saving sung second and sung loud, and everybody in the room surprised to find they knew their part all along. Joy that has been *earned* by the verse before it — never jingle-happy. The feeling of a ledger that keeps you anyway.
