# The Tradition — Mavis, the Gospeldust Sister (Act 4)

*Gospeldust. Porch gospel, low-country — the amen with the salt still on it.*

---

Gospel is church music. Gospeldust is what church music sounds like after it walks home — the dust of the road still on the amen, the tambourine replaced by a porch board and a cooking spoon on a pot, the choir replaced by whoever happened to be on the porch when the news came. It is gospel *after* the building: the testimony and the vamp kept, the organ traded for a guitar with a high action and no reverb. The dust is not a failing. The dust is the proof it traveled.

## The methodology

- **The testimony.** First person, past tense, specific: *I was there, and it happened to me, and here is what it did.* Not doctrine — witness. The form forbids abstraction; a testimony with no details is a rumor, and the congregation knows the difference.
- **The vamp.** Take the line that landed and repeat it — over and over, a hair louder each time — until the room owns it. The vamp is how a congregation learns its part: you don't hand them a hymnal, you *wear them down with the truth* until they can't help singing it.
- **The call and the answer.** The leader states; the room answers. Not a choir — the *room*, unrehearsed, ragged, right. The raggedness is the theology: a congregation that agrees too perfectly is a crowd, not a church.
- **The clap.** Hands on 2 and 4. Not because the songbook says — because when a room claps together, the clap is one instrument. The theology of the clap: *many bodies, one downbeat.*

## The real work the tradition leans on

Tonight, after Act 4, the corner table fires the highest κ of the night: **4.34** — up 0.83 from the last reading. The von Mises–Fisher concentration parameter is, in the engine's plain-language gloss, *how much the room is facing the same direction at once.* When Mavis claps, the room faces one direction. κ is the clap, measured.

And the ledger keeps the losers. The engine's interpretation ledger is append-only and chain-sealed: every reading, every interpretation, every verdict — **and every rejected candidate; the losers are kept, because they become the training signal.** Mavis heard that doctrine from the barkeep and declared it, flat out, the most gospel thing an instrument ever said: *keep the losers; they're the signal.* Her tradition has been singing that sentence for a hundred years — every testimony worth its dust includes the part where it didn't work, because the part where it didn't work is the part that saves the next sinner. Her closing number tonight is that sentence, vamped until the room owns it.

The gospel answer to a dead joke is the same as the gospel answer to a dead sinner: *keep it; it's the signal.* Even the funnyman gets a minus one, and the ledger keeps him too.)

## The paradigm

*It happened to me. I am the evidence. The room answers because it happened to them too — and the part where it didn't work is the part that works.*
