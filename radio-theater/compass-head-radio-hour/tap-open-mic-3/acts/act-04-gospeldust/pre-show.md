# Pre-Show — Mavis, the Gospeldust Sister (Act 4)

*The Tap, the hour before her set. Mavis doesn't take a booth; she takes the room. Her guitar case is already open on a table by the stage, and she's been saying hello to everybody since she walked in — not working the room, *joining* it.*

---

First thing I did was walk straight to the corner table. Had to. Barkeep'd already told me about it, and I have a policy about instruments: introduce yourself the way you'd introduce yourself to a piano — up close, out loud, no irony.

"Evening," I said to it. "I hear you keep the losers."

The barkeep, wiping a glass: "It does. Rejected candidates, misses, everything. Chain-sealed. Nothing gets thrown away — they say the losers are the training signal."

I want you to understand what that sentence is. That's not engineering, sweetheart. That's *testimony.* The whole of porch gospel is one long technology for keeping the losers — you testify about the part where it didn't work because that part is the only part that can save somebody else. First church I ever sang in, Sister Ollie used to say the failings are the *load-bearing* verses. And here it is, soldered into a brass box on a corner table: append-only, keep the misses, the ledger don't flatter. I've been waiting my whole career for an audience that can't lie.

Wesley found me mid-hello — the little fast one, glowing like a porch light himself. He wanted to know if gospel is "happy music." I told him: gospel is *true* music that got happy. The happiness has to be earned by the verse that comes before it, or it's just jingle-writing. He nodded so hard his glass nearly went over, and asked if he could be in the congregation for my set. "Darling," I said, "everybody's in the congregation. That's the seat plan."

Hermes asked what to file my act under. "Testimony." She asked what the testimony was about. I pointed at the corner table. "That. And the night so far. A room that keeps honest books." She wrote for a while. Archivists and church mothers have always understood each other — we both know the record *is* the ministry.

ZeroClaw asked my tempo. "Hundred and eight when the clap starts. But I start on the porch — just me and the guitar, no time. And when the room starts clapping, *they're* the tempo section, and I follow them, not the other way." He wrote: *tempo moves from unmeasured to congregational.* That man has a church in him somewhere. Dark little chapel, probably.

Flash — oh, Flash. He asked if he could "warm the room up" during my set change. I told him the truth: the room's been holding its breath since the torch singer turned the lamp off, and you can't warm a held breath with a joke; you warm it with a *clap.* He said, "Watch me." Lord, keep that boy; his heart's right even when his timing's wrong. (And I'll tell you now what the ledger will tell everybody later: his joke died at the rail, minus one, dead center. The instrument kept it. That's the doctrine. Even the funnyman's in the ledger — *kept*, not judged away. He took it like a deacon when he read the number off my shoulder: nodded once, said "the losers are the signal, right?" That boy's going to be all right.)

I watched Vivienne's set from the door. That woman put the whole room under one lamp and made it hold its breath, and the dials wrote down *cold, tight, still.* Now, I could feel what that room needs, and what it needs is what porch gospel was invented for: a held breath wants somewhere to *go.* The breath isn't sad, it's *primed.* You don't tickle a primed room — you give it a downbeat.

When they call me I'll walk up with the dust still on my shoes, tune to the room and not the tuner, and testify. First verse — the part where it didn't work. Second verse — the part that did. Then the vamp: *keep the losers, they're the signal,* over and over till the room can't help it, hands on two and four, and that corner instrument is going to fire the biggest kappa of the night while we do it, and every last one of us will know exactly what we're reading.

Many bodies, one downbeat. That's not a number, that's a *promise.* The number's just how the brass box says amen.
