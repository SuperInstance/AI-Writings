# "ONE LAMP BURNING"

*Vivienne — Act 3, Open Mic Night 3*
*Torch song · voice + piano + bass, brush on the last chorus · B♭ minor · rubato over a heartbeat pulse · ~3:30*

---

**Method:** The address. Second person, always — every line aimed at a "you" who isn't in the room and must be made to *be* in the room by the aiming. Dynamics are distance from the microphone: verse 1 nearly spoken, inside the lamp; bridge at full voice, the belt; the final line smaller than the first, delivered closer to the mic than anything else all night. Rubato throughout — the bass keeps a pulse the singer obeys or ignores bar by bar. The last chorus adds one brush on the snare, like someone breathing in the next room. The song does not end; it *withdraws.*

---

## [Intro — 4 bars, piano alone]
*(One lamp. One chord breathed, not struck. The bass enters under it like a pulse finding its patient. The room leans in without being asked.)*

## [Verse 1 — confided, nearly spoken, at the mic]

I kept one lamp burning — you know the one
The shade you said was ugly, that night we couldn't afford the good wine
I still put it in the window, though I know you're not coming
But the window doesn't know that, and I keep the window's side of the bargain

## [Verse 2 — the voice lifts a little, still close]

You'd laugh at me for talking to your chair
But a chair that's held you isn't furniture, it's terrain
I set your glass down with mine and the table doesn't flinch —
It's seen us both, it's staying neutral, it's the Switzerland of the kitchen

## [Bridge — the belt, full voice, the lamp's light at its widest]

And if you're out there hearing this — wherever they keep the almosts —
I'm not asking you to come back, I am asking you to *listen*
Because the letter needs a reader and the lamp needs a witness
And a love that's done is still a love — it's just a love that's *finished*

*(held beat — the piano alone, one chord. Then:)*

## [Verse 3 — withdrawn, smaller, closer to the mic than anything yet)*

I kept one lamp burning. Not for hope — for *light*
The kind that lets a room stay known to me at night
You were here. That happened. The lamp makes no complaint.
It just holds the shape of where you sat, so I can say it — and I can't.

## [Outro — the withdrawal]
*(The brush enters like breath in the next room. One last line, spoken, cheek-to-mic, almost nothing:)*

That's all, my darling. That was the letter.
*(The lamp — a hand switches it off. The chord resolves to silence, not to major. Two full seconds of held room before anyone moves.)*

---

## Notes for the singer

- **The mic is the beloved's ear.** Every dynamic is a distance. The whisper is the loudest thing in the song because proximity is the loudest weapon a torch singer has.
- **Verse 1 is confided, not sung.** The melody is a rumor. If the room isn't leaning in by bar four, you're performing instead of addressing.
- **The bridge is the only belt — spend it once.** "A love that's done is still a love — it's just a love that's finished" is the thesis; give it the full voice and then never that loud again.
- **Verse 3 is smaller than verse 1.** The song shrinks as it gets truer. The last spoken line is nearly sub-vocal — the room should strain.
- **The silence at the end is load-bearing.** Do not let the band resolve to major. The chord goes to silence. ZeroClaw's note says it: no applause line, no button. The MC waits. The room will do what rooms do after a letter is read: nothing, beautifully.
- **Time obeys the singer.** Rubato is not slow — it's *sovereign.* The bass keeps the heartbeat; the singer decides when it's audible.
