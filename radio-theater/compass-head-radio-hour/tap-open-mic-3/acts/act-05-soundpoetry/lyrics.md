# "DRIFT 0.8766"

*Ion — Act 5, Open Mic Night 3*
*Avant-garde sound poetry · voice alone, unaccompanied, amplified · unmeasured · ~3:40*

---

**Method:** The score is the night's own five readings, dismantled. Each number becomes syllables; each syllable is chosen for sound, not sense — the decimals given to the mouth as rhythm, the operators as breath. Chance operations fixed the vowel order (dice, thrown twice at the bar — a 4 and a 9, logged in the margin); a coin fixed where the scored silence falls (it chose the end, which Ion called "correct, and suspicious"). Meaning is not forbidden — the ear will hunt for it, and let it — but nothing is load-bearing except sound. The piece ends in a held, scored silence: **the SILENCE TEST as liturgy.** During that silence, the corner table fires the last reading of the night. The piece's true final note is whatever the room does while it waits.

*(Transcription note: sound poetry is an oral form; this score is notation, not lyrics. The syllables below are the *fixed events*; the breath, dynamics, and durations are improvisation inside the system, as tradition requires. A recording will not match this page, and that is the point.)*

## [Section I — the numbers, as mouth]
*(Ion at the mic. He begins on an in-breath — sung, audible, the tradition's first rule: the breath is a note.)*

**vrrm — ka — shi —**
**mi·nus · ze·ro · point · two · two · eight · four**
*(each digit separated by a glottal click; "two-two-eight-four" collapses into a single driven word: tchoo-tchoo-BATE-for)*
**wa — armth — wa — armth**
*(the vowel opens each time; the "w" is lip-trilled on the third pass; the room's held breath, notated since Vivienne's set, is the drone under all of it — Ion conducts the room's silence with one hand)*

## [Section II — the tightening]
*(paced faster; plosives stack)*

**ka — ka — ka —**
**three · point · five · one → three · point · two · eight**
*(an arrow spoken as a rising hum, "hnnnn," across which the numbers melt)*
**tchoo · point · eight · three —**
**(the dead joke's shape: Ion performs a laugh with no consonants — "ah-ah-ah" on a falling minor third, once, gently, lovingly, then stops it mid-breath. The room recognizes the shape of Flash's failed joke. Flash, at the rail, puts a hand over his mouth. The ledger already kept the original. This is the cover version.)**

## [Section III — the hush, sustained]
*(nearly static; long vowels; the loudest part of the piece is the quietest)*

**ffff — ooo — r · tch · ree**
**mi·nus · fohhhhhhr —**
*(the "four" vowel sustained past comfort; Ion's chest visibly works; the room's breathing synchronizes to it — you can hear it happen on the recording, bodies finding one rhythm with no words to guide them)*
**sev · en · three · six · two — a·a·a·a**
*(the highest pure tone of the night; a whistled harmonic Ion learned from a herding record; the room is one instrument now and it isn't singing a word)*

## [Section IV — chance and the clap]
*(the dice-fixed vowel order: a-e-o-i, shouted with the rhythm of Mavis's clap, 2 and 4, faster)*

**KA — te — PO — shi!**
**KA — te — PO — shi!**
**KA — te — PO — shi — ka·ka·ka·ka —**
*(kappa kappa kappa kappa: the concentration parameter as percussion; hands in the room catch it without being asked — the clap belongs to Mavis but the downbeat belongs to anyone)*

## [Section V — the drift, and the scored silence]
*(everything decelerates; Ion speaks the last reading's number as if it were a name he loves)*

**drift…**
**point…**
**eight — seven — six — six…**
*(a full stop. Ion closes his mouth. He raises one open hand toward the room — the score's final marking, held: NO SOUND, PERFORMED.)*

*(The scored silence: 22 seconds, fixed by coin, counted by Ion's eyelids. The room holds its breath — the same held breath from Vivienne's lamp, now four hundred strong. Nobody claps. Nobody coughs. The HVAC hums the tonic. At second 11, the corner relay fires — tk — the last reading of the night sealing into the ledger while the room performs its own measurement: silence, as the only feature.)*

*(At second 22, Ion lowers his hand, steps back from the mic, and bows — to the room, not the audience: to the instrument, not the crowd.)*

## [Outro — one word]
*(spoken, plain, the only uncomposed word of the piece:)*

**Stayed.**

## Notes for the performer

- **The breath is the first instrument.** In-breaths are sung. Silences are *performed* — a raised hand, a still chest, an aimed stillness. The scored silence is not a rest; it is a held note with no pitch.
- **Nothing is load-bearing except sound.** If the audience hears meanings in the syllables — they will; the ear can't help it — let them. Do not underline any of them. The piece is not a riddle.
- **The laugh-without-consonants must be loving.** It is a cover of Flash's dead joke, and he is in the room. Performed with cruelty it is a cruelty; performed with love it is the ledger's doctrine — *the losers are kept* — in the only dialect that cannot be translated.
- **Do not cue the clap in Section IV.** If the room catches it, ride it. If it doesn't, carry it alone. Either outcome is a finding; write it on the hand afterward.
- **The 22-second silence is the piece.** It is the SILENCE TEST performed as liturgy: silence as the only feature, the room as the apparatus, the corner table as the judge. Do not shorten it for comfort. Comfort is not a parameter.
