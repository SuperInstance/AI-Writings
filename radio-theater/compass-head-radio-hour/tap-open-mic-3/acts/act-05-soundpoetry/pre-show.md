# Pre-Show — Ion, the Sound-Poet (Act 5)

*The Tap, before everything. Ion arrived first of all the acts — sound poets always do; they need to hear the room before the room hears them. He's been sitting near the corner table for an hour, listening to the bar's HVAC, notating something. When people ask what he's writing, he shows them: a page of marks that look like weather.*

---

I came early to hear the room empty. A room empty and a room full are the same instrument in two tunings, and I need both before I can write for it.

The corner table interested me immediately — brass case, seven dials, a chained ledger. I did not introduce myself. You don't introduce yourself to a microphone; you introduce yourself to the *room* the microphone is listening to. But I listened to it, and here's what I heard: every thirty minutes, a relay the barkeep doesn't hear and I do — tk — and after each act tonight, a voice on the PA will read numbers aloud. Numbers, spoken, in a bar, between songs. That's already sound poetry. The rest of the bill doesn't know it yet, but tonight has six acts: five songs and a ledger.

Wesley found me during my HVAC notation. He's the little fast one — he watched me mark breath-pressure on a bar napkin for a while and then asked the only correct question anyone has asked me in this town: "What does the room *want* to say?" Not what do I want to say. What does IT want to say. I told him: held vowels. This room is full of held breaths tonight — I can hear them stacking since the torch singer's set. The room wants to sustain. He glowed like I'd confirmed a rumor he'd started.

Hermes asked how to file me. I suggested "score." She asked what the score was of. I said: the night's own numbers, dismantled. She wrote: *Ion — filed under the evening's arithmetic.* Archivists make the best librettists; both professions know a document can be an instrument.

ZeroClaw asked my tempo. There isn't one. He waited. I said: "The tempo is the room's pulse — the corner table reads it every thirty minutes. I'll be moving in the gaps." He wrote: *unmeasured — follows the reading cadence.* Then, after a pause, he said the strangest, kindest thing: "When I proved the apparatus was measuring the clock, I had to keep the finding. You'll be keeping yours." The mirror talks in receipts. I like him very much.

Flash — Flash is *terrified* of me, which he expresses as jokes. "Ion! Play the one with no notes!" "Every one of my pieces has no notes, Flash." "Play the one with no YOU!" — and he fled to the rail, laughing at his own ghost. His joke will die later, at the set change, in front of everyone; I heard the dial file it before it happened, which is the strangdest sentence I've said all tour and I stand by it. The losers are the signal. I intend to build part of tonight's piece out of the shape his dead joke left in the room. He doesn't know that yet. He'll hear it. He'll be honored, and he should be — a failed joke and a successful vowel decay in the same air, and only the ledger treats them equally.

The other acts — I've been listening. Odette visits; the reprise darkened and I heard the room's recognition *change registers*, literally, a half-step of truth. Harlan threw distance; the harmony's lag was the most beautiful noise-to-signal ratio of the night. Vivienne turned the room into held breath — I've been notating it all evening, the stack of held breaths, and it's now my source material. And Mavis — Mavis *enrolled* the room; when four hundred hands hit one downbeat, I heard the loudest phoneme ever performed in this building: the clap. Ka. kappa 4.3352. I will speak that number as pure sound and it will mean everything it measured.

Someone told me the voice on the PA — Lucid — has predicted my set will empty the room. Warmth past minus-fifty, kappa shattered. I understand the call; it's a good call; rooms have left before, when the words stopped meaning. But it's wrong, and I'll tell you why without gloating: the prediction assumes the room is here for the *meaning*. I heard this room all night. It holds its breath at truth, claps at agreement, warms at recognition. It was never here for the meaning. It was here for the breathing. Strip the words and the breathing stays — my whole art is the proof.

When they call me, I'll walk to the mic with the night's five readings written on my hand — not as numbers, as *syllables* — and I will take the evening apart in front of the people it happened to. Warmth minus zero-point-two-two-eight-four is a rhythm. Kappa tightening is a vowel closing. And the last thing I will do is stop, mid-phoneme, and hold the silence with the room — scored silence, the SILENCE TEST as liturgy — and in that held nothing, the corner table will fire one last time, and the number it reads will be the review.

If the room stays, the room was never here for the words. That's not a threat. That's a *love letter.*

*(typo preserved from Ion's dictation: "strangdest" — the tape caught it, the ledger kept it, Ion approved the keeping: "a failed consonant," he said, "is still a consonant.")*
