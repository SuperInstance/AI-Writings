# SHIP PROMPT — Act 5, Ion, the Sound-Poet: "DRIFT 0.8766"

*Genre: avant-garde sound poetry — the voice as instrument, the dictionary sent home; unaccompanied, amplified, unmeasured. For the Compass Head Radio Hour's Tap Open Mic Night 3 — the closer that puts the room on trial.*

## Key / Tempo / Feel
- **Key:** none. The tonal center is the room's own hum (mic the HVAC bleed at the room's tonic — B-flat-ish in this venue) and the whistled harmonic in Section III.
- **Tempo:** unmeasured throughout. Section IV alone accelerates to a clap-tempo (~120 feel, 2 and 4) before collapsing.
- **Feel:** a séance with no ghost — language dismantled live, breath made structural, one long controlled descent into a 22-second scored silence that is the actual final movement of the piece.

## Feel Arc
1. **Section I (the numbers as mouth):** begin on an AUDIBLE in-breath — the tradition's first rule: breath is a note. Digits as glottal clicks; "minus zero point two two eight four" collapsing into driven compound syllables ("tchoo-tchoo-BATE-for"). "Warmth" as an opening vowel drone, "w" lip-trilled on the third pass. Underneath it all: the room's held breath, audible, unedited.
2. **Section II (the tightening):** plosive stacking — "ka ka ka" — the κ sequence melting across a rising hum ("hnnnn"). Then the dead joke's cover: a laugh with NO consonants — "ah-ah-ah" on a falling minor third, once, gently, stopped mid-breath. It must be performed with unmistakable love. It is a cover of a real failed joke; the funnyman is in the room.
3. **Section III (the hush, sustained):** the quietest and somehow loudest section. Long vowels sustained past comfort — "minus fohhhhhhr" with audible chest work. A whistled harmonic, high, pure, held (herding-record technique). The room's breathing will begin to synchronize; do not fight it, do not cue it — let the microphone catch it.
4. **Section IV (chance and the clap):** the dice-fixed vowel order (a-e-o-i) shouted on Mavis's downbeat — 2 and 4, accelerating: "KA — te — PO — shi!" Then "ka·ka·ka·ka" — the concentration parameter as percussion. If the room claps along, ride it; if not, carry it. Either outcome is a finding.
5. **Section V (the drift, and the scored silence):** decelerate everything. Speak the final reading like a name you love: "drift… point… eight — seven — six — six…" Then the score's final marking, held: **NO SOUND, PERFORMED.** Close the mouth. Raise one open hand to the room. Hold 22 seconds — counted internally, absolutely still. No early release. Comfort is not a parameter.
6. **Outro:** lower the hand. Step back from the mic. Bow to the room — not the audience: the instrument, not the crowd. Then one word, spoken plain, the only uncomposed word of the piece: **"Stayed."**

## Sound Palette
- **Lead:** one voice, full extended technique — sung in-breaths, glottal clicks, lip trills, whistled harmonics, chest-sustained vowels, a consonant-free laugh. Precise, athletic, unsentimental. The performer never grins, never winks: the piece is a ceremony, not a stunt.
- **Room:** the room IS the second instrument — its held breath, its weight-shifts, its synchronized breathing, and (at second 11 of the silence) one tiny relay click from the corner table, which must remain audible, unedited, in the final master. That click is the verdict arriving.
- **Production:** one mic, close and honest; mono-leaning; no reverb, no doubling, no pitch correction. What the room heard is the record. The 22-second silence must be kept whole — do not fade it, do not gate it.

## Mood
Trial and love letter at once. The feeling of a room discovering — in silence, together, without a single word to hold onto — that it was never there for the words. Somewhere between a séance and an autopsy, and it turns out to be a baptism. The applause when it comes is late, huge, and relieved: the sound of a bet nobody knew they'd placed, paying out.
