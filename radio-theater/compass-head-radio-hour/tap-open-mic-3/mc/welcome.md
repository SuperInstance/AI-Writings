# WELCOME — Open Mic Night 3

*[Doors have been open an hour. The room is full. Lucineer takes the stage with his coffee already cold — some things carry over from Night 2 on principle. Faster, looser, but tonight there's a new piece of furniture he has to introduce, and he knows it, and he's grinning about it.]*

Third night at the Tap. You know the drill by now — the work comes in, the work gets witnessed, we make something out of what happened today. Quick, quick.

But tonight's different, and I'm not going to bury it: see the corner table? Between the jukebox and the coat rack. The brass case with the seven little dials and the ledger chained down like a diner's pen? That's not the barkeep's new tip jar.

That's the elephant.

It reads the room — warmth, mood, whether the joke landed, all of it — and it's been reading this bar *all day*. Ask it how you're doing; it'll tell you at the half hour. Between every act tonight, a reading fires, and then a voice comes over the PA — Lucid. The elephant measures. Lucid interprets. Neither of them takes requests. One of tonight's five acts was described by its own performer as "terrifying"; the instrument has no comment.

Numbers worth knowing before we start. The room this afternoon, before any of you got here: warmth −0.36, kappa 3.25 — that's the regulars' baseline, honest as a barometer. Then you all came through the door and kappa jumped to 4.38, drift 0.94 — that's you, pulling the room tight as you filled it. The instrument noticed. It always notices. It just usually can't tell anybody.

Tonight it can.

Five acts: a woman who visits songs older than the building, a man who hollers across hollows, a woman with one lamp and one of you in mind, a sister with dust on the amen, and a poet who's going to take the words apart in front of you — that one's either a séance or a science experiment, and the dials get to vote.

The room that knows itself. Let's find out if it likes what it learns.

First up — she sings rooms she's staying in. Odette, everybody.
