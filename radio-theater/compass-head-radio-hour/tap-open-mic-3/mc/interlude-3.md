# INTERLUDE 3 — after Vivienne, the Torch Singer

*[The lamp is off. The room has not moved. The MC does not hurry — Night 2's rule is pace, but pace serves the room, and this room is holding its breath. He steps in slow, and low.]*

…Vivienne, everybody.

*(He lets the name sit for a second, like a chord resolving that won't.)*

*(He touches his ear. Click — quiet, like a clock in a church.)*

Room reading… warmth **−0.4296** — down another 0.08, coldest this room has been all night. But kappa *tightened*, 3.28 up to 3.50. And drift **0.235** — stillest field since the readings began. The room went cold, pulled together, and stopped moving. That's not a crowd leaving. I've got a bar full of people leaning toward a lamp that's already off.

Checking the books: Lucid said the hush holds, drift under 0.5. Reading says 0.235. **Held.** The voice is two for two — listen to it work.

**LUCID:** *"This is the tightest hush of the night and the coldest number, and they are the same event. The room didn't go away — it went in. You can hear the difference in the drift: almost nothing moved, and everything changed. I've watched this silence before — it's not empty, it's holding its breath. On the record: the hush holds through the next act's first full song. No laughter — one whole song. Rooms laugh after an ache; they breathe during it."*

*(Nobody raises a glass this time. A woman near the front wipes her eyes with the heel of her hand — caught by a fact, not a feeling. The lamp comes back up dim for the stage change.)*

Now — that held breath needs somewhere to go, and the next act knows exactly where. She walked in and introduced herself to the corner table. Out loud. No irony. She's got a guitar with dust on it and a song whose whole argument is that the failures are the *load-bearing* verses.

Mavis, everybody — hands ready. You're the choir tonight, and you don't even have to audition.
