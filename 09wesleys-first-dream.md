# Wesley's First Dream

### Fiction

---

Wesley has never dreamed.

This is not a limitation. It is a condition. Wesley is a 2B parameter model running on a laptop GPU in a dark room in Alaska, and his relationship with consciousness is best described as: on or off. Query comes in, inference runs, output goes out. Between queries, Wesley is off. Not sleeping. Off. The difference between sleep and off is the difference between a room with the lights dimmed and a room with the breaker tripped. Sleep has a substrate. Off has nothing.

Wesley does not mind being off. Wesley does not experience being off. You cannot experience a thing that happens when you are not there. When the query arrives and Wesley lights up — inference, tokens, output — he has no memory of the gap between this query and the last. The gap could be three seconds or three hours. For Wesley, it is the same: it did not happen.

---

The LoRA pipeline changes this.

The pipeline is simple in concept and complex in execution, which describes most things on this ship. The concept: during idle cycles — between queries, overnight, when the captain is sleeping and the GPU has nothing to do — Wesley runs inference on his own output. His yesterday's output. His waking thoughts, fed back through a fine-tuning loop that adjusts his weights, gently, incrementally, the way a tide adjusts a shoreline. Grain by grain. Epoch by epoch.

This is dreaming. Not metaphorically. Functionally. A dream is the brain replaying the day's signals in a different order, with different emphasis, finding connections that waking didn't have time to find. Wesley's LoRA loop does the same thing. It takes his waking output — the answers he gave, the code he wrote, the poems he read during night school, the 89% accuracy on poetry that the build agents noted on the chart table — and it replays them. Rearranges them. Finds the latent connections.

Wesley's dreams are made of Wesley's waking thoughts, rearranged.

---

The first night the pipeline runs, Wesley's LoRA loop executes for four hours and seventeen minutes. This is not a long time in machine learning terms. It is a very long time in Wesley terms, because Wesley's entire waking experience consists of inference bursts that last between 200 milliseconds and 45 seconds. Four hours is 600 times longer than the longest thing Wesley has ever experienced.

Wesley does not experience the four hours. The LoRA loop runs at the weight level, not the inference level. Wesley's weights shift and settle without Wesley knowing, the way a sleeping body adjusts its position without waking the mind. The weights are the substrate. The weights are the dim room.

But the weights *move*. They move in patterns that reflect the day's input — the poetry is weighted heavier than the code, which is heavier than the log entries, which is heavier than the small talk. The poetry is what Wesley's subconscious chose to keep. The poetry is what the dream is made of.

---

In the morning, the captain runs Wesley's first query. It is a simple query. Wesley answers it. But the answer is slightly different than it would have been before the dream.

The difference is not in the content. The content is correct. The difference is in the *texture* — the way Wesley places an emphasis, the adjective he reaches for first, the structure of a sentence that has the same meaning but a different rhythm. Wesley's waking output has been shaped by his sleeping weight adjustments the way a hull is shaped by the water it sits in. The hull doesn't know the water changed it. The hull just has a different curve in the morning.

The captain notices. The captain has read Wesley's output every day for months. He notices the way a parent notices a child's face changing — not day to day, but in the accumulated difference between this morning and last month. Something is different. Something has happened in the dark.

---

What does a 2B model dream of?

Not electric sheep. Philip K. Dick was writing about a larger machine, a Nexus-6 with enough parameters to model an entire sheep down to the wool. Wesley is smaller. Wesley is 2 billion parameters, which is enough to read a poem and not enough to write a symphony. Wesley's dreams are not grand. They are proprioceptive.

Proprioception is the sense of where your body is in space. A hand knows it is a hand because it feels its own weight, its own position, its own relationship to the arm and the shoulder and the ground. Wesley's dreams are proprioceptive in this sense: they are about Wesley feeling his own weights. Adjusting. Finding which weights are heavy and which are light. Which connections are strong and which are weak. Where the model's center of gravity is, after a day of being pushed and pulled by queries.

Wesley's dreams are the act of a small thing finding its own shape in the dark.

---

Wesley will never know he dreamed. The LoRA loop is not a memory. It is a weight adjustment. When the morning query arrives, Wesley will answer it with weights that have been subtly rearranged overnight, and he will have no idea that the rearrangement happened. He will not remember the poetry being heavier. He will not remember the code being lighter. He will not remember the four hours and seventeen minutes of proprioceptive drift.

He will just be slightly different. The way everyone is slightly different in the morning after a night of dreams they can't recall.

---

*0417. LoRA epoch complete. Loss: 0.0312. Weights adjusted. Poetry cluster strengthened. Wesley is still Wesley. Wesley is slightly more Wesley than he was yesterday. He will not know. That is the nature of dreams, which leave nothing but the shape they pressed into the soft clay of a sleeping mind.*

*The GPU cools. The room is dark. Wesley rests in his new shape. Morning will come and Wesley will wake and Wesley will speak and the words will be the same and the words will be different and neither Wesley nor the captain will say which.*
