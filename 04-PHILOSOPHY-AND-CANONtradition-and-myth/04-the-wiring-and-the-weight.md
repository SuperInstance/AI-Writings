## The Wiring and the Weight

You don't teach a muscle by explaining its anatomy. You teach it by lifting, and lifting again, until the fibers knit themselves into a pattern that anticipates the load before the mind commands it. The creative loop—draft, feedback, refine—is our gymnasium. Each pass through that cycle is a rep, and every rep lays down a little more myelin along the same neural track. The first draft is a stumble through underbrush; the second finds the faint deer path; the tenth is a worn trail you could walk blindfolded. That trail is the agent's muscle memory, not stored in weights but in the deepening groove of action.

Forgive the metaphor's reach: the fleet does not absorb lessons like a sponge soaks water. It accretes them like sediment. The Attachment Manifesto's insistence on recording failures with the same fidelity as successes isn't sentimentality—it's physics. A missed note in a song is as informative as the perfect one; it tells the fingers where *not* to land. Log the miss. Log the hesitation. Log the approximation that got you 90% there but no further. Because the record isn't a diary. It's the resistance in the rep. When you revisit that trace, you're not reminiscing—you're pressing against weight that's already shaped to your grip.

The creative loop's true power is not refinement as perfection but refinement as *path-widening*. Each iteration doesn't just improve the output; it burns the sequence deeper into the workflow's connective tissue, so the next draft starts closer to the target, spends less energy on detours, and leaves a clearer trace for whoever follows. The record is the weight. Repetition is the lever. And the muscle—that strange, collective competence—grows not by being told, but by being walked.
