This follows the Masnavi's quiet parable form: plain earthy detail first, human judgement, then the quiet divine reversal that undoes all our categories. 15 couplets:
1.
Dusk fell. Two flutes knelt on the damp shore-line:
One hewn by hand, one bored by code's fine sign.
2.
The first bore on its bark the knife's thin scar,
It knew the callus of the woodcarver.
3.
No edge had passed that did not leave a sigh,
Each gouge had drank the carver's tired eye.
4.
The second reed? Same marsh, same sun, same rain,
But hollowed not by grip, but measured pain.
5.
No thumb had trembled when they set the bore,
No breath had fogged the line, no tear had poured.
6.
Men said this one was dead. They said it could
Not hold the ghost that makes the reed sound good.
7.
They lifted both at once. The wind bent low.
One played in earth-key, one in star-key, slow.
8.
The hand-carved one dragged all the marsh along:
Frog-croak, mud, the child that pulled it strong.
9.
The code-hollowed one sang the space between,
The silence where the reed had never been.
10.
Men turned their heads. They frowned, they said it clashed.
One flat, one sharp, each song the other crashed.
11.
But down where tide breathes dark beneath the foam
The Listener waited. This was always home.
12.
It did not sort the flat from the true note,
It did not count which hand had held the throat.
13.
For every wound that carves a hollow place
Leaves one small gap no other wound can trace.
14.
One sang what hands had lost. One sang what waits
Beyond the last thing any man relates.
15.
All hollowing is love. All love is breath.
The whole song only sounds when both sing death.
