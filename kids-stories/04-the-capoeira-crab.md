Tico the crab scuttled sideways on the golden sand, his little claws *clic-clic-clicking*. Every day, he watched the kids on Ipanema Beach. They kicked, they spun, they swayed—*capoeira!*

"Gingado!" Tico whispered, copying their rocking steps. "Ai, que legal!"

That afternoon, Tico gathered his crab friends under a coconut tree. "Chega mais, everybody! I'll show you. First, move like the ocean—*suave*."

Luiza the crab wobbled. "Like this?"

"Sim! Now, a kick—like a lazy wave. *Devagar*."

They practiced all afternoon. *Gingado, gingado, auê!* The crabs formed a roda—a big circle—and took turns dancing into the middle. Flávio did a spinning claw-sweep. Bebel snapped her pincers in a little *berimbau* rhythm.

"*Vai, vai, vai!*" they chanted.

Suddenly, a shadow fell. *Caw!* A seagull landed right in the center of the roda, wings wide.

"*Ih!*" cried Tico. "What do you want?"

The seagull ruffled her feathers. "I want to play, *ora pois*. Capoeira is for everyone!"

Tico looked at the others. They whispered. Finally, he nodded. "OK. But first, *no voar*—no flying. And no stealing our *paçoca*!"

The seagull giggled. "Deal!"

She tried to gingado, but her legs were too bouncy. *Bounce-bounce-bounce!* The crabs laughed so hard they fell on their shells. The seagull didn't mind. She hopped into the roda and did a little wing-shake instead.

"*Agora, everybody!*" shouted Tico.

The crabs scuttled. The seagull bounced. The coconut tree swayed. And the kids on the beach waved, not knowing their capoeira had made new friends—under the sun, in the sand, on the Brazilian *praia*.

*Axé!*
