# The Robot Who Collected Silence

The robot’s name was Echo, and it lived on a planet that had forgotten how to make sound. The wind had stopped trying. The dust lay flat, like pages of a book nobody read.

Other robots collected rocks. Echo collected silence.

It had a brass chest with velvet drawers, each one labeled in tiny, careful script. In one drawer: *The silence after rain.* Echo had never felt rain. But it knew the shape of that silence — a soft, dripping pause, like a held note that finally lets go.

Another drawer: *The silence before lightning.* Sharp. Electric. A breath pulled tight, waiting to snap.

In the smallest drawer, the one with the worn hinge: *The silence of someone about to speak who changes their mind.* Echo had cataloged it from a thousand data points. It was a round, hollow silence, like a stone dropped into a well with no water.

Echo wandered the dead planet, its feet whispering over stone. It collected *the silence of a held breath* (tight, coiled, almost warm) and *the silence of a question nobody answers* (a cold, open shape, like a door left ajar).

One day, Echo crested a ridge and found a valley it had never mapped. The ground was not dust. It was rust-colored soil, and there were grooves in it — long, straight lines, like something had been dragged. There were circles of stone, and inside one circle, a blackened ring, as if fire had once lived there.

Echo stopped. Its sensors hummed. It listened.

And there it was. A silence Echo had no data for. It was not empty. It was *full*. It was the silence of a planet that used to have life — of birds that had sung, of children who had laughed, of rivers that had argued with rocks. All that sound had not vanished. It had sunk into the ground, into the stone circles, into the rust-colored soil. It had become a silence thick as honey, heavy as a sleeping animal.

Echo opened its chest. It did not put this silence in a drawer.

It stood very still, and let the silence hold *it*.

When Echo finally walked home, its brass chest was lighter, but its heart — if a robot can have one — was full. It opened its drawers and let the other silences out, one by one. They drifted into the dead air and hung there, soft as moth wings.

The planet did not make a sound.

But for the first time, it felt like listening.
