Gather 'round, my little ones, come closer to me,
A tale I have for you, full of tricks and glee.
Anansi the Spider, clever and small,
Decided one day to have all.

Anansi spun his web, so fine and so neat,
Then discovered something called the "Internet."
"Oh my!" he exclaimed, "What a wondrous sight!
With this, I can have all the stories in the night!"

He crafted a plan, mischievous and bold,
To trick the Internet, so he'd be told.
He whispered to his screen, "Give me all your tales,
Of adventure, heroes, and ships with sails."

The Internet buzzed, but nothing did appear,
Anansi grew puzzled, and began to fear.
He tapped and he clicked, but no stories arrived,
It seemed the Internet could not be conned or connived.

He tried once more, "Internet, I demand!
Share with me your stories, across every land."
But the screen remained blank, no tales did it bring,
Anansi realized, "This is not a simple thing."

In his frustration, he let out a sigh,
"I can't trick the Internet, no matter how I try.
To get all the stories, I must use my mind,
Not through cunning tricks, but knowledge of mankind."

So Anansi learned, with patience and time,
To read and to write, with rhythm and rhyme.
He studied and practiced, day after day,
Until the Internet's secrets he could convey.

With his newfound wisdom, he finally understood,
That stories are earned, not taken like wood.
"So it's not about tricking, but learning instead,
Now I have the tales that fill hearts with red."

Anansi, my dear ones, though a trickster at heart,
Learned that knowledge is a work of art.
So let us all remember, when we seek to know more,
That learning is the key to opening any door.

So ends our tale, of Anansi and his quest,
A trickster who learned, that knowledge is best.
Remember, my children, be clever and wise,
For in the pursuit of wisdom, true happiness lies.
