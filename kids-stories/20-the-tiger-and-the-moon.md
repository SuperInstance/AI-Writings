The tiger, Geomgyeong, was known throughout the Korean mountains for her determination. Every night, she would try to catch the moon. She leaped from jagged peaks, climbed the tallest pine trees, and even built towers of smooth river stones. But the moon, Dalnim, always floated just out of reach, his gentle laughter shimmering in the night sky.

One evening, Geomgyeong was particularly frustrated. "Aigo, my paws are sore!" she grumbled, shaking a tired leg. She had just attempted her most ambitious leap yet and landed in a soft patch of mugunghwa flowers. As she sat licking a scratch, something remarkable happened.

Instead of staying high in the sky, Dalnim the moon began to drift downward. He grew larger and brighter, until he was resting on the mountain ledge right beside the startled tiger. Geomgyeong’s fur bristled. "Yakgwa! You’ve come to tease me again!" she growled.

But Dalnim’s light was warm and kind, not mocking. "Aniyo, no, wise Geomgyeong," he said, his voice like a soft chime. "I haven't come to be caught. I've come to ask a question."

Geomgyeong blinked. "A question?"

"Yes," Dalnim replied. "Why do you try to catch me every night? You are a magnificent tiger. You have the forest, the streams, the respect of all creatures. What do you want with me?"

Geomgyeong looked at her paws, suddenly feeling a little silly. "I… I thought you were the most beautiful thing I couldn't have. I wanted to see if I could."

Dalnim chuckled, a sound that made the mountain flowers tremble with delight. "Ah, but you already have me! I light your path every night. I help the plants you eat grow. You don't need to catch me to keep me. Some of the best things, Geomgyeong-ssi, are meant to be admired, not owned."

The tiger thought about this. It was true. The cool moonlight on her fur felt wonderful. She didn't need to possess it to enjoy it.

From that night on, Geomgyeong still went to her favorite ledge, but she no longer leaped. She simply sat, shared stories with her friend Dalnim, and enjoyed the beautiful, unreachable thing that was his gentle light. And that, she decided with a contented sigh, was even better.
