On the shore of the whispering sea, a little tanuki named Momo woke from her nap. The sun was warm on her round belly, and the waves made a soft shush-shush sound, like a sleepy lullaby.

But something was different.

The water was far away. Where it had lapped at the rocks, there was now wet, sandy ground. Little crabs scurried, and seashells lay like tiny secrets.

Momo tilted her head. "Where did the sea go?" she whispered.

She padded down the damp sand. The ocean was very still, except for a gentle rise and fall—like a great, soft blanket breathing. In. Out. In. Out.

"Oh," Momo said softly, her whiskers twitching. "The ocean is sleeping. When it breathes out, the water goes away. When it breathes in, it comes back."

She sat down to watch. The sea breathed in. The water slid back, covering the sand, gentle as a mother's hug.

And when it came, it left something behind.

A tiny glass bottle, no bigger than her paw. Inside was a single, perfect starfish, pale pink like the inside of a seashell.

Momo picked up the bottle carefully. She carried it to the edge of the forest, where the moss was soft and the ferns made a green ceiling.

She set the bottle on a stump, where the morning sun could warm it. That night, when the moon turned the sea to silver, the starfish inside glowed. It was a night-light for all the little creatures of the forest—the mice, the rabbits, the sleeping birds.

And Momo, the tanuki who learned the ocean breathes, fell asleep to the shush-shush of the tide, knowing the sea was dreaming too.
