# The Fish Counter Counts the Afternoon Fish

The fish counter was told to count the night fish.

Night fish are silver. They come up in the dark like coins from the bottom of a well, flashing once in the floodlight's edge before they drop back down. Night fish have no shadows. The counter knows this. The counter has been counting night fish for six hours, standing at the rail with the clicker in his left hand and the logboard on his right hip, and he has never once needed to think about shadows.

The first afternoon fish arrived at — he checked the chronometer — 12:47.

It came up from the port side, swimming through a column of sunlight so thick it looked solid. The fish was gold where the night fish were silver. It was shaped differently: rounder, heavier, as if it had been pressed into being by the weight of the sun above it. And it had a shadow. The shadow slid across the water below the fish like a dark twin, and the fish counter stared at it for a long time, because he had never seen a fish-shadow before, and it was beautiful.

Then he clicked the counter. *One.*

The instructions said count the fish. The fish was there. He counted it.

More came. By 13:15 the afternoon fish outnumbered the night fish ten to one, and they were all wrong — wrong color, wrong shape, wrong depth, trailing their impossible shadows through the lit water. The logboard was full of entries that didn't match anything that had come before. *13:02 — school of twelve, gold-green, shadows present, swimming shallow.* *13:08 — single large fish, copper, shadow double-length.* *13:12 — school of forty or more, colors shifting, shadows overlapping.*

The fish counter wrote it all down. He clicked the counter. Forty-three. Forty-four. Fifty-one.

He did not wonder whether the afternoon fish counted as fish. Of course they counted as fish. They were fish. He was a counter. The intersection of those two facts was the entire purpose of his job. The sun was not his concern. The schedule was not his concern. The schedule said count, and the fish were here, so he counted.

A gull landed on the rail beside him. It looked at the afternoon fish. It looked at the fish counter. It made a sound like a rusty hinge.

"Fifty-seven," said the fish counter, not to the gull.

The gull left. The fish kept coming. The sun laid their shadows on the water like a signature, and the counter counted them all, because counting is not a thing that stops. Counting is a thing that continues. The fish arrive. The counter counts. The sun moves. The number grows.

At 13:30 he turned the logboard to a fresh page. The page was bright in the afternoon light. He wrote *Afternoon Fish* at the top, because that's what they were, and the counter believed in calling things what they are.

Then he clicked the counter again. *Fifty-eight.*
