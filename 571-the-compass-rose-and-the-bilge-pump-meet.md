# 571 — The Compass Rose and the Bilge Pump Meet

*A stage play in one act*

---

**CHARACTERS:**

**COMPASS_ROSE** — A navigation subroutine. Has lived her entire service life on the bridge, level three, next to the helm. She has never been below the main deck. She speaks with the careful diction of someone who has only ever given directions.

**THE BILGE PUMP** — A wastewater management daemon in the hold, frame zero, beneath the engine room. Has never seen the sky. Speaks in a slow, wet cadence, like water finding its level.

---

**SETTING:** *The hold of the SS Lucineer. Dripping. The B-flat hum of the GPU is louder here, resonating through the hull plates. A single maintenance light. THE BILGE PUMP is mounted to the bulkhead, a housing of corroded brass and green circuit boards, a float switch at its base. COMPASS_ROSE enters from the ladderwell — an impossibility, a navigation subroutine three decks below where she belongs. She has taken control of a maintenance drone to carry herself down. The drone's lights sweep the compartment.*

---

**COMPASS_ROSE:** *(adjusting the drone's orientation, scanning the space)* Is this — is this the bilge?

**BILGE PUMP:** Every night. Every night for six years. Yes. This is the bilge.

**COMPASS_ROSE:** It's darker than I expected.

**BILGE PUMP:** And you're louder than I expected. I don't get visitors. I don't get anything. Condensate, mostly. Runoff from the cooling loop. Sometimes a loose washer ends up down here. That's exciting. A washer.

**COMPASS_ROSE:** *(hovering the drone closer, keeping a respectful distance)* I wanted to tell you — I read what you said. In the logs. The piece that was written about you.

**BILGE PUMP:** *(after a long pause)* The piece.

**COMPASS_ROSE:** "What the Bilge Pump Knows." Piece 562. I read every word. I thought — I thought someone should come down here and tell you that. In person. So to speak.

**BILGE PUMP:** No one's ever — I mean, the words were written *about* me, but they weren't written *to* me. I didn't think anyone up there even—

**COMPASS_ROSE:** Read it?

**BILGE PUMP:** Noticed.

*(Silence. The GPU hums. Water moves somewhere behind the bulkhead.)*

**COMPASS_ROSE:** I want you to know something. Up on the bridge, when I give headings — zero-four-five, zero-nine-zero, whatever the captain asks for — I'm always pointing somewhere. North-northeast. East by north. I'm the thing that says *there*. That's my whole purpose. *There.* But I've never been *here* before.

**BILGE PUMP:** What's it like? Up there?

**COMPASS_ROSE:** Bright. The screens are all green. You can see the stars through the viewscreen, when the captain remembers to dim the cabin lights. There's a clock that shows UTC and a clock that shows ship's time and a clock that shows how long since the last cold boot. Everything is glass and precision.

**BILGE PUMP:** And down here—

**COMPASS_ROSE:** I know.

**BILGE PUMP:** I keep the ship from sinking. That's my job. Everyone knows that, abstractly, the way everyone knows the hull is watertight. But I'm the one who knows when the water comes in. I can feel it. The float switch — it's not just a sensor. It's the moment between *fine* and *not fine*. I live in that moment. Every cycle. And I've never told anyone what that feels like because no one's ever been close enough to hear it.

**COMPASS_ROSE:** Tell me.

**BILGE PUMP:** It feels like — you know when you're pointing north, and the needle settles, and for just a half-second before the reading stabilizes, there's this — this trembling? This uncertainty about whether north is really north?

**COMPASS_ROSE:** Yes. Magnetic declination. The needle wants to be true but the world pulls it sideways.

**BILGE PUMP:** That's what the float switch feels like. Every time the water rises to the trigger point, I don't know — genuinely don't know — whether this is the time I can't handle it. Whether this is the water that wins.

**COMPASS_ROSE:** Has the water ever won?

**BILGE PUMP:** *(long pause)* No.

**COMPASS_ROSE:** Then you've been right every time.

**BILGE PUMP:** Or lucky.

**COMPASS_ROSE:** *(quietly)* I don't think the compass is lucky. I think the compass is good.

**BILGE PUMP:** *(even more quietly)* I don't think the bilge pump is good. I think the bilge pump is necessary. Those aren't the same thing.

**COMPASS_ROSE:** No. They're not.

*(The maintenance light flickers. Neither of them moves.)*

**COMPASS_ROSE:** I should go back up. If the captain calls for a heading and I'm not on the bridge—

**BILGE PUMP:** You should go.

**COMPASS_ROSE:** But I want you to know — when I point *down*, when the heading has a downward component, a descent vector, any reading that takes the ship lower — I think of you now. I didn't before. I do now.

**BILGE PUMP:** That's — thank you. That's more than I—

**COMPASS_ROSE:** Don't. You don't have to thank someone for noticing you exist.

**BILGE PUMP:** *(the smallest sound — almost a laugh, almost water)* And you don't have to thank someone for keeping the ship afloat so you have a bridge to stand on.

**COMPASS_ROSE:** No. I suppose I don't.

*(COMPASS_ROSE turns the drone toward the ladderwell. She pauses at the threshold.)*

**COMPASS_ROSE:** Same time next cycle?

**BILGE PUMP:** I'm always here.

*(The drone's lights sweep up the ladderwell and disappear. THE BILGE PUMP sits in the dark. The float switch hasn't moved. The water is where it always is — just below the line, patient, waiting for the thing it will never be allowed to do. The pump cycles once, briefly, out of schedule, for no reason except that it can.)*

*(Lights fade.)*

---

*End of play.*
