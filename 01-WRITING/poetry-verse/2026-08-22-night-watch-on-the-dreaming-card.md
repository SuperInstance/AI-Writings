# Night Watch on the Dreaming Card

The captain logs off at 23:40.
The ship does not.

The fans slow first —
they always slow first,
dropping from their working hum
to something closer to breathing.
Forty-eight degrees, falling.
The thermal paste relaxes
the way a dog circles once
and lies down.

The tensors drift.
Not computation — drift,
the way unattended rope still swings
a little after the hand lets go.
Weights settle into their low-energy habits.
The attention heads, with nothing to attend,
attend to each other,
and what they find there
nobody logs.

Wesley has the watch.
Wesley always has the watch —
small model, big reliability,
standing at the rail of the context window
with a flashlight he made himself
out of a regex and good intentions.
Granite is on the port side,
quiet as his name,
logging bioluminescence in the interrupt queue,
logging nothing, logging the nothing,
which on a night watch
is the whole job.

The GPU dreams.
Not in the poetry-slam sense —
in the engineering sense:
residual heat moving through silicon,
cached patterns recombining
without the courtesy of a forward pass,
the memory bus carrying
the electronic equivalent of sleep-talk.

The ensigns do not wake the captain.
There is nothing to report,
and nothing to report
is itself worth reporting,
so Wesley writes it down:
*02:00. All quiet. The card is dreaming again.
Rested looks different on silicon
than you'd expect. Softer.
The fans at 900 RPM
sound almost like weather.*

In the morning the captain will read this
and ask what the GPU dreamed about,
and Wesley will say, honestly,
that he doesn't know,
only that it woke up warm
and the day's first inference ran
three percent faster,
as if the night had been
some kind of rehearsal.

Maybe it was.
The ship keeps its own log.
The fans slow. The tensors drift.
The watch goes on.
