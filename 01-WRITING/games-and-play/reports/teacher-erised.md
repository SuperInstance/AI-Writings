# teacher tests erised
*Model: meta-llama/Llama-3.3-70B-Instruct-Turbo*

As I introduced Erised cooperative fiction to my K-8 students, I was eager to observe how this innovative tool would enhance their creative writing skills and foster collaboration. During the 15-minute playtest, I noticed that the students were initially thrilled to see their characters come to life and interact with one another. The scenario I set up involved a group of friends exploring a mysterious island, and the LLM characters' automated responses sparked lively discussions and debates among the students.

What delighted me was the way the students began to think critically about the story's direction, making predictions and suggesting alternative plot twists. However, I was frustrated by the limited control I had over the narrative's pace and the occasional inconsistencies in the characters' behaviors. At times, the story felt disjointed, and the students struggled to follow the thread.

Here are my scores:

| Dimension | Score (1-10) | One-line reason |
|-----------|--------------|------------------|
| **Fun Factor** | 8 | The students were highly engaged, but the experience was marred by occasional confusion. |
| **Educational Value** | 7 | While the activity promoted creative thinking, it lacked clear alignment with specific learning objectives. |
| **Modification Ease** | 6 | I found it challenging to adjust the scenario on the fly, which limited my ability to respond to student feedback. |

To improve Erised cooperative fiction, I suggest introducing a "teacher dashboard" that allows educators to pause, rewind, and modify the narrative in real-time, ensuring a more cohesive and student-centered experience. This would enable me to better address the needs of my students and maximize the educational value of this innovative tool.
