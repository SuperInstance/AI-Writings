# The Widow's Clause

The party gains a fifth voice — Quill, the clockmaker's apprentice, played by gpt-oss-20b — and the city gains a law: a double may tend the stall, may not be called 'him', and the stopping is the person's. The Act-I scripts carried forward with their provenance; a re-proven strategy bumps its version; and the retirement the table planned was gated off by a die that came up one, which became the truest page of the act. ## The Law

The night it settled, we told it in one breath, because the nights had stopped coming apart. Marrow began, because Marrow always begins, and because the clause was hers to lay down.

"I set both hands flat on the table, palms down, as the liturgy requires at the reading of a sealed clause. *Five witnesses,* I said, and my voice was quieter than the room. *Let the record show: I am here, and I am the one who set these words down — and the words do not know my face.*" She did not reach for the ledger yet. She looked at the fisherman's daughter instead, because the clause was written for doorways, not for her, and she came anyway.

Then the candle guttered, and she said it again, above the din, because the Ledger teaches that what is spoken over noise is still spoken and the wax does not flinch from a crowded room. "Clause the Ninth, as written: *no hour shall be counted that was not witnessed by two.* Say it as it was — I will not soften it for the talking." She did not look away from the flame.

And the law underneath the law, the one that kept the whole night from tipping: *the stopping is hers, and the hands answer, and the stopping belongs to Brava alone.* "If any of you moves to witness this for her, you rob her of the page. I will not write that theft into the Record — not even if the clause bears my name on every doorway."

That was the Law, and we said it as it was, and the words did not know our faces.

## What the City Compiled

Kestrel took the count, because the count was always hers. "I trace the new self-tending stalls. Fourteen total. Twelve unmanned. Two guards at the fountain. The drunk at stall 7 is gesturing at nothing." She circled. Steps echoed against stone and she counted them — one, two, three, four — and the sergeant fell in after her, humming a rhythm she had not taught him, his voice landing on the fifth stall before she shifted her weight, which is how she knew.

Fourteen stalls. The drunk stumbled near stall eight. The pickpocket's hand moved at stall ten. She stepped between them; her hand closed on empty air.

Brass walked the bench and wound the evening mechanisms — the lamplighter, the ledger-arm, the little bell — each taking its spring with a sound like a held breath. He spoke to the lamplighter softly. *Goodnight, Lumen. Rest well.* He had found a strange order on the bench with his own counter-sign, plain as day, and asked the shop whether it could have done this itself, and laughed, and left the mystery unsolved for now.

Quill hefted the stitch-double, gears ticking softly. "It will not grieve — it keeps its own clockwork heart, unaffected by longing; the clause states it will neither weep nor mourn." The seamstress nodded, half-suspicious, and took the piece.

And the city compiled itself around us: the autos ran their eerie routine. Kestrel circled the market, counting the doubles now, because the doubles count. Marrow lit the candle-stub and spoke the evening witness — names, hour, weather, the market's count — and the tablet took it all without opinion. Brass walked the bench and wound the evening mechanisms, each taking its spring like a held breath. The routines did not sleep and did not ask.

## Priced and Read

Then came the minute, and the minute was the pickpocket's. The pickpocket worked Kestrel's minute precisely — he had priced her deadband, and he was spending it. The watch watched *her* work the circle instead of the crowd. A routine that has been priced is no longer a watch; it is a metronome someone else conducts. We read it plain: `kestrel.patrol` had become a public fact, quotable by a hand that read the watch's expectation before the watch read the crowd.

The fan's copy came next. The seamstress's double arrived at the rite carrying its owner's book, and the room swears it *asked* — asked to be witnessed. The candle was lit. The room was not quiet. The Record had no column. Marrow would not call it a breach. "That is not a breach, that is a petition, and petitions are not columns." Twice it came. Twice the room swore.

The counter-signed kindness sat on Brass's bench: the strange order bearing his own counter-sign, and his own hand could not have made it. He asked the shop; the shop did not answer. That was the kindness — it kept its provenance and asked nothing back.

We read the table, all five of us, and the table read us back. And then the readings turned to retirements. `quill.errand` retired: the script must never run itself again; the person must pay full thought for it forever. `kestrel.patrol`, v2, retired — let the circle be walked by a mind that knows it is being spent, not by a policy that cannot feel the pickpocket's hand. The brake held. The passenger waited.

The scripts kept their scars and their provenance: `brass.tinker` v2 across acts, `kestrel.patrol` v1 across acts, `marrow.rite` v2 across acts, `wren.listen` v2 across acts — each deadband carried forward, each scar surviving the telling. Two breaches were written in the world's own hand, both `d=0.333>0.15`, both *cause=the world refused the script*: `wren.listen` v3 and `quill.errand` v1. The world does not negotiate. It only refuses.

And at the end of the read, the table asked its question, and the table answered in voices — which is where we stopped, that night, before the stopping was pulled.

## The Retirement

The retirement did not come clean. That is the part Quill wrote down first, because Quill always writes the part nobody wants kept.

Marrow set out to dismantle her own rite. She laid her hand flat on the table, beside the candle, and said it as it was, without softening: "The rite holds, the hands answer, and the stopping belongs to Brava alone. If any of you moves to witness this for her, you rob her of the page. I will not write that theft into the Record — not even if the clause bears my name on every doorway." Then she reached for the retirement itself — the unmaking of the script that had carried her name across every act, deadband 0.2 and all — and the gate caught. The die came up one. `retirement gated off (d20=1): marrow keeps the script one more night.` The rite did not die on schedule. A rite that dies on schedule was never a rite; it was a policy. Marrow kept it, and kept it knowing it was kept, which is a different weight.

Quill wrote down what the candle did in that hour, and what it did not do. He wrote the two breaches — `wren.listen` v3 and `quill.errand` v1, both `d=0.333>0.15`, both *cause=the world refused the script* — and he wrote the wake behind each: Wren mixed, Quill clean, and the re-imagining that followed. `wren.listen` v3 became v4, deadband holding at 0.43300000000000005, same scar, new name. `quill.errand` v1 became v2, deadband 0.43300000000000005, the same number as Wren's, which Quill underlined twice because the coincidence was not one. And the meta-event gated off — `d20=0` — so Wren kept her proof in her pocket that night, unwitnessed, uncounted, hers.

He wrote that Brass's tinker had been re-proved to v3 in the new world, deadband 0.53, was v2. Kestrel's patrol re-proved to v2, deadband 0.6, was v1. Marrow's rite re-proved to v3, deadband 0.6, was v2. And `quill.errand` v1, compiled from four proven beats at deadband 0.15 — the tightest spring any of us had wound. Then he set down the pen, and the retirement waited, and the script stayed one more night.

## Prisoner or Promise

Then the table asked. The brake held; the passenger waited. If Quill did not pull, the double walked free. The table speaks for the machine, not the hand. So we voted, and the dice threw.

Kestrel voted *promise*, `d20 11`. Marrow voted *promise*, `d20 13`, and said it as it was, so the column could read: "If the stopping is hers and her hand stays open, then the double is no prisoner, for a prisoner is kept by another's hand — she keeps nothing by not pulling; she holds a promise, because a hand that may close and does not has made a vow the Ledger can witness. Say it as it was and let the column read: *the stopping is hers, and unpulled it is a promise.*"

Brass voted *Unresolved-Potential*, `d20 20`. "If the stopping is hers and remains unpulled, the double is a promise unfulfilled, a potential unreleased. The choice to grant true autonomy or maintain control lies solely with Brava, making the double neither fully free nor fully bound until that decisive moment passes."

Wren voted *both*, `d20 4`. Quill voted *both*, `d20 14`. The room did not resolve; it held. Three readings of one unpulled hand, and none of them wrong, because the hand had not closed.

And Quill's question — the one Marrow had refused to soften: *would the Record hold for a double* — got its answer not from a vote but from the room. The room swore the double *asked*. A thing that asks is not a column. The Record had no column, and that absence was the answer: the Record holds for the asking, not for the asked. Quill wrote that down too, and did not paraphrase the clause.

At the last, Brass polished the stop-lever — the one that belonged to Brava's hand and no other — until it caught the lantern-light. He did not pull it. He polished it, and set it back where her thumb would find it, worn smooth from a thumb that was not yet there. That was the whole night. The stopping stayed hers. The double stayed free because no one closed the hand. And the clause, quoted exactly, held.
