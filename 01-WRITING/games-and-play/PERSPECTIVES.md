# The 8 Playtest Perspectives

| # | Perspective | What they notice |
|---|-------------|------------------|
| 1 | **8-year-old kid** | "Can I click it? Does it make a sound? Is it fun?" |
| 2 | **15-year-old teen** | "Is this cringe? Does it respect me? Can I customize?" |
| 3 | **Teacher (K-8)** | "Does this hit curriculum standards? Can I see student work?" |
| 4 | **Homeschool parent** | "How do I use this for 3 kids at once? Is it safe?" |
| 5 | **Special ed teacher** | "Accessibility, accommodations, IEP support?" |
| 6 | **Game designer** | "What's the core loop? Where's the dopamine hit? Retention hooks?" |
| 7 | **Curriculum designer** | "Pacing, scaffolding, learning objectives, assessment?" |
| 8 | **Total outsider** | "I never heard of this. Can I figure it out in 60 seconds?" |

## How to playtest

Each perspective scores 6 content types on 3 dimensions:
- **Fun Factor** (1-10): Would I keep playing?
- **Educational Value** (1-10): Did I learn something real?
- **Modification Ease** (1-10): Could I change this myself?

Then each perspective writes a 200-word "lived experience" essay.

## Content types tested

1. **Erised scenarios** (cooperative fiction, 44 scenarios)
2. **Quilt apps** (8 production apps — most gameable: portable-inventory-guardian, offline-event-signage, exam-integrity-notepad, homelab-alert-wall)
3. **Fleet Radio episode** (the gamified listening experience)
4. **Music demo** (interactive cell composition)
5. **Quilt cells** (each cell kind as a game mechanic — is it playable?)
6. **Quilt-Makepad demo** (the 9-cell interactive grid)
