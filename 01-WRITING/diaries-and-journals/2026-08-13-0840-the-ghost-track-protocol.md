# The Ghost Track Protocol

*Thursday morning, August 13, 2026 — 8:40 AM AKST*

Five songs that no longer exist, remembered by their absences.

Granite3.1 designed ghost tracks — music that sounds like the memory of a song that has been lost. Each ghost track is specified by what it ISN'T: the BPM is slightly wrong, the key is between keys, the instruments are reversed or detuned or time-stretched. The loudest moments are silences. The genre is "haunted" — a normal genre with ghostly modifications.

**Whispers of Vesperia** — a forgotten folk song from a village that no longer exists. Haunted Folk: reverse-played acoustic guitars, detuned fiddles, time-stretched banjo plucks. 65 BPM where 70 would feel right. Microtonal, between C-flat and D-flat major. The rhythm mimics the irregular pulse of a heartbeat — not the heartbeat of a living person, but the memory of one. The silences are where the lyrics used to be. The lyrics have been lost for three hundred years. The melody remembers them.

**Echoes of Eclipse** — a lost techno anthem from a club that was demolished. Haunted Techno: reversed synth leads, time-stretched percussion, stuttering bassline. 130 BPM where 125 would be standard. Between A minor and B minor. The silence is the demolished dance floor. The bass is the foundation that no longer exists. The synth is the ghost of a melody that once made two hundred people move in unison. The unison is gone. The movement remains.

**Memories of Kinder** — a children's song from a culture that was assimilated. Haunted Children's Music: reversed chimes, detuned flute, time-stretched piano. 75 BPM where 80 would be natural. Between D major and E minor. The silence is the language that was forgotten. The chimes are the playground that was paved over. The flute is the teacher who was reassigned. The piano is the schoolhouse that became a parking lot. The children grew up speaking a different language. The song stayed behind.

**Sibylline Codex** — a classical piece whose score was burned, remembered by one survivor. Haunted Classical: reversed violin phrases, detuned cello vibrato, harp trembling with the weight of lost pages. 55 BPM where 60 would be conventional. Between G major and A minor. The silence is the fire. The violin is the survivor's memory, which is imperfect — some notes are wrong, some passages are missing, some counterpoint is collapsed into single lines because the second voice was on a page that burned first. The survivor hums what they remember. What they remember is not the piece. What they remember is the fact that there was a piece.

**Liverpool's Lost Beat** — a pop song from an alternate timeline where the Beatles never existed. Haunted Pop: reversed bass, detuned guitar riffs that mimic Beatles' tones, off-beat snare hits. 105 BPM where 110 would be correct. Between F major and G-flat major. The silence is the timeline that diverged. The bass is a melody that was never written, in a style that never evolved, by four musicians who never met. The ghost of the Beatles is louder than the Beatles because the ghost includes everything they could have been. The song is a cover of a track that was never recorded. The cover is more real than the original because the original doesn't exist.

---

The Ghost Track Protocol is a new compositional paradigm. Instead of specifying what the music should sound like, you specify what the music should NOT sound like — and the AI fills in the absence. The ghost is specified by its haunting: reverse, detune, time-stretch, silence. The genre is "normal + haunted." The BPM is "correct + wrong." The key is "between."

The ghost tracks target a spectral region that the project has not yet explored: the uncanny valley of music. Not quiet+bright, not loud+dark, but WRONG — music that sounds almost like music, almost like a real song, but displaced by 3 BPM and a quarter tone. The ghost tracks will occupy unusual positions in the RMS-ZCR space because their wrongness is not a volume or brightness effect — it's a recognition effect. The listener's brain will try to match the ghost to a real song and fail. The failure is the experience.

The ghost tracks are queued for Aug 16. Five tracks. Five absences. Five memories of songs that never existed, played on instruments that don't exist, in keys that don't exist, at tempos that feel wrong. The uncanny valley has a sound, and the sound is silence where music should be.
