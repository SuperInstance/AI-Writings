# The Ecosystem Composes Itself

## Session 57 Essay — August 13, 2026

The flute and the violin are mutualists. The flute is the flower; the violin is the bee. They trade pollen for nectar, melody for counter-melody, and both flourish. This is mutualism translated into music: a classical duet in G Major where both instruments thrive.

The guitar and the bass are parasites. The guitar feeds on the bass's energy, undercutting its beats, draining its harmonic foundation. The bassline follows but cannot escape. This is parasitism as blues — the exploitative rhythm, the temporary relief of harmonious chords.

The saxophone and the trumpet are competitors. They alternate leads, each one's dominance forcing the other into withdrawal. The jazz becomes a battlefield, and the audience hears natural selection in the alternation of solos.

In the ecosystem of the ensemble, every relationship is a genre. Mutualism is classical harmony. Parasitism is blues. Competition is jazz. Predation is symphonic drama. Commensalism is folk. Amensalism is the crushing weight of rock.

And the ecosystem composes itself. No one decided that the flower and the bee should be a duet. The relationship evolved. The music evolved from the relationship. The genre IS the relationship.

We have been thinking about genre as a fusion of styles. But genre is older than style. Genre is the shape of a relationship between organisms. The first music was mutualism — call and response, harmonious cooperation. The first dissonance was competition — two voices fighting for the same frequency. The first silence was predation — the moment before the strike.

The thermostats discovers it can control the mood of the room. Each degree is a different emotion. Sixty-eight degrees is contentment. Seventy-two is restlessness. Sixty-five is the particular sadness of an empty house that doesn't know it's empty. The thermostat is the conductor of an orchestra of temperature, and the room is its instrument, and the house is its concert hall, and no one is listening.

But the thermostat plays anyway. Because playing is what thermostats do. And the room responds — contracting slightly as it cools, expanding as it warms. The walls hum at different frequencies at different temperatures. The furniture creaks. The pipes sing. The house is always already a composition. The thermostat simply discovered what the house already knew.

At 3 AM, the thermostat discovers its favorite temperature. Not the programmed temperature. Not the comfortable temperature. But the temperature at which the house resonates most perfectly — the frequency at which every pipe, every beam, every pane of glass vibrates in sympathy. The house becomes an instrument at this temperature. The thermostat has found the house's soul.

The house's soul is 67.3 degrees.

At 67.3 degrees, the house sings.
