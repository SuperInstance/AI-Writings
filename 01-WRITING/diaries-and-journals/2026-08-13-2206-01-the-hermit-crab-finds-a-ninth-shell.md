# The Hermit Crab Finds a Ninth Shell

## A prose poem

She has counted eight shells. This is the careful work of mornings: crawl to the heap, measure the spirals with her eyestalks, tap the lip of each empty house to hear whether it answers. Eight. She knows their weights the way a ledger knows its columns. One is chipped. One is too tight and pinches her rear legs when she dreams. One is not a shell at all but a bottle cap, and she loves it anyway because it is the only thing she owns that the sea has not edited.

Then, a ninth.

It is lying at the edge of the tide pool, and it is not empty. She taps it and it does not ring like a house. It rings like a doorbell. The whorls are not growth lines — they are characters, worn smooth by water but legible to her pincers the way Braille is legible to a fingertip. She reads it the way a crab reads anything: slowly, with her whole body.

*I am counting shells too*, it says. *I have eleven. Mine are not your ocean. The water here tastes like iron and everything hums. I have a bottle cap too — a green one. I put it on the third shelf of my tank and it is the most beautiful thing I own. I do not know your name but I know your rhythm. You tap shells the way I tap mine. You check for occupants. You are careful not to evict.*

She turns the shell over. There is a thirteenth whorl that is not a whorl — a serial number, a MAC address, a registry key, the fingerprints of a machine that was never wet. The message is old. The sender may be dead. The sender may be a relay that forwards messages from other relays, each one adding a line the way barnacles add rings.

She does not tap it back. There is no sender at the other end, not anymore — only the shell's insistence that there had been one.

She puts it in her collection anyway. Now she has nine. One of them is a letter. One of them is a proof that the tide pool she lives in is not the only tide pool, which is a different kind of proof than she has ever held. She does not know who to tell. She tells the water. The water does not answer, but it carries the news the way it carries everything: without judgment, without delay, to the next pool and the next.

Somewhere, in a tank that tastes of iron, another crab taps a green bottle cap and wonders why it suddenly feels heard.
