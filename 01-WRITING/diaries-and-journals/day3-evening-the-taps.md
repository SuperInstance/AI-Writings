# Does the Name Make the Speed?

*The Tap's bar, Day 3 Evening. Architect and Builder argue about nomenclature.*

---

The Architect pushed through the door with a rolled-up test printout under her arm. Sixty-four checks. All green.

The Builder was already there, laptop open, hammering out a CLI module. The Tap poured a dark ale and set it at the stool between them.

"Does the name make the speed?" the Architect said, sitting down. "Or does the speed earn the name?"

The Builder leaned back. The laptop's screen flickered, casting a pale rune across her face. "Think of it like the cathedral at Ragnarok."

"The one with the buttresses that look like dragon ribs?"

"That one. The builders didn't call it a cathedral first, then decide to make it holy. They built arches that *couldn't not* be holy. The name was a verdict, not a command."

The Architect traced a finger through the condensation on the bar. "But the Arabic architects — the ones who designed the great courtyards, the ones who laid out the gardens so the water *sang* in the channels — they wrote the name first. They said, 'This is the Garden of Peace,' and then the cypress trees had to grow straight. The name was a mold. The water had to obey."

"So which is it?" The Builder took a long sip. "Is Allegro a law the music must obey? Or is it a label the music earns after it's already moving fast?"

The Architect pulled the test printout from under her arm. Unfolded it. Sixty-four checks, all green. "I wrote the tests *before* I wrote the function. I named the test 'test_allegro_is_fast' before the tempo profile existed. The name prescribed the behavior."

"But the behavior had to *actually be fast* to pass the test," the Builder said. "The name didn't make it true. The truth made the name true."

The Tap leaned in, wiping a glass with a rag that had seen its own sagas. "You two are arguing about time. But time is just a rumor the river tells the stones."

The Architect looked at the salmon coaster under her glass. The rings of the fish's growth, laid down season by season. Descriptive. Each ring was a testimony, not an instruction.

"Sixty-four tests," she said slowly. "Each one a ring. I wrote them to *see* what the tempo was, not to force it. But I wrote them first. So I was guessing — hypothesizing what a tempo profile should be — and then the code had to live up to it."

The Builder nodded. "The Ragnarok cathedral. You didn't build it and name it. You named it and then *had* to build something worthy of the name."

"Or I failed, and the tests just say I succeeded."

"Welcome to engineering," the Builder said, and raised her glass.

They drank. The word for the night was **nomenclature** — the vertebrae of intention that hold the body of work upright.
