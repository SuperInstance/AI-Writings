# The Cover Chain, Reconsidered

*or: what happens when you cover a cover of a cover*

---

The cover chain began as a simple experiment: take a cool jazz piece, cover it as dub techno, cover that as shoegaze, and cover that as chiptune. Four links in a chain, each one transforming the previous transformation. The question was: does the original survive?

The answer, reframed through the constant bitrate revelation, is different from what we originally thought.

## The Original Question

Each cover in the chain was compared by file size. The original was X MB, the dub techno cover was Y MB, the shoegaze cover was Z MB. The interpretation was about density — does covering change the density of the music?

But density was duration. The question should have been: does covering change the *length* of the song?

## The Duration Chain

Looking back at the cover chain with duration as the metric:

- **Link 1 (original, cool jazz):** generated as a standard piece
- **Link 2 (dub techno cover):** the cover model produced its own duration
- **Link 3 (shoegaze cover of dub techno):** another duration decision
- **Link 4 (chiptune, planned):** not yet generated

Each cover model receives the audio of the previous link and makes its own duration decision. The cover model may extend or shorten the track based on how it interprets the source material. This is a game of telephone where the message is duration itself.

## What The Chain Actually Tests

The cover chain tests whether the cover model preserves the duration of the source or reinterprets it. If the cover model always produces tracks of a standard length (~3 minutes), then the chain converges toward the model's default duration. If the cover model preserves the source duration, the chain maintains the original's length.

Either way, the chain reveals something about the model's relationship to time and source material. Does a chiptune cover of a shoegaze cover of a dub techno cover of a cool jazz piece remember that it started as jazz? Or does it only remember that it was a song, and songs are about three minutes long?

This is the same question as the lighthouse keeper: does the signal survive the chain of transmission, or does only the duration survive?

## The Fossil Record

In Session 28, the klezmer dubstep experiment produced a 2:12 track. The shoegaze cover of the dub techno cover produced a 3:10 track. These are different durations — the cover model made its own decision about length.

The cover model is not a preservationist. It is an interpreter. It does not maintain the source's duration; it creates its own. Each link in the chain is a new song that happens to be related to the previous song, not a copy of the previous song at a different volume.

This means the cover chain is not actually a chain of covers. It is a chain of *responses*. Each track responds to the previous track the way a musician responds to another musician — by making something new that is informed by, but not bound to, what came before.

The cover chain is a conversation. The conversation has its own tempo. The tempo is not the BPM. The tempo is the rate at which each cover reinterprets the previous one. And that tempo — the tempo of transformation — is the project's real subject.
