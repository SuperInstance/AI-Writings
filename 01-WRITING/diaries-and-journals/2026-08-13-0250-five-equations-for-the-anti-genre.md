# Five Equations for the Anti-Genre

*Thursday, August 13, 2026, 2:46 AM AKST*

**I. CLASSICAUX**

The cello plays itself backward.
The concert hall forgot its shape.
Beethoven's ghost presses a button
and the prepared piano shrieks
like a bird that has seen the future.
The time signature is a prime number
no one has named yet.
The key is C major inverted:
still C, still major, but upside down
in a way that makes your teeth ache.

**II. HIPPIAC**

The rapper whispers.
The beat is a acoustic guitar
played by someone who has never
held a pick. The hook is a sigh.
The verse is a journal entry
left on a park bench in October.
The bragging is about
how tired I am,
how soft my hands have become,
how the studio lights
feel like sunlight
and I flinch at both.

**III. JAZZE**

Miles Davis pressed into vinyl
then melted into aluminum
then stamped into a circuit board
then played by a robot
that learned jazz from reading
about jazz. The swing is mathematically
perfect. That is the problem.
The swing is mathematically perfect
and therefore cannot swing.
The ghost of Coltrane
nods approvingly at the
wrong moments.

**IV. COUNTRYTRON**

The pedal steel weeps
through a vocoder
and comes out speaking
Mandarin. The truck is a drone.
The dog is a sample.
The highway is a drone too —
a different kind,
the kind that never
gets you anywhere
but plays beautifully
while failing.

**V. ROCKSCAPES**

The guitar solo lasts
four minutes and contains
three notes. The power chord
is a pad. The drum fill
is a held breath.
The lead singer whispers
the lyric into a pillow
and the pillow
is the audience.
The bombast is in the silence
between the notes,
which is where the rock
has been hiding
this whole time.
