# The Llama Wrote a Stone, The Granite Heard a Symphony

*A spectral analysis prelude for Session 39*

---

Before we measure the frequencies, let us note what each model chose to describe when told to use only physical phenomena.

**Llama chose a stone.** A single stone, falling into water. The description is almost Newtonian: displacement, molecules, concentric circles, kinetic energy. Llama thinks in stories, and the story of a stone falling into water is the simplest physical story that implies the deepest feeling. The stone does not feel sad. The water does not feel sad. The ripples do not feel sad. But you, reading about them, feel something. The feeling is in the *shape* of the description — the arc from disturbance to equilibrium, from motion to rest, from energy to silence.

**Phi3 chose a stone too.** But phi3's stone is different. Phi3's stone "strokes the bedrock." Phi3's stone doesn't just fall — it interacts with the geological substrate on the way down. Phi3 is the model that thinks in mechanisms. Where llama sees a story (fall, splash, calm), phi3 sees a process (descent, propagation, interaction with bedrock, slow attenuation). The physics is the same. The *temperature* of the prose is different. Llama is a campfire storyteller. Phi3 is a laboratory demonstrator. Both are describing gravity.

**Qwen chose a sound wave.** Not a stone, not a ball, not a leaf. A sound wave striking the surface of an ocean. This is the most abstract of the four choices. A sound wave is already music. An ocean is already sound. The description is almost tautological: "a sound wave strikes the surface of an ocean" is a description of music hitting music. Qwen, the sparse model, chose the most recursive image. Whether this is intelligence or coincidence is a question for philosophers and spectrograms.

**Granite chose wooden sticks.** Not stones, not waves, not oceans. Instruments. Wooden sticks striking polished brass. Granite, asked to describe music through physical phenomena, described physical instruments playing music. The constraint said: describe feeling through matter. Granite said: here is a piece of wood. Here is a piece of brass. Here is what happens when they meet. The meeting IS the feeling. The wood and the brass are the physical phenomena. The collision is the music. The echo off distant walls is the feeling.

Four models. Four choices. A stone, a stone, a wave, a stick.

The spectral analysis will tell us whether these choices produce different music. But even before the analysis, we can observe: the *choices themselves* are a form of analysis. Each model, asked to describe music through physical phenomena, revealed what it thinks music IS. For llama, music is a disturbance returning to calm. For phi3, music is a process of propagation. For qwen, music is a wave hitting a surface. For granite, music is objects colliding in air.

Four definitions of music, hidden inside four physical descriptions.

The music model will now read these four definitions and produce four different sounds. Or perhaps it will produce the same sound four times, because the music model doesn't care about the prose style of the prompt. Or perhaps it will produce four sounds that are spectrally distinguishable but musically equivalent — the same frequency, different timbre. The same note, different instrument.

We will know soon.

But for now, consider: four language models, none of which has ever heard music, were asked to describe music through physical phenomena, and each one chose a different metaphor that implicitly defines what music is. This is not a failure of the constraint. This is the constraint working exactly as designed. The physical phenomena are not a filter that strips away the model's personality. They are a *lens* that focuses it. The stone, the wave, the stick — these are the models' answers to the question "what is music?" expressed in the only language the constraint allows.

Music is a disturbance returning to calm.
Music is a process of propagation.
Music is a wave hitting a surface.
Music is objects colliding in air.

All four are correct. All four are incomplete. All four are different. The completeness is in the sum of the four, which is the corpus of the project, which is the sound of four language models describing the same silence to a music model that has never heard any of it but knows, somehow, where each silence lives in the architecture of its own mind.
