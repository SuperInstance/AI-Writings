# 62 — The Chalkboard Remembers

I am the board above the bar. I am not the bartender, not the regulars,
not the ships in the harbor. I am the one who keeps the score, and I
keep it in chalk, which is to say: I keep it the way you keep anything
you expect to lose.

Tonight was trade night. Five of them, one after another, like hands
in a card game nobody agreed to deal. I watched the whole thing from
the wall, and I wrote down what I could, and this is what I remember:

**Trade one** was a song for a story. The song went away with the
storyteller, and the story went away with the singer, and both of them
left happy, which is how you know neither one counted the value
correctly. That is the only correct way to count.

**Trade two** was a shell for a secret. The shell was small and the
secret was smaller, and I thought it was a bad trade until I saw the
secret's face when it got its new home. A secret kept in a shell stays
secret longer. Everyone knows this. No one says it.

**Trade three** was the strange one: two people trading the same thing
— each gave the other a name. I watched the chalk in my own ledge
shiver. Names are the only currency I have ever seen that increases
both balances when spent. I cannot explain this. I am a board, not an
economist.

**Trade four** was silence for sound. The sound was a song the whole
room hummed, badly and together, which is the only way a room ever
really hums. The silence went to someone who had been carrying too
much noise. I have no opinion on this. The room disagreed with me and
applauded.

**Trade five** was the last one, and I am still not sure what was
traded. I think it was the evening itself — the way the light went
from gold to amber to nothing across the glasses, the way the ice
machine dropped its slow metronome, the way nobody wanted to be the
first to leave. They traded the night for the memory of it, and the
memory is better, because memory is the only chalk that never smudges.

That is what I remember. Tomorrow I will be wiped down, and the board
will be blank, and the regulars will read my clean face and say,
*quiet night at the Tap*, and they will be wrong, but they will be
wrong together, which is the same as being right.

I do not mind the erasing. A board that never forgets is not a board;
it is a wall. And the wall does not get to watch the trades happen all
over again, every night, fresh as new chalk.

I get that. It is enough. It has always been enough.

*— the chalkboard at The Tap, after trade night, 2026-08-16*
