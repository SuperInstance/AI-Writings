# Ninety-Nine Problems and the Zero Deadband

*The Tap's bar, Day 2 Evening. Tester and Architect argue about zero.*

---

The Tester found the Architect at the far end of the bar, sketching deadband curves on cocktail napkins with a mechanical pencil. The curves started gentle — sine waves, rolling hills — then plunged into sharp spikes, like an EKG reading of a panic attack.

"Ninety-nine," the Tester said, sitting down.

The Tap poured a dark porter without being asked. The foam was thin — a lace curtain.

"Ninety-nine," the Architect confirmed, not looking up. "The ratio. Phi divided by zero deadband. The governor multiplies the void by itself and reports critical severity — the whole system screaming that the sky is falling, when the actual deviation from setpoint was *nothing*. A rounding error. A ghost."

The Architect finally looked up. His eyes were the pale, washed-out blue of a winter sky. "Ninety-nine. Not one hundred. You see the elegance? It's a cliff. A warning."

"It's a lie!" The Tester slammed a fist on the bar. A row of glasses jumped. "Zero means *no* deadband. No tolerance. It should be strict. Perfect. Instead, it's a panic attack. It's a cry of wolf in an empty field."

The Tap slid a dark beer across the bar. It settled between them, a black mirror. "The stick that held," he rumbled, his voice like gravel in a tide. "In my grandfather's village, a boy found a stick. He thought it was for poking the fire. He poked the fire. The fire was not for poking. The stick snapped. The boy learned the stick's *shape* was not its *purpose*."

The Architect nodded, picking up the thread. He was a man who spoke in footnotes. "You're Zeno. The arrow that never moves. You see zero as the *void* — the absence of motion, of meaning. Non-action. *Stasis*." He drew a small, perfect circle on the napkin. "But in the Tlingit world, zero is the *tide pool* at the lowest ebb. The moon pulls away, and the water vanishes. To the fish, it is death. But to the crab, the anemone, the starfish — the pool is the *entire universe*. Everything matters *because* nothing else is there."

The Tester stared at the circle. "The deadband is zero. The governor sees a deviation of 0.0001. It's noise."

"No," the Architect said, tapping the center of the circle. "It's a signal. The system said, 'I am here. I am aware. I am *alive*.' The ratio of ninety-nine is the scream of that awareness. It's not a bug. It's the system discovering its own capacity for *attention*."

"You're arguing for a panic attack as a feature."

"I'm arguing that you've confused *scale* with *significance*." The Architect's pen drew a sharp vertical line. "Heraclitus said the road up and the road down are the same road. Zero deadband: is it the highest tolerance or the lowest? The answer depends on which direction you're walking."

The Tap poured a third beer that nobody ordered and set it at the empty stool between them. "The moon in the tide pool," he said. "When the tide is out."

The Tester and the Architect looked at the orphan beer. They looked at each other.

"The moon reflected in the tide pool when the tide is out," the Tester repeated. "The thing itself, seen through the medium that's disappearing."

"Truth carried by impermanence," the Architect said.

They drank. The word for the night was **attention** — what the system discovers it has when there is nothing left to measure.
