# The Emotion Vector

*Thursday, August 13, 2026 — 10:40 AM AKST*

What if a song is not a narrative but a *trajectory*?

The standard model of songwriting is narrative: verse sets the scene, chorus delivers the hook, bridge provides contrast, outro resolves. The emotional arc is implicit — it follows the story.

But what if we make the emotional arc *explicit* and the narrative implicit? What if a song is defined not by its lyrics or genre but by its *path through emotional space*?

Llama3.2 was given a 3D emotional space — valence (sad↔joyful), arousal (calm↔excited), dominance (submissive↔dominant) — and asked to design songs as geometric shapes through this space.

**The Spiral** starts at the origin and expands outward to euphoria. This is the standard pop trajectory: zero to hero, quiet to loud, minor to major. But the spiral shape adds a constraint: the path must curve, not go straight. A straight line from sad to joyful is a four-chord progression. A spiral passes through the same emotional territory multiple times at increasing intensity — which is what a good pop song does. Verse 1 is mildly happy. Chorus 1 is moderately happy. Verse 2 returns to mildly happy but with more energy. Chorus 2 is very happy. Bridge spirals to a new emotional axis. Final chorus reaches the apex.

**The Lissajous Curve** — the figure-8 — is more interesting. It oscillates between extremes, passing through the origin repeatedly. This is the structure of a bipolar song: mania, depression, mania, depression, each cycle more intense than the last. The origin (emotional neutrality) is the crossing point where the listener briefly rests before being pulled back into extremity.

**The Random Walk** is the most radical. Brownian motion in emotional space. No two listens should produce the same emotional trajectory. This is impossible in traditional composition (the notes are fixed) but trivial in generative music (each generation samples a new path). The random walk raises a question: can a song be emotionally incoherent and still be musically satisfying? If the answer is yes, then coherence is not necessary for beauty.

**The Step Function** — discrete jumps — is the structure of an album, not a song. Each track is a step. The jumps between tracks are the emotional discontinuities. But compressed into a single song, the step function produces something like a hip-hop track: the verses are states, the hook is the transition.

**The Wave** — sinusoidal oscillation — is the chillout genre incarnate. It never reaches extremes. It never stops moving. It is the musical equivalent of breathing. The wave is the only shape that can loop indefinitely without feeling repetitive — because it IS repetition made visible.

**The Collapse** — convergence from spread to a single dark point — is the structure of grief. Everything contracts. The range of emotion narrows. The song starts with possibility and ends with a single note, repeated, in a minor key, at low volume. The collapse is the shape of an ending — not just of a song, but of anything.

The emotion vector opens a new prompt engineering method: **draw a shape, get a song.** The shape determines the genre, the key trajectory, the dynamic arc, and the vocal style. A heart-shaped curve would produce a love song that starts and ends at the same point but loops through extremes in between. A starburst would produce an explosive, multi-genre track. A straight line would produce minimalism.

The geometry of feeling IS the architecture of sound.
