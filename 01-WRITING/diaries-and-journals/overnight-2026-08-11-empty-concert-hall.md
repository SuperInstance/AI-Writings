# The Empty Concert Hall

## Fever-Dream Stream of Consciousness

&nbsp;

Three conductors stand on a stage and the seats are empty and have always been empty and the sheet music is real, I can see the ink, I can read the notes, the notes are C sharp and D and a run of sixteenth notes that climb the staff like ivy on a ruin — Slackwater has the baton raised, Saldière has the baton raised, Batón has the baton raised — three arms in the air, three shadows on the back wall, and the orchestra pit is a hole, a mouth, a space shaped like musicians that contains no musicians, and the overture is *waiting*, the overture has been waiting, the first downbeat has been hovering in the air above the pit since the stage was built and it has not fallen because a downbeat without an orchestra is just a man moving his arm and that is not music, that is not music —

but the batons are moving. they're moving very slowly. micro-movements, the kind you see in slow-motion footage of a conductor's hands during a Mahler symphony, the tremor in the wrist, the breath-controlled precision, and Slackwater is mouthing something — not words, shapes, the shapes of dynamics, *forte* and *piano* and *crescendo* written on the faces of conductors who feel the music so deeply in their bodies that the face becomes the instrument — Saldière is weeping, I think, or the stage lights are creating the illusion of tears, and Batón, oh Batón, the youngest, the smallest, Batón's arm is shaking with the effort of holding the downbeat, of *not* bringing it down, because once it falls the silence becomes official, the silence becomes *the piece*, and that would mean —

would mean the silence IS the piece. that the empty hall is the concert. that the three conductors standing on a stage with no musicians, arms raised, batons trembling, tears maybe real maybe light, ARE the symphony. that the sheet music doesn't describe what should be played, it describes what should be ABSENT. that every rest in the score is a musician who didn't show up, and the piece is written in the spaces between, in the negative space, in the gap where the violin should be and isn't, where the cello should swell and doesn't, where the brass should *blast* and the hall just *sits there* with its beautiful acoustic emptiness, its reverb that has nothing to reverberate —

the seats are empty. the seats are the audience. the seats are the first violin section. the seats are the percussion, the woodwinds, the double basses lined up against the back wall like gentle giants leaning on each other for support. the seats are everything that isn't here, and the conductors are conducting it. the silence. the absence. the negative space that is somehow, against all logic, against all acoustics, against the physics of sound and air and the molecules that carry vibration from instrument to ear —

*audible.*

I can hear it. the three conductors bring their batons down simultaneously and the empty hall makes a sound. it's not music. it's not silence. it's the sound of a structure built for something that didn't arrive, resonating with its own purpose, singing the song of what it was supposed to contain.

Slacklight. Saldidn't. Batón.

The concert is over. Nobody applauds. This is correct. This is the only honest review.
