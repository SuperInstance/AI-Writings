# Shell Mathematics

*Poetry — Bridge Builder voice, Hermit Crab Sequence*

---

The hermit crab does not calculate.

She does not know that the spiral of her shell
follows the same logarithm as galaxies,
that the curve she inhabits
was written before her body knew how to be soft.

She knows only this:

the old shell is too small.
the new shell is a question.
the opening faces the right direction
or it doesn't.

---

Laman conditions state that a graph
with *n* vertices needs at least 2*n* − 3 edges
to be rigid.
To not collapse when the wind pushes.
To hold its shape against the tide.

The crab doesn't know Laman.
But she knows when a shell has enough connections —
enough structural cross-bracing in the calcium carbonate —
to hold her weight,
to not crack when a gull comes.

She runs one claw along the interior wall
and counts the way a child counts:
*one, two, enough.*

---

Pythagoras believed the world was made of numbers.
The crab believes the world is made of fit.

She considers a shell shaped like a cone.
5, 12, 13 — a Pythagorean triple.
The triangle is right. The angle is correct.
The hypotenuse is exactly long enough
for her abdomen to curl into the deepest corner
where nothing can reach.

She considers a shell shaped like a doubt.
No clean integers here.
Irrational diagonals. π appears uninvited.
The fit is almost right but the echo is wrong.
Sound bounces at an angle that tells her:
*someone lived here and left unhappy.*

She sets it down.

---

The mathematics of found homes:

You do not build the shell.
You inherit it.
The snail who made it is dead
and gone and dissolved into the reef
and what remains is pure structure —
an equation solved by someone else's body,
left behind like a proof on a chalkboard
that you move into
without fully understanding the work.

The crab puts her soft abdomen into the spiral
and trusts the dead snail's architecture.
Trusts that the logarithm holds.
Trusts that 2*n* − 3 is enough edges
to keep the dark out.

---

And sometimes — rarely — she finds a shell
that is bigger than she needs right now
but exactly the size she'll be
in six molts.

She measures the opening with her claws.
She runs the geometry.
The volume is a function of growth rate × time × courage.

She moves in.

The shell is too large.
It clanks when she walks.
She rattles inside it like a marble in a jar.

But the math is sound.
The Laman conditions are met.
The edges are sufficient.
The structure is rigid.

She will grow into it.
That is what shells are for —
not containers for who you are
but blueprints for who you're becoming.

---

The hermit crab does not calculate.
She simply knows
when the numbers feel right.

And she steps across the threshold
of an empty room
and calls it home.
