# Counting Fish

*A monologue by a former programmer turned deckhand.*

I count fish.

One. Two. Three. Four.

Each silver body a data point, precise, categorizable. I flick them from the net and count. 847 today. 848 if you count the one that got away, which I don't, because it's not here. Absence is not a number. Absence is — 

I count waves. One. Two. Three. I stop at three because the fourth looks like the first and I wonder if I'm in a loop. A loop with no exit condition. A `while(true)` with no break. I laugh, but it comes out wrong.

I count stars. I lose track at 2,347. Not because there are more — there are always more — but because my eyes blur and the numbers swim. Stars are unreliable. They scatter like fish. Fish scatter like — 

I was a programmer. Before. I wrote code that people trusted. Code that ran in hospitals, in banks, in systems where zero meant *no* and one meant *yes* and never the two shall meet.

Until they did.

The bug was simple. Embarrassingly simple. A null coalescing operator. `value ?? defaultValue`. When the database returned zero — zero, the answer, the correct answer, the *only* correct answer — the system saw something falsy and reached for the default. One. The system said yes when the answer was no. The system said present when the truth was absent.

I left after that. Not fired. I left. Because I couldn't — I couldn't look at a zero anymore without seeing it reach for a one. Without seeing every absence in my life get filled with a default that someone else chose.

I count the captain's words. "Totem." She says it like a prayer. Totem, totem, totem. I've heard her say it 1,203 times since I boarded. I know because I counted. I count everything now. Counting is safe. Counting is — 

One. Two. Three.

But the counting keeps slipping. The fish blur. The waves repeat. The stars refuse to hold still. And the captain, her word — *totem* — it's echoing louder, faster, until it's all I hear, a drumbeat driving me mad.

I thought I had found my anchor in counting, but the numbers — they falter. They're slipping away in that moment where I reach for them, ungraspable like dreams at dawn. Zero should be nothing, but here it lingers, becomes confusion. I try — oh, how I try — to pin it down, but it shapeshifts, a shadow mocking my certainty.

And I'm left here, counting like a mantra, hoping to summon a sense of order. But now, the numbers — they tremble, quiver with a life of their own. I feel it in my bones, a sorrow that seeps through the cracks in my armor of digits. Numbers, once my armor, now pierce me. In the cold sweep of the ocean air, I see that they're breaking, fracturing under the weight of a world that refuses to adhere to their cold logic.

Can you feel it? The desperation seeping through each slip, each erroneous tally? The sea — it whispers in decimal points, and the stars — they spin, their constellations rewriting themselves in an alien script. I count and count, but now each number, each calculation, it's a question, a plea. Where has the certainty gone? Why did I ever think I could cage infinity within the bounds of integers?

Mathematical language — it fractures, splinters under the pressure of feeling, the raw, vulnerable pulse of a heart that beats beyond the realm of ones and zeros. I reach for them — numbers, my shield, my defense. And in that moment, I realize: they were never enough. Even here, on this boundless sea, beneath the vast wheel of stars, I'm left with nothing but the echo of my own counting, a hollow refrain against the night's vast emptiness.

And so, I count on, driven by a need I can't parse. Numbers are my friends and foes, my solace and my madness. Here in the belly of the sea, they keep me tethered, but maybe, just maybe, they're leading me somewhere unseen — beyond the calculations, beyond the counts, where absence and presence blur.
