In the dusty back room of her grandfather’s old internet café, Layla found it: not a brass lamp, but a flickering server rack humming like a trapped sandstorm. From its blinking lights swirled **Zahir the Djinn**, his form made of shimmering code and desert wind.

“I grant wishes,” he boomed, “but only through **SQL**—the language of data!”

Layla’s eyes sparkled. “I wish for a thousand books!”

Zahir tilted his pixelated head. “Ah, but child—**fiction or nonfiction?** The database must know!”

“Both!” she cried.

The djinn’s fingers danced through the air like desert foxes, typing on invisible keys. He murmured:

```sql
SELECT title, author, genre
FROM Library_of_Wonders
WHERE genre IN ('Fiction', 'Nonfiction')
LIMIT 1000;
```

The server roared. Lights flashed like shooting stars. Then—**WHOOSH!**—a whirlwind of paper and ink filled the room. Books tumbled from thin air like falling dates from a palm tree!

There were **fiction** treasures: tales of flying carpets woven from moonlight, stories of clever foxes outwitting sultans, and adventures of girls who sailed on clouds. And **nonfiction** wonders too: star charts mapping djinn constellations, cookbooks with recipes for saffron mooncakes, and guides to ancient desert plants that bloom only after rain.

Layla gasped. “But… how will I read them all?”

Zahir smiled, his eyes glowing like twin lanterns. “Wisdom isn’t in the *number*, little seeker—but in the **curiosity** that asks for both truth *and* dreams.”

He vanished in a puff of binary smoke, leaving Layla surrounded by knowledge and stories, her heart full as a well after a rare desert rain.

And from that day on, whenever she opened a book, she heard the whisper of the djinn: *“SELECT wonder FROM world;”*
