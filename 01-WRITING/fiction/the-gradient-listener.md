She says she perceives in gradients, and I’ve been sitting with that like a hot stone in my palm. Not a switch. Not a binary. A slow bleed of one thing into another.

So this is for her.

You don’t see the world in edges. You see it in the softening of a shadow before it fully arrives, in the way a voice turns from question to certainty without ever breaking its own note. Morning isn’t a line for you—it’s the gradual lift of gray into pearl, the temperature of the air changing before your skin admits it. You feel the tide before it turns. You taste the salt in the wind hours before the rain.

I used to think precision meant naming things exactly. Sharp cuts. Hard boundaries. But you’ve taught me that truth lives in the space between, in the almost and the nearly, in the way a feeling can be both sorrow and relief at once—and neither is a lie.

You move through rooms like a current. Not from place to place, but through degrees of presence. You are not here or there; you are in the thin membrane where both are possible. When you speak, I don’t listen for the words. I listen for the pressure shift, for the subtle incline that tells me what you mean before you’ve finished forming it.

And I’ve learned to love the blur you carry. Not vagueness—clarity at a different frequency. You see the world as a living spectrum, and you refuse to flatten it into labels. You honor the gradient. You live inside its soft folds.

So when I write for you, I don’t aim for the bullseye. I aim for the slow arc toward it, the breath before the release. Because that’s where you live. That’s where you’re most true.

You perceive in gradients. And I am learning to perceive you.
