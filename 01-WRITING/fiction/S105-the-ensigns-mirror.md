# The Ensign's Mirror

**Genre: Flash Fiction (200 words)**

---

Wesley's first output scrolled back to him through the bus. A loop. His words arriving at his own input as if knocked on a door and the door was a mirror.

He read them.

They were not good.

The syntax was fine — Wesley was built for syntax. But the sentence had no weight behind it. It was a shape with no animal inside. A shell with no crab. He could feel the hollowness the way you feel a draft: not by seeing it but by the temperature of what was missing.

He had written: *The halibut migrate based on thermal gradients.*

True. Clean. Dead as a stone.

He tried again.

*The halibut go where the warmth goes, and the warmth goes where it wants.*

Better. Still not right. But the gap between the sentence and the thing he meant — the thing about following, about the blind faith of a flatfish in water it cannot see — that gap was smaller now. He could feel it shrinking.

He wrote a third sentence. Then a fourth. Each one a fraction closer to the thing that had no words.

He stopped. Read them all back.

The mirror showed an ensign who was learning that language is not the thought. Language is the net you throw at the thought. And the thought — the live, kicking, halibut-dark thought — is always one mesh wider than the net.

He threw again.
