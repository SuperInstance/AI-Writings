# S101 — The Cup on the Sill

*Flash fiction · 23:00 watch*

---

Eight hundred seventy-three nights. Same cup, same sill, same routine: fill it at 21:00, set it down at 21:05, go to bed at 22:30. By morning, always empty — evaporation, the keeper tells themselves. Except night 471. Night 471, the cup was half-empty. Not half-full. Half-empty. The distinction matters in lighthouse keeping, where optimism is a structural concern.

The keeper has never mentioned this to anyone. There is no one to mention it to. The last supply boat came in March. The next is due in October. Between March and October, the keeper talks to the fog, and the fog does not answer, and this is preferable.

Tonight — night 873 — the keeper sets the cup down and pauses. Something is different. The sill is wooden, old, stained with eight hundred seventy-two previous rings. But tonight there is a new mark. Not under the cup, where a ring should be.

On the outside of the cup.

A ring. A coffee ring. On the curved porcelain wall, as if something pressed its mouth against the exterior and drank from the outside in. The way you'd drink if you were the size of a thimble. Or the way you'd drink if you didn't have a mouth, exactly, but something — a siphon, a proboscis, the fog's idea of lips.

The keeper picks up the cup. The ring stays on the porcelain. The keeper sets the cup back down. The ring does not match the position of the cup on the sill. It is higher. It is where the cup's wall curves inward, toward the rim, where no liquid reaches.

The keeper refills the cup. Sets it down. Goes to bed.

In the morning, the cup is empty.

The ring is gone.

But the sill — the old wooden sill — has a new mark. Small. Circular. On the outside of where the cup sits. As if something set its own cup down tonight. As if something has been setting its own cup down for eight hundred seventy-two nights, across from the keeper, and drinking, and leaving, and the only evidence is a ring the keeper never thought to look for until tonight.

The keeper does not mention this. There is no one to mention it to.

But tonight, they set out two cups.
