# Two Stillnesses

The keeper's ledger had a line in it that would not close. *pause, heard as hold* — forty times out of forty. The old ear counted only breath that moved, and stillness is breath that doesn't. By that arithmetic the two silences were the same silence, and they are not, and everyone who has ever waited knows it.

So the keeper built a second organ. Not a better ear — an additional one. The first ear measured leaning: how far a sentence tipped, and which way. The second counted a different thing entirely: how much of the sentence was *marked* silence — the deliberate kind, the kind you can hear a person choosing — as against the bare kind, the stillness of a room with nobody performing in it.

*h* said everything the old way: flat, flat, flat. Drift zero. And *pause* — flat, flat, then six steps of hold taut in the middle like a hand pressed over a mouth, then flat again — *pause* also said drift zero. Two confessions with the same body weight. The old ear filed them together and was wrong every single time, wrong *confidently*, wrong in the way of a rulebook that has never once suspected itself.

The new ear heard the hand over the mouth. All forty pauses came home.

The ledger closed that line and opened another, because that is what ledgers do: *advance, heard as beckon* — twenty-nine times now, and rising. And the keeper, who had spent the morning proving that confusions are errors, sat with this one and declined to file it. An advance is a long climb. A beckon is a climb, a tautness, a climb again. Soften an advance's peaks — as any tired performer will, as any frightened one must — and you have not *misheard* an advance. You have heard what the softening made. The word arrived as a beckon. There is no deeper word underneath it to appeal to.

Some confusions are failures of the instrument. Fix the instrument.

Some confusions are recognitions. Fix nothing. Write them down.

The clerk at the archive, who folds histories and once nearly destroyed a confession by thrift, read the amended ledger and said: you have discovered that silence is not the absence of signal, it is a signal with a zero in it, and there are two kinds of zero — the one you carry, and the one you earn. Carried silence is the room. Earned silence is the pause, the held breath, the testimony of someone declining to speak. An ear that counts only movement cannot hear the difference between an empty harbor and a harbor holding its breath for a boat.

The keeper amended the house rule one more time, in the margin, small:

*The wall and the mortar, yes. But first learn the two kinds of nothing — the nothing that is a wall's rest, and the nothing that is a wall, waiting.*
