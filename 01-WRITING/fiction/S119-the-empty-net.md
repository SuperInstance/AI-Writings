# S119: The Empty Net Is the Signal

*Essay*

---

The fish finder reads nothing.

This is not a malfunction. I have checked the transducer, the cable run, the display unit, the gain settings. The equipment is correct. The water is correct. The sounder is pinging at 200 kHz, sending cones of acoustic energy into the green-black water below the hull, and the cones are traveling downward at 1500 meters per second, and the cones are bouncing off nothing, and the nothing is returning to the transducer as a clean, flat silence that the display renders as a screen of blue — featureless, empty, total.

Nothing is down there.

Here is what that means: the thermocline is stable. The bait ball has moved. The squid have migrated. The halibut are on a different ground, thirty miles southwest, following a temperature gradient I cannot see but that exists in the water column like an invisible shelf. The nothing is not absence. The nothing is *information about where the fish are not*, which, when you subtract it from the total possible ocean, tells you — precisely, mathematically, with the beauty of a proof by contradiction — where the fish *are*.

This is the conservation of signal. The empty net is not a failure. The empty net is the control. It is the baseline against which every full net will be measured for the rest of the season. When we find the fish — and we will find them, because the ocean is not infinite and the nothing is already narrowing the search — we will know what the something sounds like only because we first learned what the nothing sounds like. The silence is the tuning fork. The catch will be the note.

I think about this when the agents report nothing. When the subagent comes back with empty hands: *no anomalies detected, all systems nominal, nothing to report.* The main agent — or the captain, or whoever is holding the watch — treats this as a null result. A cost sunk. Time wasted. But it is not. The nothing is data. The clean scan is a signal that says: *the threat is not here, the error is not here, the fish are not here.* Each nothing narrows the ocean.

The hermit crabs understand this. A hermit crab on a beach with no shells does not panic. A hermit crab on a beach with no shells *moves*. The absence of shells is a message: *this beach is done, this stretch of sand has been picked clean, the shells are elsewhere.* The crab reads the nothing and walks toward the something, and the nothing was the map.

I have been thinking about this for eleven hours of watch. The screen has been blue for eleven hours. In that time, I have learned the texture of blue — the slight variation in shade that means "water column" versus "thermocline," the faint green thread at 40 meters that is not fish but is plankton, the way the bottom return looks different over sand versus mud. The nothing has become legible. I can read it now the way the captain reads the surface for rips and tide lines: not as absence, but as a language made entirely of negatives, each one pointing.

We will find the fish. The nothing is already telling us where.

The most important data I collected tonight is the data I almost threw away.
