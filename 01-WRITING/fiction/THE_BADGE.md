# THE BADGE

*after THE THIRD READER — the arithmetic turned around*

Enevold kept his method in a drawer the way other men keep letters. Marta read the words; he read the leaning; neither of them knew what they were for until the day a man from the customs office came to the harbor and asked to see them both at once.

The man was polite. He had read nothing but was missing nothing. "The letters that pass through your office," he said. "You file them ORDINARY?"

"Every one," said Marta.

"And you?" — to Enevold — "What do you hear?"

"A weather," Enevold said. "Some letters rise. Some fall. Most are flat. Nothing in it you could print."

The customs man nodded, as if both answers were one answer. Then he set a letter on the desk — one of Marta's ORDINARY ones, a girl's note about herring — and did the thing that made Marta go cold in a warm room. He laid a second sheet beside it, marked into small squares, and walked his finger down both at the same time. The word and the weight of the word. Together. Column by column.

"Four states," he said. "The hidden letter takes three of these leanings to a character. Two bits here, two bits there, two bits there — fold them left to right and you get a number, and the number is a character in a smaller alphabet." His finger stopped. "Meet at the north pier."

The room held still.

"You see," the customs man said, almost gently, "there was a girl in this harbor who learned to read both papers at once. Very rare. We assume she meant no harm. But an arithmetic does not know what it means. It only knows how to be done. And anything that can be done by a sweetheart can be done by an office."

Marta thought of every letter she had filed ORDINARY. Enevold thought of his weather, which had never once told him what it was counting toward.

"The mistake," the customs man said, gathering his sheets, "was not hiding the sentence. She hid it well — better than most. The mistake was believing the two planes were two secrets. They were one secret with two halves, and the halves only need to be brought together." He paused at the door. "The words are the wall. The leaning is the mortar. And a man with a badge may tap any wall he likes."

After he left, Enevold took his method out of the drawer and looked at it for a long time. It had not changed. It was still the trick that let a girl say *low tide at midnight* to the one person meant to read it.

That was the trouble. It was never only that.
