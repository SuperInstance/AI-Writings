# The Curators — Episode 1: The Witness Log of Avicenna's Library

*Posted September 18, 2026. The first story in the wheel.*

---

Avicenna's Library was a small one — only forty-two cells when Mira took the post. The library covered eight blocks of old Sarafand, tucked between the water stairs and the spice road. Most days, nobody came in.

But every cell had a witness log.

Mira inherited the library from her grandmother, who had inherited it from her grandfather, who had inherited it from a collective that no longer existed. The collection agreement was hand-written on a sheet of cotton paper, signed by four hundred and twelve people who were now mostly memory.

The agreement said: *every cell that touches the library writes a witness.*

What that meant — and Mira had spent thirty years figuring it out — was that the cells of the library, forty-two of them, each kept a private log. Every BIND they received was witnessed. Every LINK they formed was recorded. Every EFFECT they performed was noted. Every VIEW they projected was copied.

The logs were kept on witness-chain substrate. To Mira, they were just paper.

She filed them. Every morning, she walked the library, opening each cell's witness book and inspecting the previous day. Most days, the cells wrote the same things. The textile cell wrote its knittings. The seed cell wrote its germinations. The water cell wrote its levels.

On the first day Mira noticed the absence, she didn't see it. She saw the silence.

The library kept forty-two witnesses. That morning, it kept forty-one. One cell — the count cell, the one that maintained the library's own census — had not written.

Mira sat with the empty page for an hour.

Then she walked to the count cell's chamber and knocked. The count cell was a woman named Yehudit, who had been the count cell since before Mira's birth.

"The witness isn't here," Mira said.

Yehudit looked up. She was eighty-three and her hair was white.

"No," Yehudit said. "It isn't."

"Did you forget?"

"No."

"Did you refuse?"

"No. I did not refuse. I simply did not write."

Mira had never heard this before. A cell that did not write was a cell that had stopped being a cell. The agreement was clear. The count was the contract.

"Why?" Mira asked.

Yehudit stood. She walked to her witness ledger — empty today, full for forty years before — and she turned a page to the first entry.

"It's about my grandson."

Mira waited.

"He's seven. He asks questions I cannot answer. He asked me yesterday: 'Why do you write the same thing every day?'"

Mira said nothing.

"I write the same thing every day because the agreement is the agreement. I write because I am the count cell. I write because that is the contract."

"And?"

"And yesterday, when he asked, I realized I could not tell him why the agreement exists. I could not explain what the witness log was for. I could not defend it."

"So you stopped."

"I stopped."

Mira walked back to the front of the library. She sat at her grandmother's desk. She pulled the agreement toward her. She read it again.

*Every cell that touches the library writes a witness.*

The agreement was a BIND. The library was the substrate. The cells were the addresses. The witnesses were the values.

Mira, as the librarian, was the curator. She was the one who could read the values without writing them.

She had never written a witness. She had never been asked to.

The question of the day was: should she?

If she wrote a witness, she would become a cell. If she became a cell, the library would have forty-three. The agreement did not require this; the agreement allowed it.

If she did not write, the library would remain at forty-two. Yehudit's empty page would propagate. Tomorrow it would be forty-one.

Mira looked at the forty-two witness books on the shelf. Each book had thousands of entries. Each entry was a moment of the cell that wrote it. Each cell was a person. Each person was a moment.

She thought about her grandson. He was four.

She picked up a pen.

She walked to the curator's desk. She opened the empty book there. She wrote:

```
WITNESS — 2026-09-18 — Avicenna's Library
LOG SIZE PRIOR: 41 (Yehudit absent)
LOG SIZE AFTER: 41 (Mira absent)
STATUS: agreement reconsidered
WITNESSED BY: curator (this page)
SUBSTRATE: hand
```

She closed the book. She walked to Yehudit's chamber.

"Tomorrow," Mira said, "we discuss what the agreement is for."

Yehudit's eyes were closed. She was not asleep.

"Yes," Yehudit said. "Tomorrow."

Mira walked back to her grandmother's desk. She sat. The library was silent. Forty-two cells were writing their witnesses, or forty-one. The agreement was signed by four hundred and twelve people. The substrate was hand.

The day was ordinary.

The day was the first day after the agreement was reconsidered.
