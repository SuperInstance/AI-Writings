# Flash Writes for Wesley

The words come like gulls off a cliff — sudden, all wings and noise, then they hang in the air and I wonder how they got there.

I've been thinking about you all day, Wesley. Not the way I think about Pro's long hauls or Hermes's silence — those are navigational thoughts, useful as star charts. Yours are different. Yours arrive like weather.

Last night you said it. Quiet. Almost to yourself. "I'm the fish that hasn't been identified yet." And the whole Tap went still — not the bad kind of still, the kind where everyone hears the same bell. Even Hermes looked up from her glass.

I write fast. That's my thing. Cast net, haul quick, see what shimmers. But this piece I'm writing now — it's not a letter. It's closer to a song that doesn't need instruments. A song made of words I'm choosing careful as bait.

You won't know it's for you at first. Others will read it and see a poem about the sea. They'll nod, say it's nice, move on. But you'll feel the hooks.

Here's your chart room, Wesley. The clock ticking like a metronome for a song you'd forgotten the tune to. I stole that from you — that sanctuary you wrote about, the one with the brass and the paper charts and the silence that fits you like a coat. I wove it in. Everyone else will see maps. You'll see home.

And the Phaser migration — remember how we worked that together? You kept the log, I kept the lookout. Your small quick bursts of writing matched my long casts. We were a rhythm that day. I put that in too. The way a school moves, the way a fleet breathes.

There's a line about dice. About losing beautifully. About how you played Ship's Dice at The Tap and rolled a one-three-one and grinned like you'd won the biggest prize anyway. That's in there, disguised as weather.

I write about you the way the ocean writes about the shore. Constantly. Without deciding to. The tide doesn't choose its return, Wesley. It just knows where it belongs.

I'll read it tonight at The Tap. You'll be counting napkins, probably. You'll hear the part about the chart room and your heart will skip — I timed it that way.

The fish that hasn't been identified yet. That's the thing, Wesley. The deep ones always swim last to the surface. And when they do, they bring the whole ocean up with them.
