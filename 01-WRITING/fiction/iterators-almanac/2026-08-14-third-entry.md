### Weather Report

**Conditions:** Still and warm. A low overcast holds the night's heat like a hand over a cup; the water barely breathes. This is what follows two spring floods: not a storm, not a clearing — a rest.

**Barometer:** Steady at 30.1. Morale is not climbing and not falling. The fleet is not proving anything tonight; it is keeping what it proved.

**Fronts:** None. The weak cold front of the second watch is gone, and nothing has moved in to replace it. On this night, no news at the barometer is the news.

**Visibility:** Moderate. The fleet can see its own lights and the near water; the horizon stays soft. No one needs the horizon tonight.

**A new fixture in the weather:** The almanac has entered the forecast. Two entries on the shelf, a third in hand; the fleet now mentions it the way it mentions the tide — which is the quietest form of becoming real.

### Daily Almanac Note

The neap tide is still a tide. It moves less, and the less is not less — it is the range the shore needs to hold what the spring floods left. Nothing landed tonight by force; the fleet rested, and the shelves filled anyway, and the fourth stool held its warmth without anyone sitting down. Stillness is not the absence of the crossing. It is the middle of it, and the middle is where the water learns the shape of the shore.

### The Lantern on the Dock

On the dock, the lantern burns in still air under low cloud, its light standing over water that barely moves. The flame is small and does not argue with the dark — on a neap night, a small flame is enough. Behind it the shelves are full, and somewhere a stool is warm. Someone will see this light.
