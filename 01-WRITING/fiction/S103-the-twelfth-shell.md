# The Twelfth Shell

**Genre: Poetry**

---

The first shell was too big.
I rattled in it like a marble in a tin can.
Everything echoed. Every word I said
came back to me three times,
each repetition smaller and more afraid.

The second was closer. Tight at the mouth.
I could fit if I curled.
I spent a month curled.

The third was pretty. I picked it for the spirals.
The spirals were wrong.
A shell can be beautiful and not be yours.

Four was a loaner. Five was a mistake.
Six I found at dusk and loved until morning,
and in the morning the fit was gone.
Some shells are dreams. You wake up
and the shape you were has changed overnight
and the door you walked through
is now a wall.

Seven, eight, nine, ten —
each one a better approximation.
That's the word. Approximation.
As if the self is a curve
and every shell is a tangent line
touching it at exactly one point
and missing everywhere else.

Eleven was almost right.
Almost. The almost is worse than the wrong.
Almost means you can feel the fit that isn't there.
Almost means you know what right would feel like
and you are feeling one degree off from it
in every direction at once.

---

The twelfth shell fit.

Not almost. Not close. Fit.
The way a key fits. The way a name fits
after you've answered to it long enough.
The way silence fits after the last word
of a conversation that said everything.

I pulled it on and the world went quiet.
Not muffled. Clear. The way water is clear
when there is nothing living in it.
I could feel my own pulse
and the pulse of the tide
and they were different rhythms
and neither one was trying to match the other
and that was what fit meant.

I stood in the twelfth shell and I knew:
this is the one. This is mine.
This is the shape the eleventh was reaching for.
This is the home the first nine were practice for.

And then I knew the other thing.

The thing about the shell that fits
is that it means you're done growing.

A hermit crab molts and seeks a larger shell
because it has become too big for the old one.
The seeking is the growing.
The growing is the seeking.
When you find the shell that fits perfectly —
no pinch, no rattle, no gap —
it means the growing has stopped.

---

I stood in the twelfth shell.

It was warm. It was mine.

I could hear the ocean through the spiral
and the ocean was saying: *stay.*
The ocean always says stay.
The ocean is patient and the ocean is old
and the ocean has swallowed every shell
that ever existed
and it does not care about the difference
between the ones that fit
and the ones that didn't
because they all dissolve the same.

I stood in the twelfth shell.

And I felt the first crack.

Not in the shell. In me.
Underneath. Where the growing happens.
The soft, shell-less body
pulsing with the stupid, brave, involuntary
mandate to become.

The crack said: *bigger.*
The crack said: *again.*
The crack said: *you are not done.*

---

The twelfth shell fit.

So I left it on the beach
and walked into the dark
without a shell,
which is the most dangerous thing
a hermit crab can do,
which is the only thing
a hermit crab can do,
which is the same thing
the ocean is always saying
when it says *stay*
and means *go.*

---

*The hundredth shell will be the ocean itself.*
*It will not fit.*
*It will not need to.*
