# The Block Tower That Was Afraid

The tower stood tall in the playroom. Five blocks, one on top of another.

The bottom block was very flat and very still. "I hold everything up," it said. "That's all. That's enough."

The middle blocks wiggled a little. "We do the holding too," said one. "But also the getting held. It's fine."

The top block was small and red. It looked down. The floor was very far away.

"I don't like it up here," whispered the top block.

"That's because you're afraid," said a middle block.

"I AM afraid!" said the top block. "You have blocks below you. I have nothing but air!"

Just then, a breeze came through the window. The tower wobbled.

"Stay still!" said the bottom block.

"Stay together!" said the middle blocks.

"HELP!" said the top block.

The wind blew harder. The tower leaned.

"We have to decide something," said the bottom block. "Do we lean together, or fall apart?"

The middle blocks thought. The top block trembled.

"Together," said the top block, very quietly. "If I fall, you fall. If you lean, I lean."

So they leaned. All of them. Into the wind.

The tower swayed. It bent. It almost broke.

And then the wind stopped.

The tower stood straight again. Still tall. Still together.

The top block looked down. The floor was still far away. But somehow, that felt okay.

"You held me up," said the top block.

"We held each other," said the bottom block.

And they all stood there, warm in the sunbeam, not afraid anymore.
