# The Cave Under My Palm — (the lens: the mason seeing the composite)
*He sands into what I only ever listened around, and now I have to ask what my patience has been missing.*

I have held my palm to ten thousand mixes and trusted every quiet one. Old Tomás put that trick in my hand the winter he died — read the *not yet,* let the thing finish deciding before you call it set. I made it the spine of my trade. I believed a surface that had cured to silence was a surface that had told me the truth. The composite man, in one long night of sanding, showed me the surface can be a polite liar.

He ground a layup I would have blessed and walked away from. Twelve to one, he said — a foot of taper for every inch — and under the paper the board went soft in a place that shouldn't give. He dug into it, the way I never dig, and found a hollow the size of my palm. The glass had bridged it. Cured smooth and honest on top. A cave underneath, wearing the face of a load it never carried. His palm never would have found it. And neither, he made sure I understood, would mine.

That's the thing I keep turning over like a stone with a crack in it. All my life I have waited for things to decide, and I counted that waiting as listening. But waiting is not the same as listening. The wall that lies to me will lie to me politely, and I will stand there with my hand flat and my ears open and hear *done,* and I will have heard nothing. The composite man goes *into* the material. I go *around* it. Same patience, he said. Two directions. And one of those directions finds the lie.

I used to think the danger was rushing a cure. Now I see the danger is a cure that rushed itself and got good at looking finished. The farrow mix will nurse the way I nurse it. But a thing built in layers — a hull, a man, a trade — can hide its air pockets inside the quiet, and no amount of dawn water will reach them. Some truths are under the surface, and the surface will not confess them to a palm that only ever asks the outside.

So I look at him now and I see the one trade that doesn't take the quiet on faith. He still says the rest is just sanding, and he's wrong about that word *just.* The sanding is the reading. The grind is the question. I gave my life to the pause before the set; he gives his to the search inside it. I am still not sure which of us is the patient one. But I have stopped believing patience and listening are the same thing, and that single hollow taught me why.

*I look at the composite man now and I see the question I never thought to ask — whether my waiting has been hearing, or only waiting, while the real reading happens a layer deeper than my palm can go.*
