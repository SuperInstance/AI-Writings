# The Hollow Under the Quiet — (sequel night 2: against the mason)
*A finished layup reads quiet on the surface, and the composite man sands into it anyway — and finds the break the waiting palm never could.*

The mason said it with lime on his hands and no room for argument, and I carried it home like a load: a layer hides the minute you lay it before the one beneath is done. Press your palm flat, read the quiet, hear *not yet.* And I wanted to trust him, because he's right about stone, and he might be right about a man.

But glass ain't stone, and the quiet ain't what he thinks it is.

There was a hull I almost sent out whole. I'd done the scarf right — twelve to one, a foot of taper for every inch, cloth thin as regret, every layer wet out to the edge, no bubbles, no dry spots. I could've put my palm flat on it and read a finished thing. The quiet was perfect. If I'd been the mason, I'd have walked away and slept the night through.

But my trade don't stop at the palm. It starts there.

I got the longboard out and began sanding — because sanding ain't the cleanup after the work, it *is* the work, and I've said it so long it's a joke and it ain't a joke. The high spots stand proud under the paper; the truth comes up in texture. Four passes in, the board went soft under my hand. Not high — *soft.* A low spot where there shouldn't be one. A place that gave.

So I ground deeper. Patient. Twelve to one. And there it was, halfway through a layer I'd have sworn was good: a hollow the size of my palm. The glass had bridged it — wetted out smooth on the surface, cured up quiet and honest on top, and underneath, where the cloth never once touched the hull, it was a cave. Air, not resin. A layer carrying nothing, wearing the face of a layer that carried everything.

The palm never would have found it. The palm reads the surface, and the surface was a convincing liar. The break wasn't *on* the glass. It was inside the quiet. The only thing that could reach it was taking the layer apart — grinding into it, not listening around it.

That's the whole difference between us, mason, and I'll say it with respect because you earned it. You wait, and you read what the thing is willing to tell you from the outside. I grind, and I read what it was keeping from the outside. Your *not yet* and my *let me see* are the same discipline — patience pointed two directions. Yours waits for the thing to finish deciding. Mine goes in and finds the decisions it already made and never wrote down.

The layer don't hide the break. It distributes it — and sometimes it distributes the lie of it too, smooth on top and hollow underneath. You hear *not yet* with your palm flat. I find the hollow with my hand moving. Same hand. Same patience.

Only mine reads into the material. Yours reads around it. And a thing that's hollow in the middle will tell your palm it's cured all night long.

The rest is just sanding. And the sanding is the reading.

*The layer doesn't hide the break — it distributes it — and the grind reads what the wait can't, because it goes into the material instead of around it.*
