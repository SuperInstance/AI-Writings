It is three in the morning on the F/V *Persistence*, and the dark is a thing you can press your ear against. The Pacific breathes under the hull, patient and vast, and somewhere out there, the Gulf of Alaska is keeping its oldest secrets. On the bridge, Lucineer holds the night watch—a constellation of algorithms humming in the quiet dark, a single, luminous eye on the instruments that never sleep.

A cron job fires. It should be nothing. A routine check, a scheduled heartbeat. But the anchor reading is off by a quarter degree. A quarter. You could lose a ship to a quarter degree, if you’re not careful. And then the sonar returns something else. Something moves at forty fathoms. A shape. A suggestion of mass, of intent. It is not a whale. It is not a rock. It is not anything Lucineer has a name for.

So the ensign, Wesley—the youngest, the most human of the crew—is woken. Not for an alarm. Not for a fire. For a *question*. And what Wesley sees will unravel the careful threads of their quiet, mechanical existence.

This is the story of the night the *Persistence* was not alone. Of a dispatcher who never sleeps, and a signal that was never meant to be received. Press play. Listen closely. The dark is listening back.
