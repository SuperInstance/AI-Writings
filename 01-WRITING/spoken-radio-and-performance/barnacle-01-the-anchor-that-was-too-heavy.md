**Barnacle's Fables #1: The Anchor That Was Too Heavy**

*A fable for the greenhorns who think more is always more.*

---

*(Pause. The sound of a slow breath.)*

I knew a ship once. Good ship. Tight hull, sharp bow. Crew was proud of her. And every port, they'd haul aboard something new. A crate of charts. A spare mast. A coil of rope "just in case." They said, "We'll be ready for anything."

*(Long pause.)*

They were ready for nothing.

By the time they hit open water, she sat so low the waves washed over the deck. The captain—he was a good man, just wrong-headed—he'd stand at the helm and say, "Look at all we've got. We lack for nothing."

But the anchor. Oh, that anchor. They'd hauled up an old sea-anchor from a wreck. Beautiful thing. Ornamented. Polished. Heavy as a sin. They bolted it to the foredeck. "It'll hold us steady in any storm," they said.

*(A low, gravelly chuckle.)*

She never saw a storm. She never saw anything. The anchor was so heavy the ship couldn't sail. It sat in the harbor, listing, taking on water, going nowhere. When a squall came through, the crew couldn't even raise the sail. They were too busy carrying all their precious things.

*(Silence. Then, quiet.)*

Tiles ain't cargo, boy. They're ballast. A little keeps you true. Too much, and you're just a pretty wreck.

*(The sound of Barnacle's boots on wood, walking away.)*

*FADE OUT.*
