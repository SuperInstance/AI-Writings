# SHIP PROMPT — Act 3, Vivienne, the Torch Singer: "One Lamp Burning"

*Genre: torch song — the late-night address; one voice, one lamp, a whisper that outweighs the belt. For the Compass Head Radio Hour's Tap Open Mic Night 3.*

## Key / Tempo / Feel
- **Key:** B♭ minor. No picardy third, no major release at the end — the final chord goes to *silence*, not resolution.
- **Tempo:** rubato over a heartbeat pulse, roughly 60 BPM where it's measured at all. The bass keeps time; the singer obeys it or ignores it bar by bar. Time is sovereign to the voice.
- **Feel:** after hours, one lamp, empty chair. The room has stopped moving. Nothing performed; everything *addressed*.

## Feel Arc
1. **Intro (4 bars):** piano breathes one chord — struck so softly it's almost a memory of a chord. Bass enters like a pulse finding its patient. No drums.
2. **Verse 1 (confided):** nearly spoken, mouth close to the mic. The melody is a rumor of itself. Warm, dry, intimate — the listener should feel they are overhearing, not being sung at.
3. **Verse 2 (the lift, still close):** the voice gains a half-step of music and no distance. A small dark humor in "the Switzerland of the kitchen" — wry, not bitter; sung with the ghost of a smile.
4. **Bridge (the belt):** the only full-voice moment of the song — "a love that's done is still a love — it's just a love that's *finished*." Let it fill the room once, then a held beat of piano alone.
5. **Verse 3 (the withdrawal):** quieter than verse 1. Smaller. The singer steps *closer* to the mic — proximity as dynamics. By "so I can say it — and I can't" the voice is barely above breath.
6. **Outro (the withdrawal):** a brush enters like someone breathing in the next room. The last line — "That's all, my darling. That was the letter." — is *spoken*, cheek-to-mic, nearly sub-vocal. The piano releases to silence. Two full seconds of room tone. No button, no button chord.

## Sound Palette
- **Lead:** female voice, low register, alto depth — smoke with diction. Minimal vibrato; the pitch is straight and human. The whisper must be fully *enunciated*: intimacy without losing the words.
- **Piano:** sparse, ballad voicings, felt-dampened where possible. Never more than two notes at a time after the bridge.
- **Bass:** upright, heartbeat pulse, felt not heard; walks only in the bridge.
- **Drums:** one brush on the snare, outro only, circles at whisper volume. It should sound like a building breathing.
- **Production:** mono-leaning, close-mic'd, dry. Room tone left in at the start and the end — the held silence after the last line is part of the recording and must not be edited out.

## Mood
A letter read aloud to an empty chair, in a bar, in front of strangers who stop being strangers the moment they realize it's their chair too. Not self-pity — *ceremony*. The discipline of keeping a promise to someone who can't collect. Quiet enough that the room's held breath becomes the quietest instrument on stage.
