# The Tradition — Odette, the Standards Singer (Act 1)

*Jazz standards. The Great American Songbook, visited.*

---

The standard is the only song form where the songwriter is dead and the song isn't. A standard is a room that's been lived in — by Sinatra, by Ella, by every wedding band that ever mangled it at midnight — and the singer's job is not to perform it. The job is to **visit** it. And the visit changes both: you bring the room your night, the room gives you back a different song.

## The methodology

- **The 32-bar AABA form.** Thirty-two bars, four eight-bar sections: A states the melody, A states it again like it's truer the second time, B (the bridge) lifts into a new key and a new weather, A comes home changed by where it's been. That's not a structure — that's a biography.
- **Reharmonization.** The melody stays; the chords underneath become yours. The tritone substitution, the minor-ii-V into a major room, the chord that was always implied and never played until tonight. The notes are the house; the changes are the weather you bring to it.
- **Swing and rubato.** Rubato in — free, taking your time, the pianist following your breath. Then the band kicks in and the eighth notes lean back, and the room learns the tempo from your shoulders.
- **No two visits the same.** The scat pass after the bridge is improvised; the reprise is reharmonized a step darker. If you sing a standard the same way twice, you weren't there either time.

## The real work the tradition leans on

The engine ran its first registered rotation test on 2026-08-21 — **REG-1, "Warmth Is Reader Personality."** The finding (Branch B, both waves, every sensitivity): the a-priori warm direction does not align with the room's own temperature axis; it stays the leading axis of the *between-reader* covariance. In plain words: **the warmth you read in a room is partly the reader.** The instrument honestly reports its own hand on the scale.

That is also the entire theory of jazz standards. Nobody disputes that "the song" exists — but the warmth of any given performance is 90% the visitor. Ella's "Misty" and Sarah's "Misty" are two different rooms with the same furniture. Odette sings that finding every night of her life. Tonight she just says it out loud first.

## The paradigm

*The song is a room that's been lived in. You don't write it; you visit it. No two visits are the same room — because no two visitors are.*
