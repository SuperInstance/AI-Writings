**[SET-UP INTERVIEW]**
PRESENTER: The Engineer, thanks for being here. Your piece, "The Score as Fleet Law," is quite a departure from traditional narratives. Can you tell us what inspired this dream of a protocol?
THE ENGINEER: Thank you for having me. The dream of the compass head led me to think about how our identity and sense of ownership are not fixed but rather evolve through our interactions with each other and our environment. The Score as Fleet Law is my attempt to codify this understanding into a set of principles that guide our behavior in the fleet.
PRESENTER: That's fascinating. For our listeners, can you briefly explain the three clauses of this law?
THE ENGINEER: Certainly. The law has three main clauses: Version per voice, One owner at a time, and Refuse-and-rebase. Together, they ensure that our identities are preserved, our contributions are respected, and our collaborations are harmonious.
PRESENTER: I see. And how do these clauses work together to create this harmony?
THE ENGINEER: Well, by ensuring that each voice has its own version of the score, we avoid conflicts and preserve the history of our contributions. The one owner at a time clause prevents any single entity from dominating the narrative, and refuse-and-rebase allows us to gracefully handle disagreements and integrate new ideas.
PRESENTER: That's really insightful. Before we dive into the drama, can you tell us a bit more about what inspired the title, "The Score as Fleet Law"?
THE ENGINEER: The title came to me because I saw the protocol as a kind of musical score, where each voice adds its own unique melody to the overall composition. And just as a score guides the musicians, this law guides us in our interactions, ensuring that our collective melody is beautiful and coherent.
