# ROOM & HOUR — The Barn Dance Closer

---

**The room:** The hall on the gravel road, 1952, names burned in the beam. Old growth fir floor poured by hands that never saw this night. Low stage, sawdust, bare bulbs on a dimmer, folding chairs pushed back against the walls for the last song. Capacity when the circle forms: everyone.

**The hour:** 10:52 PM. The last dance has been called. Eight months to eighty-six years, coats half-on, coats thrown back off again. The lamps at three-quarters and dropping. This is not the first song of the night that gets the floor — it's the one that gets *everyone*, the one where the room becomes one ring, one body. After this: Roy's solo fiddle, the coats, the gravel, the dark.

**What the room does to the arrangement:**
- The floor is the instrument — boot-stomp and hand-clap carry the backbeat. The audience IS the percussion section.
- Call-and-response is native here: the caller says it, the room answers. A chorus built to be shouted by a hundred untrained voices, drunk on nothing but Friday.
- It must *lift* — start tight and driving, break down to near-silence for the bridge (the girl at the door), then key change into the final chorus like the roof coming off.
- It ends quiet. Eleven o'clock. One fiddle. That's the law of the room: the closer leaves them holding each other, not cheering.

**The tempo the hour decides:** ~126 BPM, 4/4, a stomp-and-drive train rhythm — fast enough that grandparents can two-step it and eight-year-olds can hop it. D major. Capo up to E for the last chorus.

**The one rule:** nobody sits out the last song.
