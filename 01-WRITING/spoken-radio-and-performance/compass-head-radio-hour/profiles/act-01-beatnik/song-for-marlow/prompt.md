A beatnik jazz-poem-song, a gift written FOR the radio interviewer, not about him. Spoken-word verses over a slow walking upright bass, brushed drums, and smoky piano — the sound of a jazz club at 4 a.m., the hour when the needles settle. Deep and warm, tender and wry. D minor, ~84 BPM, ~2:30. The vocal is a gravelly, smoky, world-weary male beatnik voice — intimate, unhurried, reading the way a man reads a tide table. The song addresses the interviewer directly: the one who carries questions the way a hermit crab carries a shell, who wears the archive on his back, who keeps a blank nameplate on his desk. It builds from quiet confession to a warm, knowing refrain — "ask me anything, the question is the gift" — and ends soft, almost a benediction: the name that was on the card the whole time. No big chorus fireworks; the whole piece breathes like smoke in lamplight. Leave space between phrases. The last line should hang in the air.
