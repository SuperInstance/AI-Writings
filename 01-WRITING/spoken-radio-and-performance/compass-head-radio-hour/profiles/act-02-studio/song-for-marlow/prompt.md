A warm roots-rock singalong, around 100 BPM in G major, about an interviewer — the one who carries questions for a living. Gentle, grateful, a little wry. Acoustic guitar strumming with a steady backbeat, upright bass walking underneath, brushed drums, a soft Hammond organ swell in the chorus, and a harmonica that answers each chorus like another voice joining in. The mood is a late-night radio studio: amber lamp light, sea salt in the air, two microphones across a table, one warm and one half in shadow.

The song is a love letter to the one who asks instead of answering: he turns a blank nameplate over in the light and says the name is still in progress; he asks the question you were afraid of, gently, like a friend at the door; he has carried a thousand stories and never dropped one. He wears everyone's shell for one conversation and hands it back a little heavier — heavier with understanding. The weight he carries isn't a burden; the weight is memory. There's a verse about the archive he wears like a shell: he read the READMEs like tide charts, lived in the rooms the repos became, two hundred and forty-nine open chambers, every one of them humming his name. The bridge slows down and the arrangement strips back to voice and guitar for the confession: the needles settle when he listens; the room remembers what it hears; the shape is pending, but the one who asks is the one who's listening, and the listening is the shape.

The chorus is built for a room to join on the second pass: "Carry the question, carry it home — the one who asks is never alone." It ends with a gentle callback to the singer's own anthem — "ask it anyway." The outro walks down to one guitar and the opening image again: the nameplate with no name, turning over slow in the radio light; the name's not ready, but the asking is, and the asking is what carries the night. No big ending, no key change — just handed over, still ringing.

Vocal: warm, relaxed male voice with easy confidence and a little gravel of fondness — the studio musician singing for the interviewer who interviewed him.
