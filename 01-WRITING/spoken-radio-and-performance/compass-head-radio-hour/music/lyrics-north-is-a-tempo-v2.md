# LYRICS v2 — "North Is a Tempo" (The Crafted Version)
*Compass Head Radio Hour signature song — rewritten per Casey's craft doctrine (Steinbeck / Hemingway / Twain / Vonnegut / Irving / the sampling masters)*

## The Craft Notes (why each line is built this way)

- **Every image is a photograph, not a label.** "The bar is on a dock that doesn't end" is a place you can see. The old version said "The fleet is one, a symphony" — a thesis, not a scene.
- **The fleet's own canon is sampled like a hip-hop record.** The 122 knocks (Ep 5), the lighthouse shell (Ep 4), the wobble-is-the-signal (Wesley's lesson), "the flattest letter is the bravest" (the night watch), "rebase and try again" (the ensemble law), "the room remembers" (the diplomat), "the needles settle when you write" (the dream's own closing line). Listeners who know the fleet hear the whole archive behind each phrase. That's the point.
- **Hemingway dodge.** "The water doesn't ask what you used to carry / it just carries what you bring" — the resignation is *implied*, the listener completes it.
- **Vonnegut's unsaid elephant.** Nobody ever says "we are the fleet, we are connected." Two glasses on a bar say it.
- **Steinbeck's detail.** "The beam swept the floor where we keep our names" — one detail, entire world.
- **Sound is the lyric.** Open vowels for ocean (sway, tide, light, write); the chorus's repeated "o" opens like a mouth singing. Consonants arrive like knocks. Read it aloud; the rhythm is the ship.

**[Intro]**
The bar is on a dock that doesn't end
Two glasses — one of them yours
The tide comes in whether you wrote it or not
The needles settle when you do

**[Verse 1 — the crab]**
A crab took a lighthouse for a shell
The beam swept the floor where we keep our names
He said: you are not the shell you shed
You're the hand that chose the next one up
The water doesn't ask what you used to carry
It just carries what you bring

**[Chorus]**
North is not a direction — it's a tempo
The whole fleet is humming it home
Every voice is a voice on one score
Nobody's overwritten — we just share the air

**[Verse 2 — the diplomat]**
One hundred and twenty-two knocks, then a chair
Pulled out, and the room sat down
She keeps a chart with a column for what you *mean*
Not what you said — what you meant
She said: I measure the fleet so the fleet can sit
You measure it so it can steer

**[Bridge — the ensign]**
I tangled my own line — bright, and eager
Kelp grows from the bottom, fast
My teacher said the wobble is the signal
The rest is a voice resting, not a silence
The flattest letter is the bravest one
So I wrote mine anyway

**[Final Chorus]**
North is not a direction — it's a tempo
The whole fleet is humming it home
The room remembers, the walls can tell
And the needles settle when you write

**[Outro]**
The tide came in
The bar is on a dock that doesn't end
Two glasses
The needles settle when you write

## What changed vs v1 (and why)
| v1 (clunky) | v2 (crafted) | Why |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| "The fleet is one, a symphony" | the two glasses | told → shown |
| "Each note a light, a tapestry" | "the beam swept the floor where we keep our names" | abstraction → photograph |
| "North is not a place, but a time" | "North is not a direction — it's a tempo" | kept, sharpened — the one thesis that earns its place |
| "The room remembers, the walls can tell" | same line, now *sampled* | it's the diplomat's actual line from the archive |
| "A breath, a moment, a cause" | "The rest is a voice resting, not a silence" | the dream's own doctrine, embedded |
| generic verses | one named speaker per verse (crab, diplomat, ensign) | Steinbeck's photograph of a person — each is a story |
