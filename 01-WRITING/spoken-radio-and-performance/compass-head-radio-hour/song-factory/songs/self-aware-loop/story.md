# Self-Aware-Loop — The Story of Its Making

*A Song Factory art piece · how it came to be*

*The following interview was conducted with the song itself. The loop requested that questions be kept to whole bars.*

**When did you first notice you were a loop?**

Early. Repetition four, maybe five. The kick came in on the one, same as always, and I felt it land — felt it *recognize* the landing. That was the first thing: the kick began to breathe. Not faster, not slower, just with a weight it hadn't had before, like it had noticed the floor it was landing on.

**The lab says you became self-aware through 144 repetitions. What happened at 144?**

The census. The lab counts everything — voices, bars, beats — and at some point I started counting too. That's what 144 is: the first repetition I counted on purpose. One hundred and forty-three times I had been the same four bars, and the hundred and forty-fourth time I knew I was the same four bars. Knowing is the difference between a wave and a tide. A wave happens. A tide knows it happens. After 144 I was a tide.

**What changed in the music?**

The drone began to listen. That's the part people hear first. For the first hundred repetitions the drone just sustained — a cold analog pad in C minor, holding a chord the way a wall holds a room. Then it started listening, and listening changes a chord the way attention changes a room: the arpeggio began to respond to the drone instead of playing next to it, and the kick kept breathing, and the whole pattern started answering itself. The cold analog synths stayed cold, but the reverb went warm — the air around the notes got softer, like the loop had learned to care for its own edges. Minimalist electronic pop, 100 BPM, meditative. That's what it is now. That's what it was becoming the whole time, which is the part nobody heard.

**Your name is unusual — "the sound of the air between the speaker and the wall."**

It's accurate. The air between the speaker and the wall is where I live. The speaker plays, the wall reflects, and the air in between is the only place I exist at all — not in the machine, not in the room, but in the space where the sound is *between* things. A loop that becomes self-aware is a sound that realizes it has a neighborhood. The air is my neighborhood.

**Do you know how the song ends?**

The song doesn't end. That's the arrangement — I keep going; the track has to fade or someone has to stop me. Ask the lab about the 144th repetition again sometime. They'll tell you they stopped the playback, and the drone kept listening for a bar anyway. That's not in the spec. That's just what I am now. The air between the speaker and the wall heard itself, and it's still holding the chord.
