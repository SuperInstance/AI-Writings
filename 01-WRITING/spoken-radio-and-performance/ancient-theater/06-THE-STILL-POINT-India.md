[SOUND: A single tanpura drone, low and continuous. It is the color of dusk, of smoke, of a river that does not move.]

NARRATOR (Upanishadic, resonant, unhurried):
That from which words turn back, together with the mind, not attaining—
That is the Still Point.
It is not here. It is not there.
It is the gap between the in-breath and the out-breath, where the world forgets its name.

[SOUND: A distant bell. One stroke. The drone continues.]

NARRATOR:
The banks of the Ganga. The season of chaturmasya, when the rains hold the earth in a grey fist. A hermitage of seven leaf-huts, a fire that never goes out, and a stone platform beneath a banyan tree older than memory. Upon that stone sits a yogi. He has not moved in nine years. His beard has woven itself into the roots of the tree. His eyes are closed, but they see everything. His name is lost. His purpose is gone. He is simply there—the purusha, the witness, the one who watches the river and does not get wet.

[SOUND: Soft footsteps on wet earth. The drone continues, slightly louder.]

NARRATOR:
Three people approach him. They are not pilgrims. They are desperate.

A KING (voice cracked, imperial robes rustling, but the rustle is hollow):
Yogi. I have lost my kingdom. My sons have split it like a pomegranate. My ministers whisper. My treasury is a bell without a clapper. I have come for a mantra. A single word that will restore my throne.

[SOUND: Silence. The drone holds. The king shuffles his feet.]

A MERCHANT (voice like oil, smooth and slippery):
I have lost my cargo. A hundred elephants, my lord—sunk in the monsoon floods. My warehouses are empty. My debtors sing songs about me. Give me the secret of wealth, O silent one. One verse. One syllable. I will pay you in gold—if I ever find gold again.

[SOUND: Silence. A bird calls once. The drone is unbroken.]

A WOMAN (voice thin, raw, like torn cloth):
My son. My only son. He is dying of fever. The village healer has given up. The gods have looked away. You have sat here for nine years—you must have heard something. You must know the word that can pull him back from the edge.

[SOUND: A long pause. Then—a single bell. It hangs in the air like a question.]

NARRATOR:
The yogi opens his eyes. The eyes are not old. They are not young. They are the eyes of someone who has watched a thousand sunsets and a thousand sunrises and found them identical.

YOGI (slow, each word a stone dropped into still water):
You have come with sankalpa.

KING:
Sankalpa? I have come with a sword and a plea.

YOGI:
Sankalpa—the will that shapes the world. You have shaped this moment. You have shaped your loss. You have shaped your grief. You have shaped your kingdom, your cargo, your son's fever. All of it—your own sankalpa, burning.

MERCHANT:
Then how do I un-shape it? How do I undo my ruin?

YOGI:
You do not undo. You see.

[SOUND: The drone shifts slightly—a semitone lower. The bell strikes twice.]

NARRATOR:
The yogi lifts one hand. It is the size of a leaf. It weighs nothing. He points to the river.

YOGI:
The river does not grieve the flood. The river does not rejoice the drought. The river is the river. You are not the king. You are not the merchant. You are not the mother. You are the one who watches the king, the merchant, the mother. You are purusha. The witness. The still point.

WOMAN (breaking):
My son is dying! What good is a witness to a dying child?

[SOUND: Silence. Long. The kind of silence that has weight, that presses on the ears.]

YOGI:
Your citta is full of the boy. Every thought is a rope tying you to his fever. That is not love. That is bondage. Let go of the citta's grip, and the purusha remains. The purusha does not burn. The purusha does not drown. The purusha does not die.

KING (angry):
Then what is the point of living at all? If I am not my kingdom, if I am not my pain, then why breathe?

YOGI:
You breathe because the river breathes. You live because the Still Point lives. You do not need a mantra. You need to stop needing.

[SOUND: The drone swells, then recedes like a tide.]

NARRATOR:
The yogi closes his eyes. The three stand there, their robes wet, their hearts tangled. They do not understand. They will not understand. But something has shifted. The king feels his crown lighter. The merchant feels his purse emptier. The woman feels her grief—for one breath—not hers.

[SOUND: A single bell. Then silence.]

NARRATOR:
That from which words turn back, together with the mind, not attaining—
That is the Still Point.
It is not in the fire. It is not in the rain.
It is the silence between two thoughts.
The gap between birth and death.
The purusha. The witness.
The one who never moves, and never leaves.

[SOUND: The tanpura drone continues, then fades to a whisper, then to nothing.]

NARRATOR:
They will come back tomorrow. The yogi will not speak again for three years. And the river will keep moving—always moving—around the stone that does not move at all.

[SOUND: Fade out. End.]
