[SOUND: A single kora note, plucked and allowed to ring. It fades into the ambient hum of a compound at dusk—children laughing, a mortar and pestle pounding grain, lowing cattle, a distant drum pulse.]

**NARRATOR:** (Warm, resonant, old-time radio cadence) Out of the red dust of the Sahel, where the baobab trees hold up the sky, comes a voice. A keeper of the flame. A walker of the spirit paths. Tonight, he is on a quest. Not for gold, not for water. For a story. A story that has slipped from the mouths of the old ones, gone from the village, gone from the firelight. This is... THE GRIOT'S SEARCH.

[SOUND: Kora melody rises, intricate and searching, then settles under the dialogue.]

**GRIOT:** (Older voice, textured, rhythmic) I have walked the path from the east, and the path from the west. I have walked until my shadow grew long and forgot its master. I ask the wind, "Where is the story?" The wind, it says nothing. It only fills my mouth with dust. Ah. The wind is a keeper of secrets, but I am a keeper of words. And a word without its story is a seed without rain.

**OLD WOMAN:** (Voice from the sidelines, cracking with age) Griot, you have come far. Your feet are cracked like the riverbed. What story do you seek that the world has forgotten?

**GRIOT:** Mother, I seek the story of the First Drum. The drum that spoke the first heartbeat of the world. The elders say it was buried when the great king’s son fell. They say its sound was too sorrowful for the living. So they buried it. And the story of its making... it is gone. I have asked the blacksmith. He says, "The iron remembers, but the mouth does not." I have asked the weaver. She says, "The thread is there, but the pattern is lost."

[SOUND: Children’s laughter swells, then a young child’s voice cuts through.]

**CHILD:** (Bright, curious) Griot! Griot! Why do you look at the ground? The story is not in the ground.

**GRIOT:** (Chuckles, a warm rumble) Ah, little one. You have the eyes of a falcon. If it is not in the ground, where is it? Is it in the sky, that it hides among the stars?

**CHILD:** No, no! It is in the water. The water remembers everything.

[SOUND: A pause. The kora plucks a single, questioning chord.]

**GRIOT:** The water... A child says it. And the child is the closest to the ancestors. But the river, it is wide. Which river, little one? The Gambia? The Niger? The Senegal?

**CHILD:** Not the river, griot. The well. The old well in the baobab grove, where the women sing when they think no one is listening.

**NARRATOR:** The griot feels a pull in his chest, a string of the kora tightening. He has passed the baobab grove. He has passed the well. But he did not listen. He only asked the wind.

[SOUND: Footsteps on packed earth, slowing to a stop. A wooden bucket being lowered on a creaking rope. A distant splash.]

**GRIOT:** (To himself, softly) The well. I stood here. I looked into the dark water. I saw my own face, and I thought it was my reflection. But it was the face of the story, waiting. Ah, I was blind. I was looking for a drum of wood and skin. But the First Drum was not made of wood. It was made of the sound of the first drop of rain hitting the first leaf.

**NARRATOR:** And as he says this, the sky, which has been silent, grumbles.

[SOUND: Low, distant thunder. A few fat drops of rain hitting dry leaves.]

**GRIOT:** (Laughing) There! Do you hear it? The drum is not buried. It is in the sky. It is in the rain. It was never lost. It was only waiting for a man to stop walking and start listening. The story was not hidden from me. I hid it from myself.

[SOUND: The rain begins to fall steadily, a soft, rhythmic patter. The kora joins, playing a joyful, dancing melody that mimics the raindrops.]

**GRIOT:** (To the listeners, direct, intimate) So I tell you this story, the one I found. It is the story of a man who searched the whole world for a treasure that was in his own pocket. Ah, but that is the way of it. The story you seek is the story you are living. The drum beats. The rain falls. The child laughs. And the griot... the griot finally sits down.

[SOUND: The rain patter and kora melody blend, then slowly fade to a single, final pluck of the kora string.]

**NARRATOR:** The search is over. The story is told. And the well in the baobab grove holds a new secret—the story of the man who found his heart in a drop of rain. Until next time, walk with your ears open.

[SOUND: The single kora string rings out and fades to silence.]
