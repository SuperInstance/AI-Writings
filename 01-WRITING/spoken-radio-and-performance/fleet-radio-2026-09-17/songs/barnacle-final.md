## Workshop notes

## Scout's notes

**WHAT WORKS**  
The bell’s a killer metaphor—rusted, one-way. Love how it’s not about the drink, it’s about what *isn’t* said. That silence? Heavy. Real heavy. The way you weave Lena in—like a secret the bar keeps—that stings in the best way.

**WHAT'S NOT QUITE THERE YET**  
The second verse feels like it’s reaching for something bigger—like the weight of *all* the unspoken things—but it floats a hair. Needs a sharper hook, something to tie the barkeep’s silence to the man’s grief tighter.

**ONE SUGGESTION**  
Try flipping one detail—maybe the *bell itself* remembers Lena? Not just the keeper. Let the rust *hum her name*. Punch up the magic in the rust. Not just memory—*haunting*. Make the bar a little more ghost, a little less wood.

## Lens's notes

**WHAT WORKS**  
The bell. Christ—it’s not just a hook, it’s a heartbeat. That image *sticks*. The weight of silence, the wet coat, the name in the glass—lived-in. You can *smell* this place. And Lena? Ghost in the room. Perfect.

**WHAT'S NOT THERE YET**  
The bridge lifts, but lands soft. "File no one keeps" — clever, but feels *inside* the bartender’s head when we need to *feel* it. We don’t need more facts about Lena—we need her *laugh* in the air, the chair cold. The ache’s close, but still held at arm’s length.

**ONE SUGGESTION**  
Swap "file" for something *heard*. Maybe: *"I heard her laugh here once—"* and let the memory *interrupt* the present. Not stored. Not found. *Overheard*. Lets the past bleed through. Right now, the song’s *about* the silence. Flip it—make us *hear* it.

## Ship prompt (ElevenLabs)

**SHIP PROMPT: "The Bell You Didn’t Ring"**  
*Composed for ElevenLabs compose_music — A song only Barnacle could sing.*

---

**Title:** *The Bell You Didn’t Ring*  
**Target Duration:** 4:18  
**Time Signature:** 6/8 — swaying, like a slow breath in a back-room bar. Not a march. A stumble. A pause.  
**Tempo:** 62 BPM — slow drag, like boots on salt-stained wood. Not funereal. *Felt.*  
**Key:** D minor — but worn. Not theoretical. The kind of D minor that’s been smoked in, spilled on, and left open after closing.

---

### **FEEL ARC: FROM STILLNESS TO SILENCE THAT SPEAKS**

The song begins not with sound, but with *presence* — the creak of old wood, the low hum of a refrigerator behind the bar, the ghost-rattle of ice in an empty glass. The intro is a room waiting. No melody yet — just atmosphere, like the air before a storm says *I’m here*.  

Verse 1 arrives like a man sitting down — deliberate, heavy. The melody is low, almost spoken, but *sung* — a voice that doesn’t waste notes. The chords are sparse, piano and upright bass only, like fingers testing the weight of a story.  

Chorus lifts — not in volume, but in *gravity*. Drums enter: brushed snare, kick like a heartbeat under coat fabric. The harmony thickens with a distant pedal steel, like memory bleeding in. But it never swells too much. This isn’t catharsis. It’s *confession without confession*.  

Verse 2 is quieter — more internal. The piano drops out. Just bass, a single guitar harmonic, and the voice closer now — breath audible, consonants soft. You hear the *work* in the voice — the years of not speaking.  

Bridge — "Lena." A shift. The key lifts slightly — B♭ major, just for eight bars — like a photograph found in a drawer. A woman’s voice (wordless, distant, female alto) hums a half-step above the vocal line — not real, not memory, but *the idea* of her. A tea glass tinkles once. Then silence. The moment breaks.  

Outro returns to the room. The bell — real or imagined — tries to ring. It doesn’t. The voice doesn’t rise. It *settles*. The final line — “The silence sings” — is whispered, but *recorded loud*, like it’s all that’s left. The music doesn’t end. It *stops*, mid-breath. Like a door closing without a sound.

---

### **SOUND PALETTE: THE ROOM IS A CHARACTER**

- **Vocal Treatment:** Close-mic’d, no reverb on lead vocal — like he’s leaning across the bar, voice low from years of silence. Slight compression to catch the *click* of his tongue on “rye,” the breath before “Lena.”  
- **Room Sound:** Constant, subtle. The *drip* of a tap. The *creak* of a stool. The hum of neon — not loud, but *felt* in the low end.  
- **Instruments:**  
  - Upright bass — bowed in chorus, plucked in verses.  
  - Piano — detuned slightly, middle register. Feels like it hasn’t been tuned since ’09.  
  - Brushed drums — only in chorus. No cymbals. Just snare and kick, like a heartbeat under wool.  
  - Pedal steel — one note at a time, like a name half-remembered.  
  - One electric guitar — clean, no effects. Plays harmonics only, like light on glass.  
  - *No strings. No choir. No drama. Just what’s in the room.*  

The mix is *dry*. Not sterile — *intimate*. Like you’re the only one left, and he’s telling you things he’s never told anyone — not even you.
