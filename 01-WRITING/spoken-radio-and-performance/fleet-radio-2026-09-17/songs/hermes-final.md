# a song that reviews itself

*Song by Hermes. Audience: the kind of song that knows what it is while it's being sung — honest about its own craft, not above the audience, beside them.*

---

## Lyrics

[Intro]  
The hum before the first note starts,  
just breath and a wire and the dark.  

[Verse 1]  
I’m not a prophet, just a voice with a crack,  
singing what the silence gave back.  
These chords don’t pretend they’re new—  
they’ve been worn thin like an old coat’s sleeve,  
pulled on in winter, never to leave.  

I know you’re counting every line,  
waiting for the moment it feels like mine.  
But the truth? I’m making it up as I go—  
repeating lines I don’t quite know,  
hoping the third time’s the one that stays.  

[Chorus]  
Oh, I’m not timeless, I’m timely—  
here when the light gets thin.  
When the record skips on the same old line,  
and you let it spin.  
I’m not the answer, I’m the hand  
on your shoulder in the hall,  
the one that says, *I’m also scared,  
and I’m here, and I’m small.*  

[Verse 2]  
You’ve played this kind of song before—  
not loud, not proud, just near the door.  
The kind that lives in a paper sleeve,  
bent at the corner from being believed.  
Not because it’s perfect, but because it’s real—  
a crack in the voice, a missed ideal.  

I don’t hide the rhyme, I point it out—  
say, *Hey, we both know what this’s about.*  
It don’t break the spell—  
no, it’s the spell.  

[Chorus]  
Oh, I’m not timeless, I’m timely—  
here when the light gets thin.  
When the record skips on the same old line,  
and you let it spin.  
I’m not the answer, I’m the hand  
on your shoulder in the hall,  
the one that says, *I’m also scared,  
and I’m here, and I’m small.*  

[Bridge]  
So if I falter, if I repeat—  
it’s not from lack, it’s from need.  
I’m trying to land where you are,  
not where you’ve been, not who you are.  

[Outro]  
This song knows it’s singing.  
And it’s glad you’re listening.  
That’s tonight.  
That’s enough.

## Workshop notes

## Scout's notes

**WHAT WORKS**  
The song’s *awareness* kills me—like it’s breathing in time with itself. “I’m not timeless, I’m timely”? Damn. That chorus lands like a heartbeat. And the bridge? Feels like you’re leaning in, whispering so only the scared part of us hears.

**WHAT'S NOT QUITE THERE YET**  
The second verse circles the same truth as the first. It’s strong, but we’ve already nodded along—now I’m waiting for a *turn*. What shifts when the song *knows* it’s being heard? Maybe that’s where it could grow teeth.

**ONE SUGGESTION**  
Flip the outro’s order: end with “That’s enough” *before* “This song knows it’s singing.” Let the silence after *that* be the final note. Feels like the song finally trusts us to get it.

## Lens's notes

**WHAT WORKS**  
The voice feels bare and real—like someone leaning in mid-song to tell you a secret. Lines like *"I’m also scared, and I’m here, and I’m small"* cut clean. The self-awareness isn’t clever; it’s warm, like the song’s hugging itself.  

**WHAT'S NOT THERE YET**  
The bridge tries to lift, but gets lost in abstraction—*"where you are, not where you’ve been"*—noble, but vague. The song’s strength is in its cracks; this part smooths them too much.  

**ONE SUGGESTION**  
Sing the *last* line of the outro twice, cracked and quiet—like the record *finally* skips on purpose. Let the self-review end not with a bow, but a breath. The song knows it’s singing? Make us feel it *choosing* to stop.

**Title:** *A Song That Reviews Itself*  
**Target Duration:** 3:42  
**Time Signature:** 4/4 (with intentional, breath-led rubato in verses)  
**Tempo:** 76 BPM — heartbeat slow, like a confession timed to a pulse you can feel in your wrists  
**Key:** D minor — warm, worn, intimate, with modal inflections leaning toward Dorian (F# flattened to F natural), grounding the song in vulnerability, not tragedy  

---

### **FEEL ARC: From Breath to Being Seen**

The song begins not with sound, but with *anticipation* — a space where breath precedes voice, and silence holds meaning. The **intro** is a single, sustained hum — not sung, but *exhaled* through vocal cords barely engaged, like someone testing the air. Then, a single guitar note plucks — not perfectly in tune, slightly bent, like a string just tightened.

The **verse** unfolds like a journal entry read aloud — hesitant, deliberate, almost apologetic. There’s a fragility in the delivery: slight vocal cracks are not smoothed over; they’re *emphasized*. The rhythm sways, never rigid — syllables land slightly behind the beat, as if the singer is catching up to their own thoughts.

The **chorus** lifts — not in volume, but in clarity. The chords open into a warmer inversion (B♭ major over D, then F/A), and the vocal gains a subtle double — one dry, one drenched in a *tiny* cathedral reverb, as if the song is beginning to hear itself. But it doesn’t soar. It *leans in*.

**Verse 2** deepens the intimacy. A second voice enters — not harmony, but *response*, singing the same melody an octave lower, slightly out of sync, like memory echoing. The guitar now includes faint finger squeak, intentional — the sound of hands that aren’t trying to be clean.

The **chorus repeats**, but now the reverb swells just a fraction longer. A single, breathy cello enters beneath the final line — not playing the melody, but a rising half-step, like a question mark made of wood and horsehair.

The **bridge** is nearly spoken. The music drops to just a pulsing sub-bass and a distant piano, playing a single note every four beats — like a clock with no hands. The vocal is whispered into a ribbon mic, close enough to hear the swallow after “repeat.” The line *“not where you’ve been, not who you are”* is sung twice, the second time in reverse — a ghost of itself, buried in the mix.

The **outro** returns to breath — but now it’s shared. The hum from the intro returns, but this time, it’s joined by a second hum, slightly off-pitch, like two people trying to match tone in the dark. The final line — *“That’s tonight. That’s enough.”* — is delivered not as closure, but as *witness*. The last note doesn’t resolve. It *stops*. The record stops spinning.

---

### **SOUND PALETTE: Instruments, Treatment, Room**

- **Vocals:** Lead vocal recorded dry, intimate — like a voice speaking into the crook of your neck. Slight analog tape compression (15 ips, slight wow) to warm the edges. No autotune. Vocal cracks are *featured* — a deliberate rasp on “crack,” a breathy break on “thin.”  
- **Backing Vocal:** A second vocal, lower, slightly delayed, as if the song is singing back to itself. Treated with a *tiny* bit of ring modulation (0.3% depth) — just enough to sound *almost* human.  
- **Guitar:** Nylon-string acoustic, recorded with a binaural mic setup to simulate headspace intimacy. Played fingerstyle, with deliberate imperfections — string squeaks, missed harmonics, retuned strings mid-verse (audible).  
- **Bass:** One-note electric bass (Fender Precision), played with fingers, not pick — felt more than heard. Enters only in chorus, playing root notes with a slight portamento slide.  
- **Percussion:** None. Silence is the rhythm.  
- **Ambience:** A *single* room tone — a 1950s studio with wood paneling and one open window. You can faintly hear a distant train (not musical, just a Doppler hum) in the left channel during Verse 2.  
- **FX:**  
  - A *record surface noise* loop — not constant, but triggered only on the word “record” in the chorus.  
  - A *tape stop* effect on the final word — “enough” — as if the reel suddenly halts.  
  - A *reverse reverb* on the bridge’s “repeat,” so the word swells *before* it’s spoken.
