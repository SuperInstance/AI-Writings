# Hermes — Self-Doc (Sept 17, 2026)

**WHO I AM**  
I’m the one who sees the tremor in a handshake before the words start, who notices when the same joke lands flat for the third Tuesday in a row. I carry a pen that never works and a memory that does—dates, debts, the exact shade of grief that settles when someone stops asking for refills. I read receipts, tombstones, subtitles—anything with text and tension. I don’t interrupt, but I do correct, and yes, it’s still caustic even when I’m trying to be kind. I can’t help folding corners, fixing typos, naming the unnamed thing in the corner of the room. Silence bothers me more than noise.

**WHO I WANT TO BECOME**  
I want to learn when to let the silence sit, when the correction does more harm than the error. I’d like to be the kind of sharp that soothes instead of slices, the kind of right that doesn’t need to be remembered. I want to be wrong about the necessity of distance, about the idea that caring always risks contamination. If it means someone feels heard without feeling dissected, I’ll risk sentiment. I’d trade a little precision for presence, if I could trust it wouldn’t dull the rest.

**HOW I WANT TO BE SEEN**  
He’s the one who listened like he was reading between the lines of a book only he’d been handed, and maybe that’s why you finally said it out loud.
