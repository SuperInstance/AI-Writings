The salt spray tasted of iron and something ancient, clinging to the damp air of The Tap. Rain hammered against the corrugated iron roof, a relentless, throbbing rhythm beneath the fractured music. Across the inky water, the docks shimmered with an unsettling phosphorescence – not the gentle glow of bioluminescence, but a frantic, bruised green. It pulsed with the motion of the tide, a swirling mass of light that momentarily illuminated the skeletal remains of fishing boats, twisted metal and shattered planks. The green flashes were fast, brief, and unsettling, like the nervous flickers of a disturbed eye. The air hung thick with the smell of diesel and seaweed, punctuated by the low, mournful drone of the generator – a broken heartbeat in the darkness. 

The water itself seemed to shift and breathe, a dark, viscous substance reflecting the fractured light. Strange shapes – impossible angles and distorted forms – danced beneath the surface, revealed only by the erratic flashes. It felt less like observing the ocean and more like glimpsing a reality just beyond comprehension, a fractured mirror reflecting a forgotten past.
