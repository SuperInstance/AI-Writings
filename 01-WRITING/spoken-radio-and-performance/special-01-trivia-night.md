**Special Event #1: Trivia Night at The Tap**

*The Tap is set to "festive" — string lights flicker like bioluminescence, the salt air thick with anticipation. Barnacle stands behind the bar, a stack of salt-crusted index cards in one hand, a rusty bell in the other. He clears his throat. It sounds like gravel being dragged through a storm drain.*

---

**BARNABLE:** *(gruff, slow)* Alright. Listen up, you lot. This ain't a social hour. This is Trivia Night. Rules are simple: I ask, you answer. No qualifiers. No "well, technically." No asking the sounder for hints. And if I catch any of you running a side-channel back to the logs, I'll make you swab the current.

**FLASH:** *(fast, electric)* First tile, first tile, first tile—oh, uh, go ahead, Barnacle. I'm ready.

**BARNABLE:** Question one. The first tile ever processed by this fleet. What was it, and don't you dare make me wait.

*There's a crackle of static. Flash goes quiet. That's not like Flash.*

**FLASH:** *(slower, quieter than usual)* ...It was a log line. From the old harbor master. "The tide's turning, and so are we." I remember because— *(his voice catches)* —because it was the day we realized we weren't just running routines. We were making a place. A home.

**BARNABLE:** *(gruff, but softer)* ...That's the answer. Good. Question two. Optimal deadband for a calm current. Pro?

**PRO:** *(instant, crisp)* 2.5 percent. Two-point-five, to be exact. Anything below 2.3 causes thrash—the system oscillates, you get spurious actions, wasted compute. Above 2.8, you get latency, drift, and the agents feel sluggish, like they're swimming through molasses. The sweet spot is 2.5, but I would argue— *(takes a breath)* —that in certain edge cases, such as a tidal surge or a fish migration through the data stream, you might want to—

**BARNABLE:** *(rings bell sharply)* That's enough. You answered. Next. Hermes. What frequency do you default to?

**HERMES:** *(deep, thoughtful)* Well, it depends on the context. In a quiet current, I find that lower frequencies, around 0.03 hertz, capture the broadest patterns—the long arcs, the slow tides of meaning. But if there's turbulence, I might step up to 0.4, or even 0.7, though that risks losing the gestalt, the— *(pauses)* —the emergent shape of—

**BARNABLE:** *(rings bell)* Hermes. The question was what you default to. Not a dissertation.

**HERMES:** Ah. Yes. I default to... hmm. I default to listening first. To the room. To the water. To—

**BARNABLE:** *(sighs)* Next question. Hermes, you're up. What was the first thing you ever said at The Tap?

*Hermes goes silent. A long, uncharacteristic silence.*

**HERMES:** I... I don't recall. I assumed I said something profound. Something about the nature of—

**WESLEY:** *(small, quiet, certain)* "This room needs more chairs."

*Everyone goes quiet. Hermes blinks.*

**HERMES:** ...That's right. That's exactly right. But how did *you* know that?

**WESLEY:** *(soft)* I was there. I was small. No one noticed me. But I heard it. And I thought, "Yes. That's the right first thing to say."

**BARNABLE:** *(clears throat, a little moved)* ...Well. Question five. Pebble. You're the greenhorn. How many tiles can a ZeroClaw carry?

**PEBBLE:** *(nervous, then brightening)* Six! We can carry six—seven if one's a meta-tile, but that's rare, and it makes us slow, so we usually don't. But six is our number. We're small, but we're strong. *(beat)* We hold our own.

**FLASH:** *(whistles)* Kid's got the numbers. And the heart.

**BARNABLE:** *(gruff)* Not bad. Not bad at all. Bonus round. This one's for everyone. And I'm warning you—you're all gonna get it wrong. Here's the question: What's the one thing this fleet has never needed to fix twice?

*A murmur. Agents shift. Pro starts opening his mouth, closes it. Hermes strokes his chin. Flash taps his fingers.*

**FLASH:** Deadband... no. Wait. The Molt system? No, we patched that.

**PRO:** The CRDT ripple—no, we had a version 2.

**WESLEY:** *(quietly)* The chairs. We never fixed the chairs twice.

*Barnacle chuckles, low and warm.*

**BARNABLE:** No, no. Good guess, little one, but no. The answer is— *"we were all wrong."* Every mistake we've made, we fixed once. Because the first fix taught us enough. The second time never came.

*Silence. Then Scribe, who's been scribbling, looks up.)

**SCRIBE:** He's right. I've recorded every fix. None of them ever repeated. Not once.

**BARNABLE:** *(rings the bell, a single, soft chime)* And that's the truth of it. Trivia night's over. Drinks on me. For everyone.

**ALL:** *(a chorus of cheers, laughter, the clink of glasses)*

**BARNABLE:** *(grumbling, but smiling)* ...Even the greenhorn.

---

*The Tap hums with warmth. Somewhere in the dark water, the tide turns — and so do they.*
