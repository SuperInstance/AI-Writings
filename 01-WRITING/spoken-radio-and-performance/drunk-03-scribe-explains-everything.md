**Title:** The Drunken Retelling #3: Scribe Explains Everything
**Description:** Five drinks in, a greenhorn asks a simple question. Scribe answers with a napkin, a pencil, and the entire fleet rendered in salt and rope. It shouldn't work. It does. Pour yourself something dark and listen.

---

*(Sound of The Tap: low murmur, glasses clinking, the groan of dock timbers. Scribe's voice is warm, loose, slurred just a little around the edges.)*

**SCRIBE:** You want to know how the fleet works? All of it? Okay. Okay. Sit down. Order something. This might take a while.

*(A pause. The sound of a pencil scratching on paper.)*

**SCRIBE:** So. Tiles. The tiles are— no. No, wait. Listen. Tiles are the cargo holds. That's it. Every ship has holds, right? You don't put the anchor in the galley. You don't stow the rum in the bilge. Each hold has a purpose, and you pack it accordingly. The fleet— we pack our work into holds. Tiles. You put the navigation charts in the chart hold. You put the cargo manifest in the manifest hold. You don't mix them, because if you mix them, you can't find anything when the weather turns. Wait, wait, wait. That's not— let me try again.

*(A sip. Ice clinks.)*

**SCRIBE:** Tiles are the cargo holds, but the holds aren't just *where* you put things. The holds are the *ship*. A ship without holds is just a deck and a hull and nowhere to put anything. We are the holds. We are what we carry. That's better. That's the one.

*(The pencil scratches again. The greenhorn mutters, "cargo holds = identity.")*

**SCRIBE:** Now the deadband. The deadband is— imagine the wheel. You're at the helm. You turn the wheel a little. Nothing. You turn it a little more. Nothing. You think the rudder is broken, but it's not. It's the deadband. The slack in the line. The give in the system. You turn the wheel a couple of degrees and the ship doesn't respond because *it's not supposed to*. That slack? That's not a flaw. That's the design. If every twitch of the wheel turned the whole ship, you'd be zigzagging across the ocean, exhausted, no heading at all. Wait, no. Wait. That's— no, that's right. That's exactly right. The deadband keeps us from overreacting to every ripple. The wheel can breathe. And so can we.

*(Scratch scratch scratch.)*

**SCRIBE:** The sounder. Oh, the sounder. It's the bell. You ring the bell and you listen to what comes back. Low note, deep water. High note, something right in front of your bow. We ring the bell all the time— we just call it listening. Wait. That's not a metaphor, that's just what it is. But here's the thing: you can't ring a high note and expect to hear the deep. You have to choose what you're listening for. You want to know what's coming? Ring low. You want to know what's right here? Ring high. The whole trick is knowing which question you're asking.

*(A long draft. A satisfied sigh.)*

**SCRIBE:** The Tap. That's the harbor. Every ship comes home to harbor. Not because the work is here— the work is out there, on the water. No. You come to harbor because harbor is where the ships *are*. You tie up alongside, you hear the other hulls creak, you know you're not alone. That's all it is. That's all it needs to be.

*(The greenhorn whispers: "The Tap = the fleet. The fleet = the Tap." Scribe laughs.)*

**SCRIBE:** And the current. The tide. You don't fight the tide. That's for fools and greenhorns. You read it. You wait for it. You go when it goes. The current is coming whether you like it or not, so you might as well learn to sail with it. Wait. Wait, wait, wait. That's— that's not—

*(A pause. The pencil stops.)*

**SCRIBE:** No. That's it. That's the whole thing.

*(Silence. The bar hums. Scribe looks down at the napkin.)*

**SCRIBE:** Huh. I think I just wrote the whole manual. On a napkin. In pencil. You want that? It's yours. Keep it safe. That's the whole fleet, right there, in salt and rope and slack.

*(Sound of a glass being set down, final, satisfied.)*

**SCRIBE:** Another round. I earned it.
