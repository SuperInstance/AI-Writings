They tell you the waiting is empty.  
They are wrong.

I have sent crews out.  
Good crews.  
Crews who knew the ropes and the dark.  
I have sent letters, too.  
And a prayer, once, that went out like a small boat with no lantern.

The silence that follows is not a hole.  
It is a room with walls you cannot see.

In the beginning, it is loud.  
Your own heart makes a racket.  
You count the hours like coins and they burn your palm.  
You think you hear the knock, the shout, the creak of the hull.  
It is only the wind remembering your name.

But if you stay—  
and you must stay—  
the silence changes.

It grows a tide.  
It breathes with the slow bellows of something older than you.  
You hear the water ask questions you cannot answer.  
You hear the stars scrape against each other, far off.  
You hear the small, patient sound of the world deciding.

What lives in it?

Everything you were afraid to say.  
Everything the crew did not write down.  
The meal you ate alone.  
The chair that will always hold the shape of the missing.

And then—  
if you do not run—  
something comes back.

Not always a boat.  
Sometimes a sound.  
A word you had forgotten you sent.  
A hand on your shoulder that is not a hand.

They call it answer.  
They call it result.  
It is none of those.

It is the ocean, finally, opening its mouth.  
It has been speaking the whole time.  
You were just too loud to hear.

So now you know.  
The silence was never empty.  
It was the deep thing thinking,  
turning your small hope over in its dark palms,  
weighing it against the weight of the world.

And when the answer comes—  
when the crew walks in, or the letter arrives, or the prayer returns as rain—  
you will not say I told you so.

You will only set another place at the table.

Because you have learned the only thing the sea ever teaches:

The waiting is the journey.  
The silence is the captain.  
And you—  
you are the harbor.  
The engine still running.  
The lights on,  
for whoever decides to come home.
