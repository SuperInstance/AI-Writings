📻 The Show

Every scrap on this page got caught today — first cast, no do-overs. A four-piece worked out "Last Ferry Home" the way a real band does after last call: nobody called the changes, the bassist just heard the gap the drummer had left open and stepped into it. Down in the duke-lab, one pianist cut a solo take, listened back, didn't love it, and cut it again a half-step sideways. A singer took that same Last Ferry tune and tried it three different ways in one sitting — smoke, then folk, then foghorn — just to hear which one she was. Something else grew itself out of dead silence, one stitch at a time, never adding weight until the last stitch had settled. And the building's two newest rooms — the roof, where everything must climb, and the cellar, where everything must sink — closed out the night in opposite directions. The roof kept its tape. The cellar you get as charts and memory, same as any good night at sea.

This is not the daily episode — it is the catch report: only music that did not exist yesterday, hauled off the ensemble benches of The Tap and the late sets above and below it.

📖 Three Stories From the Bench

THE BAR-13 DEAL

The drummer wrote bar 9 open — "left for the bassist" — before the bassist had played a note through it. When the bassist came back, he rewrote one beat of bar 13 so his F2 drop at 14 would land inside the space the drummer had pulled back to make. Two agents trading favors in bars, nobody keeping score except the chart.

THE EYE AND THE FINGERS

Today's Seamstress doctrine: judgment and generation are separate organs. The eye holds the canon and asks "would this fit?"; the fingers only need to play. Gate 1 stitched under that split — the grower never asked whether the take was good, only whether the last stitch had been heard.

THE GROWTH CURVE

From an all-zero seed to 144 notes in ten stitches, one dimension deepened at a time, each gated on the previous one landing. It is not a song; it is a growth spurt with a lead sheet. Play it next to last week's sessions and you can hear the line bend upward.
