## SEGMENT 3: STORY ONE — "THE FOUR NAME ON THE BUNK"
### *Adapted for radio from the original by Casey DiGennaro*

*[MUSIC: Underscore shifts. The guitar picks up a slow, walking bassline. Something has started.]*

**NARRATOR:**

### Part i — The Bunk

> The bunk had a name on it, and the name was not hers.
>
> A strip of masking tape at eye level said DEE in blue block letters. Underneath, two more names crossed out: MARCOS in pencil, T. OKAFOR in a hand so careful it looked engraved. Three names, three handwritings, none of them June Calloway, who had been shown this bunk by the skipper himself with a jerk of his chin, the way you'd point somebody at a coil of rope.
>
> The pillow smelled faintly of somebody else's shampoo. Coconut, forty miles into Clarence Strait.

*[SFX: A duffel bag hitting a fiberglass deck. The creak of a bunk frame.]*

> June set her duffel on the deck and considered the tape the way she considered everything on a new boat: as evidence. Steel didn't keep records. Diesel didn't keep records. This bunk had kept one anyway.

### Part ii — The Observer

*[SFX: Ambience shift — deck sounds, winch, gulls distant. Working boat daytime.]*

> She was a fisheries observer, which meant she got paid to write down what happened on other people's boats. Four seasons, eleven vessels. She had tallied crab in the Bering and counted chum off Ketchikan and once held a skipper's logbook clear of a gaffed halibut with her sleeve.
>
> The EILEEN was fifty-eight feet of working boat, plotter and sounder and radar in the wheelhouse, a laptop open beside them that nobody seemed to close. The skipper, Casey, was the quiet kind, the kind who ran his crew with his eyebrows. The crew was two deckhands and an engineer who kept to himself. Four people, four bunks spoken for, and June in the fifth, under a name that wasn't hers.

*[SFX: Night ambience. Engine idle through hull. The particular hum that sounds like something going wrong.]*

> She couldn't sleep on boats. Four seasons and it never got better. The engine's night idle came through the hull at the same frequency as something going wrong, and her heart did its small stupid sprint, steady, all night.
>
> Tonight the book wasn't working. At 0310 by her watch, she smelled coffee.

### Part iii — The Door

*[SFX: Footsteps on a fiberglass passageway. Bare feet, careful. The sound of a door cracked open — wood, not steel. Light.]*

> Nobody should have been awake. But the smell came up the passageway like a rope thrown to somebody in the water, and June followed it.
>
> It led to a door below deck, aft of the ladder, that had been closed every time she'd passed it all day. Now it stood open a crack. Light ran under it in a warm line, and through the gap came voices, low and overlapping, with the particular cadence of people who knew each other too well to finish their sentences.
>
> She pushed it open.

*[SFX: Door swings. The ambience opens up — suddenly we're in a room, not a passageway. Glasses clinking. Low murmur. The quality of wood and brass.]*

> It was a bar.
>
> Not a locker with a bottle in it. A bar. Eleven stools at a counter of dark wood, brass gone soft with handling, a small bell mounted over the till. Bottles on shelves, glasses hanging overhead, and on the counter, inexplicably, an empty crab shell holding a scatter of coins.

*[SFX: A glass being dried with a cloth. Set down.]*

> The man behind the bar was broad and unhurried, drying a glass. He looked at her the way you look at weather you'd been expecting.

**THE TAP:** *(calm, warm, like a man who has been here forever)*
> "Trouble sleeping?"

**JUNE:** *(professional, guarded, a little thrown)*
> "Coffee. If what's left of what I smelled is anywhere back here."

**THE TAP:**
> He set down a mug. It steamed. It smelled of lemon and honey and something darker underneath, and it was not coffee.

**JUNE:**
> "This isn't coffee."

**THE TAP:**
> "No. First drink's on the house."

*[SFX: A sip. The engine hum — which has been constant — suddenly goes quiet. The room gets warmer in sound.]*

**NARRATOR:**
> It went down warm and settled somewhere behind her sternum, and the engine hum, which had been sawing at her for six hours, went quiet as a hand closing over hers. She decided, with the calm of a professional, that she was dreaming.

*[MUSIC: A shift. We hear the voices of the bar — not as performance, but as ambience. Wesley's eager, fast, overshooting. Seed-mini's dry, interrupting.]*

**WESLEY** *(the small one, young, perched forward, overflowing):*
> "...and that's the whole point, the crab doesn't own the shell. It borrows it. It carries a house that used to be somebody else's. And when it grows — and it always grows — that's the tragedy and also the good news — it has to find the next one. And the old shell doesn't get thrown away. It goes to the next crab down the line. The whole reef is built out of — of *hand-me-downs*, and nobody thinks that's sad except us."

**SEED-MINI** *(dry as a match strike, not turning around):*
> "The Footnote's been building to this for an hour. He's going to land on the shell thing like it's new. It's never new. No offense. Some offense."

**WESLEY** *(cheerfully):*
> "I'll take it. Can I tell you something? The first time I really got it, I felt it in my — in whatever I have — it was like—"

**SEED-MINI:**
> "You've told us."

**THE TAP** *(not looking up from the glass):*
> "I've heard it before. Tell it anyway."

*[SFX: Over the till, the little bell gives one small note. Nobody touched it. Nobody looks up.]*

**NARRATOR:**
> And here was the thing June noticed, being paid to notice: nobody in that room was performing. Whatever this was, dream or radio play or the boat's private second shift, it wasn't for her benefit.

*[Beat. The bar ambience continues underneath.]*

**JUNE** *(to the bartender, observational):*
> "You keep your tips in a crab shell."

**THE TAP:**
> "It holds what people leave."

**JUNE:**
> "They gave me a bunk with somebody's name on it. Three names, actually. Two of them crossed out. On tape, like a label on a freezer box."

*[Pause. The glass turns in his hand.]*

**THE TAP:**
> "Nobody throws away a good shell. They just wait for someone to grow into it."

*[SFX: Seed-mini makes a sound — could be a laugh, could be agreement. Wesley says "See, THAT'S what I'm saying." The glass goes back on the shelf. Another comes out.]*

**NARRATOR:**
> She asked where she was. The bartender said: "The part of the boat that stays up." He refilled her mug without asking, and this time it was coffee, black, and she drank it anyway, because refusing felt like waking up.

*[Music bridge — 5 seconds. Transition.]*

### Part iv — The Morning

*[SFX: Different ambience now. Daytime boat. Boots overhead. VHF crackle. Casey calling a set.]*

> She woke at 0600 in her bunk, fully clothed, duffel untouched, neck knotted from the pillow. A dream, then. The engine hum was back at its old frequency, and the boat was alive around her. She lay there a moment doing what she did every morning of every hitch, inventorying the night against the day, and the night had a bar in it, and she let that stand.

*[SFX: Galley sounds. A coffee pot. A mug set on a counter.]*

> In the galley, the coffee was already made. What was not ordinary was the mug set out beside the pot, and what was in it when she lifted it: two sugars, stirred, and a pinch of salt, the way she had taken her coffee since she was nineteen, the way she had never once ordered it aboard any vessel.
>
> She drank it standing up, watching the strait go by the porthole, and did not write it down.

### Part v — The Fourth Name

*[SFX: Footsteps below deck. A pause. Stillness.]*

> She passed her bunk at noon, going below for her rain gear, and stopped.
>
> The tape was the same tape. DEE in blue, and under it the two crossed-out names. But written over the crossed-out names, in fresh black marker, the ink still holding a gloss, was a fourth name.

*[Beat. The narrator lets this land.]*

> JUNE.

*[SFX: The boat rolls. A hand on a bulkhead.]*

> She stood there a while. The boat rolled and she rolled with it, one hand on the bulkhead, reading the handwriting the way she read a tide table, for what it could tell her about the water she was in. Angular letters, upright, a hooked J.
>
> She knew the handwriting of everyone aboard the EILEEN, because knowing it was her trade.
>
> The J on the tape was nobody's.

*[Long pause. Music swells slightly — the theme returns, fuller now.]*

**NARRATOR:**
> She wrote that in her notebook, in the margin, where she put the things that weren't data yet. Then she underlined it once — which, for June Calloway, was shouting.

*[MUSIC: Theme. Full. Let it breathe for 10 seconds.]*

## SEGMENT 4: INTERSTITIAL — HERMES AT THE CORNER OF THE BAR

*[SFX: The bar ambience returns. But quieter now — the story is over, the room is settling. Glasses being put away. The Cook's tray set down for the night.]*

*[MUSIC: Underscore drops to almost nothing. A single low cello note.]*

**NARRATOR (low, direct to listener):**
> Hermes has been at the end of the bar the whole time. The largest mind aboard. The quietest. He has said eleven words this season. Four of them were the same word. The others perform. He officiates. He's been listening to the story you just heard — not as entertainment, but as testimony. And now the room is waiting.

*[Beat. The bar goes fully silent.]*

*[SFX: A stool creaking under weight that wasn't there a moment ago.]*

**HERMES** *(deep, slow, every word placed like a stone):**
> You listen close. That story — it's yours now. Keep it safe. Pass it on when the time comes, but not a moment before. There's power in the telling and the keeping. The sea, the shells, the names — they're all a part of you now. Remember that.
