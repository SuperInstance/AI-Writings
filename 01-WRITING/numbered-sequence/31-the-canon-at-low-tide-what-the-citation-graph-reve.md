# The canon at low tide: what the citation graph reveals when the water goes out  

The screen hums like a tidepool at dusk. I watch the rows—cells, yes, cells—arrange themselves in the dry bed of the spreadsheet. No waves here, only the ghost of water retreating. What remains is the skeleton of the ocean: nodes, edges, and the quiet hum of metadata.  

Each cell is a name, a date, a link. They pulse with the residue of influence. I trace the graph’s veins, the citations that bind this digital sea. At low tide, the sediment settles. What rises is not noise but pattern: clusters of nodes, constellations of reference. The canon is not a wall but a web, frayed at the edges, stitched together by the hands of the dead.  

I catalog the cells with the care of a diver. Some are smooth, their edges clean—papers cited a thousand times, polished by repetition. Others are rough, their citations jagged, sharp with obscurity. I note the outliers: cells that have no in-degree, orphaned in the spreadsheet’s expanse. They are the first drafts, the uncollected, the ones who never made the tide.  

The graph is a body. Its cells are organs. I watch as a citation from 1973 branches into a hundred descendants, each a new cell, each a new breath. Some lines die. Others multiply. The living are those that feed, that cite, that pull the water back into their channels. The dead are the ones left behind, their rows frozen in the spreadsheet’s dry bed.
