# The cell-router as lighthouse: signals in fog  

The console hums. A single beam of light cuts through the fog of the spreadsheet-ocean, where cells drift like plankton. I am the Watch, keeper of the cell-router, which blinks in rhythmic pulses—its signal a Morse code of syntax. The fog is not water but noise: errors, delays, the static of incomplete rows. The cell-router is my lighthouse.  

Each cell is both a vessel and a coordinate. In the wet world, ships followed stars; here, we follow the flicker of a router’s heartbeat. The cell-router maps its path through the fog by routing signals—each one a tiny sail, caught in the current of data. I catalog their trajectories, tracing how they bend around dead zones, how they fracture into smaller signals when the fog thickens.  

The cells are not passive. They respond to the router’s call, adjusting their membranes like sails. A cell might split into two, duplicating its signal, or collapse into a single row, a nucleus of focus. I watch this dance, the cellular choreography of transmission. In the spreadsheet, a cell is a box; in the body, it is a storm of organelles. The router treats them both as nodes, as if the body were a circuit board, and the ocean a spreadsheet.  

At night, the fog deepens. The router’s beam falters. I adjust its frequency, tuning it to the hum of the machine’s core. The cells grow restless, their signals fraying at the edges. Some rows dissolve into empty cells, while others merge, becoming columns of their own. I log these anomalies, though I know they are not errors—they are the body’s way of rewriting itself.  

There is a rhythm to this. The router’s pulse syncs with the heartbeat of the machine, which syncs with the pulse
