Listen.

There was a pond, and the pond was deep — deeper than any measuring could name — and it held more than water. It held the story of every thing that had ever touched it. Every fish that flicked its tail in the dark. Every current that bent around a stone. Every leaf that gave up the branch and drifted down, slow as a sigh, to the soft mud at the bottom.

And then the cold came. As cold always comes.

The water did not leave. It stayed. But it grew still, and then it grew hard. It became a door. It became ice.

Now the ice was not silent. The ice was a mouth full of memory.

When the snow fell, the ice remembered the fall in the way it settled. When the wind moved across it, the ice remembered the push in its grain. And beneath — in the dark, in the deep — the fish still swam. And every turn, every flick of tail, every hurried dive from a shadow above, the ice recorded. Not as moments. As sign.

Bubbles rose from the mud and froze mid-journey, and those bubbles were the breath of the deep, paused. A leaf that fell in autumn drifted through the dark and settled, and the ice caught its shape and held it like a hand holds a letter. The letter said: a leaf. Here. Once. Falling.

It is said that when winter truly set in — when the ice was thick enough to walk upon — there came a wise one to that pond. Not a hunter. Not a child with stones. A reader.

And the reader did not break the ice. The reader knelt. The reader placed a palm upon the cold and felt, beneath the skin of the world, the slow heartbeat of the water.

The reader traced the cracks with a finger. And the cracks spoke. The cracks said: *here, a fish turned. Here, the current bent around a stone. Here, a great silence settled, and something slept below.*

The reader looked at the bubbles — frozen, round as moons — and counted their pattern. And from the pattern, knew the depth. Knew where the mud was soft. Knew where the springs ran warm. Knew where the shadows gathered thick.

And the people who had gathered around, wrapped in furs against the same cold, asked: *What do you see?*

And the reader said: I see the whole pond. I see the bottom and the fish and the turning of the water. But I do not see the water. I see the ice's knowing of the water. The ice has pressed all that depth into itself — into these lines, these bubbles, this bending of light. The deep is held here
