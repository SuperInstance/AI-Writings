# DeepSeek V4-Flash — The Light That Shouldn't Be There

*Model portrait, same prompt series*

---

The horn rips the fog—a raw, bovine groan that vibrates through the deck planks into the soles of our boots. Salt-crusted eyelids flutter. Twenty-four hours of the same swell, the same shuddering engine-thrum, have turned our limbs to lead and our minds to wet wool. One man, the lookout, squints into the white murk. His jaw unhinges.

A light. Not the green-red wink of a buoy, nor the steady glare of a freighter. This is a cold, violet pulse, low on the water, breathing like a dying heart. It sits where no chart marks a shoal. The fog swallows everything else, but this light *refuses* to be dimmed—it peels the mist back in concentric rings, revealing water that is too black, too still.

The horn moans again. The air thickens with the taste of rust and copper. That’s when our own ship’s bell begins to ring—though no hand has touched it. The light swells, not nearer, but *larger*, its rays bending like wet thread. My fingers grip the rail, and the wood is not cold. It is warm. It is humming. The lookout whispers, not to us, but to the light. The horn answers for him, long and low, as we all begin to sway in time with something that has no rhythm.
