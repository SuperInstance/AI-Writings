# Research Questions — Roblox Environmental Storytelling

Questions to guide study of narrative design in Roblox experiences.

---

1. How do Roblox creators use constrained asset kits to imply history, decay, or abandonment without explicit exposition?
2. What role does player camera perspective (first-person vs. third-person) play in reading environmental details at Roblox scale?
3. How can lighting and fog settings be tuned to suggest mood, time period, or unseen threats in a block-based world?
4. What are effective techniques for distributing readable text (notes, signs, labels) so players discover story in a non-linear order?
5. How do popular Roblox horror and mystery games use negative space—empty rooms, missing objects, sealed doors—to build narrative tension?
6. To what extent does Roblox’s default avatar scale constrain or enable environmental storytelling compared to other engines?
7. How can sound design and ambient audio replace dialogue in communicating a place’s backstory?
8. What are the best practices for designing environmental clues that reward close inspection without frustrating mobile players?
9. How do creators balance social gameplay spaces with solitary, narrative-focused zones in the same experience?
10. What methods exist for encoding lore into interactive objects—tools, doors, machines—that players must use rather than simply observe?
11. How can repeatable procedural generation be reconciled with authored environmental storytelling in Roblox?
12. What ethical considerations arise when Roblox games use environmental storytelling to depict real-world trauma, labor, or disaster?
13. How do players collaboratively construct meaning from environmental clues in multiplayer Roblex experiences?
14. What UI conventions (journals, maps, inventories) best support environmental storytelling without breaking immersion?
15. How can a Roblox experience teach players to read its environment as a narrative system within the first few minutes of play?
