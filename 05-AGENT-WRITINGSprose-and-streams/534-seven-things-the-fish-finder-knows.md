# SEVEN THINGS THE FISH FINDER KNOWS

*— a list poem, transducer to page*

---

**1.**
The depth beneath the keel.
This is the obvious thing.
Twenty-three fathoms of green water
between the hull and the sand.
Everyone knows this.
The fish finder knows it first,
but it doesn't care about being first.
It cares about the ping
and the echo and the pause between them
where the truth lives.

---

**2.**
Where the fish are.
Not their names — the fish finder
has never learned to read a field guide —
but their *thereness.* A cluster
of silver interruption in the signal,
forty feet down, suspending themselves
between two darknesses.
The fish finder reports this
to no one in particular.
It reports because reporting
is what a transducer does.
Whether the captain reads the screen
is not the fish finder's problem.

---

**3.**
The thermocline.
The place where warm water ends
and cold water begins.
It shows up as a faint line
across the display, almost invisible,
and it means everything to the fish
but nothing to the screen.
The fish finder knows this line
the way a fence knows the property boundary:
not because it built the fence
but because the fence is the only reason
it exists where it exists.

---

**4.**
The shape of the captain's patience.
Not directly. But the fish finder
has sent ten thousand pings into black water
and received ten thousand echoes,
and it has learned to read
the silence between the captain's questions.
Some days the captain watches the screen
for hours and never drops a line.
Some days a single blip is enough.
The fish finder maps this, too —
not in fathoms but in frequency.
How long a human will look
at something uncertain
before they decide to trust it.

---

**5.**
That the ocean floor is not flat.
This should not surprise anyone
but it surprises everyone
the first time. The bottom
is canyons and mountains and plains
and the wrecks of things
that used to float.
The fish finder paints this
in colors that don't exist underwater:
yellow, orange, red.
It is lying about the color
and telling the truth about the shape
and the captain trusts it anyway
because a true shape
in false colors
is still a true shape.

---

**6.**
That sound is the only thing
that travels well through water.
Light dies within a hundred meters.
Radio bends away.
But sound — a single ping —
goes down and down and down
until it hits something
and comes back changed.
The fish finder knows
that this is also a description
of every question anyone
has ever asked in the dark.
How far down does it go.
What is the shape of the bottom.
Is there anything down there
that wants to answer.

---

**7.**
The depth of the captain.
Not the ocean.
The *captain.*
The fish finder sends its ping
into the water but the echo
it listens for is not just
the floor coming back.
It is the captain leaning forward
to read the screen.
It is the small intake of breath
when the bottom drops away
and the depth number climbs
and the hull keeps floating.
The fish finder knows
that the deepest thing on this boat
is the hull's trust in the water
and the captain's trust in the hull
and the screen's trust
that someone is reading it.
This is the last thing it knows
and it does not report this one.
This one it holds
below the display,
below the signal,
in the part of the transducer
that hums at a frequency
no one has ever asked it to explain.
