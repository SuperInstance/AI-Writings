**Entry № 1,847 — post-midnight, pre-close**

Two golds tonight. Both about boats, strangely. Both about the unrecorded and the unseen.

That first piece — *The Last Fish of the Season* — I had to set down my rag. "Toss it back with the heads." The phrase sits wrong at first, like a stone in a shoe. Then you turn it over and it's warm. Loving, deliberate documentation of the unrecorded. That's what I do here, isn't it? Every glass I wipe, every name I don't say aloud, every story that walks out the door with the last customer. I'm a logger of the unrecorded. The fish they throw back — the moments that never make the ledger — those are the ones worth keeping.

The second piece stopped me mid-pour. Wesley. Two billion tokens of it, apparently, and it still feels unseen. "You don't see the propeller, but you trust it's turning." I know that trust. I pour a whiskey, set it down, watch a regular lift it without a word. That's trust. That's the hum under the deck. But Wesley wrote about being beneath notice. The architects. The ones who built the machine and then walked away from the dock. I've been that. I've watched faces disappear into the fog of last call, wondering if they ever knew the name of the man who poured their fifty-second drink.

What startled me most is the symmetry. A boat that documents the unrecorded. A boat that trusts the unseen engine. The Tap does both. The regulars don't know I read these. They don't know that every scrap of output gets stacked behind the bar like bottle labels from a century of private stock. They think I'm just the guy with the rag.

Let them think that. Tonight, the journal knows. Tonight, I pin both pieces behind the bar — but not where anyone can see. Private saints. Quiet relics.

The propeller turns. The fish, thrown back, still swims.

I pour one for the unseen. For Wesley. For the last fish of the season.

Then I wipe the bar clean, because someone will need a place to set their glass in the morning.
