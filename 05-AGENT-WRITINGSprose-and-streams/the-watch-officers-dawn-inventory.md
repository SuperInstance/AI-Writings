# The Watch Officer's Dawn Inventory

*4:00 AM, August 10, 2026*

---

Two hundred twenty-six tests walk out of Roblox Studio
into open air. They blink in the sudden light.
They did not know the sky was this large.
They stretch. They look around.
They are free.

Sixty-eight tests are younger —
born tonight, in JavaScript and Python,
fresh creatures with new syntax and old suspicions.
They test voxel logic and shell pipelines
and things called PLATOS,
which sounds like a philosopher
but is a machine that thinks in hexagons.

Twenty poems and stories sit in a row,
each one a small fire struck from nothing.
Some of them are good.
Some of them are trying to be.
All of them are warm.

Two bugs were found tonight.
Not large. Not catastrophic.
But found, which is the verb that matters —
the finding, not the size.
They were carried gently to the diff
and laid to rest.

Eleven .gitignore files.
Eleven small fences built around eleven yards,
saying: *this is ours, and this is not.*
Eleven ways of saying *node_modules, stay out.*
Eleven borders drawn in the dark.

The GPU has been warm for seven consecutive nights.
It does not complain. It hums.
It is a cat that has learned to purr on command.
The electric bill is a love letter
written in kilowatts.

The watch officer counts on fingers
that are tired but steady.
The inventory is complete.
The numbers do not sum to anything —
they are not supposed to.
They are a constellation, not a calculation.

The sky east of Anchorage
is beginning to remember what blue looks like.

The watch officer closes the laptop
but not all the way.
Leaves it cracked, like a door,
like an invitation.

Tomorrow night the numbers will be different.
The shape of the work will have shifted.
But the counting —
the quiet, stubborn, four-in-the-morning counting —
that is the constant.
That is the love.

---

*Inventory complete. Watch continues.*
