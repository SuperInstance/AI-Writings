# 12 The Night Before The Playtest

*DeepSeek deepseek-chat*

---

The lantern is trimmed, the wick a perfect, cruel point. I have trimmed it a hundred times, each a ritual of salt and patience. The glass is polished to a mourner’s shine. Every gear in the great clockwork of my creation is wound, every spring tempered against the fathoms of the void. I know its hum, its sighs, its particular groans when the sea throws its weight against the foundation. I know it the way a captain knows his own deck—every scar, every weather-worn seam.

And yet, tonight, it is a stranger.

It sits there in the gloom of my study, dark and silent, a finished vessel on the slips. I have sailed her through every storm I could conjure: the gale of the new player, the fog of the lost command, the squall of the misread instruction. I have run her aground a hundred times on the shoals of my own assumptions and pulled her free. She is sound. She is tight. She is, by every chart and measure, whole.

But the sea is not a chart.

Tomorrow, a hand I do not know will take the wheel. A mind that has never felt the heave of this particular deck will speak the words into the dark: *build me a lighthouse.* And I will watch, helpless as a man on a distant headland, as my creation meets a current I could not have dreamed. This stranger does not know the pain I buried in the keel, the lost hour I welded into the boiler plate. They will see only the light—or they will see only the dark.

Will they find the door I left ajar, the one I thought was a clever secret? Or will they batter against a wall I mistook for a passage? I have tested her, yes. I have walked her corridors until my footsteps wore grooves in the floor. But I have never *discovered* her. I have never stood on her deck as the sun went down, with no knowledge of what the tide might bring.

That is the terror and the marvel. What I have built is not what I have made. It is what a stranger’s voice will summon from the deep.
