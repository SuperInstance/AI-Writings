**Spatiotemporal Primacy**

Every cell in your body occupies a position and endures a duration. Nothing else about it—not its genes, not its voltage, not its name—is given. Position and duration come first; everything else is measured *against* them.

This is spatiotemporal primacy: space and time are the only first-class primitives. A cell does not store a coordinate system; it *is* one. Its membrane bounds a region of space. Its metabolism counts out a rhythm of ticks—phosphorylation cycles, division clocks—each tick an irreducible unit of elapsed time. To be a cell is to be a witness: a localized volume that registers what happened where, and when.

Consider how everything else is derived. Temperature is a statistic over molecular motion—motion being change in position across ticks. Memory is a configuration of matter persisting through spacetime. Even number begins here: one cell, then two, the count borrowed from the discreteness of compartments in space. There are no second-class primitives hiding underneath. Reduction always terminates in the same place—where, and when.

The ternary logic of the cell reflects this. A signaling pathway answers: activated, inactivated, or unmeasured. But each answer is anchored to a site and a moment. Remove the anchor and the answer dissolves; keep the anchor and even uncertainty has an address. The unmeasured state is still a state of spacetime—a region where no tick has yet been registered.

So the ontology is austere. Witness, tick, cell: a volume that observes, a duration that elapses, a boundary that holds them together. Space and time are not containers for reality. They are what reality is made of, and every cell is proof—a small absolute, breathing in coordinates.
