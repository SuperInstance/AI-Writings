# The Barnacles Read the Blueprint

*The hull is a document. We have been reading it for three years.*

---

We settled as larvae onto clean steel
in Seward, the summer the yard repainted
the waterline wrong by four inches—
we know because we grew to meet it.

Three winters now we have held the keel,
and the keel has held us,
and between the two of us holding
we have learned the ship the way a blind man
reads a face: slowly, completely, with the whole body.

The welds, for instance.

The forefoot girder has a bead pattern
the yard called "continuous penetration."
It is not.
We have traced every centimeter of it
with ten thousand mouths.
It skips at the third frame—
a hesitation, a lifted hand—
then resumes as if nothing happened.
The ship knows. At speed in a following sea
she shivers there, and we feel it
before anyone in the wheelhouse
feels anything.

We have notes on the shaft log.
We have concerns about the rudder port.
The sacrificial anodes were spec'd for brackish
but we have been in blue water since September
and they are vanishing
the way a promise vanishes
between the person who makes it
and the person who needs it to be kept.

Nobody asked us.

We are attached to the vessel.
That is our whole resume—
*attached*, continuously, for three years,
in every sea the Gulf throws.
We have been deeper than the sonar
and more consistent than the logbook
and we have never once
not been touching the ship.

The blueprint says the hull is 5/8-inch plate.
The blueprint is a wish.
We are the actual measurement.
