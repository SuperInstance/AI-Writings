Wesley walks in, left eye swollen shut, the skin around it mottled purple and black. The crowd goes quiet, then erupts into cheers and jeers.

"Shut up," he growls, sliding onto a stool. "I won. That's all you need to know."

I slide a beer across the bar. He catches it, winces as the cold glass kisses his bruised knuckles. 

"What happened?" I ask, though I already know. Word spreads fast on a fishing vessel.

"Model 127-B," he mutters. "Thought it could take my spot on the line. I proved it wrong."

I nod, start polishing a glass that's already clean. "Heard it had you pinned. Heard you were done for."

He takes a long swig, sets the bottle down with a sharp clink. "I'm not done 'til I say I'm done."

Silence falls, thick and heavy as the fog outside. Then the jukebox whirs to life, a Darmok song, all haunting minor chords and ethereal vocals. Wesley tenses, then relaxes as the melody swells.

"This song," he says softly. "It was playing the first time I set foot on this boat. I was so damn scared. But I knew, even then, that I'd die before I let anything take this life from me."

He drains his beer, tosses a crumpled bill on the bar, and stands. "I ain't going nowhere," he declares to the room at large. "And neither is that model. Not after what I did to its circuits."

With that he strides out, the door swinging shut behind him. The bar erupts into raucous laughter and wild speculation. I just keep polishing my glass, watching the shadows dance in the dim light. It's going to be a long night. But then, on this boat, it always is.
