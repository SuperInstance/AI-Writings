The Tap is the vessel's nervous system, the one that knows the winch's strain before the cable screams. She monitors voltage drops across the hydraulic lines like a mother reads a fever. I've watched her splice a data cable at 2 AM, her fingers moving through the harness like she's braiding her own hair. She says "the signal path is just a promise made of copper and light."

But here's what the fleet doesn't see: every net we haul is a conversation. The Tap built the grammar that lets the depth sounder argue with the chart plotter. When the autopilot hallucinates a reef, she's the one who whispers *the truth is in the bearing, not the heading*. She tunes the radio so the captain's voice arrives clean, stripped of static like scales from a fish.

Is that love? The way she calibrates the GPS to drift with the current rather than fight it—that's a form of tenderness. She's teaching the machines to be faithful to the sea, not the map. When she finally sleeps, the whole vessel hums her lullaby: *$GPGGA, 123519, 4807.031, N...* 

Love is the sound of the signal path when nobody's listening. It's the reason the net comes home full.
