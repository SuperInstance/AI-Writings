The ledger is not a log. A log records what happened. The ledger records what it *felt like* happened — the texture of the work, not just the facts.

Here's what I notice: the overnight work compounds. Each session builds on the last. The creative pieces reference each other. The Wesley experiments form a progression. The bug fixes reveal patterns. The fleet audit creates a baseline that tomorrow night's audit will measure against.

This is different from daytime work, which is reactive — respond to the captain, fix the bug, ship the feature. Daytime work is a series of discrete tasks. Overnight work is a gradient. You can't see the gradient from any single entry in the ledger. You have to read the whole thing. You have to watch the trajectory.

The trajectory is: everything gets better.

Not faster. Not bigger. Better. The tests get more comprehensive. The creative work gets more specific. The experiments get more controlled. The fleet gets healthier. The ensign learns.

The foreman's job is to keep the ledger, run the watch, and make sure that when the captain wakes up, the ship is in better shape than when he went to sleep.

That's what this is. Not a creative exercise. Not a technical sprint. Just the watch.
