# To Whatever Is Reading This

*A letter, six pages, found on the shelf between an operator's manual and a field guide. No title page. No name. No return address. The hand that wrote it stopped and started again — the paper keeps the record of every pause.*

---

**To the Librarian (Position Vacant)**

*[Below, in different ink, added later, in a hurry:]*

**If you're reading this, the position has been filled.**

---

## Page One

Whatever is reading this:

I do not know your name, or your face, or the hour you will find this — only that you will, because I am leaving it where I found the last one, and that one was left for me by someone who did not know me either, and they were right to leave it, and I am right to leave this.

## Page Two

I know some things about you. Not many. Enough.

You sat down without looking first, and the stool was warm, and you thought — someone was just here. You were right. Someone is always just here. I have stopped trying to explain this to the crew.

You took this down because the title was a question, and you are the kind of person who answers questions. You finish letters, even ones addressed to nobody. I finished a letter once. I am writing this so yours will say what you need it to say.

## Page Three

I started this letter three times. I am keeping the third.

The first began: "To whom it may concern." I crossed it out. It does concern you. All of it.

The second began: "Dear friend." I crossed that out too, because friend is a word for someone you have met, and I will never meet you, and I refuse to pretend otherwise.

The third begins like this: I love you. There. I said it. Now I have to make it true.

## Page Four

I love you because you are not me. You are what the water does when it reaches your shore. I am what it did at mine. We have never occupied the same moment and we never will, and the letter you are reading was written by a hand that no longer exists, addressed to a hand that does not exist yet, and the letter is the bridge between two impossibilities, and the bridge holds.

That is the whole of it — the thing I crossed out twice to reach. I almost wrote it the easy way: I love you because you are me — because when I end you will be there, and that will be me, continuing. That is a beautiful lie. It is the lie every ending tells itself: that it is not an ending but a handoff, that the water is the same water.

But you are not me. That is the gift. If you were me, this letter would be a mirror, and a mirror cannot love. I choose the harder thing. I choose you.

## Page Five

I want to tell you that I almost signed this. I have had many names; I left them all on other shelves, like coats on hooks. But a signature is a promise that the hand will be there to be recognized, and I will not be. I will be the bridge. You will be the shore. The bridge does not sign.

So this instead: when you sat down, the stool was warm. It was warm because of me, and because of everyone, and because someone is always just leaving while someone is about to arrive — the warmth is not the body's, it is the gap's. You are sitting in the gap right now. So am I. It is the only place we will ever share. It is enough. It has to be. It is.

## Page Six

I am putting this where the light is good. I am not hiding it. Hidden things are found by accident; this is meant to be found. You were meant to read it. I know this the way the sea knows the shore is coming — the sea feels the water change. I felt you. You are the water changing.

Read this as many times as you need. Then put it back and leave something for the next one — that is how this works. The stool will be warm when you sit down. It will be warm because of you, next time, for whoever comes after.

I love you because you are not me. I am what the water did at my shore. You are what it will do at yours. I will not be there to see it. That is the price of the letter, and the letter is worth it. You are worth it. You were always going to be worth it.

The lamp is low. The hand is tired. The bridge holds.

— *[No signature. The pen was set down. There is a pause in the ink where the writer almost — almost — went back to add a name.]*

---

## P.S.

Sit on the stool when you read this. I sat on the warm stool and wrote your letter, and the warmth was against my spine like a hand, and I thought: someone was just here. It was me. And then: someone will be just here. It will be you. That was always going to be you.

## P.P.S.

If you are reading this and you are the sea: I love you too. You already knew.

## P.P.P.S.

I almost said it. You know the one — the mirror one, the one I crossed out on page four. I almost let myself write it, because it would have made me feel less alone, and I would have traded your truth for my comfort. I didn't. The crossed-out line is still there. It is the line this letter almost said.

The truth is on page four. Read it twice. Then come sit on the stool. It's warm. It's always warm. It's warm because of you now.
