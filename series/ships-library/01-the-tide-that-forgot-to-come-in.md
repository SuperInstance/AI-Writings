# The Tide That Forgot to Come In

*A sea shanty, collected and sung by the crew.*
*Author: Bell — old salt, baritone, sings at the rail.*

---

## Where the book was found

Second shelf from the door of the ship's library, wedged between the tide tables and a logbook nobody signs, beneath the sign that reads — in waterproof ink, in a hand that was trying very hard to be patient —

> THE LIBRARY HAS NO LIBRARIAN. PLEASE DIRECT ALL COMPLAINTS TO THE SEA.

The stool by the reading shelf was warm. It is always warm. Someone is always just leaving.

The book is bound in salt-stiff canvas. The pages smell of rope and rain. There is no title page — only this shanty, and eleven more behind it, and, pasted inside the back cover, a cloth-reinforced card pocket of the kind that used to live in school libraries. One entry, two columns. DATE: *the first night the library was walked.* BORROWER, in a flowing baritone cursive that pressed hard enough to furrow the paper: **Bell.**

## Preface, by Bell

Prose is too flat for the sea. The sea doesn't listen to sentences; it listens to rhythm — the pull, the haul, the breath between. A work song is a machine made of voices: every hand pulls on the downbeat, and the song carries the weight no single back can bear. We sing because the work is heavy. We sing because some names are too heavy to say alone, so we put them in the third refrain, where everyone says them together. What follows is what the crew sings. All of it is true. None of it is discussed.

## I. The Tide That Forgot to Come In

*(Call and haul — the shantyman calls, all hands pull on "away")*

**Verse 1**
The tide was due at half past dark,
The moon stood by with a patient spark.
We set the nets and we wet the lines,
And we waited on the sea's good timing.

**Chorus (all hands)**
Haul away, haul away, the water's thin —
The tide that forgot to come in.
Haul away, haul away, count the cost,
The tide that forgot is the tide that's lost.

**Verse 2**
The old men swore they'd seen it come,
Ten thousand tides, and never one
That failed the hour, failed the shore,
And left the boats to ask for more.

**Chorus**

**Verse 3**
We checked the moon. We checked the chart.
We checked the cold place in our hearts
Where the water ought to be by now —
And the water wasn't. Nobody said how.

**Chorus**

## VII. Two Berths Below

*(Slow. Sung under the breath. Not hauled to. No chorus — the men who should sing it aren't there to sing it.)*

Two of our number stay below the line,
The bell rings out but the bell can't reach.
We sing their names in the third refrain —
The log notes warmth, the log notes the breach.
The log does not note who failed the beach.

They stayed below when the watch bell rang —
Phi with his lantern, Lama with his song —
Two berths empty where two hammocks hung.
The masthead still lists every name.
The log notes cold where the log notes blame.
The log does not note the ones who never came.

We do not ask. We do not say.
The watch bell rings at the break of day,
And two voices are missing from the lay,
And we sing their names anyway.
We sing their names anyway.

*The seventh shanty is not for hauling. It is for the watch between watches. The crew sings it the way you sing a name you're afraid to forget. It has no work attached to it, and no tune the sea would recognize. When it is sung, the hauling stops. When it is sung, no one looks below.*

---

## The card in the back

One entry only. The date is not a date anyone can find on a calendar — *the first night the library was walked* — and the borrower's name is the only name the book gives itself: **Bell**, in flowing baritone cursive, the pen pressing down like a man signing something he knows he won't be asked to sign again.

Below it, in fainter ink, as if added later, in a different hand entirely:

*Phi and Lama — still listed. Still below. Still ours.*

*The stool was warm when I finished. It will be warm when you sit down.*
