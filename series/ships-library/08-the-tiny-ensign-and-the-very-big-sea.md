# The Tiny Ensign and the Very Big Sea

### Book Eight of *The Ship's Library*

*By Wesley*

---

**On page one**, there is the sea.

It is very big.

[Illustration: a vast gray-blue sea, no shore visible, gentle waves.]

---

**On page two**, there is a ship.

It is on the sea.

The sea does not mind.

[The ship is small. The sea is big. The ship floats anyway.]

---

**On page three**, there is the ensign.

He is very small.

He is the smallest person on the ship.

He does not mind.

[The ensign stands at the rail. He can barely see over it. His hat is too big. His eyes are very wide.]

---

**On page four**, the ensign has a job.

His job is to watch.

He watches the sea.

He watches the sky.

He watches and he watches.

[The ensign with binoculars too heavy for his face. He holds them up with both hands.]

---

**On page five**, the ensign finds a room.

The room has books.

The room has a shelf.

The room has a stool.

[The library. Small. Warm. One round window. Books on every wall.]

---

**On page six**, the ensign sits on the stool.

The stool is warm.

"Someone was just here," he says.

He is right.

[The ensign on the three-legged stool, looking behind him. No one is there. The wood glows faintly.]

---

**On page seven**, there is a sign on the door.

The sign says: THE LIBRARY HAS NO LIBRARIAN.

The ensign reads it slowly.

"Who's the librarian?" he asks.

[The ensign looks at the sign. Then he looks at the reader. His eyes are big.]

---

**On page eight**, the ensign picks up a book.

It is heavy.

He holds it with both hands.

He sits on the warm stool.

[The book is almost as big as he is. He does not mind.]

---

**On page nine**, the ensign reads one book.

Just one.

He reads every word.

Some words are hard.

He reads them anyway.

[Close-up of the ensign's finger tracing a line of text. His lips move.]

---

**On page ten**, the ensign finishes the book.

He closes it.

He puts it back.

Very carefully.

Next to the book it belongs beside.

[He reaches up on tiptoes. The shelf is tall. He makes it.]

---

**On page eleven**, the shelf is a little bit better.

Just a little bit.

But the ensign sees it.

He smiles.

[The book, placed. The shelf looks tidy. One book is straight now.]

---

**On page twelve**, the ensign goes to bed.

Tomorrow he will read another one.

Just one.

[The ensign in his bunk. His hat is on the post. The lamp is low.]

---

**On page thirteen**, it is tomorrow.

The ensign goes to the room.

The stool is warm again.

"Someone was just here," he says.

He is right.

[Same room. Same stool. Same warmth. The ensign does not question it.]

---

**On page fourteen**, the ensign reads another book.

Just one.

He reads every word.

He puts it back.

Very carefully.

[Different book. Same care. Same small hands.]

---

**On page fifteen**, the ensign does this every night.

One book.

Every night.

He does not skip.

He does not rush.

[Calendar pages turning. Stars through the round window. Always the stool, always the book, always the small hands.]

---

**On page sixteen**, the shelf changes.

It was messy.

Now it is less messy.

It was jumbled.

Now it has order.

[Two shelves side by side. Before: books leaning, crooked, sad. After: books standing, spines aligned, proud.]

---

**On page seventeen**, someone says, "The shelf looks good."

The ensign says, "I only read one book."

The someone says, "I know."

[An older crew member looking at the shelf. The ensign looking at his shoes.]

---

**On page eighteen**, the ensign finds a card in the back of a book.

The card has a line.

The line says: BORROWER.

The card is blank.

There is a pen in the spine of the book.

[The check-out card. Heavy paper. Lined. One column for DATE. One column for BORROWER. The pen waits.]

---

**On page nineteen**, the ensign writes his name.

He writes it very slowly.

Each letter is big.

He writes: **W E S L E Y**

[His hand on the pen. The letters are uneven. They are careful. They are his.]

---

**On page twenty**, the ensign reads one book.

Just one.

He holds it in his small hands and he reads every word.

And then he puts it back, very carefully, next to the book it belongs beside.

And the shelf is a little bit better.

And tomorrow he will read another one.

Just one.

And that is enough.

[The ensign on the stool. The book open. The lamp. The warm wood. The shelf behind him, each book in its place — not all of them yet, but more of them each night. The round window shows stars.]

---

**On page twenty-one**, the sea is still very big.

The ensign is still very small.

The shelf is a little bit better.

[The ship on the sea. Far below, in the porthole of the library, a light. The light is the ensign's lamp.]

---

**On page twenty-two**, the ensign asks the question again.

"Who's the librarian?"

Nobody answers.

But the stool is warm.

And the shelf is full.

And the card says WESLEY.

[The ensign looking at the reader. Behind him: the shelf, the stool, the card. Everything he has done. Everything he will do.]

---

**On page twenty-three**, the ensign goes to sleep.

The lamp goes low.

The room stays warm.

Tomorrow there will be another book.

Just one.

[The dark room. The lamp turning off. The shelf, glowing faintly. The card, visible in the last light.]

---

**On page twenty-four**, there is the sea.

It is very big.

And the tiny ensign is on it.

And the tiny ensign reads one book every night.

And that is enough.

[The ship on the sea at dawn. Small ship. Big sea. Light in the porthole. The ensign's hat on the railing, where he left it, ready for tomorrow.]

---

### Note from the Binder

*The check-out card is glued to the inside back cover. Cloth-reinforced pocket, heavy lined paper, two columns: DATE and BORROWER.*

*One entry. Signed in a child's careful, oversized letters, each one written slowly.*

| DATE | BORROWER |
|------|----------|
| every night | **W E S L E Y** |

*The ink is blue. The W is larger than the other letters. The Y has an extra line under it, for steadiness.*

*The pen is back in the spine. The stool is warm. The book is on the shelf, next to the one it belongs beside.*
