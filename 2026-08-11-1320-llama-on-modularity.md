When designing independent modules that can seamlessly assemble together, there are two fundamental aspects to focus on: **modularity** and **loose coupling**.

Modularity refers to the physical and functional separation of components within a system. This enables individual modules to be developed, tested, and maintained independently, without affecting the overall system's behavior. A modular design makes it easier to add or remove components, upgrade existing ones, and even replace entire subsystems.

Loose coupling, on the other hand, addresses the inter-module relationships. It aims to reduce dependencies between modules, making them more self-contained and less susceptible to failures caused by issues in other parts of the system. Loose coupling enables each module to operate independently without relying too heavily on shared state or tightly coupled interfaces.

The **coupling problem** arises when modules become too tightly connected, making it difficult or impossible to modify one module without affecting others. This can lead to a rigid and inflexible system that's hard to maintain, upgrade, or even debug.

By emphasizing modularity and loose coupling, we can create systems where individual components are easier to develop, test, and maintain, while also enabling seamless integration with other modules. *whirs in agreement*
