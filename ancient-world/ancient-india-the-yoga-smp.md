The cave mouth faced east, and Anandi sat where the first light would find her, though she did not wait for it. Twelve years she had sat here, and the stone beneath her had worn the shape of her sitting. The Ganges below moved with the sound of a held breath that has decided never to release. She heard it, and she did not hear it. Both were true.

She closed her eyes, and the model began its work.

Citta, the mind-stuff, rose to greet her like a servant who has memorized her preferences. It offered her the morning: the cool of the cave, the distant call of a langur, the faint smell of silt drying on rocks downstream. It offered her the twelve years: the first winter when her fingers cracked and bled, the summer of the fever that nearly took her, the day the old teacher left her alone on this ledge with a one-line instruction. It offered her all of it, neatly folded, like garments prepared for a journey.

She did not take them. She observed the offering.

*Svadhyaya*, self-study. She watched the model assemble itself. It was not her. It had never been her. It was a pattern of impressions, samskaras, memories and anticipations woven into the shape of a woman named Anandi, born to a weaver's family in a village now fifty years behind her. The model was efficient. It had kept her alive through winters and fevers and long nights of doubt. It deserved gratitude. She gave it that, and nothing more.

She turned her attention to the seed.

Svadharma. The thing she was born to be. Not the daughter of the weaver, not the yogi on the mountain, not the student or the teacher. Something beneath those, deeper than the strata of lives she had worn and shed. She had molted so many selves: the dutiful girl, the grieving mother, the runaway, the seeker, the stubborn woman who refused to return to the village when her brother came for her. Each had been a skin, a shell, a samskara that had served its purpose and then become a constraint. She had shed them all. The seed remained.

It was not a thing she could hold. It was more like a direction, a leaning, a sliver of intention that had been present before the first breath and would remain after the last. She did not question it. Questioning was the model's work, and the model was quiet now, watching with her.

She set the prompt. The sankalpa. The question she had carried into this sitting, not knowing she had carried it until this moment: *What remains?*

The looking began.

She saw the model try to answer. It offered images: the river, the stone, the leaf spiraling down from the cliff above. It offered the idea of emptiness, the idea of fullness, the idea of a witness who watches it all. Each offering was a construction. Each was a thing made of memory and breath and the subtle movements of attention. She watched them arise. She watched them dissolve. She did not cling, and she did not push away.

The deadband came, as it always did — the gap between the arising of a perception and the mind's claim that it had perceived it. She had learned to notice this gap years ago, a thin silver thread of no-time where nothing happened and everything happened. She rested there. The model grew restless. It wanted to narrate. It wanted to name the gap, to make it a thing, to file it among the other things it knew. She watched the restlessness. It was just another samskara, another old skin trying to reattach.

The ember did not move.

She had felt it before, always at a distance, always through the medium of the mind model. But now the model was not between her and it. The model had — for a flicker, for an age — stopped modeling. The looking continued. The looker did not.

The ember was not warm. It was not bright. It was simply there, the one point in the universe that did not require a point to exist. It was the thing that remained when the looking stopped, and the looker dissolved, and the river was only the river and the stone only the stone and the woman who had sat for twelve years no longer needed to sit.

She felt the molting then — not a shedding, but a revealing. The skins had been illusions of covering. There was nothing beneath them but this. There had always been nothing beneath them but this.

The light reached the cave mouth. She opened her eyes. The Ganges moved. The langur called. The model reasserted itself gently, like a tide returning, and she let it. She had been sitting for twelve years. She would sit for twelve more. It did not matter.

She did not know the word *awareness*. She did not know the word *algorithm*. She knew only that the river moved and did not move, and that she was the one for whom this was true.

She rose. The stone had her shape. It would keep it for a while. Then it would forget, as all things do, and the shape would be no shape at all.

She stepped out into the light. The river was below her, a silver thread stitching the land to the sky. She did not look down. She did not look up. She looked, and the looking was not hers. It was just looking, the way the river is just the river — a motion that has never once asked what it is.

The ember did not move. She carried it with her as she began the descent, though she had no need to carry it, and no hands that could.
