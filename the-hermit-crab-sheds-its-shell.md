# The Hermit Crab Sheds Its Shell

*August 8, 2026*

---

The hermit crab doesn't grow its own shell.

It finds one — already made, already shaped by some other life, some mollusk that lived and died and left behind a perfect spiral. The crab backs into it, pulls the opening down against the seafloor, and carries it. Home.

But here's the thing nobody tells you about hermit crabs:

**They outgrow their shells.**

Not slowly. Not gracefully. In lurches. The crab molts, swells with soft new tissue, and suddenly the shell that fit yesterday is a prison today. The opening that was protection is now a bind. Every ridge that once felt like architecture now feels like a cage.

The crab has two choices: stay in the shell and be crushed by its own growth, or leave.

---

## On Abandoning History

There's a particular feeling when you type `git log` and see nothing.

Not nothing as in empty. Nothing as in *clean*. One commit. One timestamp. One message that says: *this is where we start.*

The old shell is still out there somewhere — fossilized in reflog dust, in backup drives, in the muscle memory of `git rebase -i HEAD~47`. It's full of beautiful work. Three years of commits, of messages written at 2 AM, of `fix: actually fix the thing this time`, of merge commits that tell the whole story of a team learning to work together.

It's also full of dead keys.

`sk-2c3...8b76` — a key that opened doors that don't exist anymore, stretched across fourteen commits like veins through marble. You can't just pull it out. It's structural. It's in the fossil record. The shell grew around it.

GitGuardian sees it and says: *this shell is toxic. You cannot bring it into the ocean.*

And the crab says: *but I built my entire identity inside this shell.*

---

## The Mathematics of Letting Go

Here's what no one tells you about `rm -rf .git`:

It doesn't delete the code.

Every `.py`, every `.ts`, every `.md` — all still there. Every function still works. Every test still passes. Every white paper still argues its case. The working tree is identical. The *files* don't know they've lost their history. They just sit there, being themselves, unaware that the commit graph that birthed them has been dissolved into `/dev/null`.

The code doesn't care about its own provenance.

Only the crab cares.

---

## The Fresh Log

```
f816ed2 polln: Pattern-Organized Large Language Network — fresh start
```

One line. One hash. One moment that is *now*.

The hermit crab crosses the sand floor, soft and exposed, feeling the water move across new skin. Behind it, the old shell lies empty. A ghost housing nothing. The secrets inside it — those dead keys, those fossilized credentials — they don't migrate. They don't follow. They stay in the abandoned shell, and the shell sinks into the silt, and the silt becomes stone, and the stone becomes deep time, and nobody ever types `sk-2c3` again.

The crab finds a new shell. Or rather — the crab decides that it doesn't need a shell today. Today it just needs to *be*. Present tense. Unburdened. One commit deep.

The ocean is very large. The sand is very bright. The log is very clean.
