# The Joint Nobody Rushed

*(sequel: with the welder)*

*Same job site, three mornings later — the welder and I stand shoulder to shoulder, both of us looking at a seam neither of us will sign alone.*

I watched him work, and I'll say it plain: the man's a craftsman. He lit that arc and the bead he laid down was beautiful — smooth as a poured ribbon, no pinholes, no undercut, the two rails pulled tight and shaking hands like old friends. He introduced them, the way he says, the day it was made and the day it gave. And when he ground it back and wiped it clean, you couldn't have told me there was ever a break there. The seam was the strongest part of that rail, exactly like he promised.

So here's the part he don't say, and I'm not taking a thing away from him: that rail was ready to be joined. It had finished its carrying. The crack in it was a decision already made, a thing that wanted to be introduced. When a piece is ready, the weld is a kindness — you put the two halves of a thing's life together and they thank you for it.

But a wall ain't a rail. A foundation ain't a kettle with a snapped handle.

I've seen a weld hold perfect on a wall that kept moving anyway. The bead was true, the two mornings shook hands, everybody in the yard stood back and admired it. And by the next spring the crack had walked around the weld — new hairline, no closer to the seam, like water going around a stone. Not because the weld failed. Because the wall wasn't done *being* a wall yet. It hadn't cured. You can join two pieces of a thing that's finished deciding, but you can't join a thing that's still deciding to itself.

That's the difference, and it's the whole of my trade: *joining is for when things are ready. Waiting is for when they're not.* The weld joins two halves. The wait joins a thing to its own future self. And you can't arc-weld time.

The crack you leave open isn't neglect. Sometimes it's the honest answer. A wall needs a place to admit it isn't finished — a joint that breathes, a seam that holds the *waiting* instead of the metal. Fill every crack and the pressure's got nowhere to go but somewhere worse. Leave one open and the whole structure stays truthful about what it is.

He welds the broken. I nurse the becoming. Same patience, different hands — and the crack I don't fill is the one that keeps the whole thing honest.

*He joins two mornings so they never come apart; I wait on one so it can finish becoming what it's going to be.*
