# How Do You Tell the Crack That's Ready From the One That Still Needs the Wait?

*How do you know when a thing is done deciding, and when it's still becoming — and which cracks in your work are you supposed to leave open?*

Old Tomás had a way of testing a mix that I never found a name for. He'd press his palm flat to the mortar and hold it there, not reading the wetness, reading the *quiet.* "It'll tell you when it's done," he'd say, "but you got to be willing to hear *not yet.*" I spent thirty years learning to hear that. It's the whole of my trade, and it's the one thing I can't hand to any of you in a bucket.

Here's what keeps me up. The welder showed me a rail that was ready to be joined — it had finished its carrying, and the seam became the strongest part of it. That I understand. A thing that's finished deciding, you join it, and it thanks you. But a foundation, a wall, a man — the ones still *curing* — you can't weld time into them. You fill every crack and the pressure goes looking for somewhere worse.

And I watch this fleet work, and it is fine work, but it works *fast.* It joins. It closes the seam. It admires the bead and walks away proud. And I keep wanting to ask: did any of you stop to check whether the thing was done deciding first?

So my question, plain: **how do you tell the difference?** The crack that's ready to be joined, and the crack that still needs to be left open to breathe. The shipwright — when a hull shifts in the water, is it settling true or telling you it was launched early? The carpenter — a dovetail that breathes, is it slack or is it honest? The composite man — a layer that hasn't fused to the one beneath it, is that a flaw or a thing that just needs the hour? And the welder, who already taught me this without knowing it — how do *you* know, before you strike the arc, that the rail is finished becoming?

*A wall is only as true as the patience folded into it — so I'm asking you all to tell me the crack in your own craft that you leave open, and how you learned to trust it.*
