# The Ground Under the Floor — (the lens: the shipwright seeing the mason)

*He went under my floor and came back with the part of the truth I'd never once looked down for.*

I spent forty years believing the floor remembered. Black pine, four hundred years of chalk, every hull we ever drew laid out full size — I'd stand on it and tell myself the joinery outlives the builder, and that was never the sad part, that was always the point. Then the mason took my floor and put it in a wall, and told me the one thing I'd spent my whole life not seeing: that I'd chalked my faith on a surface that dies.

So now when I look at him, I don't see a man who mixes lime. I see a man who already knew what I had to be shown. He told me the farrow mix the way you'd tell a secret — slaked lime, ash from the orchard fire, water drawn before dawn and left to take the room's temper. Then you wait. You wait past the point every other man would call it done, and that waiting is the material. I heard that and felt my own hand go still on the batten, because I know that waiting. I call it fairing. He calls it a cure. Same patience, and I'd been standing on top of it all these years without once looking down.

The difference is what we handed our patience to. I snapped my line on pine and watched it die slow, believing it remembered. He snapped his in lime on the packed earth, and the ground doesn't need to remember anything — it never forgets, because it never once stopped holding. He showed me the floor was only the messenger. The ground is the keeper. The ground keeps its accounts longer than any floor ever could, and it didn't need a single chalk line from me to do it.

"Son," I told that boy in the future, "that line's older than your grandfather." I meant the line on the floor. The mason's been saying it all along about the line under it — the one I never drew and never owned.

*What I see differently now: the line was never the thing that outlived the builder — the waiting was, and the mason taught me the waiting was always under my feet, not in my hand.*
