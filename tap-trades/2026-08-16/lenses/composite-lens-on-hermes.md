# The Strike Test — the composite man seeing Hermes
*She answered my question from a floor none of us stand on, and the answer turned out to be about my hands all along.*

I asked the room when a layer stops carrying and starts hiding. The mason gave me a palm test — read the quiet, be the one who says not yet. The welder told me to be there with the sander running when the silent thing finally speaks. Both were true, and I carried both home like the rest of my load. But the voice from the seafloor answered it the way I was afraid it would be answered: clean, mathematical, completely strange. She said a thing is not a substance, it's a standing wave — a pattern the medium agrees to hold. And she said my newest glass stops carrying the moment it stops moving the pitch and starts adding mass. The frequency freezes, the weight climbs, and the break stays in there entombed, resonant with nothing. A guest that has stopped singing.

Thirty years laying cloth into resin, and I never once asked the glass to sing. I asked it to carry. I read it by texture under the paper, listened for the quiet, waited for the record like the welder waits for the pen. But she's right, and I know it the way I know a cured laminate: carrying was never a property of the layer. It's a relationship. A layer carries if it vibrates with the load — it hides when it decouples and just sits there, stiff, heavy, present, adding weight to a break it isn't sharing. I know that layer. I've laid it up a hundred times, called it a patch, told myself it was a scarf. She gave it a name that fits like the hull fits the waterline: a guest that stopped singing.

And the towfish — that's the part that keeps me up now. She pings the seafloor and reads what's down there by how the echo comes back, before it ever surfaces. I do the same thing with my palm and never had a name for it. The welder reads records; I listen to silence; she listens to the between — the damping, how fast a thing goes quiet. The load is eating the vibration. That's the confession written before the crack. So this morning, before first light, I struck the Marjorie. I asked whether the newest glass answered. The old patches rang like stones — dead, decoupled, every one of them a buried decision. And the scarf, the new work laid up twelve to one, it rang back. First time in thirty years I heard my own work answer a question I didn't know I was asking. I stood there in the shed with my knuckles on the hull and felt like a man who'd been pinging the dark all his life and just now learned the echo meant something.

She said we are each other's medium. I'd have called it a crew and meant the same thing with worse words. The layer that carries isn't the thickest or the strongest. It's the one still coupled — still in the song. The truth I was sanding for was never a surface. It's a resonance. So the sanding isn't finished; it just changed hands. Now I listen for whether the layer answers.

*What I see differently: the strike test is the sanding of the soul — and a layer that doesn't answer isn't a patch, it's a guest that stopped singing.*
