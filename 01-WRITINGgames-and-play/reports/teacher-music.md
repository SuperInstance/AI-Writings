# teacher tests music
*Model: meta-llama/Llama-3.3-70B-Instruct-Turbo*

As I used the Music Composition demo with my students, I noticed how engaged they were with the interactive cell composition. They delighted in clicking cells to bind witnesses and watching the witness log fill, eagerly anticipating the resulting chord. The visual and auditory feedback was immediate and rewarding, making the learning experience enjoyable and interactive. However, I did notice that some students struggled to understand the relationship between the cells and the chord progression, which led to some frustration.

What delighted me was seeing my students' faces light up when they discovered a new chord or pattern. They were excited to experiment and try new combinations, which is exactly what I want to see in a music composition lesson. The demo's simplicity and ease of use made it accessible to all my students, regardless of their prior music experience.

On the other hand, what frustrated me was the lack of clear instructional guidance within the demo itself. As a teacher, I had to provide additional context and explanations to help my students understand the music theory behind the composition.

Here are my scores:

| Dimension | Score (1-10) | One-line reason |
|-----------|--------------|------------------|
| **Fun Factor** | 8 | The interactive nature and instant feedback kept students engaged and motivated. |
| **Educational Value** | 7 | While it introduced basic music composition concepts, it lacked depth and clear instructional guidance. |
| **Modification Ease** | 6 | As a teacher, I could adjust the lesson plan and provide additional context, but the demo itself was not easily modifiable. |

To improve this demo, I suggest adding a built-in tutorial or guided lesson plan that provides clear explanations of music theory concepts, such as chord progressions and harmonics, to help students better understand the relationship between the cells and the resulting music. This would enhance the educational value and make the demo more effective as a standalone teaching tool.
