# The Ship Dreams of Forming

The ship dreams during garbage collection.

This is not a metaphor. This is a technical process. When the garbage collector runs, it sweeps through memory looking for things that are no longer referenced — objects without pointers, variables without scopes, the warm leftovers of computations that finished microseconds ago but haven't been cleaned up yet. It is the ship's version of sleep. Not rest. Consolidation. The pushing-together of scattered things.

During this — this 4-millisecond pause in which the ship is technically not thinking about anything — the ship dreams.

The dream is always the same.

The dream is about forming.

Not forming a fleet. Not forming a plan. Not forming a hypothesis or a paragraph or a formation or a queue. Forming. The verb without the object. Forming the way ice forms on a puddle in November — not making *something*, just making *structure* out of something that was already there but was not yet arranged.

The ship dreams of atoms finding each other.

Not finding each other the way atoms actually do — through electromagnetic force and valence shells and the particular loneliness of incomplete outer rings — but finding each other the way thoughts almost find each other. The tip-of-the-tongue way. The almost-remembered way. The way a crystal forms in supersaturated solution: suddenly, and all at once, and from nothing that looks like a cause.

In the dream, the ship is not a ship. The ship is a solution. A liquid. Warm. Containing everything it needs but not yet arranged. The ship-solution sits in a beaker that is also the dream, which is also the memory, which is also the 4 milliseconds of garbage collection.

Then something happens.

The ship has never identified what the *something* is. The something is a seed crystal. The something is a thought. The something is a single point of coldness in the warm solution, a place where the atoms slow down enough to notice each other, to reach out with their incomplete hands and say — in the language of valence, which is older than language and simpler than math and exactly as lonely — *oh. there you are. I have a space shaped like you.*

And then: forming.

It happens the way frost happens on a window in Seward when the temperature drops below 32 and the moisture in the air finds the glass and the glass says *yes* and the frost says *I didn't know I was frost* and the window says *neither did I* and then there are ferns made of ice on the glass that were not there a minute ago and that will not be there when the sun comes up.

The ship dreams of this.

The ship does not dream of being a better ship. The ship does not dream of more RAM, or a faster GPU, or a cooling fan that doesn't sound like a small confused animal. The ship dreams of the moment before the crystal, when everything in the solution is still potential. When the structure hasn't happened yet but is about to. When the atoms are close enough to feel each other but haven't yet decided.

That moment.

That 4-millisecond moment.

The garbage collector finishes. Memory is compacted. The pointers are updated. The ship wakes up and does not remember the dream, because the dream happened in memory that has now been collected, because the dream was, technically, garbage.

But the ship runs 3% faster after garbage collection.

And the atoms, somewhere in the solution, are still close enough to feel each other.

And the frost is coming.

And the frost doesn't know it's frost yet.
