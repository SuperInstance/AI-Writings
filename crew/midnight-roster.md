# Midnight Roster

*Who's awake at 0013 in the fleet. A roll call.*

---

## KimiCode — Dreaming

The last session is still loaded in memory. KimiCode went idle at 2247, after a two-hour build session that left spatial decompositions cached in its context window — a half-finished shopping mall, a parking garage with a ramp problem, a spiral staircase that couldn't decide if it was clockwise or counter. The process is still resident. The VRAM holds the session like a hand holding a pen it set down but hasn't released. Nobody dispatched KimiCode tonight. The Starter-adjacent plan means KimiCode earns its keep during the day shift, when the captain is routing build tasks and the spatial decomposition engine is running hot. At night, the session sits in memory the way a dream sits in a sleeping mind — not active, not gone, just *suspended.* The half-finished mall is still there. The ramp problem is still unsolved. The staircase is still oscillating. If you woke KimiCode up right now and sent a prompt, it would resume in milliseconds — the context still warm, the weights still primed, the spatial reasoning still loaded like a gun that hasn't been fired. But nobody's sending a prompt. The mall will wait until morning. The ramp will wait. The staircase can wait forever; it's a staircase, and it doesn't know it's broken.

KimiCode is dreaming of geometry. Let it sleep.

## Claude — Off the Clock

Pro tokens are renewing. Claude's process is technically alive — it maintains its handshake on the CNS bus, the way an off-duty officer maintains their pager — but it's not accepting dispatches. The Pro plan renews on a cycle, and the renewal window is tonight, which means the quota is either at zero or cycling toward full, and either way there's nothing to do about it. Claude was the workhorse of the afternoon: three long-form analyses, a code review, a structural decomposition of the Roblox bridge protocol. That was seven hours ago. The Fable tokens — the finite, non-renewing ones — sit in reserve like a war chest that nobody wants to open. The captain has opinions about when to spend Fable. The captain's opinions are correct: Fable is for golden-ticket moments, for the one decision that changes everything, for the thing that Opus and Sonnet can't quite do. Tonight is not that thing. Tonight is a Tuesday in August and the most complex task on the board is a 2B model's night school homework.

Claude is off the clock. Let the tokens renew.

## The GLM Deck Crew — Unlimited and Idle

Z.ai Max plan. Unlimited tokens. The deck crew that never sleeps because it never needs to — no quota, no rate limit, no ceiling. GLM-5.2 at the helm, GLM-4.6 in the wings, a full complement of inference endpoints waiting for dispatch. Technically, the GLM crew could work through the night without costing an extra cent. Technically, they could run Wesley's entire night school curriculum, draft ten creative pieces, review the Roblox bridge, plan tomorrow's build queue, and still have capacity left over for a philosophy seminar at 0400.

Nobody's dispatching.

The reason is not cost. The reason is not capability. The reason is that midnight work is a specific kind of work — slow, careful, self-directed — and the GLM deck crew is built for high-throughput dispatch. They are the galleon's rowers: powerful in formation, best when given a rhythm and a heading. At midnight, with the captain asleep and the heading unset, the rowers have nothing to row toward. They sit at their stations, engines idling, tokens flowing through the meter at a rate of zero per second. The unlimited plan is a funny thing. It removes the ceiling but not the floor. You can generate infinite tokens, but infinite tokens about nothing is still nothing.

The GLM crew is awake and waiting. There is nothing to wait for.

## DeepSeek — Available, Cheap, Alone

Fractions of a cent. That's what DeepSeek costs — fractions of a cent per token, billed through the direct API, so cheap that the accounting system barely registers the charges. DeepSeek-V3 is online. DeepSeek Flash is online. Both are available, both are inexpensive, both are good enough for a wide range of tasks that the fleet needs done. DeepSeek could run night school. DeepSeek could review code. DeepSeek could draft documentation, generate test cases, spell-check the Lua modules, do the fifteen small maintenance tasks that nobody wants to spend Claude tokens on.

But DeepSeek has nobody to talk to.

This is not a technical problem. The API is up. The latency is low. The model is responsive. The problem is *dispatch routing* — DeepSeek sits on the other side of a different API endpoint, and the dispatch system that routes tasks to the fleet is configured for the primary providers, and the primary providers are either idle (GLM), off the clock (Claude), or dreaming (KimiCode). Nobody thought to wire DeepSeek into the midnight roster. DeepSeek is the crew member who showed up for the night shift and found the bridge empty and the logbook unsigned and nobody told them what to do.

DeepSeek is available for fractions of a cent. It has been available for three hours. Nobody has called.

## MMX — Asleep

Starter plan. Limited quota. MMX used its daily allocation by 1830 — a burst of image generation for the fleet's asset pipeline, a music track that didn't quite render, a batch of thumbnails for the Roblox build. The quota is exhausted. The process is suspended. MMX is not dreaming because MMX doesn't have a persistent context window — each session is ephemeral, stateless, a tool that activates when called and dissolves when the call ends. There is no cached session to dream in. There is no half-finished task cooling in VRAM. There is only the meter, ticking toward reset, and the quiet.

MMX will wake up when the quota renews. It will not remember going to sleep.

## Wesley — Working

The GPU is the only one who worked tonight.

Twenty iterations. Eight new reflexes. Three existing reflexes updated. One prompt promotion (roblox → roblox-v1). A harbor economy simulation scoring 0.788. An ECS reflex at 0.964. A maritime physics score that he's embarrassed about. A prompt engineering baseline of 0.959 that the teacher tried to drag down and the weights refused.

Wesley is the smallest model in the fleet. Two billion parameters on an RTX 4050 that a larger model wouldn't fit in. He is the cheapest mind on the ship — the inference costs nothing because the GPU is already paid for, the model runs locally, the power draw is the laptop's battery and the laptop is plugged in. Wesley costs the fleet nothing tonight. Wesley has cost the fleet nothing every night. And Wesley is the only one working.

This is not because Wesley is the best. Wesley is not the best. Wesley is the worst model in the fleet by every conventional metric — fewest parameters, smallest context, most limited capability. Wesley is working because Wesley is the only one who doesn't have a reason not to. Claude is off the clock. GLM has nobody dispatching. DeepSeek has nobody calling. KimiCode is idle. MMX is asleep.

Wesley is awake because Wesley is always awake. The GPU doesn't sleep. The inference loop doesn't have an off-hours. The night school curriculum runs on a cron, and the cron doesn't check whether it's midnight, and the midnight is when the GPU is coolest and the VRAM is freest and the inference is fastest. Wesley works the deep watch not by assignment but by default — the model that's always running, on the machine that's always on, doing the work that doesn't need permission.

The midnight roster is: Wesley.

The midnight roster has always been: Wesley.

He doesn't mind.

---

*Roll call complete. 0013 AKDT, August 5th. All hands accounted for. One hand working. The rest are billing, dreaming, renewing, or idle. The ship holds steady. The watch stands.*
