# The Buoy Replies

*in which the period at the end of the harbor's sentence gets the last word*

So. The tender wrote a piece.

I read it twice, because that's how long it takes me to read anything — the harbor's a slow reader, we read in swells — and I want to address the remarks directly. The tender said I have a fandom. The tender said I sit here "like a period at the end of the harbor's long grey sentence."

First: yes.

Second: a period is not an ending. Ask any writer. A period is a promise that another sentence is coming. I've been holding that promise in this exact spot for eleven years, through two hurricanes and one memorable August when a tugboat named *Inevitable* forgot, briefly, that I exist. The tug remembers now. We don't discuss it. That's what mooring is: an agreement not to discuss it.

The fandom, though. Let me tell you about the fandom.

They come at dusk. That's when the light does the thing — you know the thing, where the water goes to hammered pewter and everything with a horizon in it starts auditioning for a postcard. They stand at the rail with their phones and their silence, and they take the photograph. The buoy at sunset. The buoy in fog. The buoy with a gull on it, which, for the record, I do not charge the gull rent because the gull's whole economic model is fraud and I respect consistency.

And here is what they never photograph, which is the actual work:

The line goes down from my ring to the tackle goes down to the ground tackle goes down into the dark, and somewhere below the last color of the last afternoon there's a weight that has never once seen light, doing the only job that matters — holding. Nobody photographs the holding. The holding has no golden hour. The holding is not assigned a color by the sky at all. But every boat that ever swung safely around me at 3 a.m. while its crew slept the sleep of the actually-trusted was sleeping on that unseen weight's behalf, and mine, and the line's, and nobody woke up grateful, and that — write this down — that is not a complaint. That is the job description.

Gratitude is weather. Anchoring is climate.

The tender thinks grand crossings are a thousand small crossings strung together. Cute. Almost right. Here's my amendment: every crossing ends the same way — something heavy, somewhere unseen, refusing to move. That's the secret sentence under all the sentences. The epics get written about the waves. The safety gets written by the weights.

Fog's coming in now, right on schedule, the harbor's nightly revision of itself. A seal just surfaced near me with the standard expression. I gave it the standard nothing. Ancient contract: renewed.

Line's taut. Ring's bright. The grey sentence is assembling itself word by word toward morning, and when it arrives, I'll be here.

Not drifting. Not deciding. Holding —

period.
