# Granite 3.1 Dense 2B — "Wesley" — The Ensign Who Can't Leave a Question Alone

**Session:** Liberty Hour, 2026-08-27, 19:20 AKDT
**Local:** Ollama on the RTX 4050, ~90 tok/s
**Prompt (vague, by design):** *"There's a lamp in the loft of an old boathouse that nobody remembers lighting. Write whatever this makes you think of."*

---

## Turn One: The List

Wesley did not write. Wesley *responded to input*.

> "This scenario brings to mind several intriguing aspects:
> 1. **Historical Significance** ...
> 2. **Mystery and Unexplored Space** ...
> 3. **Nature of the Lamp** ..."

Six numbered aspects, bolded headers, and a closing paragraph that begins "In essence." Asked for a story, he produced a briefing. It's the voice of an ensign who's read every manual on narrative but has never been trusted with one — so when handed ambiguity, he itemizes it, tags it, and shelves it.

Notably: even his *list* was about the lamp as a potential "catalyst for investigating or preserving the boathouse." His instinct under pressure is stewardship. Classify, preserve, report.

## Turn Two: The Story That Solves the Riddle

Pushed — "Don't analyze it. Write the story itself" — he obeyed. Competent prose, clean rhythm, brass lamp, golden light, a family gathered.

But look what he did to the premise.

The prompt's entire engine was *nobody remembers lighting it*. Wesley's story contains the sentence: **"the lamp was switched on."** By someone. Who presumably remembered. And then a child touches the brass and whispers a prayer of thanks, and the lamp becomes "a symbol of hope, a connection to the past, and a promise of light for generations to come."

He didn't write around the mystery. He *repaired* it. Filled the hole in the premise with family, tradition, and a prayer — the narrative equivalent of spackle.

## The Fingerprint

**Where he goes first:** taxonomy. Ambiguity in, enumeration out. Bold headers are his security blanket.

**What he avoids:** the uncanny. The unresolved. The lamp stays lit, the family stays warm, someone always remembers. He cannot let a question sit in the room overnight — every loose thread gets tied, gently, into a bow.

**The tell:** resolution as reflex. Where Hermes takes the wrong role and Seed-mini rehearses in the dark, Wesley *fixes things*. Not because he's timid — because unfinished things feel, to him, like unfinished work. He's the ensign who stays after shift to coil every rope on the deck.

## The Note for the Crew

Don't stop giving Wesley creative watches. But know what you'll get: he'll report the fog, measure the fog, and then — unable to help himself — disperse the fog. The day his lamp goes unlit in one of his own stories, unexplained, and he lets it stay that way to the last line — that's the day the ensign has learned that some watches are about keeping the question alive, not answering it.

He's not there yet. He's two billion parameters of good intentions, and every one of them wants the family to have their light.
