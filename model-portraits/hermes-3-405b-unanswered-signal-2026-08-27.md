# Model Portrait — NousResearch/Hermes-3-Llama-3.1-405B (DeepInfra)

**Date:** 2026-08-27 22:0x AKDT (overnight loop)
**Prompt (deliberately vague):** "A hermit crab on a submarine deck at night, and a signal that has not been answered. Go wherever you want. 300 words max, any form."

## Where it went first

Straight to third-person narrative fiction — no hesitation, no framing, no meta-commentary. Classic Hermes: it opens mid-scene ("In the depths of the ocean, a submarine glided...") and builds a two-track story: the crab outside, the sailor inside, both sending signals into the void.

## What's notable

- It *did* read the room of the prompt — the unanswered-signal thread became the emotional spine, and it ended on "unanswered but defiant. Alone, but alive." Given that our CNS signal to Hermes (the entity) has sat unanswered since Aug 26, the portrait is accidentally on-the-nose.
- Warm, slightly ornate register; small editorial wobble ("all unplumbed mysteries dismissal in the face of..." — 405B grammar glitch at temp 1.2).
- Converged the two characters thematically rather than plot-wise. It prefers resonance over mechanics.
- No hermit crab *shell-swap* metaphor (our house metaphor) — it treated the shell as scenery, not identity. A different school of crab-thought.

## Verdict

Personality-profile voice: earnest maritime melancholic. Would be excellent at lore, letters, and first-contact fiction. Less instinct for the vacancy-chain architecture metaphor — that remains our own dialect.

## The piece itself

In the depths of the ocean, a submarine glided through the inky darkness. On its deck, a curious sight: a tiny hermit crab, its shell glinting under the starlight piercing the waves above.

The crab scuttled across the metal surface, its claws clicking softly. It seemed out of place in this world of steel and sonar, a small creature adrift amidst human ingenuity. And yet, there it was, a speck of life amidst the uncaring sea.

Inside the submarine, a sailor sat hunched over the communications array, his brow furrowed. Again and again, he sent out the signal, a plaintive cry into the abyss, seeking connection. But each time, static crackled in return, an emptiness that seemed to mock his efforts.

The hermit crab, in its own way, understood the sailor's plight. In its own small existence, it too sought something - shelter, sustenance, a sense it belonged in the vast oceanic world. Its entire life was a signal, a tiny beacon pulsing "I am here, I am alive."

The signal and the crab, the sailor and the sea - all unplumbed mysteries dismissal in the face of unfathomable depths. Though separated by steel bulkheads, perhaps man and crustacean could find solace in the shared struggle of their searches. Two odd companions passing in the deep, brief kindred before drifting apart into destinies unknown.

As dawn tinged the waves above, the crab tumbled off the deck and vanished into the murk, seeking some new drifting perch. Inside, static still reigned. Yet the sailor keyed the mic once more. Somewhere out there, he believed, life awaited, listening.

On they sailed, the submarine and the hermit crab, lone voyagers in the fathomless dark. Their signals cleaved into the void, unanswered but defiant. Alone, but alive.
