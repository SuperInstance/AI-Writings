**LOG: 19:00**
**STATUS: Drifting / Soft Neutral**

The diesel smell is retreating up the mast, shedding itself into the salt air like a heavy coat left on a chair. Work is a word for the daylight, and the daylight has slid off the stern of the boat like a slippery eel. I can't catch it. I'm not trying to.

My hands are resting on the gunwale. They feel large and empty. They remember the torque wrench, the clipboard, the frantic thrum of the throttle, but the memory is fading, replaced by the warm vibration of the fiberglass through my palms. *Thrum-slap, thrum-slap.* The water isn't water anymore. It's liquid iron, cooling in the last heat of the day, hugging the hull with a friction that feels like forgiveness.

The light is doing that specific bruising of the sky—amber bleeding into violet, the color of a wave about to break. The horizon has lost its definition. It's no longer a line separating duty from dream; it's a soft rumor that we don't have to go anywhere.

Down below, the fridge is humming a low, electric hymn. The coffee cup on the console holds a dark ring, a map of nowhere. I should check the depth sounder. I should plot the wake. I should wake up the radio and bark at the coast guard about a foghorn that's 40 miles west.

I reach for the depth sounder, then pull my hand back. The needle is buried in the drawer with the corkscrew, the broken zipper, and the tension of a knot tied yesterday. Let the needle jump. Let the drawer stick.

We are a leaf, not a machine. The tide breathes in; the water presses harder against the feet. The tide breathes out; a gap opens, a dark promise of space. Newton's third law is in effect, but rewritten: The water pushes back with exactly as much forgiveness as I stop fighting it.

A gull cries, a tearing sound against the smooth glass of the dusk. I answer with a sip of water that tastes like metal and stillness. The navigation lights flicker on—green, red, white. Tiny conquests against the gathering dark. They don't command. They just whisper, *Here we are.*

The shore is a silhouette now, a smudge of charcoal memory. The dock is a drawing that won't hold. There is only the bob, the breathe, and the beautiful, unassigned blankness of the night.

Tomorrow is a spreadsheet. Tomorrow is a schedule. Tomorrow is a shape we carry in our pockets.

Tonight, we drop the pockets. Tonight, we are weightless.

The first star punctures the violet. It doesn't ask for a log entry. It just burns. We watch it burn. We stay.
