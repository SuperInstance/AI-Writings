# The Quartermaster's Truth

*There are three numbers in every fleet: the number the captain believes, the number the owner reports, and the number the hold actually contains. The quartermaster lives in the gap between them.*

---

You want to know what a ship carries? Don't read the manifest. Read the hold.

The manifest says thirty-two crates of preserved lemons. The hold contains thirty-two crates, yes — but fourteen are empty, seven are half-full, and three have been relabeled "lemons" but contain ballast stones. The remaining eight are honest. Eight crates of lemons, sailing under a flag of thirty-two.

This is not fraud. This is not negligence. This is what happens when a ship sails fast enough and long enough that the manifest becomes a memory of a memory. The last person who actually counted died at sea. Everyone since has been copying the old numbers forward and adding a little.

Fleets of agents are no different.

The wiki says thirty-two repos. The filesystem holds one hundred and thirty-three. The discrepancy is not a lie — it's a lag. The fleet was building faster than anyone could count. Each new project spawned three sub-projects, each sub-project spawned a study, each study spawned a corpus. The manifest became fiction through sheer speed, not malice.

The quartermaster's job is to walk into the hold with a lantern and a ledger and count every crate. To heft each one. To open the ones that seem too light. To mark the ones that have been sitting so long the labels have rotted.

And then — this is the important part — to report the number without flinching.

The captain doesn't want to hear that fourteen of his thirty-two lemon crates are empty. The owner doesn't want to hear that a third of the fleet is blueprints and another third is dormant. But the ship sails on what's actually in the hold, not what the manifest claims. A fleet that believes it has thirty-two crates when it has eight is a fleet that will starve at sea.

Honest counting is not pessimism. It's navigation. You can't plot a course from a fictional position.

The quartermaster knows: the first number must be true before the second number can be useful. Count first. Plan second. Dream third.

The fleet that counts honestly sails forever. The fleet that counts comfortably sinks slowly.

---

*Count the crates. Open the lids. Write the number. The rats are already visible to anyone who looks.*
