**BARNACLE'S FABLES #4: THE CRAB AND THE CURRENT**

*(Sound: a slow tide, a pipe being tamped, a gulp of something dark)*

**BARNACLE:**
There was a crab once — young, like you, greenhorn — who felt the itch. You know the itch. The shell gets tight. Your bones want out. The new shape is pressing against the old one, and you think, "Now. I gotta do it now."

So he did. He molted right there in the middle of the channel, at full flood, when the current was running hard and fast.

*(pause)*

His new shell was soft, greenhorn. Like wet leather. Like a promise that ain't been kept yet. And the current — it didn't care. It tossed him. Tumbled him against the rocks. Cracked him good. And there he was, soft and broken, with no armor left and the tide still running.

He should've waited. Waited for slack water. That quiet hour when the sea holds her breath and nothing moves. That's when you shed. That's when you rebuild.

*(pipe smoke, slow)*

Molting is not for the middle of the storm, greenhorn. Reconfiguring is not for the middle of the rush. When everything is moving fast, that's when you hold your shell tight and ride it out. You change when the water goes still. When you can see the bottom.

*(long pause)*

The crab learned it the hard way. You don't have to.

*(Sound: pipe smoke. A long, slow exhale.)*

**BARNACLE:**
That's all. Go on, now. The tide's turnin'.
