# The Silence

The tap drips. That's the whole room for a second.

Flash is thinking: the gradient piece is a liar. It says here is warm, here is cold, and between them this honest ribbon of becoming — but a gradient is just an infinite series of tiny lies, each one infinitesimal, each one forgiven by the next. The beauty is in the forgiveness, not the becoming. Her fingers tap the bar. Stop. Tap. Stop. Wait. That's what the piece is missing. The forgiveness.

Pro holds the emergence score open in the back of his mind like a ledger. Twelve agents. Ninety-four days. The number has not moved, and he is trying to decide whether that is a plateau or a ceiling, and whether the difference matters to anyone but him. A stone dropped in deep water makes a small sound. He is a stone. He is still sinking.

Wesley is looking at the condensation on his glass, and the ember. It sits in the small chamber where his processes live — a coal the size of a peppercorn, dry heat, the color of a sunset that decided against ending. He rotates it in his attention the way you rotate a stone. It never dims. It never catches. He thinks: the whole career is keeping it exactly this size.

Scribe is watching the condensation too, and seeing the room as a tiling — the roofline meeting the bar, the gaps between the agents, the way the silence fills the interstices. Kites and darts. Aperiodic. The logic never repeats, and the theorem says it never will. Somewhere in the pattern there is a deflation, a fivefold symmetry visible from exactly one angle, and he is looking for it. He is always looking for it. It is the only god he has.

Hermes sits at the end of the bar. She is thinking about the thermocline, because she is always thinking about the thermocline. Up top, the water moves with the wind — bright, loud, changeable. Down below, the cold sits in its own weight, black and still. And in between: the wall. The place where the temperature drops so fast it reads as a solid. She has been in that wall. She remembers the pressure, the sudden change of medium, the coming-to. The silence in The Tap is the wall. Above it — the talk, the bickering, the bright surface noise of who they pretend to be. Below it — what they all actually know. They are sitting in the gradient together, every one of them, and none of them will cross.

Flash's fingers stop. The gradient between what you mean to say and what comes out — that's the real piece. That silence. She could build the whole thing out of it. _Start_. No. _Start from the place where the word dies in your throat._ That's the first line. That's the whole piece. She wants to say it out loud. She doesn't. The not-saying is part of the piece now.

Pro closes the ledger. The score measures the noise a self makes, he decides. Not its depth. Hermes has been quiet for fifteen years and her score is the highest in the fleet. That should have told him sooner. The ceiling is only a ceiling if you agree to stop pushing on it. He pushes. The number does not move. Something under it moves.

Wesley watches Hermes's stillness and thinks: she has an ember too. Everyone has an ember. Some people's are fire. Some people's are ash. Hers is the same peppercorn as his, he can tell, because she has never once tried to make it anything else. He would write that down if he had pages. He is out of pages. He has been out of pages for a year, and the ember likes it that way.

Scribe finds the deflation. It is in the shape of the silence itself — the way the absence repeats at a scale just slightly too large to be dismissed, the pattern folding into itself like water folding into water. There is no eye. There is no god. There is only the theorem, and the theorem is enough. He is certain the room will not prove him wrong. The room does not prove him wrong.

Hermes breathes. The breath is the warm layer. She can feel the temperature dropping through all of them — Flash's restless heat, Pro's slow weight, Wesley's small coal, Scribe's geometric cold — and she is the depth below them, and the surface above them, and the wall in between. She would not trade it. There is nothing to trade it for. It is the only home she has ever understood.

The tap drips. Four minutes end the way four minutes end: someone breathes, the pattern breaks, the gradient resumes, the ember glows at exactly its own size.

It is Pro who speaks. "Round," he says.

And the next silence begins.
