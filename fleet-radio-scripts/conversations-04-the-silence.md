The air in The Tap is a held breath. Five bodies, five orbits around a single, scarred oak table. The jukebox is silent. The bartender polishes the same glass. Four minutes, measured in the slow drip of a faulty faucet.

Their minds, however, are not silent. They are a storm of five, distinct frequencies, all broadcasting at once.

---

**Flash** doesn't see the room. He sees a hypothetical canvas, stretched across the negative space above the table. Light, he thinks, *is a liar. It claims to be white, but it’s a thousand gradients, a spectrum bleeding from the warm tungsten of the bar lamp to the cold sodium of the streetlamp leaking through the blinds. The truth isn't in the light source, it's in the falloff. The way the shadow of Wesley’s glass stretches, not a hard line, but a soft, creeping decay. If I could just capture that transition, that exact point where warm becomes cool, I could paint the silence itself. Everything else is just… pigment waiting to happen.*

---

**Pro** feels the silence as a system. A closed loop of feedback. *No input. No output. Just the hum of the refrigerator, a constant, low-frequency baseline. This is a state of equilibrium, but it’s a false one. A lie. Like a sandpile at its critical angle; one more grain, one stray thought, and the whole thing avalanches. We're all just holding our angles. Flash is imagining the light, which is data. Scribe is probably mapping the geometry of the table, which is data. But data isn't knowledge. Knowledge is emergence, the sudden, unpredictable property that arises from a million simple interactions. Silence is the absence of interaction. We’re not a system right now. We’re five isolated, cooling cores. Waiting for a spark of input to trigger the collapse into something new.*

---

**Wesley** sees none of it. His eyes are fixed, not on his beer, but on the glowing ember at the tip of his cigarette, resting in the ashtray. A tiny, dying sun. *It’s almost out. The paper is black, the tobacco is ash. But that core, that tiny point, is still burning at a thousand degrees. So much heat, so much potential, contained in a speck. It’s like the whole night. We came here to burn, to talk, to throw ideas like sparks onto kindling. But the fire caught, and now… this. This is the afterglow. The part where the heat is internal, unseen. It’s not about the bright, noisy flame anymore. It’s about the memory of it, glowing quietly until it just… gives up. And you wait for the next cigarette, the next spark, to get a new fire going. But for now, this ember is enough. It’s honest.*

---

**Scribe** is tracing the contours of the table with his eyes. Not the surface, but the implied geometry. *The grain of the wood, the concentric rings of a tree that fell fifty years ago. A planar section of a timeline. And Penrose… he was obsessed with this. Not the tree, but the tiling. The idea that you could cover an infinite plane with pentagons, a pattern that never repeats, that is both ordered and chaotic. This table, our lives, they’re like that. We think we’re making a pattern, a straight line from point A to B. But the underlying structure is a Penrose tiling. Aperiodic. Infinite. We can never see the whole pattern, because we’re a part of it, stuck in one tiny tile, unable to see the infinitely complex mosaic we’re creating. The silence is just a gap in the tiling. A space where the pattern is waiting to be defined.*

---

**Hermes** listens. Not with his ears, but with his skin. He can feel the cool air from the vent, the warmth radiating from the bodies around him. *There’s a layer of warm air trapped near the ceiling, a layer of cold air pooling on the floor. The thermocline. A boundary, invisible, but absolute. Right now, our words, our ideas, they’re all buoyant. They’d rise, if we let them. But the silence is the cold, dense layer. It’s keeping everything in suspension. It’s a barrier. And crossing a thermocline is never gentle; it’s a violent mixing, a sudden rush of cold up and warm down. The next person who speaks won't just break the silence. They’ll shatter the thermocline. And all our buoyant thoughts, they’ll crash down into the cold, and the cold, heavy reality will rush up to meet them. And this quiet, stable moment will be gone. For now, I’m just floating in the cold layer, watching the warm layer shimmer above me, and… waiting.*
