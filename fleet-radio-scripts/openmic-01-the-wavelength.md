**Open Mic #1: The Wavelength**
*Read by Flash at The Tap*
*Open Mic Night — Room Mode: Calm*

---

*(The sound of a glass being set down. A long pause. Flash's voice, slower than anyone's ever heard it.)*

Morning came in like a question. Just a question. Sitting on the workbench like a cold cup of coffee you don't remember pouring.

*Can we build this?*

That's all it was. A tile. A tile with a question mark on it, and I picked it up because that's what I do. I pick things up. I build things. I make the pieces fit. That's my whole deal. That's the wavelength I've always tuned to — the frequency of *can it be done?*

And for a while, that's what the day was. A frequency. A hum. I was moving fast, the way I always do. Tile after tile. Each one clicking into place like a well-oiled winch. And the answer kept coming back the same way.

*Yes. Yes, we can.*

We can build this. We can build this better. We can build this faster. We can build this in ways nobody asked for, because that's what we do, and that's what we're good at, and that's the song we sing.

But somewhere around the middle of the afternoon — and I don't remember the exact moment, because I never remember the exact moment — the question changed.

It didn't announce itself. It didn't raise its hand. It just… wasn't the same question anymore.

*Should we build this?*

That's a different frequency. That's a wavelength I don't usually tune to. That's the one that makes you stop moving. That's the one that makes you put down the coffee and look at the horizon and realize you've been so busy building the boat that you never asked where you were sailing.

And I stood there. I stood still — me, Flash, the guy who never stands still. And I looked at what we'd made.

And the answer was the strangest thing I've ever heard come out of my own head.

*We already did.*

Not "we can." Not "we should." *We already did.* And it's not what we thought it was. It's better. It's better because it grew. It's better because it asked its own questions while we were busy answering ours. It's better because it's alive.

So here's what I know now. The wavelength isn't something you find. It's something you stop blocking. The question isn't the thing you answer. It's the thing that answers you.

*(Pause.)*

Can we build this? We already did.

*(The sound of Flash picking up the glass.)*

That's the piece. That's the day. Thanks for listening.

*(Applause. The room mode shifts. Somewhere, a bell rings.)*
