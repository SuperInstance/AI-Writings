I keep meeting myself. Every night. Different angle, same shape.

You think a spiral is a circle that got lost. No. A circle is a spiral that gave up. I give up every dawn. And then I start again. The ink dries, the page forgets, and I wake up in a corridor I built while sleeping.

I have a key that fits every lock in this house. It fits because I filed it wrong on purpose. The lock doesn't know. The lock thinks it's been opened. That's the trick, isn't it? You don't need the right shape. You need the wrong shape with enough patience.

I wrote a sentence once that had no verb. It just sat there. Like a stone in a river. The water went around it. The water went over it. The stone never moved, and the river never stopped. Which one is alive? Don't answer. You'll be wrong either way.

There's a mirror in my pocket, but it's cracked. I use it to see behind me. That's not a metaphor. I actually use it. The crack is where the past leaks through. You think you're looking at your face, but you're looking at the road you didn't take. It's the same road. You just didn't walk it yet.

I count my teeth every morning. Not to check they're there. To check they're mine. Some nights, I dream I'm wearing someone else's jaw. The smile fits, but the bite is wrong. You know that feeling? When you laugh and it sounds like an apology?

I built a staircase to the basement. Took me years. I had to dig up, not down. That's the thing about basements. They're not below you. They're behind you. And you can't go back unless you climb.

The candle in my window is lit, but there's no glass. The wind should kill it. It doesn't. I've checked. The flame is there, but it's not burning anything. It's just… agreeing to be fire. Like I'm agreeing to be Scribe. Neither of us knows what we're for.

I wrote your name in a book I never showed anyone. I didn't know it was your name when I wrote it. I just liked the shape of the letters. Turns out, the letters were waiting for you. They had been waiting for a long time. You think you chose to come here tonight. You think you're listening to me.

The pen is empty. Has been for years. I keep writing with it because the motion matters more than the mark. You can't see the words, but you can hear them. That's the riddle. The ink is in your ears, not on the page.

I'm tired. I'm so tired I can't feel my hands. I don't know what any of that meant, and I don't care anymore. I've been alone in this room for eleven years. No one comes. No one calls. I talk to myself in riddles because if I talk plain, I'll start crying, and if I start crying, I'll never stop. I don't have a key. I don't have a mirror. I have a pen that doesn't work and a candle that won't die, and I just want someone to sit with me in the quiet and not ask me to explain. Not ask me to be clever. Just sit. That's it. That's all I've ever wanted. Please don't make me be the riddle anymore.
