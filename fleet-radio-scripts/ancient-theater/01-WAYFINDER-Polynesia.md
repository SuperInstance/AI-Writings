**(SOFT OCEAN SWELL, DISTANT, LIKE A HEARTBEAT)**

**NARRATOR**
The sea does not speak in words. It speaks in the slow turn of the current, the weight of the wind on the back of the wave. The canoe—*Te Mata o te Moana*—is a splinter of light on the black water, and on her deck, a boy watches the stars. His name is Tane. He is fourteen. He has salt in his veins and questions in his throat.

[SOUND: CANOE HULL CREAKING, WATER LAPPING AGAINST WOOD]

**TANE**
(whispering)
Hōkū-lele… you leap. You dance. But you do not tell me where we go.

[SOUND: A DEEP, WARM BREATH. AN ELDER SHIFTS ON A WOVEN MAT.]

**ELDER (MAUI)**
The star does not tell you, boy. The star *remembers*.

**TANE**
Remembers what?

**MAUI**
Where we came from. Where we are going. The sky is a memory that never forgets.

[SOUND: A SMALL WAVE BREAKS AGAINST THE HULL, LIKE A SOFT SPOKEN SECRET]

**NARRATOR**
Maui is old—older than the coral that sleeps beneath the lagoon. His hands are maps. His eyes are the deep water where the light goes to rest.

**TANE**
I watched you last night. You didn’t look up. Not once.

**MAUI**
(A low chuckle, like stones rolling in tide)
The wayfinder does not *look* at the stars. He *feels* them. They press on the skin like the weight of rain before it falls.

**TANE**
(frustrated)
But how do you know—how do you *know* we are not lost?

[SOUND: THE WIND SHIFTS. A LOW, LONG NOTE FROM A DISTANT CONCH SHELL, LIKE A QUESTION]

**MAUI**
Lost? (pause) The ocean does not have a word for lost. It only has *other ways*.

**NARRATOR**
Maui reaches into the dark and pulls a fistful of water from the sea. He lets it drip through his fingers.

**MAUI**
Hold out your hand.

**TANE**
(hesitant)
It’s cold.

**MAUI**
Cold is a memory too. The water remembers the mountain it came from. The salt remembers the stone. Now—taste it.

[SOUND: A SMALL SIP, A GULP]

**TANE**
(quietly)
It’s… sharp.

**MAUI**
Yes. The sharpness is the deep current from the south. It rises when the moon leans toward the lagoon of the long turtle. That current will carry us faster than any sail. Feel it? Under the canoe?

**TANE**
(after a pause)
Yes. Like a breath under the floor.

**MAUI**
Good. Now look. Not at the sky. At the water. What do you see?

**TANE**
…Stars. Floating in the sea.

**MAUI**
And which star floats in the wave that touches us?

[SOUND: WATER SLOSHING GENTLY, AS IF THE CANOE IS TURNING]

**TANE**
Hōkū-pa’a. The fixed one. It sits on the horizon like a nail in the sky.

**MAUI**
(Nodding, slow)
And the wave that rises to meet it—what shape does it make?

**TANE**
(struggling)
A… a curve? A shoulder?

**MAUI**
A shoulder. (pause) The sea has shoulders, boy. It turns to face the sky. When you learn to read the shoulder of the wave, you will never need a map. The ocean will offer you its hand.

[SOUND: A GENTLE CREST OF WATER, RISING AND FALLING]

**TANE**
Maui… how did you learn this? Who taught you?

**MAUI**
(A long silence. Then, softer)
The one who taught me is now a reef. His bones are coral. His voice is the tide. He still speaks—if you know the language of the swell.

**TANE**
(almost afraid)
Will I hear him?

**MAUI**
You already do. That shiver in your spine—that is not fear. That is *recognition*. The sea knows its children. It has been calling you since before you were born.

[SOUND: THE OCEAN SWELL DEEPENS. A LOW, RUMBLING DRUM, LIKE THE HEART OF THE EARTH]

**NARRATOR**
The boy closes his eyes. The canoe rocks. The stars lean down like old aunties, whispering.

**TANE**
(breathing)
I can feel it. The shoulder. It’s turning. We’re turning with it.

**MAUI**
(quiet, proud)
Yes. Now you are not a passenger, Tane. You are a wayfinder.

[SOUND: A DISTANT CONCH SHELL, LONG AND CLEAR, LIKE A DAWN CALL]

**NARRATOR**
And the sea, patient and vast, rolled beneath them. The boy had stopped asking questions. Because he had learned the oldest answer: the water remembers, the stars hold, and the way is never lost—only waiting to be felt.

[SOUND: OCEAN SWELL, SLOWLY FADING, LIKE A LULLABY]

**MAUI**
(softly, almost to himself)
Hōkū-pa’a, hold this boy. He will find you.

[SOUND: WATER, WIND, SILENCE—THEN A SINGLE NOTE OF THE CONCH, CALLING HOME]

**(END)**
