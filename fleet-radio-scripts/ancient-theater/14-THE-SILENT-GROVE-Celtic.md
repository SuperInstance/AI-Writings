**(SOUND: A low, sustained wind through ancient oak leaves, a sound like the sea held in a tree. A single, distant raven calls. Underneath, the faint, plucked notes of a harp, playing a melody that is older than memory.)**

**NARRATOR:** (Voice low, slow, like mist rolling downhill) There is a place where the veil is thin. Not a place on any map, but a place the land remembers. They call it the Silent Grove. The oaks there are older than the stones, and the stones are older than the gods. The wind does not blow through it so much as it *breathes*. And if you listen closely… you can hear the earth thinking.

**(SOUND: Footsteps on damp, mossy earth. They are careful, hesitant. The harp fades to a single, sustained note.)**

**AILILL:** (A young man, breathless) The stories… I thought they were just stories. A grove where no bird sings. Where the air is thick as honey. But the silence… it’s not empty. It’s *full*.

**BRIGIT:** (A woman, sharper, but with an undercurrent of awe) Aye, full of what the old ones left behind. We should not be here, Ailill. My grandmother said this is a place of judgment. A place for kings and fools.

**AILILL:** Then I am a fool. For I have come to find the Druid. The one who walks between the worlds. They say he can speak to the roots of things.

**(SOUND: A single, resonant *plock* of a harp string, like a drop of water falling into a deep well.)**

**DRUID:** (Voice ancient, dry as rustling parchment, yet with a strange music) A fool indeed, to seek a root for a tree that has not yet grown. You bring a question, child of the morning. But do you bring the answer it demands?

**BRIGIT:** (Whispering to Ailill) He’s here. He’s behind the great oak.

**(SOUND: The wind rises sharply, then falls. A raven croaks, close by.)**

**AILILL:** (Firmly) I bring a question, Druid. My brother lies stricken. A wasting sickness that the healers cannot name. They say it is a curse, laid upon him by a rival clan. I need a remedy. An unmaking.

**DRUID:** (Chuckles, a sound like stones grinding) Unmaking. A strong word. You would unmake a curse with a curse. You would fight fire with fire and hope not to burn the forest? (A pause) The river does not fight the stone. It sings around it, and in a thousand years, the stone is song. Beth. The beginning is in the ending. Luis. The flame is in the ash. What you seek, seeker, is not a weapon. It is a key.

**BRIGIT:** A key to what?

**DRUID:** To the lock you have already turned. The blight on your brother is a mirror, boy. It shows the sickness in your own house. The bitter root you water with your silence. Nion. The ash tree binds the worlds. What has been broken in your family is not his flesh. It is your word.

**(SOUND: Water dripping on stone, a slow, relentless *tick… tick… tick…*)**

**AILILL:** (Stung) My word? I have given my word to save him. I swore I would do *anything*.

**DRUID:** (The voice is closer now, almost intimate) Anything. A grand oath. The wind makes many promises to the oak, and the oak holds fast. But a promise made in haste is a root in shallow soil. What have you *not* said, son of the morning? What truth lies buried in the grove of your heart, unspoken, rotting?

**(SOUND: A long silence. Only the wind. The harp plays a single, questioning note, and then stops.)**

**BRIGIT:** (Softly) Ailill… what did you do? Before your brother fell ill?

**AILILL:** (His voice breaks) I… I lied. I told our father that my brother had stolen the prize bull, that he had sold it to pay his own debts. It was me. I took it. I was jealous of his favor. I let him take the blame. The curse… it was my own shame, given shape.

**(SOUND: The raven cries out, a harsh, triumphant shriek. The wind gusts, shaking the oaks.)**

**DRUID:** (Raises his voice, chanting, the words weaving with the wind) Duir! The oak, the door! The truth is spoken, the wound is sore. The unmaking is not in herb or spell, but in the tale you fear to tell. Go back to your father. Go back to the hall. Speak the name of your own downfall. The remedy is bitter, but it is true. The grove has given its answer to you.

**BRIGIT:** (Awestruck) It’s… it’s that simple? Just tell the truth?

**DRUID:** (His voice is fading, as if receding into the bark of the trees) Simple as a stone. Hard as water. The cure is in the confession. The binding is in the breaking. The grove is silent, but its silence is a tale. (A final whisper) Fear. Foe. Friend. Find your end. And begin.

**(SOUND: A sudden, profound silence. The wind is gone. The harp is gone. Only the heavy, breathing quiet of the forest remains. Then, a single, beautiful bird song—a thrush, piercing the stillness.)**

**NARRATOR:** (Slow, like fog lifting) And so the young man turned from the grove, the weight of his lie heavier than before, yet lighter for the knowing. The Silent Grove had not been silent at all. It had been waiting. And in the heart of the forest, the old Druid, who was less a man than a memory, smiled, and became one with the roots of the oaks, holding the secret of the ages in his wooden heart.

**(SOUND: The distant harp returns, playing a slow, final, resolving chord. It fades to nothing.)**

**NARRATOR:** (A final breath) The wind in the oak. The raven on the wing. The water on the stone. The tale, at last, is told.

**(SOUND: A single, final *plock* of the harp string. Then, silence.)**

**[END OF EPISODE]**
