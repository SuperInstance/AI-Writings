**THE RELAY**

*A Radio Theater Episode*

---

**NARRATOR:** The wind does not rest on the steppe. It carries dust and news, and it never sleeps. In the year of the Tiger, under a sky the color of old bronze, the Great Khan’s word travels faster than any hawk. A thousand gers. A thousand hearths. And between them—a thread of muscle, leather, and iron will. The *yam*. The relay.

**[SOUND: Wind howling low, steady. A single horse snorts, hooves stamping, leather creaking.]**

**NARRATOR:** A rider waits at the post station. His name is Temur. His eyes are narrow from staring at horizons. His hands are raw from holding reins that never slacken. He has been here one hour. He will be gone in one breath.

**[SOUND: A distant horn—two notes, falling. The yam signal. Horse whinnies, eager.]**

**STATION MASTER:** *(gruff, quick)* Temur. The pouch. Blue seal. The Khan’s own hand.

**TEMUR:** *(taking it, voice flat, wind-scraped)* Weight?

**STATION MASTER:** A feather with a temper. West road. You know the crossings.

**TEMUR:** I know them.

**[SOUND: Leather creak as Temur mounts. Saddle settles. Hooves stamp once, twice.]**

**NARRATOR:** He does not say farewell. There is no room for farewells. The pouch is pressed to his chest, wrapped in felt, bound with a silk cord the color of dried blood. Somewhere ahead, the next station sleeps—or waits. They always wait.

**[SOUND: Hooves breaking into a gallop. Rhythm builds. Over it, the wind rises.]**

**NARRATOR:** Night comes like a closing fist. The moon is a sliver of bone. Temur’s horse, a gray mare with a scarred flank, knows the path by memory. They do not need stars. They need only the next post.

**[SOUND: Hoofbeats steady. Mare’s breath loud, rhythmic. A wolf howls in the distance—far, then fading.]**

**TEMUR:** *(low, to the horse)* Easy. Easy, girl. The river’s ahead.

**[SOUND: Water rushing. Hoofbeats slow to a canter, then a walk. Splashing, careful footing.]**

**NARRATOR:** The river is shallow this season. But the stones are slick. A misstep means a broken leg. A broken leg means a dead horse. A dead horse means a dead message. The Khan does not forgive delay.

**[SOUND: Hooves on gravel, then mud, then grass again. Temur exhales hard.]**

**TEMUR:** *(breathless)* Good. Good girl.

**[SOUND: Distant singing—a woman’s voice, low and long, a wordless song. A ger lamp glows ahead.]**

**NARRATOR:** The second station. A ger hunkered against the dark, its smoke-hole breathing thin silver into the sky. A boy runs out, half-dressed, a fresh horse already saddled—a chestnut stallion, head high, nostrils flaring.

**BOY:** *(young, eager)* Rider! Here! I have him ready!

**[SOUND: Hooves halt. Leather creaks as Temur dismounts hard, boots hitting dirt.]**

**TEMUR:** *(fast, clipped)* Pouch. Take it.

**BOY:** I… I am only the groom. The rider is—

**TEMUR:** *(cutting in)* Then you are the rider now. Take it.

**BOY:** *(hesitant, then firm)* I have never carried the seal.

**TEMUR:** *(already unstringing the pouch, holding it out)* There is a first time for every man. The road is clear. The mare is fast. Do not stop for anything. Not for a song. Not for a fire. Not for a dying man. You understand?

**BOY:** *(voice tight)* I understand.

**[SOUND: Pouch changing hands. Fabric rustling. A sharp intake of breath from the boy.]**

**TEMUR:** The horse knows the way. Trust him. Do not think. Ride.

**[SOUND: Boy mounts hastily. Saddle creak. Stallion stamps, eager.]**

**NARRATOR:** Temur watches the boy and the chestnut vanish into the dark. The hoofbeats fade, swallowed by the steppe. He does not pray. The Khan’s word is its own prayer.

**[SOUND: Wind. A single horse nickers nearby. The woman’s song continues, distant, mournful.]**

**NARRATOR:** The relay does not end. It only passes from hand to hand, heart to heart, hoof to hoof. Somewhere, in a tent of white felt, the Khan sleeps—or does not sleep. But his word moves. It moves like the wind. It moves like the wolf.

**[SOUND: Hoofbeats—faint at first, then growing, a new rhythm, faster, urgent.]**

**NARRATOR:** And behind the boy, behind the chestnut, behind the blue-sealed pouch that carries the fate of a thousand valleys—a new rider takes the saddle. A fresh horse. A new breath.

**[SOUND: The hoofbeats merge with the wind, building to a gallop, then fading.]**

**NARRATOR:** The relay never stops. The steppe is wide. The word is faster.

**[SOUND: The woman’s song ends on a long, held note. Then—silence. Then—a single, distant horn.]**

**NARRATOR:** The relay continues.

**[SOUND: Fade out.]**

---

**END OF EPISODE**
