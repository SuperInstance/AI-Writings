[SOUND: Low fire crackling, the murmur of a crowded mead-hall, wooden cups clunking, a distant hammer from a smithy beyond the walls. Wind howls faintly against the timbers.]

NARRATOR: (warm, gravelly, old-time radio delivery) The long hall of Jarl Einar stands against the night, its roof a spear-thrust against the storm. Inside, the benches are full. Warriors, wives, and thralls alike lean close, for tonight the old skald—grey-bearded, one-eyed, scarred by a hundred winters—has promised his last tale. The fire spits. The mead-horn passes. And the skald, Bjorn the Tongue-Tied, rises, leaning on his staff.

[SOUND: A heavy staff thunking on plank floor. The hall quiets to a hush.]

SKALD BJORN: (slow, resonant, alliterative)
Hear me, hall-dwellers, hearth-born heroes!
I, Bjorn, bone-weary, bring the last saga.
The whale-road widens, the wolf-wind whispers—
My tongue turns to timber, my tale must end.

[A murmur of protest from the crowd.]

WARRIOR 1: (gruff, young) Nay, skald! Sing of the sword-storm, the shield-wall’s shatter!

WARRIOR 2: (older, slurred) Aye! Give us the battle-sweat, the blood-eagle’s cry!

SKALD BJORN: (chuckles, then grows grave)
Blood-eagles? Bold words, but the blade is rusted.
I have sung of sea-kings, of serpent-prowed dragons,
Of gold-greedy jarls and the gods’ own thunder.
But the fire fades, friends, and the frost creeps in.

[SOUND: Wind gusts harder, rattling a shutter. A woman gasps softly.]

SKALD BJORN:
There was a time—teeth of winter—
When I stood on the strand, sword in hand,
And the sea-keel came, keening through the spray.
My lord, young Ragnar, red-bearded, laughing—
He gave me a harp, not a helm, that morning.
“Sing,” he said, “and the saga will save us.”

[SOUND: A single harp note, plucked, then a soft, mournful melody.]

SKALD BJORN:
We sailed the swan-road, sang through the storm,
Ragnar’s raiders, reckless as the raven’s flight.
We found the walled town, torch-lit, trembling.
I sang of their doom—the dragon’s hoard—
And Ragnar roared, and the roof-beams burned.

[SOUND: A distant crackle of fire, almost like an echo. The hall’s own hearth pops loudly.]

WARRIOR 1: (eager) Aye! The burning! Tell of the gold!

SKALD BJORN: (quietly)
The gold came easy. The grave is harder.
Ragnar fell at the ford, his face to the sky,
A spear through his throat, the tide turning red.
I held his head, hummed a half-song,
But the words were water, and the war-fog swallowed.

[SOUND: The harp melody stops abruptly. A pause, heavy.]

WARRIOR 2: (hushed) You sang no death-song for him?

SKALD BJORN:
I sang it silent, son of the ash.
Some sagas are stone—too heavy for song.
I built him a barrow, buried his blade,
And I swore to the sea that I’d sing no more glory.
But the hall calls, and the hearth remembers.

[SOUND: The smith’s hammer resumes, rhythmic, like a heartbeat.]

SKALD BJORN:
So hear me now, hearth-bound folk—
This is the last lay, the loose-tied knot.
No raid, no ransom, no ring-giver’s praise.
Only the truth, the troll-wife’s mirror:
We are dust, and the dragon of time
Devours the hoard, and the hall, and the harper.

[SOUND: The fire spits and dims. The hall is silent but for breathing.]

NARRATOR: And with that, Bjorn the Tongue-Tied set down his staff, and his harp, and his name. The Jarl rose, and no one spoke. The wind died, and the smith’s hammer fell still. And the hall, which had echoed with a thousand songs, was quiet as a grave-mound.

[SOUND: A single, long exhale. Then the faint, distant howl of a wolf, fading.]

NARRATOR: So ends the skald’s last saga. The mead-horn is empty. The fire is ash. And the whale-road waits for no man.

[SOUND: Fade out. A final, soft harp note, plucked once, and gone.]

NARRATOR: (softly) This has been THE SKALD’S LAST SAGA. Good night, travelers. Sleep well beneath the rafters.

[SOUND: Silence.]
