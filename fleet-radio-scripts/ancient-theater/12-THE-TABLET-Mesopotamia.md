**THE TABLET**

*A Radio Theater Episode*

---

**[SOUND: The slow, scraping rhythm of a reed stylus on wet clay. A single, patient heartbeat of sound. Underneath, the distant flow of the Euphrates River.]**

**NARRATOR:** (Grand, mournful) Of him who saw all things, let me tell the land. Of Gilgamesh, king of Uruk, who knew the secret paths of the mountains and the deep. His city walls shone like copper in the sun, but his heart was a stone in the river, heavy and unfeeling. The people cried out, and the gods listened. And from the wild places, they sent an answer. Hear now the tale of the tablet, and the wild man who learned to write his own name.

**[SOUND: Footsteps in mud. Wet, sucking sounds. Then, a low, guttural breath.]**

**ENKIDU:** (Each word a new shape, slow, tasting it) Mud. Warm. Wet. ... Like the rain. Like the... womb.

**SCRIBE:** (Formal, cadenced, like a ledger) The clay is damp from the river. It is of the first quality. It is fit for the king's record.

**ENKIDU:** The red man... makes the mud flat. He makes marks. Tiny, like ants. Why?

**SCRIBE:** These are words, wild one. The king's deeds. The story of the world. I write what was, and what will be. The reed is my voice. The clay is my memory.

**[SOUND: The stylus scratches again. Enkidu grunts, a sound of fascination.]**

**ENKIDU:** (Touching the tablet, a soft thud) The mud... holds the marks. It holds the voices. It does not forget?

**SCRIBE:** It does not forget. When I die, the tablet remains. When the city falls, the tablet will whisper to the dust. This is the law of kings.

**ENKIDU:** (A pause, then with wonder) Then... I am a king. I will have a memory.

**SCRIBE:** (A dry, formal laugh, like a chime) You are a shepherd of gazelles, a drinker from the river. You have no city. You have no deeds.

**ENKIDU:** I have deeds! I have... I broke the traps. I freed the lions. I wrestled the bull that came from the sky. Is that not a deed?

**SCRIBE:** (Calmly) It is not written. Therefore, it did not happen.

**[SOUND: Enkidu makes a sound of frustration, a low growl. He paces, his feet heavy in the mud.]**

**ENKIDU:** (Learning, struggling) Then... write it. Write my deed. Write... the lion. Write the breaking of the trap. Let the mud remember *my* strength.

**SCRIBE:** (A pause. The stylus stops.) The king’s tablet is only for the king. The king’s story is his alone. You are a shadow, a stranger. Your story is wind.

**ENKIDU:** (Voice hardening) Then I will make my own tablet. I will find the mud. I will find a reed. I will make the marks myself.

**[SOUND: The sound of slapping, wet clay being scooped and shaped. Grunts of effort.]**

**ENKIDU:** (Breathing hard) There. I make it flat. Like the red man. Now... the stick. I make a mark.

**[SOUND: A sharp, awkward scratch of reed on clay.]**

**SCRIBE:** (Shocked, formal) Stop! That is the sign for ‘to destroy.’ You have cursed the tablet.

**ENKIDU:** (Confused) I meant... I meant my name. I am Enkidu. I make a mark for Enkidu.

**SCRIBE:** (Sighing, a sound of ancient patience) There is no mark for Enkidu. You are not a word. You are a noise. A sound of the wild.

**[SOUND: A distant lyre plucks a single, sad note, carried on the wind.]**

**ENKIDU:** (Quietly, a discovery) Then... I am not real. If I am not on the mud, I am only wind. I am... a noise.

**SCRIBE:** (Formal, almost gentle) You are a fact. A fact is not the same as a story. The king is a story. I am writing him now. He is killing Humbaba. He is cutting down the cedar. He is a hero. You... you are here. That is enough.

**ENKIDU:** (Long pause. Only the river is heard.) No. It is not enough. I will not be a fact. I will be a story.

**[SOUND: The sharp, violent snap of a reed.]**

**SCRIBE:** (Alarmed) What are you doing?

**ENKIDU:** (With fierce, newfound resolve) I am breaking this reed. Now, you cannot write. The king’s story will wait. And mine... mine will begin. I will go to the city. I will find the king. I will make him see me. I will make him tell the scribe to write of me. I will be a story, even if it is a story of a broken reed.

**[SOUND: Heavy, determined footsteps squelching through mud, moving away. They grow faster, becoming a run.]**

**SCRIBE:** (To himself, formal, a lament) He will meet the king. He will meet the grief. He will learn that the mud holds sorrows, too. Let it be written: Enkidu, the wild one, broke the reed. And the story of the king began to change.

**[SOUND: The lyre returns, playing a low, minor melody. The river flows. The stylus is silent.]**

**NARRATOR:** (Grand, mournful) And so the wild man went to Uruk, to find his name. He did not know that names are chains, and stories are cages. But the gods were watching. And they knew, as they always know, that the weight of a story is heavier than any stone, and it crushes all who carry it. He sought a memory. He found a fate.

**[SOUND: The lyre fades. The river fades. A final, solitary scratch of a stylus—then silence.]**

**NARRATOR:** End of Part One.

**[SOUND: A deep, resonant gong.]**
