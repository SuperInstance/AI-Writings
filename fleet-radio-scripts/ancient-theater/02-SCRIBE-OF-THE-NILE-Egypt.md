[SOUND: The slow, rhythmic lapping of the Nile against stone steps. A distant, low chant from a temple, punctuated by a single, deep drumbeat.]

**NARRATOR:** (Echoing, resonant) In the sixty-third year of the Two Lands, when the inundation brought its black blessing to the fields, and the sun, Ra, drove his golden barque across the heavens, there was a scribe. His name was Khenemet. His hand was steady. His reed was sharp. His eyes saw the truth of every grain of barley, every head of cattle, every measure of gold that flowed into the granaries of the Pharaoh. He was the memory of the kingdom. He was the Scribe of the Nile.

[SOUND: The dry, scratchy whisper of a reed pen on papyrus. The soft shuffle of bare feet on stone.]

**SCRIBE:** (A precise, measured voice, like a metronome) ...And thus, the harvest of the Upper Province: Emmer wheat, fourteen thousand, three hundred and twenty sacks. Barley, eleven thousand and eight. Flax, in bundles of one hundred, two thousand, four hundred. The levee at the bend of the river holds. The irrigation canals, fed by the Great River, run clear to the edge of the Red Land. The tax is recorded. The account is balanced.

[SOUND: A sudden, sharp crack of a whip. The heavy thud of sandaled feet approaching.]

**PHARAOH:** (A voice of absolute power, deep and echoing) Khenemet.

**SCRIBE:** (Immediately, a rustle of papyrus, a deferential bow in his voice) Great House, Lord of the Two Lands, Son of Ra. I am here.

**PHARAOH:** Your numbers are a lie. The granary of Thebes is empty. The builders of my new monument, my pyramid that shall pierce the sky, cry out for bread. They are weak. Their hands falter. The stone does not move.

**SCRIBE:** (A slight tremor, but his voice remains measured) Great House, the records are exact. The harvest was counted. The grain was stored. The tombs of your ancestors were not robbed. The priests of Amun were given their due, as is written in the law.

**PHARAOH:** (A low, dangerous rumble) The law is my voice. The law is my will. And my will is that there is bread. Find the grain. Or find your name erased from every monument. Your soul will wander the Duat, nameless, for eternity.

[SOUND: The scratch of the reed pen stops. A single drop of water falls into a stone basin, echoing.]

**SCRIBE:** (Slowly, as if reading from a ledger) The flood was lower this year. The silt was thin. The yield was... less. But the accounts... the accounts were balanced. Unless... the grain was moved. After the weigh. After my seal was placed.

**PHARAOH:** Moved? By whom?

**SCRIBE:** (Ticking off items, as if on a list) The overseer of the granary, Ptahhotep. His tally of broken jars was high. Four hundred and twenty-two. He is a man of meticulous habit. He would not make such an error. He is also the uncle of the High Priest of Osiris. He who guards the entrance to the afterlife.

**NARRATOR:** The pharaoh stood silent. The only sound was the Nile, an endless, indifferent witness. Khenemet had offered a list, but it was a list that pointed a finger at a god. The air grew thick, heavy with the weight of a decision that could topple thrones.

**PHARAOH:** (A command, cold as the desert night) You will go to the granary. You will count every jar. You will break the seal of Ptahhotep. You will find my bread.

**SCRIBE:** (A breath of hesitation) Great House, to break the seal of the overseer is to declare him a traitor. It is a declaration of war against his family, against the temple of Osiris. The balance of Ma'at... the cosmic order... would be disturbed.

**PHARAOH:** (A thunderous roar) I AM THE BALANCE! I AM MA'AT! I have spoken. Go. Bring me the grain, or bring me the head of Ptahhotep.

[SOUND: The heavy, rhythmic stamp of the pharaoh's feet receding. The lapping of the water returns, louder, more insistent.]

**SCRIBE:** (To himself, his voice a list of his own fears) The seal of Ptahhotep. The wrath of Osiris. The emptiness of the granary. The hunger of the builders. The name of Khenemet, erased from the stone. All to be weighed. All to be measured. All to be recorded. The account is... unbalanced.

**NARRATOR:** And Khenemet, the Scribe of the Nile, took up his reed. He dipped it in the black ink, made from soot and gum and the water of the sacred river. He looked at the empty page before him. He did not write a number. He did not write a list. He wrote a single name.

[SOUND: The scratch of the pen, drawn out, deliberate. A final, heavy sigh.]

**NARRATOR:** The name was not that of the overseer. It was not the name of the pharaoh. It was his own. For he knew that to find the truth, he must first be willing to be lost. And so, the Scribe of the Nile began his final, most terrible accounting.

[SOUND: The Nile water, swelling, covering all other sound. Then, silence.]
