[SOUND: A single huehuetl drum, slow and solemn, like a heartbeat in the dark. A distant conch shell horn, long and low. A low, continuous volcano rumble beneath.]

**NARRATOR:** In the Valley of Anahuac, where the sky is a stone and the earth a sleeping beast, there lived a daykeeper called Teoxi. He knew the count of days. He knew the weight of each hour, the appetite of each sign. He could taste the rain before it came, and smell the gold in the mountain. But this morning, when he woke, the air was wrong. The air was silent. The obsidian mirror at his bedside was dark, like a dead eye. No reflection. No sign.

[SOUND: A sharp, brittle crack — obsidian splintering.]

**TEOXI:** (whispering, breathless) Nahui Ollin… no. No. Where is your face? Where is your mark?

**NARRATOR:** He walked to the temple steps, his feet numb, his heart a hollow gourd. The people were already gathered below, waiting for the day’s name, the day’s fate. They saw his face, and their murmur died like a fire without breath.

[SOUND: The crowd’s murmur fading to absolute silence. A single dog barks, then stops.]

**TEOXI:** (to the crowd, voice strained) I have read the sky. I have counted the stones of the sun. But brothers, sisters… the count is broken. There is no sign today. The day has come without a name.

**WOMAN IN CROWD:** (fearful) What does that mean, daykeeper? Do we live? Do we die?

**TEOXI:** It means… we are standing at the edge of the world, and something has forgotten to turn the wheel.

[SOUND: A low, guttural rumble from the volcano, closer now. A nervous shuffle of feet.]

**OLD MAN:** (cracking voice) Teoxi, you have spoken to the gods. You have fed the obsidian. Speak to the day-signs themselves. Ask them where they hide.

**NARRATOR:** And so Teoxi closed his eyes. He let his breath become smoke — curling, shifting, revealing. He called to the Thirteen Heavens and the Nine Underworlds. He called to the sign of the crocodile, who is patient and old.

**TEOXI:** (chanting, rhythmic) Cipactli, you who swim in the first sea, where is your tooth?

[SOUND: A soft splash of water, then silence.]

**TEOXI:** He is silent. He has forgotten his hunger.

**NARRATOR:** He called to the jaguar, the lord of the dark.

**TEOXI:** Ocelotl, you who drinks the night, where is your claw?

[SOUND: A deep feline growl, but distant, fading, as if swallowed by fog.]

**TEOXI:** She does not answer. She has let her fire burn out.

**NARRATOR:** He called to the monkey, the dancer of the trees.

**TEOXI:** Ozomatli, you who laughs at death, where is your song?

[SOUND: A single, hollow whistle, then nothing.]

**TEOXI:** (voice breaking) They have all gone. They are not lost. They are hidden. Something has stolen their names.

[SOUND: The volcano rumbles louder. The ground shakes. A child cries.]

**CHILD:** (small voice) Daykeeper… did we do something wrong?

**NARRATOR:** Teoxi looked at the child. And in that small face, he saw a flicker — not of a sign, but of something older. A memory. A story. The time before the signs were born, when the gods themselves were hungry and the days were just raw stone.

**TEOXI:** (softening) No, little one. Not wrong. We forgot something. We forgot that a day is not given. It is made.

**WOMAN IN CROWD:** What must we make, Teoxi?

**NARRATOR:** Teoxi lifted his hands. He looked at the obsidian mirror — still dark, but now it seemed to breathe. He understood. The day-signs were not above them. They were within them. The count was not a wheel—it was a heart.

**TEOXI:** (stronger now) We will make the day together. You—bring the copal smoke. You—bring the drum. And you—bring the fire from the last ember of the last night. We will not wait for a sign. We will become the sign.

[SOUND: The conch shell horn, but this time — urgent, rising, alive. The huehuetl drum picks up a faster, joyful rhythm.]

**NARRATOR:** And so the people of the city did as their daykeeper sang. They danced. They burned the copal. They beat the drum until their hands bled. And Teoxi stood at the center, his arms wide, his heart open, and he spoke the name that had never been spoken before.

**TEOXI:** (loud, triumphant) Today is the day without a sign! Today is the day of our own making! I name it — YOLOTL! The heart! For we are the heart, and we will not break!

[SOUND: A great burst of wind. The volcano rumbles — but not in anger, in recognition. The obsidian mirror shatters into a thousand glittering points.]

**NARRATOR:** And when the sun rose, it rose differently. The sky was no longer a stone. It was a drum. And the day — the unnamed, unclaimed day — had a face. It was the face of the people, looking back at the heavens, unafraid.

[SOUND: The drum fades into a single, resonant heartbeat. Then silence.]

**NARRATOR:** Some say the day-signs returned the next morning, shy and curious, like children who had wandered too far. But Teoxi never forgot the day without a sign. He carved it into his own chest with a piece of obsidian, so that his heart would always remember the day the people made light out of nothing.

[SOUND: A soft, final drumbeat. Then the volcano rumbles, low and gentle, like a sleeping giant sighing.]

**NARRATOR:** In the Valley of Anahuac, the day without a sign is still taught to the children. They are told: when the gods are silent, you must sing. When the sky is empty, you must fill it with your own blood and breath. For the count of days is not a prison. It is a promise.

[SOUND: Long fade. The conch horn, one last time, distant and warm.]

**NARRATOR:** Tlazohcamati. Thank you. The day is yours.

[SOUND: Silence. End.]
