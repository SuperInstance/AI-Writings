**THE SONGLINE**

**A Radio Theatre Production**

---

**[SOUND: The deep, primal drone of a didgeridoo. It is not a melody, but a heartbeat. Beneath it, the whisper of an ancient wind skimming across vast, empty plains. A single pair of feet, bare, landing softly on packed red earth, in a steady, deliberate rhythm.]**

**NARRATOR:** (A voice like sun-baked stone, wise and patient) Out here, the sky is not a ceiling. It is a story, stretched thin and blue, waiting to be read. The earth is not dirt. It is a library of memory, pressed into ridges and creek beds. And the path... the path is not a line on a map. It is a song. It has always been a song. And on this morning, under a sun that has witnessed a million mornings, an old man walks it. His name is Wati. His feet know the chorus.

**[SOUND: Footsteps continue. They are the only counterpoint to the didgeridoo's drone.]**

**WATI:** (His voice is gravel and wind, each phrase a footfall)
The sun is up.
It climbs the same hill it climbed
for my grandfather's grandfather.
The spinifex is sharp.
It remembers the first fire.
The crow calls.

**[SOUND: A single, harsh caw from a crow, distant.]**

**WATI:** (Continuing, without pause)
The crow calls for the one
who is coming.
The one who walks
with heavy feet and a louder heart.
He is young.
He thinks the song is a map of places.
He does not yet know
it is a map of time.

**[SOUND: A second pair of footsteps, younger, faster, less certain. They crunch over the earth, breaking twigs, disturbing stones. They stop abruptly.]**

**JARRAH:** (A younger voice, breathless, slightly impatient) Wati! Wait. I have been following your tracks for an hour. The elders in the community said you walk the songline today. They said I should come. To learn.

**[SOUND: The older footsteps slow, but do not stop. The didgeridoo drone deepens, grows more resonant.]**

**WATI:** (Walking)
Learning is not standing still.
The red dust is on your boots.
It is not on my feet.
The dust and I are old friends.
We do not need shoes to speak.

**JARRAH:** (A little frustrated) I want to know the path. The waterholes. The sacred sites. Show me the map. I have a GPS. I can mark it for the others. So we don't get lost.

**[SOUND: The older footsteps pause. The drone stops abruptly. There is a profound, almost deafening silence, filled only by the wind.]**

**WATI:** (Softly, with great weight)
Lost?
You cannot get lost, boy.
The land is not a maze.
It is a body.
We are its heartbeat.
You are not lost.
You are simply not listening.

**[SOUND: A single, sharp crack of a clapstick. Then another. A slow, deliberate rhythm begins, like a pulse.]**

**WATI:** (The rhythm of his speech now matches the clapsticks, a new song)
The waterhole at Kalparu
does not exist because rain filled it.
It exists because the Rainbow Serpent
sang it into being.
Her throat was dry.
She dug deep with her tail.
The water rose up to meet her song.

**JARRAH:** (Skeptical) That's a story. A myth. The water is there because of the water table. Geology.

**WATI:** (Laughing, a dry, rustling sound)
Geology!
A big word for a small way of seeing.
Your science tells you *how*.
Our song tells you *why*.
The Serpent is not a myth, boy.
She is the memory of the water.
She is the reason the water remembers to be here.
When we sing her, we remind the water to stay.
We sing the land awake.

**[SOUND: The clapsticks stop. The didgeridoo returns, low and mournful. The footsteps resume, both pairs now, in sync.]**

**JARRAH:** (After a long pause, quieter) What if we forget the song? What if we stop singing?

**[SOUND: The wind picks up. It howls, not in anger, but in loneliness. The footsteps halt.]**

**WATI:** (His voice is now barely a whisper, yet it carries immense power)
Then the water forgets to rise.
The Serpent sleeps too deep.
The mountain forgets its name
and crumbles.
The stars lose their paths
and fall.
The world...
the world becomes a blank page.
And we are the ink that has run dry.

**[SOUND: A long, resonant pause. The didgeridoo drone fades to a bare hum.]**

**WATI:** (He takes a step. Then another. His voice becomes a gentle, resilient chant)
But we will not forget.
The song is in my blood.
It is in the earth beneath your boots.
It is in the crow, the wind, the sun.
You carry it too, boy.
Even when you do not sing it,
it hums inside you.
That is the songline.
It is not a path to walk.
It is a truth to live.

**JARRAH:** (His voice is now humble, awed) Show me. Teach me the first words.

**[SOUND: The clapsticks begin again, softer this time. The younger footsteps fall into the rhythm of the elder. They walk together.]**

**WATI:** (The rhythm of walking returns to his speech)
The sun is up.
It climbs the same hill...

**JARRAH:** (Joining, hesitant at first, then stronger)
It climbs the same hill...

**WATI:**
...it climbed for my grandfather's grandfather.

**JARRAH:**
...for my grandfather's grandfather.

**[SOUND: The didgeridoo swells, full and warm. The wind becomes a gentle sigh. The footsteps are now perfectly in unison, a single heartbeat on the ancient earth.]**

**NARRATOR:** And so, the song continues. It is not a map. It is not a myth. It is a lifeline, thrown across the centuries. And as long as there is a voice to sing it and feet to walk it, the land will never be silent. The songline will hold.

**[SOUND: The didgeridoo and clapsticks fade slowly into the wind. The footsteps continue, growing fainter and fainter, until only the wind remains. Then silence.]**

**[END OF EPISODE]**
