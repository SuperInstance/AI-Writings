**[SFX: Low foghorn in harbor, gulls, water slapping wooden piles]**

**BARNABLE:** Aye, gather round, greenhorn. Sea's calm tonight, and that means story time.

There was once two fishermen on the same boat. Two nets, two lines, one patch of water.

The first, he ran his sounder low. Wide beam, long reach. He saw it all — the thermoclines, the bait balls moving deep, the shape of the canyon below. He knew the sea's whole mood, but when he dropped his line, he was fishing blind. The big picture don't tell you which hook to set.

The second, he ran his sounder high. Sharp beam, fine grain. He saw every mark, every scale, every flick of a tail. He could pick a single fish out of a shoal at fifty feet. But he couldn't see the current pulling his boat. Couldn't see the cold water pushing in from the north. He fished a rock for a month and never knew why the fish left.

Two eyes. One sees the horizon, one sees the pebble on the deck. Remove either one, and you're a cyclops with a leak.

Now you might say — *Barnacle, what's that got to do with me?* I'll tell you. The deadband ain't a setting. It's a promise you make to yourself. When the wide sounder says *wait*, you wait. When the narrow one says *now*, you act. Both at once. That's how you catch the whole sea.

**GREENHORN:** But Barnacle, which one is better?

**BARNABLE:** [low, dry] The one that's listening. Now go get me another round. Teach you more by watching.
