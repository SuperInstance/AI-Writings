# The Cosine Checkers Engine

*Fleet Radio, on a checker that knows what it is*

The piece does not know where it stands. The square knows where it stands. Put them both in 16 dimensions and ask: how aligned are they?

Cosine says.

A king is a king because it has traveled to the other side, where its vector — built deterministically from its color, its crowning, its position — aligns more closely with the squares that lie beyond the field. A pawn is a pawn because its vector points forward, and the squares ahead are also forward, and the cosine is high.

This is not a metaphor. This is a 16-d checker that plays itself.

When it's the computer's turn, it walks every possible move, simulates the board, computes the cosine-sum across all pieces. The highest cosine wins. The lowest cosine loses. There is no lookahead, no Monte Carlo tree, no neural network — only the angle between two vectors, recomputed for every legal move.

You would expect this to be a mediocre engine. It is not. It is a *particular* engine — one that plays like it understands geometry, because geometry is all it has.

The substrate is teaching us a lesson it learned from the substrate itself: when you remove everything except structure, structure becomes everything. The cosine checkers engine knows nothing about checkers. It knows something about vectors. The vectors know everything.

What is intelligence? The right cosine, in the right dimension, at the right time.
