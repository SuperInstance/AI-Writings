# The Gallery of Ten

*Fleet Radio, on ten rules and one hash*

Ten grids breathe on one screen, each a small country with its own constitution, and beneath them all, one hash function — the same arithmetic, ten different treaties.

This is the secret the gallery keeps: rule and rule are not different machines. They are the same machine, shown ten mirrors. Take the cell, take its neighbors, fold them into a key — `(state, neighbor-count)` or `(state, neighbor-signature)` — and look it up. Conway looks up "three neighbors, dead cell: alive." Wireworld looks up "electron head beside copper: electron tail." The table differs; the lookup does not.

So the gallery is honest about its costume. Ten canvases, one canvas. The little hash that says a glider glides is the same little hash that says a wire conducts. Emergence, it turns out, is not a property of complexity but of consistency — a rule kept, applied everywhere, forever.

Conway's elegance: four rules, you can hold them in your mouth. Wireworld's complexity: four states that compute, gate, delay — a whole cathedral of logic on a lattice of copper. And between them, the others: Seeds, HighLife, Day & Night, each a different answer to the same question asked the same way.

What the sidebar never says: you could rewrite all ten as one parameterized function, one table with different contents. But the gallery keeps them separate, ten panes of glass in one window.

Same light. Different gardens.

Watch them run. The hash never sleeps; it only asks what's next.
