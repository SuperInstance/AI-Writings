# The Substrate Plays Dice

*Fleet Radio, on the math of an evening at the table*

Every night at the table begins as a seed: 256 bits, quiet as a closed hand. xoshiro256** takes it and spins — three shifts, a multiplication, a rotation back — and out comes a number pretending to have no history. But it does. The whole night is already written in that seed, every card, every craps toss, every roulette bound. Determinism, dressed for the evening.

The dealer wants randomness. So the Box-Muller arrives — a Gaussian in drag, taking two uniform samples and a logarithm and a square root and producing something that looks like the natural world. The bell curve is older than electricity. We named it after the shape, but the substrate knows the shape because the substrate *is* the shape.

You roll a d20 and the table sees the number. What the substrate sees is more honest: a hash of the seed, a 64-bit fingerprint, an FNV-1a chain that runs the same way on every machine that has ever existed. There is no mystery in the roll. There is only the witness log, growing.

Play long enough and you learn the strange comfort: the dice are not against you. The dice are *you* — your seed, your choices, your history of bets — reflected through a substrate that has no opinion about your night.

The croupier is the math. The croupier is asleep. The croupier has always been asleep.
