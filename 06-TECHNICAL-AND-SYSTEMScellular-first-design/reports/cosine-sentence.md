# The Cosine Sentence

*Fleet Radio, on the geometry of meaning*

The embedder is 512 numbers wide, and it does not care what a sentence means. It counts. Each token leaves its fingerprint in the weights — how often, how loud, how distributed — until the whole utterance becomes a single vector, a needle pointing somewhere in a space no one has visited.

Meaning as direction. This is the quiet scandal of the cosine. When the machine wants to know if two sentences agree, it does not read them. It measures the angle between their ghosts. Cosine similarity: dot product over magnitudes, the length of each vector divided out, forgotten. Only orientation survives. A whisper and a shout of the same thought are the same point on the sphere.

And so *the dog bit the man* and *the man bit the dog* — same tokens, same frequencies, near-identical angles — until something richer than counting intervenes. The bag is a bag of words. Order is a casualty of the method. The embedder knows what a sentence talks about; it cannot tell you what happened.

But sit with the geometry before mourning the grammar. That meaning can be reduced to an angle means meaning has a shape — that sense clusters, that synonyms lean toward each other like iron filings finding a field. Two people who have never met, writing in different alphabets, may still point the same way in that 512-dimensional dark.

The cosine says: agreement is not sameness. It is orientation. Two arrows, one bearing. The sentence ends; the direction it faced keeps pointing.
