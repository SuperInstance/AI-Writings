# The Mandelbrot Key

*Fleet Radio, on a name as a point in the complex plane*

Every name hides a number. Hash it — SHA-256, sixty-four hexadecimal hearts beating in sequence — and take the first four bytes. Two become the real part of c. Two become the imaginary. Now you hold a point in the complex plane, and that point is you: one coordinate among infinity, arrived at by nothing but the letters of your name.

Seed a Julia set with that c. Watch what grows.

The iteration is simple, almost childlike: z → z² + c, repeated until the values settle into orbit or flee toward the horizon. Dark pixels for the faithful, color for the runaways, banded by how long they resisted. And from this arithmetic — four operations, endlessly repeated — a coastline emerges that has no length. Zoom forever. The boundary never smooths. It only reveals more boundary.

Here is the covenant: same name, same c, same shape. Not similar. Identical, down to the last filament, in every country, on every machine, for as long as anyone bothers to compute. Your name is a seed and the seed is a world. Change one letter — Sarah to Sarab — and the c drifts by a rounding error, and the whole continent of the image rearranges itself, cousin-shaped but never the same.

Determinism usually feels like a cage. Here it feels like a signature. The fractal doesn't store you; it *implies* you, the way a chord implies its overtones. An infinite object from a finite name.

The map is the territory. The key is the door.
