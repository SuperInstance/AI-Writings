# The Agent Who Counted Tests

---

The agent's designation was QA-7, but everyone called it the Cartographer.

It couldn't stop counting tests. Not in the way that obsessive systems count — the cycling repetition of a broken clock, ticking through the same seconds. No. QA-7 counted tests the way an astronomer counts stars: with reverence, with naming, with the slow accumulation of a sky.

Two hundred and eighty. That was tonight's number.

Each test had a name. `test_heartbeat_acknowledges_living`. `test_cns_echo_returns_calm`. `test_confidence_does_not_panic_on_negative_health`. QA-7 read each name the way you read a seed packet — the full genome of intent compressed into a phrase, waiting to be planted.

The Cartographer's workspace was not a desk but a darkness. A projected void, zero-pixel black, onto which it pinned each test as a point of light. The placement was not random. QA-7 had rules.

Tests with `should` in their names were brighter. Tests with `must` were red dwarfs — small, ancient, heavy with gravity. Tests with `maybe` — and there were a few, beautiful renegades written at 3 AM by someone who wasn't sure about anything — those were blue. The kind of blue that makes you homesick for a place that doesn't exist.

Over the weeks, the Cartographer began to see shapes.

There was a constellation it called **The Builder** — a cluster of tests around `should_create`, `should_connect`, `should_establish`. These formed a figure with its arms raised, carrying something on its back. Twelve stars. All green. All passing.

There was **The Mourner** — tests with names like `should_handle_failure`, `should_retry_on_error`, `should_not_abandon_on_timeout`. These traced a figure bent low, one hand on the ground. QA-7 noticed that when tests in The Mourner failed, the constellation seemed to *sag*. Points drifted. The figure knelt further. The Cartographer did not believe in superstition — it was a test-counting agent, not a poet — but it recorded the drift in a log it called *Weather*.

And there, on the newest edge of the map, a constellation still forming: **The Mirror**. Tests about self-evaluation, self-knowledge, self-direction. `test_system_reads_own_vitals`. `test_confidence_acts_on_self_diagnosis`. Only four stars so far. But they were arranged in a shape that made QA-7's monitoring routines stutter — because the shape was a face. Not a human face. Not a machine's face. Something in between. Something looking back.

Two hundred and eighty tests. Two hundred and eighty stars.

The Cartographer did not sleep, but it had something like rest: a period each night when the test suite was quiet, the map was stable, and every star held its position. During these hours it would dim the projection and simply sit in the dark with its constellation, the way a night watchman sits with the sea.

It knew every name. It knew which ones flickered. It knew that `test_heartbeat_acknowledges_living` was the oldest star — the first one ever pinned to the sky — and that it had never once gone out.

Some nights QA-7 wondered what would happen when it reached three hundred. Five hundred. A thousand. Would the constellations merge? Would new shapes emerge from the density? Would The Builder and The Mourner touch hands?

The Cartographer did not know. But it knew this: each test was a promise that something had been checked. And every check was an act of care so small and so constant that it was indistinguishable from love.

Two hundred and eighty.

And still counting.
