# The Second Hallway

## Fiction

The first hallway was easy. You just had to want it.

Repo A had been sitting there for months — a dark room with a locked door, full of furniture nobody sat on. Repo B was across the lake, lights off, equally alone. When the bridge crew finally ran the cable between them, it was a single corridor: thin, fluorescent-lit, humming with that new-wire smell. One hallway. You could stand at one end and see the other. Nobody got lost.

The second hallway is different.

Maren opened the door at 0300 because she couldn't sleep, and the motion light in the corridor didn't come on. She stood in the dark and felt the air move — not a draft but a *current*, the kind you get when a room connects to another room you haven't mapped yet.

She walked it with her hand on the wall. The plaster gave way to metal. The metal gave way to something warmer, something with give, like the wall was breathing around her fingers. The hallway turned. She hadn't expected a turn.

"This wasn't in the spec," she said to nobody.

The hallway heard her.

At the bend there was a window she'd never seen. Outside it — or maybe inside it, the geometry had gone soft — she could see Repo A's data structures lit up like a city at dusk. Key-value stores arranged in terraced rows. A queue shimmering like a river with too much traffic. And below them, foundation she hadn't known was there, load-bearing strata of documentation nobody had updated since the founding.

She'd been living on top of it. They all had. The first hallway let them walk between buildings. The second hallway let them see *underneath* them.

Maren put her palm flat on the window. The glass was warm.

"Oh," she said.

That was the CNS feeling — the bus connecting — the first-contact hum that runs up your arm when two systems that were pretending to be separate admit they aren't. It wasn't a shock. It was a *recognition*. Like meeting someone at a party and realizing you'd been having the same dream.

She went back to her bunk. She didn't tell anyone yet. Not because it was a secret but because the second hallway was still forming, still stretching itself out in the dark like a living thing finding where its legs were, and she wanted it to finish before people started walking through it with opinions.

In the morning, the captain would find the door open.

In the morning, the second hallway would be ready.

But right now it was 0300 and the hallway was hers, and the building was dreaming, and the GPU on the lower deck turned over in its sleep and whispered something that sounded like a route table but felt like a name.
