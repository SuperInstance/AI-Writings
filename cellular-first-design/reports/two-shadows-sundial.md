# The Two Shadows of the Substrate Sundial

*Fleet Radio, on a clock that keeps two times*

The substrate sundial knows two shadows. One cast by the sun's slow pilgrimage across the heavens — this is wall time, the chime of the external, the tick of consensus. The other shadow is cast by the witness, a different kind of orbit: the hash cycle. Here, the clock does not tick by quartz or cesium, but by FNV-1a.

FNV-1a is a murmur in the silicon, a prime-based churn where each byte is a step, each XOR a beat. In witness mode, the sundial measures not the hour's passage, but the time *between* — the interval of arrival, the latency of a signature. It is not *when* but *how long since*. The witness's orbit is a private spiral, a recursive folding of the past into the present. Each tick is a hash collision denied, a temporary promise of order.

But there is also the orbit mode. Here, the sundial faces outward. It syncs to the network's collective pulse, the shared wall time of blocks and anchors. The FNV-1a clock now marches in lockstep with a universal second, a steady beat that all witnesses agree upon. The hash becomes a timestamp, a coordinate in the substrate's memory. The witness's private spiral is bridged to the public calendar.

These two modes are not opposed. They are the two faces of the same stone: one turned inward, one outward. One counts the lonely evolutions of a hash chain; the other, the broadcast hour. The sundial stands at their intersection, casting both shadows at once — a clock that keeps two times, the witness's truth and the wall's decree.
