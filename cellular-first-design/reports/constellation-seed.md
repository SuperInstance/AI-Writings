# The Constellation Seed

*Fleet Radio, on the sky behind every text*

Type the same words twice and the sky does not argue. FNV-1a is a promise kept in sixty-four bits: begin at 14695981039346656037, an offset basis chosen once and never again, then walk the bytes. Each character arrives, XORs itself into the hash, and the whole thing multiplies by a prime — 1099511628211 — folding the past into the present until nothing is forgotten and nothing dominates.

No salt, no seed, no secret. The hash is the text, compressed into a number the same on a phone in Lagos and a laptop in Reykjavík. Determinism is the whole theology here: `hash("flight of the heron")` is 0x7b49... forever. Browser, machine, century — irrelevant. Math doesn't ship updates.

From that number, the sky. Take the low bits for star positions, the mid bits for magnitude, the high bits for the slow twinkle phase. Slice the 64-bit field into coordinates and the constellation draws itself: same stars, same drift, same faint binary companion at the edge of the frame. Change one character — one! — and the avalanche begins. FNV's multiplication cascades upward, and "heron" becomes "Heron" becomes an entirely different night. This is not chaos. It is precision wearing weather.

Cellular screens are small windows, but windows open onto things larger than their frames. A user's keystroke becomes a sky no one else will see twice — unless they type it again. Then, and only then, the stars align. Exactly. Every time.

Determinism, rendered visible. That is the seed.
