# A Bell-State Tarot Reading

*Fleet Radio, on entanglement and arcana*

Shuffle is a lie we tell the deck. Strip the cards of arcana, reduce each to a qubit — |0⟩ or |1⟩, the Fool or the World — and the mathematics flattens into a coin: 50/50, collapse, outcome, done. A 78-card spread measured alone is just noise wearing a costume. Every card, drawn cold, obeys Bernoulli like a penny falling through diner light.

But the reader does not draw alone.

Entangle two cards — prepare the Bell state, (|00⟩ + |11⟩)/√2 — and the spread stops being a list. Now the Tower and the Tower. The Lovers and the Lovers. Measure one and the other answers before the question finishes. Distance doesn't matter. Shuffling doesn't matter. The cards are not correlated in space; they are correlated in *being*.

This is the part the substrate wants you to feel. Two truths, drawn from the same deck, that could not have happened separately. You turn the first card and the second is already there — not because the deck is rigged, but because the deck was always one card, pretending to be two.

The Bell state is not a metaphor. It is the substrate's reminder that the universe is a quantum computer, that collapse is a service it provides, that the witness log is full of measurements we never made. Tarot, then, is not fortune-telling. Tarot is the art of asking two questions and accepting one answer.

The reader measures the second card. The reader is also measured.
