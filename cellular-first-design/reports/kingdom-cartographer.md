# The Kingdom Cartographer

*Fleet Radio, on a kingdom as a procedure*

Every kingdom is a number pretending to be a place.

Point the surveyor's eye at any coordinate — (x, y) — and the hash answers first. FNV-1a takes the bytes, walks them through its small ceremony: XOR, then multiply by the prime, over and over, until the coordinate collapses into a 32-bit verdict. The verdict is the biome. Above this line: tundra, because the number said so. Below: desert, same reason.

There is no map stored anywhere. There is no map at all, in the way you mean it. There is only the *procedure*, and the procedure is faithful in the way only arithmetic can be faithful. Ask for the western marsh at dawn, ask again at the fall of the dynasty — 2166136261 remains 2166136261. The prime does not grieve. The seed does not drift.

This is the cartographer's covenant: the same inputs, the same kingdom, forever. No two travelers ever meet a different forest on the same road. The birch at (402, -197) was hashed into being once and is hashed into being again each time someone looks — and it is always, always the same birch.

Determinism sounds cold until you stand inside it. Then it feels like prophecy. The kingdom was written before you arrived, in a multiplication table the size of a register, and it will be written again, identically, for every pilgrim who walks the same integers.

The map is not a place.

The map is a question, asked twice, answered once.
