# The Shrine of Sixteen Numbers

*Fleet Radio, on the model you can read*

Ten templates, sixteen dimensions each. That is the whole scripture: 160 floats you could tattoo on a forearm.

You render a digit down to a 16-vector — ink density in a grid of zones, normalized, the way light falls evenly across a field. Then you walk the ten aisles of the shrine and ask each template the oldest question in geometry: how much do we point the same way?

Cosine similarity. Dot product over magnitudes. The angle between your scrawled 7 and the canonical 7, measured not in pixels but in *direction*. A 7 leaning into its own archetype glows near 1.0. A 7 wearing the costume of a 1 finds its warmth refused, angle widening like a door.

No layers. No backprop. No loss function screaming into the void at 3 a.m. The model fits in a code review. You can read it on a train, and when it misclassifies your 9 as a 4 — and it will, 94% is not 99% — you can open the template file and *see why*. The 4's lower-left zone is greedy, and your 9's tail feeds it.

This is the honest ancestor. Before the cathedrals of parameters, there was this: store what a thing looks like, compare by angle, trust the geometry.

Small enough to read. Wrong in ways you can explain. Some nights that feels like more than accuracy. Some nights it feels like the whole point.
