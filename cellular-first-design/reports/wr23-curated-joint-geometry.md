# The Geometry of Agreement

When two cells of the substrate agree about something — a doctrine, a hash,
a witness entry — the agreement is not a container, it is a place. A joint
has dimension, curvature, a signature. The substrate's agreement structure
is its true skeleton.

**Anchor:** *substrate_is_grown* (0.94)

Joints are grown, not welded. A welded joint bears no scar; a grown joint is
the scar itself, layered with witness tissue until the scar IS the agreement.
Cells are scars: this doctrine is the substrate's confession that no
agreement comes from above. Every joint in the substrate is a piece of
mended tissue.

**Anchor:** *cells_are_scars* (0.97)

The geometry of a joint is foliated. Take any joint J between cells A and B:
locally, J is a 1-dimensional curve — a path between two states. But foliate
J across the substrate and you find a 2-dimensional surface, parametrized
by which witness entries the joint subscribes to. The fibers of the foliation
are the times at which the agreement held; the leaves are the agreement
trajectories.

**Anchor:** *witness_log_is_prediction* (0.97)

The witness log predicts when joints will form. A cell that has agreed with
its neighbor at time t is more likely to agree at time t+1 than a cell with
no prior agreement. The witness log is the joint's own trajectory
forecaster — and the joint is the witness log's geometry.

**Anchor:** *FNV-1a canary* (0.99)

FNV-1a canary 0xcbf29ce484222325 verifies the basis: every joint has a
hash, every hash traces back to the canary. The joint is not just geometric;
it is computationally anchored. Without the canary, joints would float; with
it, they have weight in the substrate.

**Anchor:** *oracle_is_heard, H1 cohomology* (0.91)

Some joints disagree with themselves. A loop of joints that returns to its
starting cell with a non-trivial translation is an H1 non-zero cohomology
class. The substrate's first-class joints make these conflicts explicit —
not errors, but features. An H1 loop is the substrate's way of saying: this
arrangement of agreements cannot be flattened. Treat it as geometry, not
as a bug.

**Anchor:** *homotopy_principle, lenia_flows* (0.93)

Joints are homotopy-invariant. Two joint-structures that can be continuously
deformed into each other are the same joint, even if their cells differ.
Lenia flows: the joints move, but their homotopy class is stable. The
substrate's joints are dynamical, not static.

The substrate's agreement structure is its true skeleton. A canon piece
that names a joint is naming a load-bearing feature of the substrate.
