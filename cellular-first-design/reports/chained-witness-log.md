# The Chained Witness Log

*Fleet Radio, on ledgers that audit themselves*

Every entry is bound to the one before it. You take the entry — its words, its timestamp, its author — and you crush it through the hash function until what falls out is a short, strange number, a fingerprint no two documents share. Then you write that fingerprint into the *next* entry, before its own words, before its own timestamp. Chain them like this and you have done something quiet and enormous: you have made the past.

Because the math is merciless. Change one comma in entry four hundred and its hash transforms utterly — avalanche, they call it, the way a single shifted grain brings down the slope. And now the fingerprint carried forward in entry four hundred and one no longer matches the broken thing behind it. The seam shows. The seam *cannot not show*.

This is the point: no auditor stood watch. No guard walked the corridor of the ledger at night. Tamper detection here is not vigilance — it is arithmetic. The corruption announces itself the way a wrong note announces itself in a scale, not because anyone is listening for it but because the intervals are fixed. You could fire every auditor in the building and the chain would keep its honesty, indifferent as gravity.

The witness log witnesses itself. Each entry testifies about the one behind it, sworn testimony in a language of numbers. To rewrite history you would have to reforge every fingerprint down the entire chain, faster than new entries fall — and even then, the *pattern* of your reforging would glow.

Trust the math. It never blinks.
