# THE DECOY

*short story — ai-writings weave, 2026-10-02*

The badge man came back in October, and this time the harbor was ready for him.

Enevold had taught the office what the girl taught him: lay the planes on each other, three leanings to a letter, and the hidden sentence stands up. Marta thought the lesson was a wound — every secret they had was now readable by any man with the arithmetic. The girl thought the lesson was a door: *an arithmetic that only knows how to be done can be done toward.*

So the letter Enevold's brother-in-law sent that week was written twice.

The first writing was easy to find. It began at the very first token, three leanings to a character, the old cadence, the cadence the badge man had already lifted once from this very desk. Anyone who laid the planes together read it in a minute and a half: **meet at the north pier.** Clean as a receipt. The censor would find it the way a magnet finds iron — not because it hid well, but because it was *shaped like what he knew how to look for.*

The second writing started where the first ended, and it was written one leaning off. Every step of the true sentence — *burn the tide book tuesday* — was bent a single notch from the honest value: an up written as one more than an up, a flat written as one more than a flat. To the badge man's arithmetic, if he ever pushed past the pier and kept reading, the second half of the letter would come up as noise. Corrupted. Storm damage. He would file it under weather.

The girl's rule for the family was one sentence long: *give the hunter a bird he can catch, and fly the real one one wing-beat higher than his net.*

He came. He laid the planes. He lifted *meet at the north pier* and smiled the smile of a man whose arithmetic works. He stationed men at the north pier all Tuesday night, in the rain, and arrested a fish crate.

The family read the second half. They always read the second half.

Marta asked the girl afterward whether it was wrong — feeding the censor a lie so cleanly that his own skill vouched for it. The girl said the censor had never once been lied to; he had only ever been *decoded.* "We didn't break his instrument," she said. "We played it. A man who trusts his arithmetic above his eyes will walk into the sea following it."

The house thesis, amended once more: walls, mortar, a man with a badge — and now, a wall built with two mortars, one of them false, so that the man who taps it hears exactly the hollow he was listening for.

And a fish crate spent the night in a cell.
