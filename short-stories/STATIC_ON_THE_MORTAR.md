# STATIC ON THE MORTAR

# ai-writings — the noise result as plot: how a tone channel dies gracefully
# while a plaintext channel dies lying.

The storm started small. One step in every ten arrived wrong.

On the north desk, the plaintext reader filed its verdicts with the same
confidence as always. *Beckon. Withdraw. Hold.* It had one key and it turned
it in every lock. When the storm doubled — two steps in ten wrong — the north
desk did not slow down. It did not say *uncertain*. It said *beckon* in a
voice indistinguishable from the day the storm began, because a bag of words
has no way to confess damage. It scored one correct answer in eight, which is
a coin with a preference, and it reported the coin as certainty.

On the south desk, the channel reader leaned into the tape.

The tape was a lantern-keeper's utterance, six steps of momentum, and the
storm had knocked one step sideways. The reader measured the drift profile —
three segments, signed, summed — and found the shape still nearest to *advance*.
It said: *advance, at a wobble.* It was right, and it knew how right it was.

Three steps in ten wrong. The south desk's verdicts began to lean. *Probably
withdraw.* Two of three correct. The margin over the north desk did not shrink
as the storm grew. It widened — the more steps the storm broke, the more the
plaintext reader randomized and the more the tone reader fell back toward the
only honest thing a reader can be: its prior. Not below it. Toward it.

That was the finding, and the keeper wrote it into the lantern log:

*The wall held in weather that should have erased it. Words broke like words —
all at once, while insisting they hadn't. But the mortar is graded. Take a
third of it away and the wall still knows which way it's leaning; it just says
so with a shrug. Take everything away and the mortar doesn't lie, it just
returns to the resting lean of a wall nobody has spoken to.*

At six in ten wrong, the south desk answered *hold-still* to every utterance.
This is the correct answer for a message with no message in it. The north desk
answered *beckon*.

The keeper closed the log with the house line, amended for weather:

The words were the load-bearing wall, and the story was in the mortar —
and mortar, unlike stone, fails the way water goes still. Not a collapse.
A settling back to level. A channel that can say *less sure* instead of
*same as ever* is the only channel worth trusting on a stormy night.
