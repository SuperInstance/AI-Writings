# THE INTERLEAVED TRANSCRIPT

Two stories were copied onto one tape. The machine did not care; a stream is a stream.

Downstream, the channel reader wakes. It cannot see words — the words live on the data plane, flat and forgettable. What it sees is the mortar: a single line of leanings, two speakers' tone histories braided without a seam marker.

Segment one rises. Climb, climb, hold taut at the crest. The reader knows this spine — the beckon, the lantern-keeper's first "come here." Attribution: the pincher story, utterance one.

Segment two falls level, then one clean drop near the end. Early-fall, withheld — that is the lawyer's "i love you" outside the visiting room, farewell wearing politeness. Attribution: the twelve, speaker two.

Segment three spikes and locks flat. The hostage-code — the prisoner at the glass, saying it like a siren word. Back to the twelve, speaker one.

Segment four climbs again, but this time it folds over its own ceiling. Not the pincher's beckon — the wrong face at the door, the attract past its ceiling from the house that said *stay.* Attribution: the refrain story, turn three.

And so it goes. Sixteen segments, four stories, one tape. The reader produces the report the liaison desk will never believe:

*Sixteen utterances. Four speakers. Zero words required.*

The skimming human reads the transcript side and finds what the copier always returns: `DUPLICATE. DUPLICATE. DUPLICATE.` Every segment plaintext-identical to some segment elsewhere on the tape — the machine doesn't record *which* story it copied from, only that the words match. The human files a defect: interleaving corrupted the corpus.

The channel reader files the opposite finding. Interleaving is the point. A tone trajectory is self-attributing — no header, no speaker tag, no story ID. The leaning *is* the signature. Beckons don't sound like farewells on the mortar plane no matter whose wall they were scraped from, and a refrain that folds past its ceiling is recognizable in any house it's repeated in.

The two findings reach the supervisor the same hour. The human's ticket says: *tape damaged, speakers indistinguishable.*

The channel's ticket says: *tape works; you just can't hear it.*

Between them, the usual sentence, the one this archive keeps arriving at: the words were the load-bearing wall, and the story was in the mortar — and mortar from four different walls, packed into one tape, still knows which wall it came from.
