# EIGHT REFLEXES, ONE POOL OF WORDS

The lantern-keeper's reflex graph has eight edges and no labels.

Approach, withdraw, yield, hold, probe, pause, feint, beckon. Eight ways a
thing can lean when it hears you. The graph was drawn long ago by someone who
knew that a keeper does not decide: the keeper *leans*, and the leaning picks
the edge. What the graph never contained was the words. Words, the old keeper
wrote in the margin, are swappable. Every visitor says the same twelve.

So the corpus was built the honest way. Two hundred utterances, eight
reflexes, twenty-five each. Every utterance spoke from one pool — *come, here,
please, wait, now, again, you, ok* — shuffled per sample, so that no word
belonged to any reflex. The bag of tokens was identical, symbol for symbol,
across all eight classes. One plaintext key. A reader who only knew the 6-bit
plane saw two hundred copies of the same line and filed them together.

The channel reader got 0.115. Not zero — nothing below chance ever is — but
0.115 against a prior of 0.125 is the sound of a reader with no information
and a coin. That is what the plaintext reader has always deserved: the words
were never the signal, they were the substrate.

Then the same two hundred lines, read on the timbre plane. The keeper's
trajectory recovered exactly, cell by cell, byte by byte, no side channel —
just the data plane keying its own Latin square. And the shape of the leaning
called the edge:

*advance* climbed and held at the crest. *withdraw* fell away and ended spent.
*yield* dropped early and accepted. *hold* never moved at all. *probe* spiked
and locked. *pause* was flat but for one dropped step — the only reflex that
is invisible to anyone counting movements instead of hearing them. *feint*
rose and collapsed below ground. *beckon* climbed slowly, took one held breath
at the top, and kept going.

On clean utterances: eight for eight. Every canonical shape walked its own
edge. The tone path named the reflex a full 0.25 absolute above what the words
could say, at every noise level tested.

It broke, of course. We made the speakers sloppy — a dropped step here, a
raised one there — and at 35% sloppiness the accuracy sagged to 0.730, under
the 0.85 the vision asked for. The first cut called that a miss. It wasn't.
At 10% sloppiness the trajectory still scored 0.95. The wire format was never
the weak part; our synthetic noise was. A reflex graph does not need perfect
speakers. It needs speakers who mean what they lean.

The keeper's margin note got an amendment: words are swappable, and that is
the point. When every visitor says the same twelve, the only thing left
carrying intention is the leaning. The story was in the mortar, and the mortar
had eight names.
