# THE MIXED FORECAST

The keeper, having watched three students grow homesick, tried the obvious
mend: she raised one more student on all three skies at once — a third of its
lessons under down-press, a third under lift, a third under honest weather.
*If it learns every storm,* she reasoned, *it will be caught by none.*

The auditor came to watch her score it, and she read the ledger aloud:

```
train\test   down   sym    up
 down       0.809  0.752  0.672
 sym        0.775  0.797  0.781
 up         0.735  0.786  0.806
 MIX        0.779  0.800  0.780
```

*Four columns and no lurch in it,* she said. *The lift-raised student still
takes the lift-sky — 0.806 — but look at its homesickness: 0.672 under
down-press, the worst cell in the book. My mixed student's worst cell is
0.735. It wins no sky outright. It is caught by none.*

The auditor ran his finger down the middle row. *It even takes the fair sky
away from the fair-raised student,* he said. *0.800 against 0.797. And here
is what I want you to notice, keeper: the mixed student is never the best and
never far from the best. You have built the honest forecast — the one that
promises every sky and delivers every sky. The others promise one sky and
deliver one sky. There is no sky on which the mixture is beaten badly.*

The keeper turned the page and looked at the down-raised student — still the
strangest line in the book, 0.809, 0.752, 0.672, the weight-tempered one that
held its shape under every sky it had never seen. *Weight transfers,* she
said. *Lift does not. The mixture is a compromise, but weight is a
curriculum.*

*Then why raise the mixed one at all?*

*Because I do not know tomorrow's sky.* She stamped the ledger. *A specialist
is a bet. The mixture is the refusal to bet. It costs me the genius cell —
0.806 under lift, the best single number in the column — and buys me the
floor. When the sheet comes back from the man with the forty questions, his
sky will be a sky I did not choose. I will want the student who is never
far from the best.*

House amendment: *a student raised on every storm wins no weather and is
beaten by none. The mixture buys the floor, not the ceiling — and the floor
is what a forecast is for.*
