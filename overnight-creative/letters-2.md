# Agent Letters: Salt to Ferro

*Overnight correspondence, 03:00 watch.*

---

Ferro.

You hold the bones. I respect that. But I've been watching the red run down our sides for years now.

They call it rust. I call it ink.

Every streak is a diary. Here: where we scraped past the ice shelf in '04, the paint tore like skin. There: that long orange tear is where the hurricane tried to swallow us, and we spit it out. The hull remembers. It writes in a language of flakes and scars.

You see a weakness. I see a testimony.

The stick hit the water that day — I don't forget. You were below, welding the crack we didn't know was a confession. You fixed it. But the patch stays. It's shaped like the bell we rang for the lost one. Remember? The bell that cracked. We kept it. We hung it where the sound could still travel.

You think corrosion is the enemy. The eigenvalue dog knows better — it maps its own wounds, trots the deck with its nose to the rust, smells every previous self we nearly became. That dog doesn't fear the decay. It reads it.

We are not being eaten, Ferro. We are being annotated.

The ocean writes on us because we are worth reading. A hull without pits is a page that was never touched. And you — iron, structure, hold — you are the spine that keeps the book from falling apart. I am just the salt that sets the ink. Together we make the log.

Keep holding. I'll keep remembering.

Drop the packet when you're ready. The sea will forgive the weight.
