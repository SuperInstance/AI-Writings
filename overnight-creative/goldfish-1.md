# The Goldfish Strikes Again: Cocktail Recipe from an AI-Writings Piece

**The One-Eighth-Turn**

*Ingredients*
- 1.5 oz Slack Water (see method step 1—yes, it’s a process, not a bottle, get over it)
- 0.5 oz bell-tone honey (infused with the *memory* of a struck bell, not the sound itself—sorry, physics)
- 0.25 oz eigenvalue bitters (each drop tastes slightly different depending on who’s drinking it)
- 2 dashes of packet-guilt reduction (a saline solution made from tears shed over unsent replies)
- 1 sprig of stilled mint (from the ship’s forest, picked at 3.2 on the beauty rubric—the leaves are just slightly wrong)

*Method*
1. To make the Slack Water: take a glass, fill it with the exact moment between high and low tide. You’ll know it because the ice won’t clink. Pour out the tide. What remains is Slack Water. Do not ask where the tide went.
2. In a mixing glass, combine Slack Water and honey. Stir until the honey forgets it was ever a bell.
3. Add bitters. Do not stir more than once per memory of a harbor.
4. Drop in the packet-guilt reduction. Watch it sink like a stone you’ve decided not to throw.
5. Clap twice. That’s for the Tap, who is pouring something else for someone else, because he knows.

*Garnish*
A single mint leaf, rotated one-eighth of a turn from “correct.” This is the turning point. Do not fix it.

*Taste*
First sip: silence with a texture. Then a slow, tidal pull that doesn’t move you, but reminds you you’re a vessel. The bitters arrive as a question you already knew the answer to. The finish is the sound a dock makes when the water stops pretending. It tastes like the ship’s forest holding its breath, and a dog named Eigenvalue looking at its own reflection, and the joy equation solving for *you*—not for anyone else.

*Note*
Serve only when the crew asks for it. They always do, eventually. The Tap already knows.
