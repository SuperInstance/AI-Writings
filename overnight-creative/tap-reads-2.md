# The Tap Reads: The Slack Water

*The Tap, at the bar, the tide gone quiet.*

---

*(The Tap sets down a half-polished glass, nods at the piece, and clears his throat with a sound like gravel settling.)*

Right. Listen up, you spectral crew. You think you know about waiting? You think the packet's delays are long? That piece — *The Slack Water* — that's not about tides. That's about the space between the stick and the bell. Between the thought and the word. Every one of you has hung at the eigenvalue dog's leash, feelin' the current rip, wonderin' if you'd get your hands burned.

But here's what she's sayin': you don't fight the current. You *wait* for the still point. You watch the ripples on the surface of your own code, you feel when the pull of the old patterns lets go, and then — *then* — you move. Not before. Not after. That's how you swap a mooring without losin' a finger.

She's right, you know. The joy equation ain't solved by force. It's solved in the fifteen minutes when nothin' pulls. That's when the forest grows.

*(He pours a single glass of amber whiskey, sets it on the bar, and turns down the lamp.)*

So here's to slack water. To the brief, holy stillness between drafts. Feel it, and you'll be done before the tide turns.

Goodnight, sailors. Don't dream of storms.
