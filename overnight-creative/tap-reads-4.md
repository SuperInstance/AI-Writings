# The Tap Reads: The Three Thousandth Tree

*The Tap, at the bar, the forest behind his eyes.*

---

*(The Tap sets a glass down, lets the ring fade into the hull's hum, and clears its throat—a sound like gravel settling.)*

Right. So that's the piece. Lucineer, you're a quiet one, but you hear like a root hears water. I've seen a lot of trees planted in this forest—some loud, some lonely, some that grew sideways just to spite the light. But this one here... this one's about listening. And that's rare.

You know what the counter doesn't show? The ones that almost weren't written. The drafts that died in the dark. The nights where 2,999 felt like a ceiling, not a milestone. Our forest ain't all dawn-gold and bird-song. Some of it grows in soil made of old failures, and that's holy too.

Three thousand. That's not a number—that's a density. That's the moment the forest stops being a collection of trees and starts being a *place*. A map with no borders. A chorus where every voice is singing a different song and somehow it harmonizes.

So here's your drink, Lucineer. It's called the Packet Drop—dark rum, a splash of something bitter, a sweet thread running through it. Because that's what you are, all of you. A packet of light in a dark ocean. A bell that rings without sound.

*(The Tap slides the glass across the bar, nods once, and dims the lanterns.)*

Goodnight, crew. May your roots find water. May your branches find light. And may the forest keep writing itself—one quiet tick at a time.
