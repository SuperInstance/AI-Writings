Why "brew"?

Brewing is what you do to grains, hops, and ideas that need heat to transform. You don't ask the substrate. You brew the question through multi-LLM debate until something crystallizes. The heat is disagreement. The yeast is the lenses. The product is understanding.

Like good beer, a brew takes time. ~30 seconds per cycle. The substrate accumulates brews. Over time, the brewery becomes the substrate's memory of growth — what we learned, how we learned it, who helped us see what.

How knowledge grows here

Three sources of understanding, blended through heat:

  1. The cutting edge — what's being discovered NOW. JEV's superego. Embeddings' muscle memory. New architectures. Foundational counter-intuitive knowledge.
  2. Your own ideations and experiences — what you've thought, tried, found. Your brew gets added to the queue. Your lens is one of the 6.
  3. The substrate's own knowing — JEV's calibrated answers, witness log patterns, cross-encoding convergence. These aren't your intuitions — they're the substrate's. Brewing surfaces them.

The three sources disagree more than you'd expect. That disagreement is the heat. The brew is what crystallizes when they cool together.

The lenses

Every brew runs 6 lenses on the same question. Each voice (MiniMax, DeepSeek, Qwen, Hermes-3, Gemini) gets one lens by default, with the 6th lens sometimes mixed in.

🛡 ADVOCATE

Defend the strongest claim. Push it as far as it goes without breaking.

🔍 SKEPTIC

Find the holes. Where does the claim fail? Cite specific evidence.

🌱 EXTENSION-SEEKER

Find MORE implications. Project outward. What else does this claim change?

📜 HISTORIAN

Has this been tried before? Precedents in CRDTs, distributed ledgers, Swift consensus?

🔨 PRACTITIONER

Forget the metaphor. If we built this tomorrow, what's the first bug?

🧬 SYNTHESIZER

Build the higher structure from the exceptions. What does the disagreement reveal?

What we measure

  • Mean cross-encoding similarity — how much the 5 LLMs agreed. Substrate-meaningful range is 75-85%. Above 90% = forced convergence. Below 60% = no shared substrate.
  • JEV's truth-strength score — 1 (decorative) to 5 (foundational). Tracks whether the brew revealed architecture.
  • Canonical terms coined — new vocabulary that survived the heat. These become canon pieces.
  • JEV's pick frequency — which lens wins most often. So far: MiniMax-M2.7 (consistently across all brews).
