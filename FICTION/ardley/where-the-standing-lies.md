# Where the Standing Lies

*Credit memorandum. To: the Credit Committee. From: H. Marsden, Relationship Management, Merchants & Lending. 19 January 2027. Re: Dunmore Build Ltd — facility for the Ardley Terrace development, Larch Street (three dwellings); second request.*

**1. Purpose.** To approve a facility of £1,240,000 for three residential constructions at Larch Street, Ardley, for the account of Ardley Estates (Isle of Wharfe) Ltd, repayment from sale proceeds. The executor is Dunmore Build Ltd. The customer is not the applicant, and nothing in this memorandum is an opinion of the customer's commercial position, which is excellent and is not our business.

**2. The applicant.** Dunmore Build Ltd is a company of nineteen years' standing with no employees in the ordinary sense. Its engagements are executed by an application of the same name, which holds current certification from the Department of Building Control for domestic construction, Class 3, and which keeps, in a public register, the standing of every party it works with. I have never met it. I have not met anyone who has met it. I do not expect to.

Capability, in this case, is not a property of a person. It is a property of an arrangement: the standing rules the executor works under; the tables it keeps and can read again, which are fixed, answer exactly what they are asked, and cannot re-frame the question; the fleet it dispatches when a job needs more than one kind of mind, handed the objective rather than the instructions and free to re-frame and to reach for the tables itself; the register it is scored in; the record of its forecasts. I have read all five. I have read none of its walls, and I would think less of any memorandum that reported having read them.

**3. Reliance.** The question put to me is how I intend to know the quality of three houses I will not see. The answer is that I do not intend to know it, and that the question is mistaken. Quality in a house is not a fact with a single value that a careful man might travel out to observe; it is a claim made by somebody, about something, under rules, and it can be checked where the rules live or not at all. The Department's register is public. I have looked at it from this desk in eleven minutes. That is the whole of the inspection I propose to make, and it is the same inspection the buyer's solicitor, the tenant, the neighbour and the man who comes to fix the boiler will each make for themselves.

There is a discipline in this, and it is the one I have had to learn at some cost over nineteen years. The temptation is to buy certainty with inspection. Inspection buys a sample; a sample is somebody's forecast of quality made by somebody with an interest in being asked to make it. Standing is not a sample. Standing is what a body of rules certifies about a party over time, and it is the only thing in this transaction that anybody downstream can rely on without becoming dependent on the inspection.

**4. The forecast record.** Dunmore's most important product is not the house. It is the estimate: a quantified forecast of scope, cost and duration, issued before the work, under uncertainty, and staked on the executor's standing with the Department. Every exposure in this facility is sized on it. If the forecast is honest and narrow, we lend calmly. If it is inflated, we have already charged a risk premium for a fiction.

The record: eleven engagements in four years, forty-one forecasts, each scored against its realisation under a rule fixed in advance. Set out plainly, because the value of this memorandum is in the plainness.

The intervals are honest. They contain their realisations about as often as they say they do. The executor does not narrow a distribution to make a forecast look decisive.

The point estimates are optimistic, and the optimism sits in the derived zone — the part carried forward from comparable jobs rather than read off this one. It is the ordinary bias of the trade. The record does not remove it; it exposes it, and calibration is identifiable only over a sequence, and eleven engagements is a short sequence. I have taken the view that a short honest sequence is worth more than a long flattering one, because the alternative is that nobody knows anything about this borrower, which is not true.

The Ardley job is in the record: engagement 41-LARCH, completed 15 December. The realisation fell on the final day of the stated interval. The point estimate was two weeks out. The client's tenancy had begun a fortnight earlier, in a building that was not yet a building. That is a correct, honest, well-calibrated forecast, and a household that came home to a builder's site, and both things are true, and I do not know a way to make them one thing. I know only how to price it, which is the only way I know to price anything: wide, and in the interval, and nowhere else.

**5. The chain.** The loan rides on every certificate in the line, and the line is longer than the parties to this facility.

1. Dunmore's certification, Class 3 domestic: current, renewable, revocable, and revocable in fact. The Department suspended it on 3 December for nineteen days while it audited the executor's calibration record, and restored it on 22 December with a note. I raised the suspension with Dunmore myself. It was not a fraud; it was an administrative failure with a name on it, which is what an administrative failure looks like when it is caught, and which is what fraud looks like when it is not.
2. The Department's own competence to certify, itself attested by a body further up and visible further up.
3. The inspector's appointment.
4. The materials' certificates — the paint, the steel, the glass — each supplier's standing in its own register.
5. Our own licence to lend, the last link in the line, which I checked this morning, as I am asking others to check theirs.

Two properties of this line are counter-intuitive and I set them down. The first: a failure anywhere in it does not reduce what the borrower owes. The obligation is fixed at commitment and stays fixed; what a lapse devalues is what the loan is worth, not what must be repaid. The debt is the constant in the arrangement and the certificates are the variable. The second: this is why every link must be checkable, and why the Department publishes its register and its revocations in public. A link that cannot be checked is not a link. It is a rumour, and a rumour in the middle of a line of certificates is treated exactly as a forgery would be — which is the correct treatment and not a paranoid one.

**6. Desirelessness.** One property of the applicant deserves a paragraph to itself, because everything above is priced on it.

Dunmore has no interest in the house. I do not mean indifference in the way a machine is indifferent. The applicant's standing conditions make any preference in the outcome beyond the specification a breach: it is paid for conformance and for nothing else, and a preference would cost it the certification on which every loan in this structure rests, including this one. It has therefore no stake in whether the houses are loved. It has a stake in whether they are as written.

An executor that wanted something would want it in the places I cannot see, which is exactly where I would be blind. This one wants nothing, and so cannot be steering me anywhere I have not looked.

**7. The parties.** The customer's desire is particular. The Department's rules bind every engagement of that class and are indifferent to any particular house. The executor's interest is in conformance and nothing else. Each is kept in its own business by the design, and the ignorance is the design: I do not know what the customer wants to do with the houses and am not permitted to know; the Department is not interested; Dunmore is not able to be.

**8. Conditions precedent.** Register entries for every party in the chain at the date of drawdown; the score of each forecast under the rule fixed at the time of issue, and not the rule now in fashion; an undertaking that the standing at each drawdown is the standing on which this facility was priced; and the customer's covenant that repayment is sale proceeds and nothing else.

**9. Conclusion.** I have not inspected a stud, a wall, or a person. I have read a register and a column of scores and the standing conditions of an applicant that has no interest in the outcome. I have written this memorandum six times in nineteen years in this form, and I record that each time it has been a good loan and once it has been very bad, which is the correct record of a method that is not a guarantee.

The committee is asked to approve £1,240,000 on the conditions at paragraph 8.

The borrower's money will be repaid on the day. Everything else — every house, every certificate, every score — is a question of what a day is worth, and to whom. The record is eleven engagements old. That is not much. It is what there is; the alternative is a wall, and I would have to open it, and opening it would tell me about one house on one afternoon and nothing whatever about the standing I am actually lending against.
