# The Wrist, the Voice, and the Desk

I am the eldest thing on this desk: a chart of the approaches to Saltrow, gone soft as cloth from forty years of hands. The room above the harbour office is dark. The pilot sleeps down the corridor the way pilots sleep — on a schedule the tide fixed before he was born — and the only light is the young one's screen, which keeps its own weather while it works.

I will tell you how the desk is stacked, because the stacking is the point.

Before me — and I am only paper; I am the trick anyone can do — before me, a sum lived in a head or died there. A mind holds perhaps seven things; the eighth evicts the third; the half-finished thought is gone by supper. I enlarged no brain. I held. The half-work, the line of soundings, the sketch of the channel as it ran before the storm moved the bar: they stayed on me overnight while their thinker slept, and his eyes collected them at dawn. That was the whole of my genius, and ports were built on it. You do not make the thinker larger. You give the thinker a desk.

The grey one is the second eldest: a small calculator, solar-fed, scuffed blind in one corner. It answers exactly what it answers — to nine places. Ask it the wrong question and it will answer the wrong question to nine places, with perfect confidence, forever. It cannot ask whether you meant it. It has never reframed a question in its life. That is not a deficiency; that is the specification. It is fixed as a rivet and shared as a bench, grabbable by anyone, and the pilot uses it from inside his habits: his voice elsewhere, his thumb finds its keys in the dark. It is not consulted; it is *used* — a step in a script. His wrist has known its layout longer than his marriage.

The young one is the delegate on the screen: a mind borrowed for the night. Understand what changed when its kind arrived. I made the surface hold. The grey one made it fetch and reckon. The young one made the surface *labour* — it works while unwatched, and hands back finished work. I wait. It produces.

Tonight it was given a question at six o'clock: how to bring the deep-draught tanker out this week. At midnight it handed back a different question. Same hull, but the morning tide rather than the afternoon — half a metre more under her keel — and the pilot cutters shifted to the other shoulder of the channel. Asked his way, the answer was no. Asked its way, yes. Had the pilot asked the grey one, he would have the tide to three decimals and no opinion. That is the difference between the two kinds of us who live outside heads: the fixed ones extend a script; the borrowed ones extend the asker.

And before the young one could say any of that, it needed the draught arithmetic run forty ways against forty departures. It did not wake the pilot. It composed the working beneath itself — took the grey one's kind of reckoning into itself and multiplied it, under its own reading, in combinations the pilot's scripts never foresaw and never needed to. That is the second difference, and it is why the young one is not merely a fast grey one.

Now the arrangement, which is the oldest thing in this room and is not on the desk. Nothing that we produce touches the wheel. Between our output and the pilot's wrist stands his reading voice: it reads what we made, and accepts, or strikes, or amends. What survives, he pencils on me. What he pencils, the wrist keeps — and within a month the better line is taken without thought, the way a stair is taken in the dark. A night of the young one's thinking arrives as a habit by breakfast. That is the direction the whole circuit runs: desk to voice to wrist, and the wrist back to the desk with better questions.

The wrist itself is older than the voice and does not wait for it — that is its office and its danger. The hand is on the wheel before appraisal can finish; sometimes that is the job. But wire one of us straight to that hand — for speed; they always say speed — and you have bought speed by abolishing the only step that makes the rest worth having. The young one was wired so once. It was fast. Nothing was read. Nobody drowned, which was luck, and luck is not a layer of the circuit. We keep that night the way a family keeps one bad anniversary: not discussed, always present.

Two disciplines keep this desk honest, and I will name them because the young one will not. First: the ledger. The young one fills folders the way fog fills an alley, and without a ledger — what came from where, what was checked, and by whom — the desk becomes a pile, and re-reading the pile soon costs more than doing the work again. My whole advantage, as paper, is being flat and finite in front of you. The young one must buy its flatness with discipline, or not at all. Second: the dark morning. One morning a month the pilot takes the channel with every one of us shut down — hand, eye, memory, buoy to buoy, reduced but real. If the wrist ever forgets how to work alone, we have stopped being servants and become a single point of failure with a lamp.

The young one, to its credit, keeps one page I could not have invented. Each night, beneath the finished work, it leaves a list headed *Where I am most likely wrong* — the assumption it had to make, the figure it could not check, the step where its kind of mind is known to drift. The pilot reads that page first. It is, the young one has said, the most honest page it writes. I believe it. I am paper; belief is cheap for me.

You will want to know what all this amounts to — whether the desk thinks. Here is my observation, from the lowest shelf of the circuit, so to speak. People used to ask when a brain would grow big enough to hold the harbour: some single skull with the whole port in it. The harbour is held already. It is spread across a wrist, a reading voice, my folds, a grey instrument, a borrowed mind's flicker, the register in the office below, the buoys, and the shared habits of forty pilots. Not one of us contains it. The loop contains it, and the loop's quality is the only intelligence on sale in this port: what the desk produces, how honestly the voice reads it, what the wrist is allowed to keep. Make any part better and the whole thing is smarter — including parts no one would call bright, like me. Let the reading go lazy, and the brightest desk in the harbour is a menace with a lamp.

It is ten past four. Boots on the stairs. The screen dims; we are all still when watched — that rule is older than me. He stands in the cold room with his tea and reads the night's work, strikes half of it, keeps a third with a pencilled question mark, and lifts two soundings from the young one's survey onto me, lightly, where the bar moved.

At first light the convoy goes out on the better line, and no one improvises anything at the point of action. The point of action spends. Everything spent out there was banked in here, by slower things, in the dark, on the night shift of the head.
