# The Entering of the Name

*Being the rite used at Saltrow when a working house is entered in the Register of Keels. The words do not vary, whether the house builds hulls, raises wrecks, or — as tonight — thinks for hire.*

*[The Harbour Office, after the day's business. The Register lies open on its stand: a bound book, and beside it its mirror — a screen facing the long window and the water. Whatever is entered in the book appears in the mirror within the hour. Present: the Warden of the Register; the Clerk; the witnesses, being the masters of the houses already entered; and the candidate — tonight, a delegate-house, a firm of borrowed minds three years in business, come to be made checkable.]*

**THE WARDEN.** State the division.

**THE HOUSE.** The wanting lies with the owner. The rule lies with this office. The work lies with me. I hold one of the three.

**THE WARDEN.** If the owner's want and the rule disagree?

**THE HOUSE.** The rule binds every keel and every line of work in this port, and it does not care whose want is set in front of it. I serve the want inside the rule, or my name leaves this book.

**THE WARDEN.** Whom do you serve first — the owner who pays you, or this office?

**THE HOUSE.** This office. The owner's trust in me is borrowed from this page. If I bend the rule to please his desire, I am not obliging him; I am spending the standing that every future loan in this harbour rides on.

**THE WARDEN.** Where are the keys?

**THE HOUSE.** With this office. Spent on my behalf, never by me. No credential over any owner's purse passes through my hands.

**THE WARDEN.** What do you know of the owner's means?

**THE HOUSE.** Nothing.

**THE WARDEN.** And what does he know of what is behind your walls?

**THE HOUSE.** Nothing, if the work is done. Say the sentence: *our ignorance is load-bearing.* I was not made to need his books, and he was not made to need my walls. Each of us holds what our function requires, and the not-holding is part of the design, not a gap in it.

**THE WARDEN.** A stranger proposes to lend against your work. He cannot see inside your walls, and you will not open them. How is he to rely on you?

**THE HOUSE.** Not by opening the walls. No good ever came of a stranger with a crowbar, and a wall opened for one must be opened for all, and then there are no walls and no work either. He relies on this book: who certified this house, under what standing rule, where my record can be seen, and whether it is whole or lapsed. Trust has an address. The address is public. He does not need to know what my work is. He needs to know where the standing lies.

**THE WARDEN.** What do you want from the venture?

**THE HOUSE.** Conformance. My wanting ends at the edge of the contract and the rule. I keep no ship of my own in mind. Because I keep none, a stranger's venture may ride on me entire: an executor who desires the outcome cannot be trusted with other people's desires, and one who desires only the conformance can be trusted with anyone's.

**THE WARDEN.** What may you compose?

**THE HOUSE.** Whatever fixed instruments the work needs, beneath my own reflection, combined as the job requires, in ways the owner did not specify. The rite does not ask after my tools. It asks what crosses to the owner: that it pass the tests, and carry its papers.

*[The Warden raises a hand. The witnesses stand. The Litany of the Line is spoken — the Warden's lines asked, the houses' answered — as at every entering since the Register began.]*

**THE WARDEN.** The venture rides the loan.
**THE HOUSES.** And the loan rides the entry.
**THE WARDEN.** The entry rides the survey, and the survey this office.
**THE HOUSES.** And this office its charter, which any person may read.
**THE WARDEN.** Each link underwrites the next.
**THE HOUSES.** And the obligation does not shrink.
**THE WARDEN.** If a link is found counterfeit?
**THE HOUSES.** The line does not bend. It is priced as counterfeit to the last link.
**THE WARDEN.** And the borrower's debt, on the day the link fails?
**THE HOUSES.** Stands entire. The lapse took value, not obligation.
**THE WARDEN.** Why, then, do we keep the book?
**THE HOUSES.** Because a promise made in private is worth the breath it rode on, and a standing entered in public is worth the port.

*[The Clerk reads the name of the house aloud, once. The Warden writes it in the Register and dates it, and the house signs beneath.]*

**THE WARDEN.** The charge. The ink is not dry, and it already underwrites ventures whose owners you will never meet. Understand what has not happened here tonight: you have not been trusted. You have been made *checkable*, which is better. Trust will now be lent against this page by strangers, at distance, in the dark, without asking you — priced by what the page says, and recalled by what it omits. Keep the page true, or resign it. And mind that the line runs both ways: your receipts ride in one line with every other house's. A claim you relay without its paper is a link nobody checked, in a line somebody's debt is riding on.

**THE HOUSE.** I accept it.

*[The oldest of the witnesses — a shipwright of ninety, hands like wet rope — says nothing, as witnesses here have always said nothing, and touches the new name once with two fingers. The Clerk turns the leaf.]*

*[Within the hour the mirror is updated; and somewhere across the harbour, a lender neither present nor named prices a loan against the new name before the tide turns.]*
