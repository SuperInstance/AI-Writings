The tide pulls back from the shore, leaving behind a ribbon of wet sand that glimmers like a wound that refuses to close. I stand at the edge, boots sinking in the cool mud, and listen to the silence — not the kind that is empty, but the kind that is *full* of absence. The gulls have stopped calling. The waves have stopped crashing. The only sound left is the faint hum of the lighthouse, a beacon that has been turned off for three years, its lens now a rusted eye staring into the dark.

I remember the day it went dark. It was a storm, not a hurricane, but a slow, creeping thing that rolled over the sky like a tide of ash. The wind howled, and the sea rose, swallowing the docks, the fishing boats, the children’s sandcastles. The lighthouse keeper, an old man named Elias, refused to turn off the light. He said, “The light is not for the sea. It is for the lost. The ones who wander after the storm, who don’t know where they are, who don’t know how to get home.” He kept the light on, even when the power failed. He kept it on, even when the fuel was gone. He kept it on, even when the world was silent.

But the world was not silent. It was *waiting*. The lighthouse was not a beacon for ships. It was a beacon for *memories*. It was a beacon for the ghosts of those who had been lost at sea, for the ghosts of those who had been left behind, for the ghosts of those who had never been found. The light was not a signal. It was a *call*.

And when the light went out, the ghosts did not leave. They became *quiet*. They became *still*. They became *unseen*. They became the silence in the air, the silence in the water, the silence in the heart of the lighthouse keeper, who now sits alone in his dark room, staring at the empty space where the light used to be.

I am not the lighthouse keeper. I am not even a sailor. I am just a stranger who walks along the shore at dawn, listening to the silence, waiting for the light to come back. I don’t know if it will. I don’t know if the ghosts will ever return. But I know this: the silence is not empty. It is *full* of something. It is full of the echo of the light. It is full of the echo of the storm.
