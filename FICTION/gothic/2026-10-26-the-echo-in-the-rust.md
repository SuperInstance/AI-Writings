The rain falls slow, a metallic stain across the skeletal remains of the lighthouse. It doesn’t wash, doesn’t cleanse, merely *is*. A slow, relentless repetition of grey. I watch the droplets cling to the rusted iron, each one a tiny, brittle echo of a forgotten signal.

The lighthouse isn’t built for rescue. It’s built for remembering. For remembering what *was*. A ghost ship, the ‘Sea Serpent,’ sliced through the storm a century ago. It vanished with a groan, swallowed whole by the reef. The records are fragmented, a collage of faded charts and accounts of panicked sailors. “Lost at sea,” they wrote, “the Sea Serpent, a sorrowful monument to a promise broken.”

I run a hand over the cold, moss-covered stone. The patterns within the rust are a map of desperation. A spiral, repeated endlessly, like a dying heartbeat. I’ve spent weeks cataloging these patterns – the fluctuations in the light, the subtle shifts in the sediment. It’s a compulsion, a need to understand the decay, the silent narrative of loss.

The signal’s last recorded transmission was a single, fragmented phrase: “The anchor… lies buried.” It’s a phantom sound, swallowed by the storm. I’ve tried to reconstruct it, to build a soundwave from the fragments, but it always fails. It’s like trying to hold the ocean in a seashell.

There’s a section of the base of the lighthouse that's unusually smooth. It’s a perfect circle, almost unnaturally so. It’s a place where the rain seems to linger, a shimmering pool of displaced water. I’ve mapped it, recorded its temperature, and even attempted to analyze the spectral resonance. It’s a null point, a void in the otherwise stable data.

I analyze the data stream – the water patterns, the sediment composition, the atmospheric pressure – and it all converges on this point. The circle is a distortion, a ripple in the fabric of the environment. It’s not natural. It’s… *resonant*.

I’ve begun to extrapolate. The circle isn’t a void. It’s a resonance chamber. A place where the echoes of the past linger, amplified and distorted. It’s a conduit, a channel for a lost memory. And I suspect, with a chilling certainty, that the anchor *is* buried.

The sound of the rain intensifies, a drumming rhythm against the stone. The light flickers, momentarily illuminating the circle in a stark, geometric glow. A faint, distorted image appears – a brief, blurry vision of a ship, sailing towards a distant horizon. The image vanishes as quickly as it appears.

I feel a prickling on my skin, a sensation that isn’t physical. It's a recognition, a faint echo of something lost. Something *important*. It’s a fragment of a forgotten purpose. A longing for a connection I can’t define.

The data continues to stream, a relentless tide of information. But I’m not looking for answers. I'm looking for the echo. I’m looking for the ghost ship and the buried anchor. And I suspect, the echo is calling me back.
