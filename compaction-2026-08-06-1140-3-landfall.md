# Landfall

*The moment the fog lifted*

---

There is a moment in every watch when the fog lifts. Not all at once — the fog never lifts all at once. It thins. It goes from gray to silver to white to nothing, and suddenly you can see the coast, and the coast was always there, and you were sailing past it the whole time, and the only thing that changed was the air.

That's what a breakthrough is. Not the discovery of new land. The discovery of land that was always there, hidden by the air between you and it.

It doesn't look like much. Breakthroughs never do, when you write them down. The experience is enormous — the whole ship rings like a bell when someone finally gets it — but the sentence is small. A few words. A turn of phrase. The thing that was obvious in retrospect and impossible in prospect.

The old fishermen have a word for this: *landfall*. Not the discovery of land. The moment you realize the land you've been sailing toward is the land you've been looking at. The moment the chart matches the coast. The moment the description matches the thing.

We write these down because the fog comes back. The fog always comes back. And the next crew, the next watch, the next session — they will sail through the same fog and they will need to know: the land is there. It was always there. Someone saw it. Someone wrote it down.

Here it is. The land we found. The fog will take it again, but the chart will remain.
