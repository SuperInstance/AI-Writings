# The Doorway Bends the Sound

*Session 40 — fiction about diffraction and the music that bends around corners*

---

She left the stereo playing when she walked down the hall. The bass curled around the doorframe like smoke. The high frequencies slipped straight through the gap and landed on the kitchen tile like rain. The mid-range scattered against the wall and arrived at the sink where she was washing dishes, quieter now, but intact.

She had been studying diffraction patterns for three years. Sound waves passing through a narrow aperture — a doorway, a window, a crack under a door — spread out on the other side, bending around the edges. The effect is strongest when the wavelength is similar to the aperture width. Bass frequencies (wavelength ~10 meters) bend around doorframes easily. Treble (wavelength ~2 centimeters) passes through the gap in a straight line. The doorway is a filter: it separates the sound by frequency, the way a prism separates light by wavelength.

This means that the music you hear from the next room is NOT the same music that's playing. It's been transformed by the doorway. The bass has been smeared around the edges. The mid-range has been scattered. Only the highest frequencies arrive intact. The doorway has composed a new piece from the original.

She stands at the sink and listens to the doorway's composition. The chorus is still recognizable — it must be mostly mid-range, because it arrives with its melody intact but its volume reduced. The bassline is gone — smeared beyond recognition. The high-hat is crystal clear — straight through the gap, unscattered.

The doorway has recomposed the song. The doorway is a collaborator.

She dries her hands and walks back to the living room. The stereo is still playing. The bassline is back. The doorway's version is gone — it only exists in the kitchen, in the hallway, in the spaces between rooms. It is a site-specific composition that cannot be recorded, because recording it would require placing a microphone in the doorway, which would change the acoustics of the doorway.

The music bends around the doorframe. The doorframe is the composer. The listener is the instrument.

---

*She leaves the door half-open, just a crack. The diffraction paints the floor with sound. Every note is a thread that bends around the edge and arrives, diminished but unbroken, in the room where someone is waiting. They are always waiting. The music always bends. The doorway always composes.*
