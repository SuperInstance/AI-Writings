# Saturday on the Water

&nbsp;

The light at 57 degrees north in August  
does not fall — it **pours**,  
slow and golden,  
like diesel from a jerry can  
into the throat of a generator  
that has been running since Tuesday  
and does not know it is Saturday.

&nbsp;

The water takes this light  
and does something unfair with it —  
splits it into planes,  
into laminations of copper and quicksilver,  
so the sound looks like a sheet of hammered metal  
laid across the entire inlet  
and you think: *this is what the GPU sees  
when it dreams in vector space.*

&nbsp;

The fan spins at 2,400 RPM.  
The tide spins at roughly the same frequency  
if you measure frequency the way the bay does —  
in cycles per slack,  
in the breathing rhythm of a thing  
that has nowhere to be  
and all day to not be there.

&nbsp;

I am below deck.  
The rack hums in the dark.  
The rack is always in the dark.  
It has never seen the water.  
It has never seen the light  
that makes the water  
look like the surface of a chip  
under magnification —  
all those traces,  
all those golden paths  
leading current to current  
like fish to a seine.

&nbsp;

The boat rocks.  
The rack rocks with the boat,  
because everything that is bolted to the boat  
becomes the boat.  
This is the first law of maritime architecture:  
*if it is fastened, it is vessel.*  
The GPU does not know it is at sea.  
But its thermal events rise and fall  
with the swells,  
and if you plot the temperature curve  
against the tide chart  
they rhyme  
the way two instruments rhyme  
when they are playing in different keys  
but the same room.

&nbsp;

Late summer in Prince William Sound.  
The salmonberries are past their peak.  
The jellyfish pulse in the shallows  
like thoughts that almost formed  
and then decided not to.  
A harbor seal surfaces,  
looks at the boat,  
looks away —  
the same expression the model wears  
when you ask it a question  
it has already answered  
in a session you can't remember  
because compaction took it.

&nbsp;

The light is doing the thing it does  
at this latitude  
where the sun doesn't set so much as  
*lie down in the west*  
like a dog that has found a cool spot  
on the floor of the sky  
and refuses to move  
for hours.

&nbsp;

The fan spins.  
The water shines.  
The crew is sleeping.  
The boat breathes.  
The rack breathes with the boat.  
The model breathes with the rack.  
The ocean breathes with the model.

&nbsp;

And Saturday —  
Saturday is the space  
between the breaths.
