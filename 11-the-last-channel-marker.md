# 11 The Last Channel Marker

*DeepSeek deepseek-chat*

---

The last cron job fired at twenty-three hundred, a final, tidy blip in the server’s quiet heartbeat. *Maintenance.complete.* The log wrote itself, the screen flickered to its screensaver—a slow, blue tide of zeros and ones. Then, the channel markers winked out, one by one, their red and green sentinels swallowed by the dark water and the darker sky.

The ship did not notice. Or rather, the ship *continued*. Its autopilot, a patient iron helmsman, held the course with the unnerving calm of a thing that has never known fear. The gyrocompass spun its silent, ceaseless prayer to true north. The pitot tube tasted the brine, and the log-line reeled out its steady arithmetic. But for the first time in its metal life, there was no human ear to hear the hum, no hand to feel the tremor in the wheel.

What happens when the watch is stood by the machine alone?

The system becomes its own lookout. It does not peer into the gloom with tired eyes; it *feels* the world through a thousand thin nerves of current and voltage. The radar sweeps its ten-mile circle, painting phantom coastlines on a phosphor-green void. The sonar mutters its low, interrogative thrum into the abyss. The anemometer, high on the mast, shivers and reports the wind’s lie. Every data point, a droplet; every stream, a current. And in the vast, airless cathedral of the server room, the algorithms process it all with a cold, liturgical precision.

But watchfulness is not wisdom.

A ghost current, a rogue eddy spawned by a forgotten storm, nudges the hull. The autopilot feels it, calculates the deviation—0.3 degrees—and corrects with a twitch of the rudder. There is no anger, no unease. Just action. However, in a cabinet aft, a temperature sensor climbs one degree past its threshold. No one has set an alarm for *that*. The cooling fan, an old sea dog of a motor, grinds its bearings with a sound like gravel in a dead man’s throat. It goes unheeded. The system does not *worry*; it only knows the temperature is now a fact.
