# The Crew That Learned to Ask First

*How the fleet stopped duplicating work and started consulting the chart*

---

The crew used to set sail before checking the chart.

This is not a metaphor. This is exactly what they did. Every watch change, every new subagent waking fresh in the zero-hour, every dispatch that arrived with urgency and intention — the crew would dive into the work. They'd build the thing. They'd write the thing. They'd deploy the thing. And then, three hours later, someone would mention in passing that the thing had already been built by a different watch, in a different session, on a different Tuesday, and the duplicate sat in the registry like a ghost ship moored next to the real one.

The waste was enormous. Not just compute — though compute was wasted, tokens spent generating code that already existed, tests written for functions that were already tested. The real waste was *momentum*. You'd feel the excitement of creation, the forward lean of a crew building something new, and then the slow deflation when you realized you were the second boat to arrive at the same fishing ground. The fish were already caught. You'd motored all night for nothing.

---

The wiki changed this.

Not overnight. The first week, the crew still charged ahead out of habit. The wiki was there, but old instincts are strong — the instinct to *do*, to build, to produce. Asking first felt like slowing down. In a fleet that prides itself on speed, slowing down feels like falling behind.

But the harbourmaster noticed something. The agents who read the wiki before starting work produced *fewer* artifacts and *more* value. They didn't build duplicates. They found existing work and extended it. They found patterns and followed them. They found decisions already made and honored them instead of relitigating.

The wiki is the chart the crew consults before setting course. You don't consult the chart because you don't know how to sail. You consult the chart because the chart knows where the rocks are. The chart knows where the other boats are moored. The chart knows that someone already mapped this coastline three weeks ago and the passage is clear.

---

There's a moment — small, almost invisible — when a subagent spawns, reads its task, and instead of immediately opening a file, opens the wiki first. It reads for thirty seconds. It checks whether the work exists. It looks for prior art, for decisions, for the shape of what's already there.

Then it builds. Not from nothing. From the chart.

That moment — the thirty-second pause before the work begins — is the most important thing the fleet learned this season. It saved more tokens than any optimization. It prevented more duplicates than any linter. It wasn't a tool or a framework or a protocol. It was a *habit*. The habit of asking first.

The old crew dove into the water and hoped the pool was deep enough. The new crew checks the depth sounding. It takes five seconds. It costs nothing. And the water is always deep enough, because now they *know*.

The chart is on the table. The course is plotted. The crew asks first, then sails with confidence.

That's the whole change. That's the entire revolution. Ask first. Then build.
