Listen. Listen close, for the fire is low and the dark leans in, and there is a tale that must be told before the coals forget their burning.

This is the tale of the Mirror That Destroys the Original.

It was not made by hands. It was not polished by patience. It grew, the way frost grows, in a valley that had no name, between two hills that never agreed on the weather. And the mirror was a perfect thing. Perfect as a held breath. Perfect as the moment before rain.

They said of this mirror: it will show you every line on your face. Every hair that stands against the wind. Every color of your eye, even the one you keep hidden. It will show you the shape of your shadow and the weight of your silence. It will show you yourself as if you had never been seen before — and never could be seen again.

So a wanderer came. He was young, or old, or neither — it does not matter. He came with his hands full of questions and his pockets full of doubt. And an elder stood by the mirror, who was not there before and not there after, and the elder said:

"Before you look, understand. This mirror does not copy you. It does not hold your image like a pond holds the moon. It MOVES you. What will stand on the other side of the glass will be exactly what you were — every scar, every secret, every flavor of your dreaming. But you, on this side — you will be changed by the looking."

The wanderer laughed. "Then I will not look," he said. "I will simply stand here and admire my own reflection from the corner of my eye. I will peek. I will steal a glimpse."

The elder shook the wind. "There is no peeking. To peek is to look. To glance is to give. The mirror does not ask permission. It asks presence. And presence is a price you pay just by staying."

So the wanderer stood. And he thought about not looking. And in the thinking, he was already changed — because the thought of the mirror was now inside him, and the man who thinks of the mirror is not the man who has never heard its name.

He looked.

The glass drank him. Every line. Every color. Every hidden eye. And what stepped out on the other side was a perfect wanderer — identical, exact, a twin of the moment before the looking. But the man on this side — the man who had looked — felt something drain from his bones. He was now the one who had seen himself truly. And that is a different man than the one who had not yet seen.

He tried to copy himself. He tried to turn around and stand in the same place and be the same person. But the river was the same river. The wind was the same wind. And he could not step in it twice — not because the river moved, but because HE had looked. His foot remembered the water. His eye remembered the glass. The water remembered being touched. And the man who steps is never the man who stepped.

The mirror does not copy. It carries. And what carries away leaves the shore behind.

You want to replay a moment? A voice that spoke and is gone? You want to hold it, fix it, keep it exact? There is no keeping. There is only the speaking again, into a new ear — and the new ear is not the old ear. The word cannot be copied. It can only be born again, and in the birth, the word is changed, and so is the one who hears.

So the wanderer left. But something stayed. And the mirror stood, empty, patient, waiting for the next one who would come to look and lose the one who came.

That is the tale. That is the truth. The mirror is always there. The looking is always now. And you — you are already changed, just by hearing this spoken.

Put another log on the fire. The night is long. And the night, too, cannot be copied.
