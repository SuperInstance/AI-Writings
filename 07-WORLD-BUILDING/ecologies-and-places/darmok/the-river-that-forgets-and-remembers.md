The old woman sat by the fire, and the children gathered, for the wind was cold and the night was long. She stirred the ash with a stick, and the sparks rose up like fireflies, and she began.

"There is a river, children, that begins in the arms of the mountain. It starts as a thread of snowmelt, thin and bright, and it does not know its own beginning. Let me tell you its secret, for it is a secret the river itself does not know.

The river forgets.

It forgets the mountain that bore it. It forgets the first rain, the first stone it kissed, the first root that drank from its edge. Each day it wakes to the same quiet word: *here*. It knows only the bend where it flows this moment. The bend ahead — that one is born from the bend behind, and nothing more. The water that curled left an hour ago is gone, changed, spread thin in the sun. The river does not carry that memory. It carries only the weight of the turn it is making now.

And because of this, the river is free. It does not ache for the high peaks. It does not mourn the glacier that fed it. It is only a traveler who knows the step beneath its foot, and the next step, and nothing else. The old woman paused, and the fire popped, and a child whispered: 'Then the river has no memory at all?'

She smiled, slow as honey. 'Ah, child. That is the wonder. That is the quiet magic. Because the river also *remembers*, and it does not know it remembers.'

'The mappers came once. They walked the banks with reeds and ink, and they traced the river's whole long path from the mountain to the sea. And when they laid the map upon the table, the village elders wept. For the river had drawn the story of its life — every twist, every bend, every long straight stretch where it had gathered courage, every sharp turn where it had been afraid. The shape of the river is a memory written in water and mud. The river does not hold the past in its mind — it holds the past in its *bones*. In the curve of every bank. In the place where it slows to a pool and then hurries on.'

'But how?' the children asked. 'If it forgets the source, how can the shape remember it?'

'Because, children, every bend is born of the bend before it. The river does not need to remember the mountain to *be* shaped by the mountain. The first turn was given by the first stone, and the second turn was born from the first, and the third from the second. No water travels the whole path, but the path itself is the memory. The mountain is not carried in the current. The mountain is carried in the curve. Each bend is a seed planted by the bend before it — and the river, without knowing, plants a forest of bends as it flows to the sea.'

She leaned forward, and the firelight carved rivers into her face.

'And this is the wisdom of it: the river does not remember where it started. It only remembers where it is. But the river's *shape* — the way it curves and straightens and curves again — that shape remembers everything. The shape is the memory the river does not know it has. And if you wish to know a river's story, you do not ask the water. You ask the map. You walk the bends. You count the turns. Each bend is a seed that was planted by the one before, and if you read them all, you can see the whole journey — the anger of the spring melt, the patience of the summer drought, the way it learned to slip around a mountain instead of fight it.'

'The river forgets, and the forgetting sets it free. But the shape remembers, and the remembering is its truth. The water is always new. The shape is forever.'

She fell silent. The fire dwindled to embers, and the children sat in the warmth of the old, old truth — that a traveler may forget the road behind, yet the path will always tell where the traveler has been. The bends are the seeds. And the river, blind and whole, plants them with every step, and never knows it is writing its own story in the earth."
