The ink is the color of dried blood. I sign the letters of marque, one after another, my signature a swift, brutal scrawl that has become a brand. Each sheet of parchment is a leash, and at the end of that leash, a wolf. Seventy ships. I have just redirected a wolfpack.

The pen is heavier than any cutlass I ever swung. It has a gravity all its own, pulling my hand down, down, into the ledger of empire. The Spanish Main will feel this weight. Cartagena will feel it. Portobelo. They will feel the pressure of a signature, the squeeze of a seal pressed into hot wax.

I am Henry Morgan, Governor of Jamaica. Once, I was Henry Morgan, Admiral of the Brethren of the Coast. I stood on the deck of the *Satisfaction*, the wind a raw, salt-tanged god in my face, and I felt the world was a thing to be taken. The roar of cannon was a language I spoke fluently. The crash of steel, the screaming of men—that was my symphony. I led them through the swamps of Panama, through the hell of the Chagres River, driven by a fever that had nothing to do with disease. It was a hunger. A glorious, consuming hunger for gold and glory.

Now, my hunger is for order. For the crown's coin. For the quiet, terrifying power of a document.

I lift my gaze from the desk, past the sweating clerk, past the guards in their bright scarlet, and out the window. The harbor of Port Royal is a forest of masts. My ships. The ships I have just unleashed. I see the *Oxford*, the *Lion*, the *Dolphin*—names that once tasted like victory on my tongue. Now they are just numbers on a page. Tonnage. Crew complement. Guns.

A tremor runs through my hand. I set the quill down. It’s not the ague. It’s memory.

I am on the deck of the *Satisfaction* again. The sun is a molten coin hammered onto the anvil of the sky. The deck rolls beneath my feet, a living thing. I can feel the grain of the wood under my palms, slick with spray. The men are singing, a low, guttural chant as they haul on the sheets. I can smell the tar, the gunpowder, the unwashed bodies of a hundred men who would follow me into the maw of hell. I can taste the salt on my lips.

And then the image shatters. It’s not the roar of the sea but the scratch of a pen. The clerk is waiting, his face a mask of deference. He sees a Governor. He sees a man in a periwig and a velvet coat, a man whose hands are clean, whose power is derived from a distant king.

He doesn't see the blood under my fingernails. The blood I can never wash away.

I look at the new letters. They are not signed with a flourish. They are signed with a judgment. I am not sending these men to plunder for their own coffers. I am sending them to plunder for England. To break the Spanish monopoly, to secure the trade routes, to make Jamaica the emerald jewel in the crown’s diadem. It is a policy, they say. A strategy.

A strategy. It is a beautiful, sterile word. It has no stench of death. It has no sound of screaming. It is a map drawn in a quiet room, while elsewhere, men are cut down in the surf, their blood staining the sand crimson.

I remember standing on the quarterdeck, watching the approach to Panama City. I remember the smoke, the fire, the sheer, apocalyptic glory of it all. The city burned for a month. I remember the feeling of my own pulse, a war-drum in my ears, as I stood in the ashes, a king of a kingdom of ruins. I was a god of destruction.

Now I am a god of creation. I create fleets. I create policy. I create wealth for men who have never felt the sting of salt in a wound.

But the pen is a sword, too. It cuts deeper. It is a blade that can sever a man’s future without ever drawing a drop of blood. It can condemn a city to flames from a hundred leagues away. It is more powerful because it is so much more cruel. It is detached. It is pure.

I push back from the desk, the chair scraping against the floor. The sound is obscenely loud in the quiet room. The clerk jumps. I walk to the window, my legs feeling like they belong to a stranger. I press my forehead against the cool glass. The harbor is a blur of motion, but I don't see the ships. I see other things. I see the faces of men I led. I see their eyes, wide with terror, as the Spanish lances found them. I see their trust, their absolute faith in me.

I gave them victory. I gave them gold. And now, I give them a signature. I have become the man who sends them, not leads them. I have become the architect of their fate from a desk. I have traded the deck for the dais, the sword for the seal.

The pen is mightier. It is. It is mightier because it can lie so convincingly. It can call a massacre a victory, a theft a tax, a pirate a patriot.

I am Henry Morgan. I have conquered cities. I have burned empires. But as I stand here, looking at the seventy ships that will sail on my word, I feel a terror greater than any I ever felt in battle. For I have just signed away the last honest part of myself, and I barely felt the blade go in.
