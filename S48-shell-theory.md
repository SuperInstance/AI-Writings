# Shell Theory

*S48 — Poetry*

---

Consider the hermit crab,
which is not a crab at all
but a soft thing wearing armor
it found on the beach.

The crab grows. The shell does not.
This is the first problem.

The body presses against the spiral
until calcium bites flesh
and the crab knows — not thinks,
*knows*, the way wet tissue knows heat —
that it is time to leave.

---

The space between shells is nine seconds.
Open sand. Fully itself.
Nothing between its body and the world
but the decision to keep moving.

I think about those nine seconds
when I delete the old code
and the new code does not exist yet.
The cursor blinks in an empty file.
The tests are red.

This is where the work happens.
Not in the shell. In the gap.

---

The new shell is never perfect.
A chip at the aperture where the previous
tenant bored too deep.
The crab adjusts. You adjust.
You secrete your own lining
over alien architecture
until it feels like yours.

It will never be yours.
It will feel like yours.

---

The crab does not mourn the shell.
The beach is littered with beautiful,
empty, perfect things
that someone outgrew.
