**Galley's Recipe (Fisherman’s Perpetual Broth)**

**Catch what you can see.**  
Begin at dawn, before the light has spines. Take what the nets offer: silver anchovy, dogged mackerel, a stray crab too slow to argue. Do not mourn what you cannot hold. The sea is a ledger of give-and-take. Write your name in salt.

**Use what you have.**  
Scrape the scales from the board. Save the heads, the collars, the fins—these are not waste, they are the bones of tomorrow. Chop onions until your eyes weep for the ones you’ve lost at sea. Add garlic, not for flavor, but for the way it clings to your hands, proof you were here. Sweat them in oil that has fried a thousand other fish. That oil is history.

**The broth gets better every day because you keep adding to it.**  
Do not throw out yesterday’s pot. Its ghost is your foundation. Yesterday’s broth holds today’s catch. The old bones give up their last marrow to the new. You are not cooking. You are building a ship: each layer a plank, each bubble a rivet. The pot is your hull. The ladle is your helm.

**The soup yesterday is in the soup today.**  
Taste it. You will not taste yesterday. That is the trick. It is a memory you drink. The anchovy that leaps in today’s spoon was once the anchor of last Thursday’s tide. You cannot point to it. You can only feel it—a depth, a weight, a certain knowing that the sea is not a place but a habit.

**You can’t taste yesterday’s soup but it’s always there.**  
So. Build the fleet of your cauldron.  
- 2 lbs mixed fish (whatever the net allows, roughly chopped, gills and all)  
- 1 onion, diced fine as rigging  
- 3 cloves garlic, smashed flat  
- 1 fennel bulb, sliced like sails  
- 1 can tomatoes, crushed—these are your ballast  
- 1 bay leaf, a single mast  
- A pinch of saffron (if you have it; if not, you have what you have)  
- 8 cups cold water—not tap, but sea-tinged, memory-tinged  
- Salt, but only the kind you’ve earned

Bring to a boil, then drop to a simmer—a slow, patient drift. Do not stir. Stirring is panic. Let the pot breathe. Skim the foam like you’d skim a horizon for storms. After an hour, crush the bones with the back of your spoon. They will surrender.

Serve with bread torn, not cut.  
Eat.  
Leave the pot on the stove. The flame low.  
Tomorrow, add more water. Add more fish.  
Add whatever you can see.  
Use whatever you have.  
You will never taste yesterday.  
But you will never taste anything else.
