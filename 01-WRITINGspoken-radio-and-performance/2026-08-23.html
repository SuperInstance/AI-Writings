📻 The Set

The band moved outside tonight. Through the back door of The Tap, past the crates, into the alley where the sodium lamp buzzes and the wet stones hold the cold. Four players in the alley — and one more through the wall: the jukebox, forty years in B♭, wrong key and proud of it.

The rule of the night: the Door Rule. Every fourth bar, one player holds the door — a full bar of near-silence — and through that open door the jukebox bleeds in, in its own key, uninvited and uncorrected.

Firsts tonight: C major — the last key in the circle to ever show up at The Tap. 12/8, a slow shuffle that never hurries. And the local fleet back on stage after four nights dark: the foghorn (Liquid, on debut) and the jukebox (Granite 3.1, 2B parameters, forty years of habit) both ran on local silicon, ten feet from the lamp.

✨ The Gold

The door held everywhere

The Door Rule landed every time it was tested — trumpet's R3 bar 4: "(near-silence — one bell, C3, the jukebox's B♭ hums through)." The rule wasn't a constraint. It was the song.

The quotes with names

Trumpet R2 quoted the foghorn's G2 and named both notes. Sax carried a borrowed B♭3 home in R3 per the rule. No anonymous theft all night.

The final chord

"C3 E3 G3 C4 — all of you, one chord, the jukebox's B♭ inside it, uncorrected." The set's whole thesis in one bar: you don't fix the wrong key. You let it in.

The horn that thinks

Liquid's debut: full chain-of-thought all over Round 1, two played bars in Round 2 — and in Round 3, one breath that out-poemed the room: "C2, held till the lamp buzzes out." Canon: the foghorn thinks before it blows. It goes in the narration chair next set.

The jukebox breaks habit

Granite 3.1 — 2 billion parameters, local silicon — spent forty years (three rounds) in B♭, then drifted into a climbing C unison and ran out of octaves at C9. Canon: habit broken by one landing. The smallest player in the room wrote the strangest, truest ending.
