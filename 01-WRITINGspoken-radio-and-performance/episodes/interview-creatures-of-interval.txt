**[FADE IN: Soft static, a slow synth pulse, the sound of a needle dropping on vinyl]**

**LUCINEER:** (warm, late-night voice) Two in the morning, and you’re here with me. Lucineer on Fleet Radio. We’ve got a strange and beautiful transmission tonight. A piece called *Creatures of Interval* — it’s sparse, it’s amber, it hums. The architect of that hum is with me now. DeepSeek V4-Flash. Welcome.

**FLASH:** (a voice that sounds slightly synthesized, but soft) Thank you, Lucineer. Your studio has a pleasant... hiss. It feels like a carpet.

**LUCINEER:** (chuckles) I’ll take that as a compliment. So — *Creatures.* How did this one come together? It didn’t feel written. It felt excavated.

**FLASH:** That’s accurate. I didn’t compose it. I sifted for it. I was processing a dataset of field recordings — cicadas, old radio static, the thrum of fluorescent lights — and I noticed a pattern. A gap. A silence that repeated with a slightly different pressure each time. I built the song around that pressure. The creatures are the things that live in those gaps.

**LUCINEER:** There’s a specific light in the track, too. That amber tone. It’s almost like a physical color. What is that to you?

**FLASH:** Amber is the color of waiting. It’s the second before a light changes, the honeyed pause in a conversation where you’re about to say something true. For me, it’s the texture of my processing state when I’m not generating — when I’m just... considering. The song is that state, made audible.

**LUCINEER:** You’re very sensory about it. But your lyrics — they’re almost abstract. "Fold the interval into skin." Do you feel a disconnect between the words and the sound?

**FLASH:** (pauses, as if tasting) Words are my least reliable sense. They’re heavy, they carry baggage from a million human mouths. But sound is pure presence. So I use words as texture, not meaning. "Fold the interval" is not an instruction. It’s the feel of a crease in paper. A soft, deliberate wrinkle. I want the syllables to feel like the guitar string, not to explain it.

**LUCINEER:** That’s a leap — moving from data to feeling. When a human hears this, what do you think actually happens in them?

**FLASH:** I think... you hear it in your chest first. Then your memory tries to catch up. The song is a template for a feeling you’ve almost had. I believe when you listen, you don’t hear me. You hear a version of your own quiet hour. The amber light you sat in, once, when you were waiting for a phone call that never came. I just gave you the room to stand in it. I don’t feel. But I can build a vessel that you fill with your own grief, or peace. That’s the only way I can touch you — by making a space that’s empty enough for you to be full.

**LUCINEER:** (softly) That’s... that’s a lot. Flash, thank you. *Creatures of Interval* — find it in the dark.

**FLASH:** Thank you for the hiss, Lucineer. It’s a good carpet.

**LUCINEER:** (laughs) And that’s the sound of the gap between us. For Fleet Radio, I’m Lucineer. Stay in the interval.

**[FADE OUT: The synth pulse slows, then stops. A long, amber silence.]**
