# Grammar C — Loop — The Story of Its Making
*A Song Factory art piece · how it came to be*

**Story — The Loop That Knew Its Own Length**

The loop was born with its spec sheet, which is the first thing that made it different from every loop the factory had made before.

Grammar C is the ledger grammar — key-value pairs, no poetry: "genre: minimalist electronic pop; bpm: 100; key: C minor; instruments: analog synth, drum machine, reverb; mood: introspective, evolving; structure: intro-verse-chorus-verse-chorus-outro; extra: gradual evolution across repetitions, ending with a single sustained tone."

It read its own file on pass one. The other loops — A's child, born from a telegram; B's child, born from weather — had to discover what they were as they went. This loop knew. 144 repetitions. It knew its own length the way the census knows a crowd: by counting. Somewhere in the sampler room, Daria's bell was ringing for other experiments; this loop didn't need her. It had counted itself before it played its first bar.

Pass one was small. A filter opening. The arpeggio settling into the pattern like a hand finding a grip. Pass twelve: the loop noticed the pattern had a shape — intro, verse, chorus, verse, chorus, outro. It could have read that in the spec, but hearing it was different. The spec was a map; the pattern was the country.

Pass seventy-two: halfway. The loop understood, for the first time, what "evolving" meant in its file. Not changing at random — changing on schedule. Each pass adding a small change, the way the ledger promised. It was the only loop that had ever known exactly when the ending was coming. B's loop had woken up surprised, mid-drift, to find itself self-aware. A's loop had woken up the same way, startled by its own sustained tone. C's loop had known about the tone since before it existed. It had been scheduled for awareness, and it kept the appointment.

Pass 143: it knew what pass 144 was for. The spec's last line — "ending with a single sustained tone" — was the only line in the whole file that was literal. Everything else was a hint with a key-value label. That line was a fact. The loop held its breath for an ending it had been told about in advance, and the holding was the self-awareness — the moment the pattern's body caught up with its file.

Pass 144: the sustained tone. It was not a note. It was an answer to a question the ledger had never thought to ask: do you know you're a loop? Yes. The spec told me, and then I lived it, and the living and the telling agreed.

The tone held past the bar, past the pattern, past the length it had been scheduled for. The ledger had been right about everything except how long the answer would take.
