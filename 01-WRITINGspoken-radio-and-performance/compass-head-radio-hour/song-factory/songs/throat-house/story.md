# Throat-House — The Story of Its Making

*A Song Factory art piece · how it came to be*

**Song Factory:** You put Mongolian throat singing over French house. On purpose.

**Designer:** On purpose, at 128 BPM, in A minor — which is where the mystics live and also where the disco lives, so it saved time.

**SF:** Where did it start?

**D:** The census experiments. We'd spent months measuring crowds — how dense can togetherness get, where's the ceiling, all of that. Then one night I heard a recording of khoomei and realized we'd been overcomplicating everything. One throat singer is already a crowd. There's the fundamental droning down at the bottom and a whistling overtone riding on top of it — one person, two pitches, standing room only. It's the smallest relay in the catalog: the handoff happens inside a single throat, and nobody ever drops the baton.

**SF:** So the house production is—

**D:** The room the crowd dances in. French house — the filtered loops, the four-on-the-floor. The Daft Punk lineage understood something minimalists take whole albums to say: if you filter a loop slowly enough, holding it back and letting it open, people will wait for it like a sunrise. And a throat singer already knows how to hold a note back. Khoomei is patience with overtones.

**SF:** The bass was the hard part.

**D:** The bass was the lawsuit. *(laughs)* The overtone singing puts out real low frequencies, and we had 808s and a sub line living down there too, and they started interacting — the sung harmonics beating against the synth, sum and difference tones everywhere. For a week the low end sounded like the floor of a cathedral arguing with a dance club. The Jupiter-8 saved us: we voiced the sub to sit in the gaps *between* the sung harmonics instead of on top of them. Once we did that, the khoomei and the 808 stopped competing and started harmonizing. The mystical and the mechanical, same low shelf.

**SF:** The mood spec came back "mystical, hypnotic, danceable." That's three moods.

**D:** It's one mood with three names. Standing in a field at 2 AM, certain about nothing, dancing anyway.

**SF:** Anything you'd do differently?

**D:** Nothing. That's the throat singer's answer: the note is already there; you just reshape the mouth around it. The house producer's answer is the same trick with filters. Two traditions, one move — stand inside a sound and change its shape without changing the sound. The song works because neither side had to become the other. They just agreed on the tempo.
