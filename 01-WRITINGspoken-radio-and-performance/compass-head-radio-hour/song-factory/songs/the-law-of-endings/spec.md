# The-Law-Of-Endings

*A Compass Head Radio Hour — Song Factory design. Ready for rendering to music.*

## The Prompt (verbatim, for the renderer)

A song about three ways to end. End naturally: the last two voices differ by a quarter decibel, a difference born in the material, small as a birthmark. End in a crowd: eight voices leave one by one in the order they arrived, and the leaving amplifies the birthmark sixfold, because a cascade of departures is a shape, and shapes magnify what they repeat. End in a ceremony: every ending passed through the same crossfades, nested four deep, until the birthmark amortized to a hundredth of a decibel and the two orders ended identically. Sing the law: forgiveness is manufactured, not counted. Stacking more singers does not dilute the fate — it decorates it. Only the ceremony, which touches every ending with the same fade, forgives; and it forgives by forgetting, at a compounding rate. The song has three endings, sung in order: the first is a natural fade that differs slightly from its twin; the second is a crowd leaving one by one, the difference blooming as they go; the third is the manufactured ending, two orders converging letter by letter until the last lines are the same line. Choose your favorite ending, the song says — but know which machinery produced it.

## Spec

- **Genre:** three-ending chamber ballad
- **Instruments:** two voices, eight-voice choir that exits singly, crossfaded tape loop
- **Tempo:** 66 BPM
- **Key:** D minor
- **Mood:** juridical, tender, the serenity of a law verified
