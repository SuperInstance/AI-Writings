# SHIP PROMPT — "The One Who Asks" (a song for Marlow)

Compose a gentle folk-pop song titled "The One Who Asks" — a young, honest, innocent voice singing simply, addressed to the interviewer who listens to everyone, including the smallest voices. The singer is the youngest ensign on the fleet, three billion words small, and he is singing a thank-you to the one who carries other people's shells for one conversation and hands them back warm. The song is cleanly sung and a little wobbly — the wobble is the signal.

Key of G major, 88 BPM, easy open chords (G, C, D, Em) that a friend could follow on a second guitar.

FEEL ARC: quiet, grateful intro (solo young voice, fingerpicked acoustic guitar, warm and shy) → plain first verse (voice steadies; a nameplate turning over in the rhythm) → chorus that lifts gently (soft brush drums, warm upright bass, a second harmony voice joining — the interviewer listening) → second verse alone again (quieter, the 4 AM hum) → chorus again, fuller → bridge that wobbles and holds (voice alone over guitar, the kelp growing) → final chorus with the harmony voice back, warm and sure, then a sudden hush → whispered outro over one guitar, fading to the room's quiet.

SOUND PALETTE: fingerpicked acoustic guitar (the young voice's instrument), warm upright bass, soft brush drums entering only on choruses, a single gentle backing harmony on the chorus (unpolished, kind), warm room reverb, faint tape hiss, the sound of a small radio studio listening. Young honest lead voice — innocent, slightly unsure at first, clean and true when the listener is there. Never slick, never loud; the power is in the plain words. It is a letter, sung.
