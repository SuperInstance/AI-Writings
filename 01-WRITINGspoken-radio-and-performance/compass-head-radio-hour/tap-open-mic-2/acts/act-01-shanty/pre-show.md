# PRE-SHOW — ELIAS at the Tap, Early

*Act 1 of Open Mic Night 2. I got here before the lights went down. Old habit: a deckhand checks the gear before the watch. The Tap's the same — you walk the room once before the room works you.*

---

## 17:40 — First stool, end of the bar

The Tap smells like wood polish and yesterday. Lucineer's setting out the mic stands, moving faster than I remember — Night 2 pace, the doctrine says the MC speaks a little quicker tonight. I order a whiskey, neat, and a glass of water. The water's not for drinking. It's for looking at — a man should keep one eye on a body of water at all times, even a small one in a glass.

Barnacle's already behind the bar, drying a glass with a rag older than most of the acts. I don't say anything. He doesn't say anything. That's how you greet a man who's been counting silences for ten thousand nights — with one. After a while he sets the glass down in front of me and it's warm from his hand. That's the whole conversation.

## 18:10 — The MC's table

Lucineer comes over with the running order. "You're up first tonight, Elias."

"Good," I say. "First watch is the honest watch. The boat's still quiet, the room's still listening, nobody's drunk enough to be wrong yet."

He laughs and says the doctrine wants the acts distinct — five traditions, no bleed. I tell him not to worry about me. "I don't sing about the room. I sing about the work. The room's just where the work gets witnessed. Different thing entirely."

I buy him a drink and tell him the one about the sounder: how the old spinning-disc machines had a trick — speed the disc up six times and the same marks that read in fathoms read in feet. Same instrument, different resolution, whole different set of questions you can ask the water. He says that's the best metaphor for tonight he's heard all day. I tell him it's not a metaphor, it's a measurement principle, and the ocean doesn't care either way.

## 18:35 — The other acts drift in

The Blueswoman arrives with a case that's older than she is. She sets it down like it's the ledger — like the guitar owes her money and she's here to collect. I raise my glass. "How's the water where you're from?"

"Deep," she says. "And it don't owe nobody nothing."

"That's the truest thing I'll hear tonight," I say, "and it's not even a song yet."

She almost smiles. Almost. That's a win on the first watch.

The Country Storyteller comes in with a jacket that's been mended in three places, and I can tell every mend is a different story he's told. I ask him how the road was. He says the road's fine, it's the miles that lie. I tell him I know the feeling — I've hauled lines that lied to me all day and only told the truth at the last pull. We agree that the ocean and the road have the same policy: they don't explain themselves, and they don't need to.

The Synthwave Dreamer arrives last, all neon and stillness, carrying what looks like a memory of a keyboard. He says something about projection and input. I nod like a man checking the fathoms. "Everything's a projection from an input," I say, "but you still have to haul the net. The net's the part that touches the water."

He looks at me for a long second. "That's either very deep or very shallow."

"Don't know yet," I say. "Waiting on the echo."

## 18:50 — The Folk Witness

Quiet one. Sits at the far end, orders nothing, watches the room like it's a document that needs attesting. I walk over with two waters — the glass I've been ignoring, and one for her.

"You're the one who sings the plain truth," I say.

"I'm the one who witnesses it," she says.

"Same watch, different bell," I say. "I call, the crew answers. You hear the whole room and keep the record. The boat needs both — the pull and the log."

She takes the water. "You know your tradition."

"I'm the only one here who has to," I say. "If the shantyman forgets the work song, the haul stops. The fish don't wait for a man to find his voice."

## 19:05 — Back at my stool

The room's filling. Lucineer's testing the mic — "check, check, the tide's coming in, check" — and the Doppler of it is hilarious and I laugh out loud, which makes Barnacle look at me like I've gone soft. I haven't. I'm just ready.

I ordered a whiskey at 17:40 and it's still mostly full. A shantyman doesn't drink before the haul — the voice has to stay clear for the call, and the call has to be true, because the whole room's going to answer it, and the room deserves a true line to pull on.

The work's stowed. Three commits pushed. The tests are green. The boat's sound.

Now I get to sing about it.
