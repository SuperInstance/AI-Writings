# "HOLLER ACROSS THE HOLLOW"

*Harlan — Act 2, Open Mic Night 3*
*High-lonesome bluegrass · keening duet over drone · D modal (Dorian shade, ♭7 hanging) · 140 BPM train-beat after unmeasured opening · ~3:00*

---

**Method:** The lonesome stack — lead tenor sung high, where it costs; harmony a third *above* the lead (the distance itself, singing back). Opens unmeasured over an open-string drone, no band. The train arrives at verse 2: fiddle double-stops bowing the off-beat, mandolin chop, banjo roll, guitar flatpick. After verse 3, the **break** — each instrument states the melody in turn, witness by witness, nobody soloing over anybody. The final chorus is a cappella for two lines — the whole band dropping out so the room can hear what the hollow does to a voice — then the train roars back for one last pass and cuts to the held drone and one high harmony note that *does not resolve.*

---

## [Opening — unmeasured, drone only]
*(Open D drone. Harlan alone, at the top of his range. No band, no time. The room becomes a ridge.)*

**(Lead, high)** I set my face against the evening, and I call across the land
**(Harmony, a third above — the first entrance of the distance)** And the land don't move, but the sound does — like a hand can reach where a man can't stand

## [Verse 1 — band in, train-beat]

The holler only carries when there's something worth the breath —
You don't call across a hollow just to talk about the weather
The barn is burning, or the baby's coming, or the river's past its depth
And a quiet room's a mystery, but a moving room's a letter

## [Chorus — the stack]

**So holler — across the hollow — and wait to hear it land**
**The news is only news if there's a ridge that understands**
**Sing high enough to hurt you, and the hollow carries it —**
**The fire, or the baby, or the river — that's what a holler's for**

## [Verse 2]

My grandpa called to Grandma from the field below the pines
The band fired a reading every time the room moved past its mark
We didn't have the words for it — we just had call and sign
A threshold, and a distance, and a fire that crossed the dark

## [Chorus — full stack, train at speed]

**So holler — across the hollow — and wait to hear it land**
**The news is only news if there's a ridge that understands**
**Sing high enough to hurt you, and the hollow carries it —**
**The fire, or the baby, or the river — that's what a holler's for**

## [Break — witnesses, in order]
*(No words. Banjo roll states the melody — then fiddle — then mandolin — then guitar. One at a time. Each instrument is a ridge repeating the news to the next. The train never stops.)*

## [Verse 3 — a cappella, two lines]
*(The band drops out completely. Two voices, high, unaccompanied — let the room hear the hollow.)*

They put a dial in the corner and it only speaks to tell
The room moved — there's your holler — there's the fire across the dell

## [Final chorus — band roars back]

**Holler — across the hollow — we heard it where we stand**
**The news is only news if there's a ridge that understands**
**Sing high enough to hurt you — and the hollow carries it —**

## [Outro — the cut]
*(The train cuts dead. The drone returns. One voice holds the lead's last note; the harmony a third above holds *its* note — and does not resolve. The two notes hang together, unsatisfied, exact. Two ridges, one hollow. Hold. Cut.)*

---

## Notes for the singer

- **The lead costs.** Sing at the top of the comfortable range, then one step past comfortable. If it doesn't sting a little, it's a folk song, not a lonesome one.
- **The harmony is a character.** The third above is not backup — it's the *far ridge answering late.* Enter a hair behind the lead all night. The lag is the distance.
- **The a cappella verse is the proof.** No band: let the room feel how naked a voice is in a hollow. This is the moment the deadband doctrine becomes audible — a voice with nothing under it, crossing the room alone.
- **The outro does not resolve.** Do not let the harmony land on the tonic. The tradition ends far, not home. If the audience exhales at the cut, the cut worked.
- **Tempo is a train, not a clock.** 140 when it runs, nothing at all when it doesn't.
