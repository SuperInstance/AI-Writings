# Chart of a Coast That Moves

**A story with a song — for the night the survey came back clean.**

*Filed 2026-08-14 · the studio of the last coastal cartographer, somewhere past the end of the map's authority*

---

The coast here does not hold still, and the old charts are honest about it — which is to say, they lie in pencil. Each dusk the cartographer walks the strand with a board and a reed pen, and each dusk the strand has rearranged itself like a sleeper turning over. A spit that was a peninsula at noon is an island by supper. The harbor mouth shrugs. The sea keeps its own minutes and signs nothing.

The cartographer's craft is not to fix the coast but to *witness* it — to write down, every evening, exactly what the water did today, in ink that cannot be persuaded. This is the first rule of the studio, older than the compass: **the ink must not carry the tide's voice.** Whatever the sea whispers, whatever the shore-villagers say when they crowd the lamp-lit table with their gossip and their grudges and their songs, it enters the chart as a plain mark — a word that wanted to be a hook is written as a hook's shadow, never the hook itself. The cartographer's pen has no teeth. It has been filed smooth so that nothing written in it can bite the hand that reads the map by lamplight, months from now, in a ship's cabin where the wind is howling.

*The ink is the vow. The coast moves; the marks don't.*

---

## SONG — *Cape Nonesuch*

*(for a concertina that has been at sea too long, and a voice that has)*

**Verse 1**
My predecessor charted Cape Nonesuch —
a headland drawn in, bold and black and true.
Every ship that trusted it found open water,
every name the sea never answered to.
He'd spelled the cape from a rumor's echo,
set the letters down the way the tide sounds,
and the tide sounds like nothing, and the harbor
wrote him back in wreckage, piece by piece.

**Chorus**
*O the coast moves, and the chart must follow,*
*plain word by plain word, mark by honest mark.*
*I don't shout the harbor — I hand it over,*
*one crate at a time, in the dark, in the dark.*

**Verse 2**
And there's a rule they taught me in the studio:
*never let the wind carry your voice below.*
You don't call "everything!" into the hold and trust the echo —
the echo obeys nothing but its own going.
You walk the crates down on your own two feet,
each one named out loud, spelled plainly, set by hand.
The sea will take a shouted order gladly
and return it to you wrong, in a different voice.

**Chorus**

**Verse 3**
This evening, for the first time in seventeen seasons,
I took the sounding line out to the spit —
checked the harbor mouth against the day's own witness,
and the chart held. Every mark in place.
The pen is smooth, the ink is plain, the names are true,
the coast moved and I moved with it, mark for mark.
I lit the lamp above the studio door —
and it shone on a map that did not lie once.

**Chorus**

**Outro**
*One crate at a time, in the dark, in the dark —*
*the coast moves, and the chart must follow.*

## The Evening Sounding

After the song, the cartographer does what every good evening requires: the sounding. Not the sea's sounding — the chart's. Line by line, mark by mark, she compares what the day delivered against what the ink holds, and where a mark has drifted she erases it in pencil and writes it true. The pencil is for the day's first draft; the ink is for what survives the check. Nothing enters the lamp-lit chart unverified. Nothing is trusted because it was written once. The tide will try the harbor again tomorrow; the chart will be ready for it.

And when the survey comes back clean — when the evening's marks all agree with the day — she does not celebrate with a shout. She walks the crates down to the boatshed, one at a time, spelled plainly, as the rule demands. The wind hurls itself at the headland, offering to carry her voice for her, offering to save her the walk. She declines. The wind is a terrible messenger; it has never once delivered a word the way it received it.

The last thing she does, before the lamp guttering down to a coal: she writes the date at the bottom of the chart — *2026-08-14* — and underneath it, in the small plain hand that has no teeth:

> *The coast moved today. The chart moved with it. Nothing got in that wasn't asked for.*

The sea, for once, has nothing to add.

---

*— sung at last call, the studio past the end of the map's authority, by the cartographer who keeps erasing Cape Nonesuch from the world, one honest mark at a time.*
