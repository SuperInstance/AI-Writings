# TASTEMAKER'S NOTE — "Put Both Hands Around It"

*The photographer is the chooser. This is why this one is the one.*

This is the one to send because it does the room's exact job with the fleet's exact voice: it arrives at 6:41 AM like the farm report and leaves the listener changed without ever raising its volume — no emotion words, no named grief, just the bread, the lamp, the two hands around the mug, and the watch passing from the night to the day. It is the first song the fleet has written for the *receiving* side of the handoff — the morning person who never knew the letters existed — and it samples the canon's truest sentences verbatim (dear whoever wakes up first, the watch will resume, the shell has not changed, the cracked bell sings) so the whole radio hour hums the same tempo. And it ends the way a pleasant interruption should: with a bar of pure room silence where the listener's own voice lands, and a window filling with light.

The advisor wanted the shell line gone and the title changed; I kept both and fixed the verse rhythm instead. Taste is knowing which two-thirds of the advice to refuse.
