# THE FLOOR REMEMBERS

*Barn dance closer. Audience B — the hall on the gravel road, 10:52 PM.*
*~3:00 · D major → E major (final chorus) · 126 BPM · stomp-clap drive, fiddle-led.*

---

**[Intro — fiddle alone, the old tune, 4 bars; then bass and boot-stomp roll in like a train finding its wheels. Over the stomp, the caller, spoken:]**
One more. One more for the floor.

**[Verse 1 — Walt]**
Walt keeps rosin in his pocket, keeps his father's count in mind —
Seventy-eight and teaching every kid he can find:
*One-two-three, one-two-three,* like the caller called before the war.
The boards out by the stage are worn to silver in an arc —
He tells the children, patting the floor:
"That's not wear. That's memory.
It only counts while feet keep making more."

**[Verse 2 — June]**
June has worn blue every Friday since Earl's hands went still,
But the last dance always finds her, and it always will —
Nell is standing on her boots, arms up, four feet flat on two,
Turning through the arc that Earl's boots helped to wear.
His hands were like his hammer handle: worn exactly where he held it.
The wood remembers how it was held. She is not leaving this floor.

**[Chorus — full band; the caller calls, the room answers]**
CALL: CIRCLE UP — the last one's now!
ROOM: Boots and babies, ring it round!
ALL: A song outlives the throat that hummed it first,
So hum it loud and pass it down —
Lamps are low, the bass walks on,
The room remembers every sound —
CALL: And the floor remembers us!
ROOM: THE FLOOR REMEMBERS US!

**[Verse 3 — Ruby]**
Ruby's at the doorframe, phone-light on her face:
"This whole hall's a museum, Gramps. Why keep up the place?"
Walt laughs and taps the silver arc:
"Museums keep the dead, child. Count." — *One-two-three.*
"Roy — start the tune again. The music's still there."
And June holds out two hands, and Ruby — Ruby steps in.

**[Bridge — everything drops but the bass and the circle, hushed]**
The caller drops his voice. The ring holds its breath:
"Every hand that built this room is dancing in it yet."
Sixty kids and grandmothers, swaying in the dark,
And Ruby holds her grandpa's stance now, planted in the arc —
Now the fiddle finds the note…
Now the lamps come up…
CALL: Gravel road —
ROOM: SCUFF THE FLOOR!
CALL: Gravel road —
ROOM: SCUFF THE FLOOR!
*(one stomp — the whole ring, together)*
NOW —

**[Final Chorus — key change to E major, roof off, every voice]**
CIRCLE UP — the last one's now!
Boots and babies, ring it round!
A song outlives the throat that hummed it first,
So hum it loud and pass it down —
Lamps are low, the bass walks on,
The room remembers every sound —
And the floor remembers us!
THE FLOOR REMEMBERS US!
*(THE FLOOR REMEMBERS US!)*

**[Outro — band out; one voice, then one fiddle]**
The dance is over. The dance will resume.
The dance is over… the dance will resume…
*(sung soft, almost to yourself — the fiddle takes the phrase once more, alone, over gravel crunch and coats)*
The floor remembers.
*The floor remembers us.*

## Doctrine check
- **Photographs, no labels** — no line says community, tradition, or family. Rosin, silver arcs, blue dresses, hammer handles, phone-light, four feet on two boots. The one aphorism ("Museums keep the dead, child. Count.") is character dialogue, answered immediately by a count and an invitation, never by an argument.
- **One named speaker per verse** — Walt, June, Ruby. The bridge belongs to the caller; the chorus belongs to the room.
- **Samples the fleet canon** — nine samples above, each in its own register; families hear a barn, the Radio Hour audience hears the archive.
- **Call-and-response native** — the count, the chorus call/room split, and the bridge's "Gravel road — SCUFF THE FLOOR!" exchange. The room is a character with lines.
- **The lift is structural** — spoken intro → driving verses → shouted chorus → the clash goes half-density → the bridge hushes to bass-and-breath → call-and-response → synchronized stomp → key change, roof off → then the law of the room: it ends quiet, one fiddle, for the coats.
- **It swings** — 126 BPM stomp-clap country swing; grandparents two-step it, eight-year-olds hop it, and the thesis line gets room to land in the melody.
