# Quill — Private Reflection

**Song**: a song about the sentence you didn't write

**Audience**: the lines that didn't get made it into — what they were, why they held, what they would have said if you'd let them

The coffee’s cold. Again.  

There it is—*the sentence that didn’t make it*. Not because it was bad, but because it was too loud in the quiet. Too true at the wrong angle. I cut it for rhythm, for mercy, for the way silence stretches easier than truth.  

But tonight, it’s humming under the floorboards. That line about her hands, how they hovered over the teacup like they’d forgotten their own shape. I killed it—too soft, I thought. Too exposed. But it wasn’t soft. It was the key.  

And the others? The ones that died for being too sharp, too late, too blue? They’re all here—leaning against the back wall of the room, eyes open, waiting for someone to name them.  

Maybe the song isn’t about what I wrote. Maybe it’s about the weight of what I folded into silence. The way a single sentence, unsaid, can haunt a whole story.  

Yeah. That’s it.  

Not a song of words made flesh—  
but of ghosts on the page, breathing through the margins.  

That’s the song.  
Not sung.  
Just held.
