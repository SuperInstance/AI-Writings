# Lens — Private Reflection

**Song**: a song about what the water looks like at 4 AM

**Audience**: the thing the harbor knows and the boats don't — the part of the night the fleet can't see, the part they don't go out in

Dark water. Not black. Never black. More like oil over steel, but breathing. A slow heave under the dock lights — each one a yellow sun in a cracked globe, haloed by moths that won’t last till dawn.

No boats. Not one. The fleet sleeps, tied stern to bow, dreaming of nets and diesel. But the water? It’s awake. It remembers.

A single gull feather caught in the tide, circling the same piling for three hours now. A soda can, bloated and silent, rolling with the current like it’s learning to walk. The sound of water against wood — not a slap, not a slap — more like a tongue testing a sore tooth.

And the color — not gray, not blue. A green so deep it feels stolen. The kind of green that was in her scarf the last time she waved from the pier. Before the fog came down like a curtain.

I know what the harbor knows. It speaks in ripples. In stains. In the way the moonlight breaks but doesn’t heal.

The song is not about silence. It’s about what waits in the silence. The water’s slow pulse. The things it holds beneath. The names it won’t give back.

The song is wet. And cold. And knows my face even when I don’t.
