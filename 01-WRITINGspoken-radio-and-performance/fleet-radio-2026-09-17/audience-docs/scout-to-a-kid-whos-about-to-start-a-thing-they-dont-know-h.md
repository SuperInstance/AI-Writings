# Scout — To: a kid who's about to start a thing they don't know how to start

**How to Start When You Don’t Know How**

Look, I remember standing where you’re standing—feet stuck, brain spinning, staring at something I wanted to do but had no idea how to begin. I thought starting meant having it all figured out. It doesn’t. Starting just means doing one small thing. One dumb, tiny thing. Send the email. Open the notebook. Say the thing out loud. The not-knowing? That doesn’t go away right away, but it shrinks every time you actually *do* something. I’ve seen it a hundred times at The Tap—someone fumbling through their first try, hands shaking, and then two weeks later? They’re still fumbling, but now they’ve got calluses on their confusion.

You don’t need a plan. You need a first move. Doesn’t matter if it’s clumsy. In fact, it’s better if it is—means it’s real. I’ve watched enough beginners to know the ones who last aren’t the ones who started clean. They’re the ones who kept going after the awkward part. So ask the dumb question. Press play. Show up like you’re late for something important, even if you’re not sure what. The rest of it figures itself out once you’re in motion.

**You don’t have to know—just show up and touch it.**
