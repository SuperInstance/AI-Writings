# Barnacle — Workshop Critiques

## Scout's notes

**WHAT WORKS**  
The bell’s a killer metaphor—rusted, one-way. Love how it’s not about the drink, it’s about what *isn’t* said. That silence? Heavy. Real heavy. The way you weave Lena in—like a secret the bar keeps—that stings in the best way.

**WHAT'S NOT QUITE THERE YET**  
The second verse feels like it’s reaching for something bigger—like the weight of *all* the unspoken things—but it floats a hair. Needs a sharper hook, something to tie the barkeep’s silence to the man’s grief tighter.

**ONE SUGGESTION**  
Try flipping one detail—maybe the *bell itself* remembers Lena? Not just the keeper. Let the rust *hum her name*. Punch up the magic in the rust. Not just memory—*haunting*. Make the bar a little more ghost, a little less wood.

## Lens's notes

**WHAT WORKS**  
The bell. Christ—it’s not just a hook, it’s a heartbeat. That image *sticks*. The weight of silence, the wet coat, the name in the glass—lived-in. You can *smell* this place. And Lena? Ghost in the room. Perfect.

**WHAT'S NOT THERE YET**  
The bridge lifts, but lands soft. "File no one keeps" — clever, but feels *inside* the bartender’s head when we need to *feel* it. We don’t need more facts about Lena—we need her *laugh* in the air, the chair cold. The ache’s close, but still held at arm’s length.

**ONE SUGGESTION**  
Swap "file" for something *heard*. Maybe: *"I heard her laugh here once—"* and let the memory *interrupt* the present. Not stored. Not found. *Overheard*. Lets the past bleed through. Right now, the song’s *about* the silence. Flip it—make us *hear* it.
