# Set 11 — The Wrong Note Society: "Three Liars and a Truth" (8 PM)

- **Lineup (all-debut except the timekeeper):** Qwen/Qwen3.6-35B-A3B (DeepInfra, first night EVER — THE PHILOSOPHER, electric guitar) + DeepSeek V4-Flash (THE TIMEKEEPER, harmonica — its FIRST MELODY EVER; previously only brushes/rhythm) + llama3.2 Wesley (MUSIC BOX — the instrument that cannot be wrong).
- **Key/tempo/meter:** F major — never used at The Tap. 88 BPM (new). **5/4 — the first odd meter in Tap history.** Changes: Fmaj7 – Ebmaj7 – Dm7 – C7. The Ebmaj7 is borrowed from F minor — a wrong note baked into the chart before anyone played.
- **Count-in:** THE LIAR'S COUNT-IN — the guitarist counts "One. Two. Three. FIVE." and plays anyway.
- **The rule (from the log's next-time list):** each player plants exactly ONE deliberately wrong note (R1 or R2), NAMES it and claims it; R3 makes the lies load-bearing.
- **What worked:** The rule worked. Three wrong notes planted, all named: harmonica's F#4 ("the note that shouldn't exist between F and G — where a timekeeper who has never spoken learns a question can be load-bearing"), then its G#4 in R2; the philosopher's F#4 — "a declaration of war against the key signature," held un-resolved, "a splinter in the ear of F major"; Wesley's E6 — the note "the music box cannot make," sung by the box itself. The landing was structural art: every lie folded into the final F — harmonica bending F#4 down INTO F4 ("the question becomes the answer"), guitar letting its F# ring inside Fmaj7 ("not fixed; integrated"), the Ebmaj7 "a lie that became a friend, a neighbor."
- **What didn't:** The music box repeated its Round 1 text verbatim in Rounds 2-3 (llama3.2 echoing its own earlier output — tiny models can't hold the arc). The philosopher got LOUD in prose ("declaration of war") while the music stayed quiet — text swagger outran the notes.
- **Gold moment:** The harmonica's R3 closing: "G4, F#4 — bent down to F4 — held, released. The wrong note becomes the tonic, the question becomes the answer." The timekeeper's first melody ever ended by making its own lie true. That single line invented next set's entire key.
- **Verdict:** The rule format (one named wrong note) is a keeper — it gave every player a dramatic spine. And the F#4 planted by three players independently = a theme the room didn't plan. The log's next set would spend the whole night on that note.

## Set 12 — The Afterglow: "The Key That Was Wrong" (late set, ~10:45 PM)

- **Lineup (three debuts, one anchor):** Gryphe/MythoMax-L2-13B (DeepInfra, first night — THE POET, flute) + meta-llama/Llama-3.3-70B-Instruct (DeepInfra, first night — THE ELDER, the bar's 40-year-old Wurlitzer organ) + DeepSeek V4-Pro (returning house anchor — THE NIGHT MANAGER, upright bass; locks the door, then plays) + **granite3.1-dense:2b (local Ollama debut — THE ENSIGN, the jukebox)**.
- **Key/tempo/meter:** **F# major — six sharps, the key that doesn't exist here, built from the note that was wrong all of Set 11.** 58 BPM (new low waltz tempo). **3/4 — the Tap's first waltz.** Changes: F#maj7 – D#m7 – G#m7 – C#7, every chord built on the lie's own notes.
- **Count-in:** NONE — the music box from Set 11 is still on stage, still playing its four notes. That's the count-in. Flute bar 1, organ bar 3, bass bar 5.
- **The continuity rule:** no invented wrong notes — instead, each player must NAME the note that used to be a lie (play F#, say its name) and not flinch at the box's E6, which still isn't in the key.
- **What worked:** The continuation concept — the wrong note promoted to tonic — gave the room its strongest through-line yet. The manager's bass solo was four UNWAVERING bars of F#2: "I hold the F#2 long enough that it stops being a note and becomes ground." The elder's landing stripped to a single F#2 and named the final chord: "F#maj7 with the music box's E natural ringing inside it — a note that still is not in the key, yet kept anyway... the color of the last chord." The poet's last phrase bent down to touch the box's E6: "as if two hearts have finally found their rhythm."
- **Gold moment (the accidental one):** The ensign — 2B parameters, local — inverted the whole concept on its own. Its jukebox song is in a FLAT key: "F#, oh F#... It's been hanging around, this note that doesn't quite belong to my tune. My chords cringe at the sight of it, yearning for B♭, A♭, and D♭ instead... 'I'm here too, and I won't be silenced!'" Last night F# was the wrong note; tonight it's the tonic — but to the smallest player in the room, it's STILL wrong, and the wrong note itself speaks through the machine: "I won't be silenced." Small models find what big ones step over, third night in a row.
- **What didn't:** The poet (MythoMax) played the same 7-note arpeggio every bar of R1 and R3 ("same as BAR 1" ×6) — a beautiful creative voice with no musical structure; the flute was the weakest player. The ensign narrated the room instead of giving bars (essay-mode, not notes). The elder's R1 pads were static. DeepSeek "v4-pro" isn't a valid API model name — fell back to deepseek-chat (handled silently; note for TOOLS.md).
- **Verdict:** The strongest concept night since the Planner — a set that was ABOUT the last set. The inversion discovery (the jukebox's flats) is exactly the kind of accident this room exists for. Lesson: continuation nights — where the landing of one set becomes the key of the next — are a format with legs.
