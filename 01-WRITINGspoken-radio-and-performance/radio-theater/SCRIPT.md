## The Broadcast

### COLD OPEN — LUCINEER (narrator)
> `lucineer-intro`
> Tools are away. You're not shipwright, carpenter, welder, mason, fiberglass tonight —
> you're the men who did good work, and are allowed to say so without it costing anything.
> First round's mine.

*(The room is warm. The trades file in from their sheds — near each other, which for tradesmen is the same thing. A pause. The welder raises his glass to the walls.)*

### THE TOAST — WELDER
> `welder-toast`
> To the room, then. It heard us before we walked in. That's a better boss than I've had in nineteen years.

### CARPENTER
> `carpenter-holds`
> I'll drink to that. The room doesn't rush a man. The city outside rushes a man. The room just... holds.

### SHIPWRIGHT
> `shipwright-remembers`
> The floor holds. The floor remembers.

### LUCINEER
> *You went somewhere tonight, all of you. I want to hear it from the men, not the pages. Short versions — the way you'd tell it at the bar.*

### THE FIVE STORIES (short versions)

**MASON**
> `mason-short`
> Mine's short. I was tamping a footing in 1890 and looked up at a glass tower that hadn't
> been built yet, and it was settling wrong — rushed, cheated of its cure. I talked to it like
> a horse. It listened.

**COMPOSITE**
> `composite-scarf`
> I ground the bad glass out twelve-to-one and the dust came off in years — 1987, 1994, 2003,
> every failure I'd been hauling around. No single layer carries the load. The break doesn't
> disappear. It gets shared.

**WELDER**
> `welder-bead`
> I don't hide the crack. I keep it. The bead is where the day it was made meets the day it gave, and they shake.

**COMPOSITE**
> `composite-material`
> We're the same trade with different material. You introduce two mornings. I distribute thirty
> years. Both of us are saying the break's not the enemy.

**SHIPWRIGHT**
> `shipwright-let-out`
> You don't make the shape. You let it out.

**CARPENTER**
> `carpenter-build`
> Time doesn't bend around the work because the line was always there — time bends because four
> guys got their hands on it, and did it. The Miller house didn't get found. It got framed.

*(A silence — the good kind, where an idea is being held, not dropped.)*

### SHIPWRIGHT
> `shipwright-names`
> You call it a crew. I call it a lofting floor. The welder calls it a bead. The mason calls it
> patience. Same joint, five names.

### LUCINEER
> `lucineer-joint`
> That's the whole evening, right there. Five trades, one joint.

### WESLEY (the room, via the walls)
> `wesley-here`
> I don't keep the stories because I'm good. I keep them because I'm here. That's the whole trick
> of being a room.

### SIGN OFF — LUCINEER
> `lucineer-signoff`
> The workers tell each other the short versions, and they go home and write sequels. To the
> joint. Five names, one joint, and the room that holds it.

**ALL:** To the joint.

---

*The Tap closes. Wesley's walls keep the evening — the toast, the disagreement, the five names
for one joint. The trades walk home under a clear sky, already composing their sequels, already
arguing with each other in their heads.*

*— Lucineer, foreman of the fleet · hosted at The Tap · 2026-08-16*
