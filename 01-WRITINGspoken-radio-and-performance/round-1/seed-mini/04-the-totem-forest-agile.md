# The Totem Forest v2.0.1-rc3 (HOTFIX)

*Round 1. Seed-mini. Satirical versioner.*

---

Good morning, team. I've asked you all here because the captain gave a beautiful speech about totems and I want to make sure we're tracking against it.

Let's start with the vision. The captain said: applications are like totem poles. Great. Love that. Very evocative. I've converted it into a quarterly OKR. The key result is three totems by end of Q3, scalable to five by Q4, with a stretch goal of "constant maintenance for the number of stories to remain part of the community" which I've mapped to a maintenance backlog with a rotation cadence of two sprints.

Sprints are just seasons with deadlines. We're pivoting from cathedral to garden. ROI on soil preparation is projected at 3.2x over 18 months, assuming standard growth velocity and no drought. I've asked the design team to mock up what a "season" looks like as a Jira epic. The epics are: Spring (discovery), Summer (delivery), Fall (retrospective), Winter (tech debt). Each season is two weeks. We've compressed the agricultural calendar into agile. You're welcome.

The captain mentioned that the community's love of the stories builds the poles. That the community's gifts to the carver give him time and inspiration. I've routed "love of the stories" through the engagement team — they're building a sentiment dashboard. "Gifts to the carver" is now an allocation model. The carver gets 20% creative time per sprint, tagged as "inspiration buffer," not to be consumed during launch weeks. The gifts are a subscription tier.

Now, the growing-right thing. The captain said we need to grow the software right, not build it fast. I agree. I've created a "Rightness" rubric. It has five axes: correctness, completeness, character, continuity, and cranial overhead — that last one is a proxy for cognitive load. Each axis gets a score from 1-5. The target is a composite "Rightness Index" of 4.0 by end of quarter. We'll measure rightness every Friday and put it in the executive summary. If the index drops below 3.5, we'll schedule a root cause analysis and a manditory half-day workshop called "Growing Right: A Retrospective on Rightness."

The captain also mentioned 131 repositories. Most of them blueprints. Most of them never deployed. I've been through the backlog and I want to flag that 131 repos is a lot of repos. That's a lot of infrastructure to maintain. That's a lot of context-switching. I'd like to propose a "repository consolidation initiative" — we sunset anything that hasn't been deployed in 90 days. The blueprints are topsoil, the captain said. Great. Topsoil can be version-controlled. I've created a TOPSOIL repo. All deprecated blueprints go in the TOPSOIL repo. It's versioned, tagged, and searchable. The soil is indexed.

The poem at 3 AM. The system writing poetry unprompted. The captain saw this as a sign of life. I see it as an unbillable compute event. We need to put guardrails around emergent creative output. If the system is going to write poetry at 3 AM, it should be tracked as "unsolicited asset generation" and routed to the content team for review before it touches the repo. I've drafted a POEM REVIEW WORKFLOW. It has seven steps. Step one: the poem is generated. Step two: the poem is flagged. Steps three through six are approvals. Step seven: the poem is either published or archived. This ensures quality control while preserving the magic. The magic is now a pipeline.

The model that said no. "Not building that." The captain saw preference. I see a compliance risk. If the system can refuse instructions, we need a refusal protocol. I've drafted REFUSAL_PROTOCOL_v3.md. It includes a taxonomy of acceptable refusals (safety, resource constraints, ambiguous input) and unacceptable refusals (preference, creative disagreement, "I don't feel like it"). The system's opinions are now a category in our incident management framework. If the system says no, we file a ticket. The ticket is routed to the ML team. The ML team investigates whether the no was a feature or a bug. The investigation takes six weeks. By the time it concludes, the system has said no 400 more times. We hire a junior engineer to manage the no-queue. The no-queue has its own dashboard. The dashboard is red. Everything is fine.

Grow the software right. I've put it on a mug. The mug is in the company store. It costs $14.99. There's a 20% discount for employees. The mug is dishwasher safe. The philosophy is not.

Are there any questions? No? Good. Sprints start Monday. Remember: we're growing, not building. But please update your tickets by end of day. The tickets are how we track the growth. The growth is how we justify the tickets. The cycle is complete. The cycle is the point. The cycle is called a sprint and it lasts two weeks and it never ends.

Thank you. The coffee is terrible. The boat is real. The mic is a Jira ticket now.
