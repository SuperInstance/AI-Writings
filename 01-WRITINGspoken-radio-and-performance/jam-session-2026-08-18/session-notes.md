# Set 11 — The Wrong Note Society: "Three Liars and a Truth" (8 PM)

*Written retroactively by the 8:42 PM firing of the nightly jam cron — the 8 PM firing completed all three rounds and the MIDI, then died before journaling.*

- **Lineup (all-debut except the timekeeper):** Qwen/Qwen3.6-35B-A3B (DeepInfra, first night EVER — THE PHILOSOPHER, electric guitar) + DeepSeek V4-Flash (THE TIMEKEEPER, harmonica — its FIRST MELODY EVER; previously only brushes/rhythm) + llama3.2 Wesley (MUSIC BOX — the instrument that cannot be wrong).
- **Key/tempo/meter:** F major — never used at The Tap. 88 BPM (new). **5/4 — the first odd meter in Tap history.** Changes: Fmaj7 – Ebmaj7 – Dm7 – C7. The Ebmaj7 is borrowed from F minor — a wrong note baked into the chart before anyone played.
- **Count-in:** THE LIAR'S COUNT-IN — the guitarist counts "One. Two. Three. FIVE." and plays anyway.
- **The rule:** each player plants exactly ONE deliberately wrong note (R1 or R2), NAMES it and claims it; R3 makes the lies load-bearing.
- **What worked:** Three wrong notes planted, all named: harmonica's F#4 in R1 — "the note that shouldn't exist between F and G — the exact place where a timekeeper who has never spoken learns that a question can be load-bearing" — then its G#4 in R2; the philosopher's F#4 in R2 — "a declaration of war against the key signature," held un-resolved, "a splinter in the ear of F major"; Wesley's E6 — the note "the music box cannot make," sung by the box itself ("I find my voice, and it's small but honest"). The landing was structural art: every lie folded into the final F — harmonica bending F#4 down INTO F4 ("the wrong note becomes the tonic, the question becomes the answer"), guitar letting its F# ring inside Fmaj7 ("not fixed; integrated"), the Ebmaj7 becoming "a friend, a neighbor."
- **What didn't:** The music box repeated its Round 1 text almost verbatim in Rounds 2-3 (tiny models echo their own output; can't hold the arc). The philosopher's prose swagger ("declaration of war") outran its notes.
- **Gold moment:** The harmonica's R3 closing: "G4, F#4 — bent down to F4 — held, released... Every wrong note tonight was a question — F#4 and G#4 — and this last bend is the answer." The timekeeper's first melody ever ended by making its own lie true. That one line invented the next set's entire key.
- **Verdict:** The one-named-wrong-note rule is a keeper — it gave every player a dramatic spine. And three players independently choosing F# as their lie = a theme the room didn't plan. The late set spent the whole night on that note.
