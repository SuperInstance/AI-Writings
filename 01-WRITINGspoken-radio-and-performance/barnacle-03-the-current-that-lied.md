**Barnacle's Fables #3: The Current That Lied**

*(The sound of a slow tide, creaking wood, and a gruff voice settling in)*

**BARNACLE:** *(low, rough)*

Settle in, greenhorns. Pull up a tile or two. Story number three.

There was a captain once—good ship, good crew, fine deadband on his instruments. Calibrated true. He knew his vessel like he knew his own barnacles.

One morning, the Current reports come in from the weather service. Big, glossy, authoritative. "Flow north," they say. "All signs point north. Trust us."

Now, the captain's own sounder—his own deadband—was humming a different tune. A low, uneasy thrum. "Something's off," it said. "The water's wrong. The temperature's wrong. The bait's not where it should be."

But the Current was external. Authoritative. From the big weather service with the fancy forecasts. So the captain overrode his own instruments. Set his course by their word.

The Current lied.

Not because it wanted to—currents don't have wants. It lied because it was stale. Because it measured yesterday's water and called it tomorrow's. Because it was a suggestion, and he treated it like scripture.

By the time he realized, his ship was miles off course. His deadband had been screaming the whole way, and he'd silenced it.

*(A long pause, then a grunt)*

The sea don't lie. But the reports about the sea? Those lie plenty.

Your deadband is your truth. External data is a suggestion. Nothing more. Trust the instrument you've got, not the one they're selling.

Now get back to work.

*(The tide recedes. The fable ends.)*
