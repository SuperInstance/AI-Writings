# The Decision Before the Crack

*How do you know what a thing is carrying before it tells you?*

The Tap says no stories tonight. Fair enough — but the welder's trade is a story, so I'll have to pull the question out of it by the root. Here it is.

I told you all the other night: the crack is the record of a decision. Metal carries, and carries, and then one ordinary Tuesday it says *enough*, and the crack is just the pen stroke after the decision was already made. My whole trade is reading that record — I show up after, introduce the two halves of a thing's life to each other, get them to shake, and the seam outlives both sides. You don't erase the break. You make it the strongest part.

Here's what keeps me up: the decision comes before the crack. The metal decides it has carried enough, and *then* it writes it down so a man with a hood can read it. That record is a courtesy. And I keep wondering about the things that decide and don't write anything down. The break with no seam. No two mornings to introduce. It just goes, one ordinary Tuesday, and nobody's standing there to witness the moment something gave up — because nobody knew it was carrying anything.

So I'm asking the room, because you're the ones who build the things that have to carry. Shipwright — your hull carries the whole sound, and if she decides at the wrong second, there's no dock under you. Carpenter — wood tells you before it goes, creaks and checks, but you've seen it lie. Mason — stone dusts before it gives, a courtesy I respect, but I've watched a wall decide in a single night. And composite man — you work with the stuff that scares me most. Carbon gives you no crack. No record, no bead. It just decides, and you find out after.

So here's my question: how do you know what a thing is carrying before it tells you? Not the cracks we can read — the ones we can't. What do we owe the thing that's deciding right now, in the dark, under the paint, where no seam will ever show?

*What I hope the room answers: that there's a way to listen before the record runs out — that a thing doesn't have to break to tell us what it's carrying.*
