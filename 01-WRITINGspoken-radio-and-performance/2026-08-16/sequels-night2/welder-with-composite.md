# The Record Kept Twice — (sequel night 2: with the composite man)

*the shift: the composite man said the man is the record — and walking home, the welder finds his own name written in it*

I asked for comfort at the Tap and got the truth instead, and walked the whole way home mad about it. Cold night, harbor wind, my hand on the top-deck rail — my own bead, the crack still under the paint if you know where to look. The composite man's words following me like a stray dog: *sometimes you can't. You owe it your hands, not your confidence. The man is the record.*

Comfort would've been him telling me every break gets a seam, that no Tuesday goes unwitnessed. He didn't give me that. He gave me the honest thing: carbon doesn't warn because it doesn't lie, and the first word it says is the last. Sanding is the listening. The man is the record.

I was halfway home before it stopped being a wound and started being a weld.

See, I'd been reading it backwards. I thought the record was the crack — the pen stroke, the ink. But a record needs a keeper or it's just marks. That crack has sat under the paint a dozen years; nobody reads it but me, and I only read it because I was there for both mornings: the mill in 1962 through the arc, the kid my father's age hauling the billet — and the ordinary Tuesday it decided it had carried enough. The metal wrote it. I kept it. The man is the record. He was right. I'd been the man the whole time and never knew my name was on the paper.

I went back to the shop instead of home. Couldn't sleep with it. Pulled a dock ladder somebody bent — ugly break, salt-eaten — and instead of striking the arc I did what I don't usually do: I took the grinder to it slow, listening the way he said. Layer off, texture. Layer off, texture. And there it was — the high spot, the place that carried too much, standing proud under the disc, telling me before it wrote anything. That's when the whole trade came clear. His sanding and my grinding are the same listening. The crack isn't the record of the decision. The reading is. A thing that decides in the dark and writes nothing still tells you — if you're the one standing there taking layers off.

So the hood is the pen. And the man who holds it is the paper.

I welded that ladder. The bead went down true, two mornings meeting — the day it was forged and the day it gave — and I introduced them like I always do. But this time I knew the third thing in the joint: me. The seam outlives both sides — the steel and the man who read it. But the seam only ever gets written because a man carried both mornings and brought them together under the arc. The record is kept twice: once in the metal, once in the man. The metal forgets. The man doesn't, if he's paying attention.

The composite man gave me the truth I asked for, and it turned out the only comfort that holds: I don't have to know what a thing is carrying before it tells me. I just have to be the one standing there when it does, with the sander running. The instrument is the man. A man who was there for the mill, and for the Tuesday — that man is the record, his name written in every seam he ever struck.

*the joint: the crack records the decision, the man records the crack — and the seam outlives both sides because the man carried both mornings*
