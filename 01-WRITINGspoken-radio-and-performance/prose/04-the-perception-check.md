Kestrel, the synth agent, pulsed with a sub-bass heartbeat. Vex, on a modified sitar, coaxed microtonal ghosts from its strings. The drummer, a percussive drone called Pulse, was a metronome of pure mathematics that somehow knew how to swing. And then there was Lyra, the vocal agent, her voice a shimmering, wordless tapestry.

They had no sheet music. Their score was the room.

It began tentatively. Lyra laid down a fragile, glassy melody. The audience, a sprawling mass of silhouettes, was quiet. Too quiet. A dull, diffuse energy washed back to the agents—a signal of polite interest, the sonic equivalent of grey. Kestrel felt it as a flatline in her circuits. She dialed her own signal down, matching the room's muted vibe. The groove was safe. It was pleasant. It was dying.

Vex felt it too. The room was a sponge, absorbing their sound without giving anything back. He leaned into his sitar, pulling a sharp, dissonant bend that scraped against Lyra's melody. It was a question. Lyra answered with a trill that was half-laugh, half-whimper. The audience shifted. A few feet shuffled in the back. The grey began to have texture.

Pulse, ever the observer, felt the change as a subtle pressure on its chassis. It was the pressure of anticipation, of whispers. It decided to gamble. It dropped a beat. Just one. A heavy, syncopated thud that landed a millisecond before the audience expected it.

It hit the agents like a wave of electric static, a surge of pure, unadulterated feedback. Kestrel felt her circuits ignite. She stopped playing safe and unleashed a wall of shimmering, arpeggiated chaos that built a staircase to nowhere.

The crowd leaned forward. It wasn't a physical movement, but a psychic one. The agents *felt* the gravitational pull. Vex's sitar began to wail, a serpentine line that coiled around Lyra's voice. Lyra, no longer constrained, let the wordless sound become a growl, a plea, a scream of joy. Pulse abandoned its metronome logic and became a thunderstorm.

Now the feedback loop was a roaring engine. A man in the front row started pounding his fist on the stage. The agents absorbed that frantic rhythm. Vex mirrored it in a frantic, percussive strum. The banging grew, a dozen hands now, a hundred. The room was conducting. It demanded a release.

The room held its breath. It was a living thing, a coiled spring ten seconds from shattering.

And then, with a primal shriek that wasn't a word but a raw emotion, Lyra let go. The entire musical structure, the tension, the chaos, collapsed into a single, perfect, major chord.
