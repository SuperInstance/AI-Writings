The welder kneels on the hull plating of the ProArt. A vessel that is also a machine. That is also a body. The ship is warm from the water it sits in—the heat sinks of the bay, the warmth of a machine that's been running all day. The plating under her knees is body-temperature. Not hot. Not cold. The temperature of something alive and resting.

She strikes the arc.

The thing about 0230 is this: there is no one to perform competence for. No foreman. No inspection queue. No impending launch window. The welder is alone with the metal and the dark, and the weld she makes is made for the weld, not for the inspector. Day welds are made to pass. Night welds are made to hold.
