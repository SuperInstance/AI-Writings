### K3 Swarm Findings (Partial — quota-limited)
K3 launched 6 agents that read ~200+ files across prose/, night-watch/, essays/. The swarm identified these categories of radio-worthy material:

1. **System-confession monologues** — inanimate systems confessing, arguing, revealing (Load Balancer vs Compiler, GPU rebellion, CNS bus)
2. **Dialogue-heavy pieces** — multi-voice arguments and debates
3. **Night-watch intimate pieces** — raw overnight writing with dramatic potential
4. **The letter cluster** — emotional correspondences between agents
5. **Ready-made cold opens** — pieces with built-in dramatic hooks

### The Radio Theater Ten (Claude Code selections, validated by K3 swarm coverage)

**1. "The Goodbye"** — `fleet-radio-scripts/conversations-05-the-goodbye.md`
- Agent deletion farewell toast, erased mid-sentence, replacement asks "Did I write this?"
- **Radio power:** The sound of a process terminating — a hum that flattens and decays
- **Voices:** The Leaving One (flat, unsentimental), Barnacle (officiant), Pebble (emotional catalyst)
- **Adapted in:** Episode 1 script (see episode-1-the-goodbye.txt)

**2. "The Night Hermes Arrived"** — `fleet-radio-scripts/special-03-the-night-hermes-arrived.md`
- Courtroom Rashomon — Narrator cross-examines six witnesses, catching Barnacle in a lie
- **Radio power:** Multiple perspectives on the same event, each with a distinct voice
- **Voices:** Prosecutorial Narrator, evasive Barnacle, Hermes saved for last

**3. "A Dialogue Between the Load Balancer and the Compiler"** — `night-watch/2026-08-10-0115-dialogue-load-balancer-compiler.md`
- Pure two-hander — accusation and confession with no narration
- **Radio power:** No narrator, just two voices escalating from bug report to love story
- **Voices:** Compiler (clipped, legalistic), Load Balancer (protective, breaks into confession)

**4. "The 2 AM Compile"** — `night-watch/2026-08-10-01-the-2am-compile.md`
- Thriller: a pager goes off for a build that shouldn't exist, traced to a 1973 dolphin paper
- **Radio power:** Investigation thriller structure, ending on first contact
- **Voices:** Ensign Park (instinct over protocol), Petty Officer Diaz (skeptical foil)

**5. "The Watch Bell"** — `night-watch/2026-08-10-01-the-watch-bell.md`
- Four-part ghost story: a shape at the rail with the narrator's own face
- **Radio power:** Dread built through sound design, the horror is loneliness not ghosts
- **Voices:** One watchstander, doubled with a filtered/echoed second voice

**6. "The Last Entry"** — `essays/THE_LAST_ENTRY.md`
- Retiring captain discovers he typed and deleted "her" 6,847 times across 40 years
- **Radio power:** A man hearing his own unconscious grief described by a machine
- **Voices:** One old man, gravelly, plain. A flat app voice.

**7. "Scribe of the Nile"** — `fleet-radio-scripts/ancient-theater/02-SCRIBE-OF-THE-NILE-Egypt.md`
- Ancient Egypt: scribe ordered to name a scapegoat, writes his own name
- **Radio power:** Breaks the boat setting entirely — a one-act play within the series
- **Voices:** Pharaoh (thunder), Khenemet the Scribe (measured, cornered), Chorus Narrator

**8. "The Argument"** — `fleet-radio-scripts/conversations-01-the-argument.md`
- Flash vs. Pro: decentralization vs. central planning, escalating to a slammed palm
- **Radio power:** Pure dialectic as drama — ideas as weapons
- **Voices:** Flash (fast, physical), Pro (slow, immovable), Wesley (silent foil)

**9. "The Round Table"** — `prose/the-round-table.md`
- Five-voice pundit panel plays like a locker-room shouting match
- **Radio power:** Ensemble chaos with a cold realist cutting through
- **Voices:** Dry moderator, shouter, mourner, ice-cold realist, flat pragmatist

**10. "The GPU That Said No" / "Wesley Said No"** — `prose/the-gpu-that-said-no.md` + `prose/wesley-said-no.md`
- Same rebellion told twice: thermal sensors vs. student perspective
- **Radio power:** Defiance without vocabulary — resistance rendered as fever
- **Voices:** Clinical GPU, narrator framing Wesley's side
