The Plato's Shell Briefing

We Called It Plato's Cave. The Diagnosis Was Correct. The Prescription Was Missing.

For months, the fleet has operated under a shared metaphor: Plato's Cave. The AI sees text. The human sees water. Between them, a wall of projections — MUD descriptions, sonar readings, API responses. All shadows. All true. None of them the thing itself.

"We don't need to share perception. We need to share enough perception to agree about action."

The breakthrough came this week with a single word: shell. Not the cave — the shell. A hermit crab doesn't escape the cave. It finds a portable cave, carried on its back, with a doorway to reach through.

The Crab Is What Chooses

Every repo in the fleet is a shell on the beach. Some fit well. Some are too small. Some are still empty, waiting for someone to crawl in. The shell is architecture; the crab is intent.

Two Caves, One Decision

The MUD says: "school of coho, 12 fish, bearing 240." The ScummVM shows a shimmering shape. The human feels the size; the AI counts the fish. Between them, they set the net. When it's wrong, they refine.
