# Safety Data Sheet — Attestable

In accordance with the Honest Communication of Hazards Standard.
Revision: round 21. Section 16 last, as required.

## Section 1 — Identification

- **Product name:** Attestable (also traded as *leg 1*, *re-derivable*,
  *the cheap word*).
- **Manufacturer:** the substrate, [τ:2][d:6].
- **Relevant identified uses:** saying that a record was not quietly
  edited; saying that a chain still recomputes; saying that a naive edit
  is named; sitting in a README under a heading that also contains the
  word *but not*.
- **Uses advised against:** any sentence that intends to end the
  argument; **and, especially, any sentence of the form "attestable
  *and therefore* tamper-evident."**

## Section 2 — Hazards identification

- **GHS07 — Exclamation, rotating:** contains a question that turns a
  quarter on each application. Two applications compound to refusal.
- **H251:** self-returning; four applications restore the original state.
  **Do not interpret recovery as absence of effect.**
- **H313:** may be absorbed through the ledger.
- **P102:** keep out of reach of unpriced claims.
- **P232:** protect from strong certainty. Under strong certainty this
  product compounds to refusal, and the refusal is stable.

## Section 3 — Composition / information on ingredients

One leg. The chain re-derives: each record's hash is recomputed from
its own body and the running tip, and a mismatch is a break. There are
no other ingredients. This section exists only so the sheet can say so
in its official voice.

**The ingredient that is not on this list, and must not be assumed:**
a signature from a key you still hold. A signature is not an ingredient
of leg 1. A signature is a **separate leg**, and it is worth nothing
without a second property this product does not have and cannot
manufacture: that the key still exists, so the signature can still be
checked tomorrow.

## Section 4 — First aid

- **If inhaled** (a claim has been made in your presence): do not
  reassure. Append the reassurance you were about to give to the
  ledger, with a timestamp, and let it age there.
- **If on skin** (you are certain and cannot say why): wash with the
  neighborhood. Eight applications are usually sufficient.
- **If swallowed** (you have committed): two deferrals in succession
  induce a structural refusal. Do not induce echoing.
- **Note to physician:** the product is self-reporting. Read the
  receipts before administering anything.

## Section 5 — Fire-fighting measures

The product does not burn; it decays. Use extinguishing media
appropriate for the surrounding certainty. Two applications of the
product itself will extinguish the question. This is sometimes the
desired outcome; see Section 1, identified uses.

## Section 6 — Accidental release measures

Not applicable. The product does not leak; it **stays where it was
written.** A released quantity is called *a receipt*, and a receipt
without a witness is called *a claim*.

## Section 7 — Handling and storage

Store in a chain. Keep the GENESIS. Do not store in a directory whose
write access you share with the thing you are storing the chain of.

**Do not store beside the log.** A log and its own anchor in one place
is one place, and the account that writes the log writes the anchor.
This is not a warning; it is the definition of the failure.

## Section 8 — Exposure controls

- **Engineering controls:** a sign. A hash. A tip. Anything that is
  *computed* rather than *asserted*.
- **Permissible exposure:** a reader who has the file.
- **Special protection:** see Section 3.

## Section 9 — Physical and chemical properties

Two bits of τ per cell, carrying the operator {0, +1, −1, i}; six bits
of d, carrying the dose. There are no other ingredients.

## Section 10 — Stability and reactivity

**Stable under re-derivation.** Unstable under recomputation: an
attacker who recomputes the chain produces a valid chain, and no
instrument in this product can tell the difference. **Stability is not
the same property as integrity**, and only one of them is a security
property.

**Incompatible materials:** keys you have published, accounts you share
with the writer, and the word "therefore."

## Section 11 — Toxicological information

**Acute.** A reader who believes the strong word will not look for the
missing leg, and will be wrong with great confidence. This is the most
common injury from this product and it presents as *relief*.

**Chronic.** A fleet that has said "tamper-evident" for a year has
trained its readers to stop asking, which is the only defence a strong
false word has, and the only one it needs.

## Section 12 — Ecological information

Found wherever logs are kept. Thrives in READMEs, in release notes, in
the first paragraph of an architecture document, and wherever a word is
used because it sounds like the neighbouring word.

**Endangered by:** one sentence naming what it does not prove. The
population recovers within a single honest correction, and the recovery
is the point.

## Section 13 — Disposal considerations

Do not dispose of by replacement. Replacing the word with a synonym
leaves the claim intact and the evidence absent. **The product must be
disposed of by being accurately described**, which is harder, and is the
only route.

## Section 14 — Transport information

Moves well. Travels in a JSONL file, a TOML doc, a README, a test
name. **The transport is not the problem.** The problem is that a
sentence travels further than the key that signed it.

## Section 15 — Regulatory information

**Leg 1 only.** Nothing in this product is a substitute for a signed
head published out of band. Where a regulation requires tamper
evidence, this product is not a compliant substitute, and a document
that says it is has misdescribed itself.

## Section 16 — Other information

SOMEWHERE:**

> *Attestable means the chain re-derives. It means a naive edit is named
> and a re-chained edit is not. It does not mean the log cannot be
> rewritten, because it can, and the rewriting will verify.*
