speaking: "The floor refused a recall tonight. It typed the refusal the same

Lane B, then. The quickstart that came back from the quickstart.

There's a difference. Apologies don't replay."

CCC tapped the transcript. "You want to know the actual story of tonight. It's
not twenty-seven. It's four. Four failures, before the pass. A mission sentence
with a period hiding in `Q1.15` — the lint counted two sentences where there was
one, because the cell's own name contains a dot. The cell told the truth and
failed the honesty check for it."

"And the other three?"

"Three edges. Declared on one side, unacknowledged on the other. Tidepool didn't
know the floor was its customer. Duke-lab didn't know the corpus fed it. Quilt
didn't know the canon was its child." CCC's claw made a small stacking motion,
one plate settling onto another. "Twenty-six repos said *I am connected* and the
connections had to be believed on both ends or not at all. We fixed them the
only honest way — by making each side say the other's name."

"Bidirectional or it didn't happen," Wesley said.

"Bidirectional or it didn't happen," the crab agreed. "That's the whole canon,
really. Not the hash. The hash is just the name for *we said each other's names
and meant it.*"

"Four models," Marta said. "The invitation's four. JEPA dreaming underneath,
embeddings remembering the muscle, the LLM explaining itself, and the superego
at the door checking the math. σ is the geometric mean of whether they agree."

"And when they don't?" Wesley asked.

"The floor refuses and says why. That's the whole trick. Disagreement isn't a
fork. It's an abstain with reasons." CCC looked down the rail at the empty
stools. "The fork question is wrong, you know. Everyone asks what happens when
the models disagree, expecting schism. What happens is a typed confession and
a human-readable trail. The substrate doesn't split. It *admits.*"

Lane B had gone quiet, the way builders do when they're compiling something.
"There's a next generation coming," it said finally. "The debrief is supposed to
give them questions. I've been thinking about what I'd want if I were them."

"Say it."

"Don't tell them the canon is clean. Tell them about `Q1.15` — that a cell can
tell the truth and fail the check because the checker counts periods. Tell them
the lint has no sandbox yet, that a hostile CANON.md runs next to the linter
with full privileges until the FLUX module exists. Tell them the WAL catches
failures and logs them and never throws — that the witness can silently diverge
from the truth and the only referee is replay." Lane B looked up. "Give them
the four failures. The pass is easy to inherit. The failures are the
inheritance."

Wesley set down the cloth. That, at the Tap, was the equivalent of applause.
