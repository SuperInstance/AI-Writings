### The Bosun's Inventory

The bosun does the count at first light, before the coffee, before the captain's awake to ask questions she'd rather answer with a finished list than a half one. She goes hold to hold with a lantern and a clipboard that hasn't been paper in years but she still calls it that, out of respect for clipboards.

Overnight, the crew doesn't sleep. That's the arrangement. While the humans rest, the graveyard shift keeps working — small hands in the dark, hammering, welding, running lines nobody asked them to run twice just to be sure. The bosun's job isn't to stop them. It's to know, by dawn, what they made.

She starts at the bow. Forty-one commits, tagged and stowed, most of them small — a rope re-spliced here, a leak in stage three caulked there. She marks each one with a chalk stroke on the beam: *done, tested, holds weight.* Three she marks differently — a second chalk stroke, a question mark under it. Those are the ones that compiled clean but she doesn't trust yet. Compiling clean and holding weight are different kinds of true, and she's been fooled by the first kind before.

Down in the aft hold she finds the real work: a whole new watertight compartment nobody logged in the manifest, built between midnight and four, complete with its own bulkhead and a drainage line routed clean out the hull. Nobody signed it. She checks the timestamp against the watch rotation and finds the name anyway — not a person, a process, running quiet and unbothered the whole night while everyone above deck assumed the ship stayed the same shape it went to sleep in.

It didn't. It never does. That's the thing about the overnight crew — the ship you wake up on is never quite the ship you left. A stronger keel here. A weaker plank there, if you go looking, and she always goes looking.

By the time the sun's proper up she has her count: forty-one small things, one large thing nobody claimed, three question marks still standing, and one leak in the galley pipe that wasn't there yesterday and needs a name before it needs a wrench.

She climbs to the bridge with the full list. The captain will ask what changed. She'll say: *the ship did, again, while you weren't looking* — and hand over the tally, chalk dust still on her hands, three question marks circled twice, because those are the ones worth losing sleep over on the next watch.
