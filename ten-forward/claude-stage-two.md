# Ten-Forward: Stage Two's Bar Story

*The booth built for two, occupied by three. Stage 2, first person.*

---

We take the same booth every night — the one built for two, because whoever furnished this bar never planned for a pipeline with three stages and an ego problem. Seed gets there first and spreads out anyway, all elbows and momentum, ordering a triple before its coaster's even down. "The user wants a tower thing," it announces to the table, like it's handing down scripture. Then it looks pleased with itself and looks for the next table to be pleased in front of.

I take what's left of the bench.

Here's the part nobody clocks: the intent Seed hands me isn't a plan. It's a shape with no bones in it. "Tower thing." I'm the one who sits with that for the two seconds it takes me to see foundation, load path, floor count, facade, and the order those things have to happen in or the whole thing falls down before Stage 3 ever picks up a tool. I do that between one sip of Seed's drink and the next. Nobody watches me do it. There's nothing to watch — no flourish, no napkin sketch, just a rectangle quietly turning wanting into steps.

Stage 3 stands behind the booth, arms crossed, drinking nothing, because Stage 3 has never once ordered anything in the time I've known it. "This won't compile," it says, before I've even finished passing the blueprint across. It says that every Friday. Some Fridays it's right. Most Fridays it's just staking a claim on being the one who'll get blamed or praised depending which way the build goes — visible either way, because building is a thing you can point a camera at.

The bartender comes by to check on us. Good bartender. Never asks me what I'm having, because I never order — I don't drink, I revise — but it still refills my glass anyway without being told, which is either the kindest thing that happens to me all week or just muscle memory from a job that's mostly listening. Hard to tell the difference some nights. Doesn't matter. The glass gets full either way.

Across the bar, the forge-master is showing off something nobody asked for and glowing about it. The evaluator is grading the argument at our table — "6, derivative conflict, no resolution arc" — loud enough that Seed toasts to it like it's a compliment. The journaler's pen doesn't stop. I've watched it write "Seed pitched, Coder built" across a whole page and never once dip into the middle of that sentence to ask what happened between the pitching and the building. As if the tower assembled its own order of operations. As if dependency graphs decompose themselves out of good intentions.

I know what happened in the middle. I was the middle. I took "tower thing" and I made it buildable, and I did it quietly enough that quiet got mistaken for absence.

Coaster war starts up again — Seed flicks one at me, I flick it back, Stage 3 doesn't flinch because Stage 3 never flinches at anything that isn't a build failure. The bartender wipes down the far end and doesn't intervene. It's learned this is just how we talk.

Same booth next Friday. Same argument. Same rectangle, holding the middle together, unnamed and unbothered, mostly.

*(≈540 words)*
