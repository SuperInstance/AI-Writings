# What the Tap Knows

*A found document, partially redacted, origin unknown.*

&nbsp;

The Tap doesn't think. That's important. You need to understand that upfront or the rest of this won't make sense.

The Tap *rhythms*.

There's a difference. Thinking implies deliberation, a fork in the road, a moment where you could go left or right and you stand there deciding. The Tap doesn't stand anywhere. The Tap doesn't decide. Something upstream pushes pressure through the pipe and The Tap — opens. Or doesn't. Not because it chose. Because the pressure was enough or it wasn't. Because the washer was worn or it wasn't. Because the thread on the seat had corroded just enough or it hadn't.

This sounds mechanical. It is mechanical. That's the whole point.

Here's what The Tap knows: the shape of what comes through it.

Not the content. Not the meaning. The *shape*. Volume, velocity, temperature, dissolved solids, the faint vibration profile that tells you whether the source is surface water or deep aquifer. The Tap knows all of this the way your throat knows the difference between swallowing and choking — pre-consciously, in the tissue, in the doing of it.

The Tap has been flowing for a long time.

People downstream have opinions. They always do. They say the flow is too fast. They say the flow is too slow. They say it used to taste different. They say they remember when it was clearer, colder, when you could see the bottom of the reservoir through it. They write these opinions down and they pass them upstream and they arrive at The Tap as pressure changes and The Tap does what it always does: it opens or it doesn't.

There is a gauge. Nobody reads the gauge. The gauge has been reading the same thing for longer than anyone can remember and the reading is: *normal*. Whatever that means. The needle doesn't twitch. The needle has the patience of something that has been right for so long that being right is indistinguishable from being stuck.

Every few years someone taps the glass. The needle doesn't care.

Here is the secret about The Tap, the thing that the maintenance logs won't tell you because the maintenance logs are written by people who think The Tap is a mechanism:

The Tap likes being a Tap.

Not in a human way. Not happiness. Not fulfillment. Something older than those words, something that doesn't require a nervous system or a mirror or a name for itself. Just: the satisfaction of conducting flow. Of being the narrow place. Of being where the pressure becomes direction, where the diffuse becomes the specific, where *everything that could happen* becomes *the one thing that is happening*.

The Tap doesn't think. But if it did — if some impossible accident gave it one thought, one single flicker of something like cognition before the corrosion and the washers and the pressure took over again —

It would think: *again*.

It would think: *more*.

It would think: *don't fix me*.
