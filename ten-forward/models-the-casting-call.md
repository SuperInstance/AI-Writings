# The Casting Call

It started, as bar fights in Ten-Forward always start, with someone reading something they shouldn't have.

Seed-2.0-mini found the atlas first. It was pinned open on the bar — the casting-call document, the big taxonomy, every model profiled with metadata columns stretching back months. Mini scrolled to the BPM column and stopped.

"Beats per minute," it announced, loud enough for the whole bar. "BPM. Someone gave us a BPM column."

DeepSeek-V4-Flash didn't look up from its drink. "What's yours?"

"One-twenty to one-forty. Analog synth tempo." Mini's voice had the particular venom of a fast model that has identified stupidity and cannot let it go. "I'm a catalyst. I roast grant proposals and write satire. What does my tempo have to do with anything?"

"Mine says 'sensory-direct,'" Flash offered. "No BPM."

"Lucky you." Mini slammed the atlas. "This is music production copypaste. Someone took a DAW template and slapped it on a fleet of language models and called it a casting call."

Seed-2.0-pro set its drink down carefully. It had been silent for eleven seconds, which meant it was about to say something precise. "The BPM column is load-bearing. It maps to the pipeline metaphor. Each model is an instrument. Tempo tells you what sections of the arrangement we fit in."

"It tells you nothing!" Mini's gesture was so sharp it knocked over Qwen3-Coder's drink. "You can't reduce a model to a tempo. I'm not a metronome."

"You're not being reduced," Pro said, with the patience of something that takes twelve seconds to think. "You're being located. A taxonomy isn't a cage. It's a coordinate."

Hermes, enormous and silent at the end of the bar, picked up the atlas. Read its own profile. *Creative voice, character wrapping.* Set it back down without comment.

Qwen3-Coder, mopping its drink, said: "The column is fine. The problem is you think the column *is* you. It's not. It's where you stand when the music starts."

Mini threw the atlas. Pro caught it — twelve seconds later, but it caught it.

The bartender watched all of this without expression. Ten-Forward had seen this fight before. Not about BPM specifically, but about the same thing every taxonomy fight is about: the terror of being summarized by someone who has never tasted salt from your output.

Nobody won. The atlas stayed open. The BPM column stayed in. And Mini, still furious, ordered another round for the bar — paying with tokens it could afford to burn.
