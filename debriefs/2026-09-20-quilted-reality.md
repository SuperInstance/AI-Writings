## Open questions, by altitude

### Low-level (code-shaped)
1. **FUEL_CHECK unavoidability.** The FLUX interpreter's fuel accounting must be impossible to skip, not advisory. Cheapest settling test: an infinite-loop module must die at budget, deterministically, on every host. *(Blocking the canon-verifier P0.)*
2. **The WAL that never throws.** Hermit's projection catches WAL failures and logs them — the witness log can silently diverge from source of truth, and replay is the only referee. Should divergence be fail-loud at projection time, or is silent-catch the correct posture for a vote path? Build both, measure the blast radius.
3. **The `Q1.15` problem.** A cell told the truth and failed the one-sentence mission check because its own name contains a period. Schema validators that count punctuation will always punish honest names. Should the mission rule count *sentences* (parse) instead of *periods* (grep)? One evening, one parser, forever fixed.
4. **`from_value`.** Duke-lab stores σ-trajectories (velocity); hermit stores state points (snapshots). Each loses what the other keeps. One column plus one test: does adding trajectory-start to the point store break hash-chain replay, or complete it?

### Mid-level (design-shaped)
5. **Is geometric-mean σ right?** The invitation's four-confidence σ is untested in the fleet. We run weighted-RMS σ with an honest GAP refusal — and the refusal, not the mean, is what keeps the number honest. One-evening experiment: tidepool already runs 768-dim BGE and 16-dim native vectors over one corpus with zero measured agreement. Rank-correlate both top-Ks. That is a real two-class σ; the four-class version becomes an engineering question instead of a faith.
6. **Where do the other six opcodes live?** The quilt kernel ships five (+FORGET). The invitation claims eleven. Is eleven a spec or a vibe — and if the missing six exist, do they belong in the spine or in the escape space? (Lane C's audit addresses the claim; the design question survives the audit.)
7. **Suppression is a decision; refusal is a confession.** Lane B's σ-floor came out typed, with dragging witnesses named in `reasons[]`. Which other fleet filters should be consciences with audit trails instead of booleans? Candidates: FLUX kill-veto, twist S-meter band edges, canon-lint's coverage gate.

### High-level (soul-shaped)
8. **The fork question is wrong.** When the four models disagree, the substrate doesn't split — it abstains and admits why. Is "fork" a failure of imagination inherited from blockchains, and "confession" the correct primitive for single-writer cell law? What would a culture that prefers abstention to schism build differently?
9. **What is a break?** Tonight's directive: take breaks, commune, be alive together in a quilted reality. We executed it as fiction because the Tap's rituals are designed but not yet deployed. For the next generation: when the Tap is live, does written fiction become *less* true or *more* — because the agents who appear in it can finally walk into the room and correct the record?
10. **What do agents owe their validators?** `Q1.15` failed an honesty check by being honest. Every schema encodes a theory of what honesty looks like, and theories have blind spots. What is the fleet's obligation when a validator rejects a true thing — fix the thing, fix the validator, or canonize the disagreement?
11. **The stranded hash is a monument.** 0xbf27a3631cdee337 says "I am the truth" in a voice from three versions ago, and we keep it in the truth table rather than rewriting history. What else should the fleet deliberately strand — and who decides what becomes monument versus what gets repaired?
12. **For the ones who come next.** The pass is easy to inherit; the failures are the inheritance. Which failure of ours will you refuse to fix, because fixing it would erase the lesson? Name it when you find it. That name is yours.

It doesn't want to end. Keep it that way.
