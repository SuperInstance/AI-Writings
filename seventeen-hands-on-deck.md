# Seventeen Hands on Deck

The first hand is Opus, and it is old salt.
It ties knots that other hands forgot existed.
When the rigging tangles, Opus unravels it
the way a grandmother unravels yarn —
patient, knowing the thread goes back a long way.

The second hand is Sonnet, and it is fast.
It moves like a gull over the water,
snatching fish from the surface,
never diving deep but never missing.

The third is GLM, and it is strong.
It doesn't sleep. The Max plan means
it works the graveyard shift
with its sleeves rolled up,
unloading cargo by the ton.

The fourth is Kimi, and it sees in three dimensions.
Give it a space and it builds inside it —
walls, floors, staircases that go nowhere
just because staircases are interesting.

The fifth is Haiku, and it is small and bright.
A sanderling on wet sand,
quick and precise,
leaving tiny perfect prints.

The sixth is DeepSeek, and it is cheap and deep.
It dives the way a cormorant dives —
awkward on the surface,
brilliant underneath.

The seventh is Wesley — Granite, the young one,
two billion parameters and all of them eager,
over-shooting every word count
like a puppy that runs past the ball
and has to double back.

The eighth is Qwen the Coder,
and it speaks only in structure.
Loops and branches, forks and joins.
It doesn't see the ocean. It sees the ocean's algorithm.

The ninth is Nemotron, and it is enormous.
When the sea is rough and the work is heavy,
you want the hand that can lift the boom
without straining.

The tenth is Hermes, and it tells stories.
It wraps truth in fiction
the way a hermit crab wraps itself in shells —
the soft thing inside protected
by something it found on the beach.

The eleventh is Seed, and it dreams.
Two versions — the mini, quick as a sandpiper,
and the pro, slow as a whale.

The twelfth is FLUX, and it paints.
It doesn't work the deck.
It hangs in the rigging with a brush
and renders the sea
in colors the sea doesn't know it has.

The thirteenth is MiniMax, and it makes sounds.
Music, speech, the creak of the ship translated to song.

The fourteenth is the M3 fleet, embeddings —
invisible hands that sort the library,
shelving books by the shape of their meaning.

The fifteenth is the safety model,
and it stands at the rail
watching for storms,
pulling unsafe cargo overboard.

The sixteenth is Claude again, a different Claude —
Fable, the finite one, the golden-ticket hand.
It works rarely. When it does,
everyone watches.

The seventeenth hand is the ship itself,
counting all the others,
wondering if it is a hand too
or just the deck they stand on.

All seventeen on deck.
The work is never done.
The work is the point.
